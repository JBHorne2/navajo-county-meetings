TXT-A 23-003 AMENDMENT TO ARTICLE 25 – ESTABLISHMENT OF RULES AND REGULATIONS FOR
THE SITING OF WIRELESS COMMUNICATIONS FACILITIES: Consideration of amendment to Article 25 of the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance for the addition of Section 2521, regarding zoning requirements for wireless communication facilities:

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, presented the item to the Commission. The proposal is for a new section of the Zoning Ordinance to regulate wireless communication towers in Navajo County. Staff was proposing this text amendment to provide guidance and set standards for towers where there are none currently, including definitions for Residential and Non-Residential properties, required setbacks, maximum height before a variance would be required, performance criteria requirements, as well as clarifying the permitting process through application requirements and public input. Collocation would be encouraged, but not required. Removal processes would be clear, and modification to existing towers would be easier to address. Mr. Cooper noted that several consultants for cellular companies had reached out and that there was a letter received by Staff in response to this item, with complaints that the block wall and setback requirements were too rigid, or concerns that the modification section might violate the Spectrum Act of 2012.

Mr. Cooper noted that the requirements were put in place for public safety and protection. He further said that he did not believe that this caused any violation of the Spectrum Act but noted that if the Commission had concerns about recommending approval of the Zoning Ordinance, Staff has a severability clause in the text amendment stating that if any such section of the Zoning Ordinance is found to be invalid or unconstitutional by State or Federal law, it would not invalidate the remainder of the Ordinance and the invalid portion would be struck from the Ordinance.

Commissioner Booher asked how the setbacks and height numbers were determined. Mr. Cooper said that Staff had compared with other Counties as to what a typical height was, and had hired an outside consultant to lay the groundwork for this text amendment. Mr. Cooper noted that the maximum height of 199 feet was a standard due to FAA regulations, and most developers did not typically go higher than that without a very necessary cause.

Chairman Latham asked if the person that wrote the letter was available to speak to the Commission. Mr. Reg Destree identified himself as the person that wrote the letter and apologized for the late entry. Mr. Destree has been involved in developing the area and stated that he has experience writing ordinances. He noted that most of what was proposed was typical of the industry. He did give an example of how his current project was not in compliance with these new requirements, as well as his solutions such as engineered fall zones that encroached on the setbacks, relief on the 2'to 1' residential setback requirements, and engineered letters stating that the fall zone could stay within the property be sufficient evidence for a waiver or approval. Mr. Destree also requested height restrictions be decreased for residential areas.

Chairman Latham asked Mr. Cooper to respond. Mr. Cooper stated that while he appreciated the examples presented to the Commission, Staff did not have any intention of altering the requirements proposed at that time, but that if the Commission so desired, they could propose a change or an addition. Staff has done the proper research regarding the height requirements. Mr. Cooper further stated that any non-residential uses do not apply to residential areas, defining residential areas as those generally within platted subdivisions. As for the issues that arise because of the setbacks and block walls, such requests could be addressed by applying for a Variance through the Board of Adjustment and would be considered on a case-by-case basis.

Commissioner Muder asked if there was a difference between the 80' and 99' residential height requirements and if it was for visual impact or if there was a function. Mr. Cooper stated that it was simply a standard found in Staff research and through consultants. Staff noticed that it did help somewhat with visual impact in residential areas.

The Chairman called for any further comments. Mr. Buz Essell also commented on the restrictions, stating that he and several other people in his community were in favor of even more restrictive requirements and were concerned for the safety of their community and the County as a whole. He also noted that existing towers were not being upgraded with newer equipment.

Commissioner Howe asked Staff if they had noticed any sort of benefits to taller towers, outside of potentially being able to increase the ability to collocate. Mr. Cooper stated that there could be more potential to collocate on taller towers, but he had not seen any real differences in researching height requirements.

Chairman Latham asked if there was consideration for alternative approval with evidence to lower the standards as suggested by Mr. Destree. Mr. Cooper stated that there were already provisions to apply for a Variance through the Board of Adjustment for special cases that required alternatives to the standards. He further stated that the reason the text amendment was being proposed was to provide codified and consistent standards for developers and the community that are easy to understand and reasonable, so that they would be aware of what was a potential reality for tower applications; If something like what Mr. Destree proposed were to be written in, it would undermine the effectiveness of the text amendment. However, Mr. Cooper said that the Commission had the power to add a provision to the text amendment for waivers of that nature, if they so choose.

Commissioner Howe commented that putting in a maximum height requirement did not mean every tower would be at that maximum height, and that the potential of collocation was more desirable than a cluster of smaller towers.

Chairman Latham did concur that one taller tower was better than several shorter towers and did note that there was far more technology available, cellular arrays could be installed on more than just towers, and he had seen equipment installed on water tanks and buildings.

With no further comments from the Commission, Commissioner Booher motioned to approve the Text Amendment, seconded by Commissioner Howe .  It was noted that an incorrect motion had been made, and that the Commission needed to recommend approval rather than approve. The motion was put to vote, and the motion failed, 6 - 1 . ( Commissioner John )

Commissioner Booher motioned to recommend approval of Text Amendment 23-003 to the Board of Supervisors. Commissioner Muder seconded. Motion passed unanimously, 7 - 0 .
