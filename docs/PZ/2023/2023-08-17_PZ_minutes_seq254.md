SUP 23-009 AZ11-153 ROOSTER WIRELESS COMMUNICATION FACILITY, DISTRICT III: Consideration of a request by Pinnacle Consulting for a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction of an 80' monopole wireless communication tower and associated facilities in the White Mountain Lakes area. APN#: 304-36-003H, T11N, R22E, S13 of the GSRM. Owner: Richard C. and Pauline G. Brooksby. Agent: Pinnacle Consulting.

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, presented the item to the Commission. Mr. Cooper showed the Commission an overview of the area and the various landmarks and features within the vicinity of the proposed tower. The project was located within the White Mountain Lakes area just off Bourdon Ranch Road. Mr. Cooper stated that the Applicant proposed an 80-foot Monopole Wireless Communications tower and associated facilities. Mr. Cooper shared with the Commission a site plan of the project, as well as the side views of the proposed tower. Mr. Cooper also showed the Commission some simulated visuals of what the tower would look like once erected.

Mr. Cooper then went over the Applicant's community outreach efforts. The Applicants hosted a public meeting on June 7th, 2023 and Mr. Cooper provided the Applicant's summary of questions and concerns that had been brought up by the public, along with a list of attendees.

Mr. Cooper then shared with the Commission a Determination of No Hazard to Air Navigation received late that morning from the FAA. Mr. Cooper stated that the document would be included in the Board of Supervisors packets and provided paper copies to the Commission as Staff had received it just that morning, long after uploading the packet for the Commission.

Mr. Cooper then went over Staff's recommended conditions if the project was approved.

Commissioner Muder asked Staff to clarify the stipulation regarding construction time. Mr. Cooper explained that the Applicant would have to start construction within 12 months, or the Special Use Permit's privileges (SUP) would lapse.

Chair Latham asked Staff to explain the removal process for the components and tower if it is not utilized. Mr. Cooper explained that once a component or the tower was no longer in use the component would be removed, or in the event of the tower no longer being in use, the entire facility would be removed.

With no further questions, Chair Latham opened the Public Comment period.

There were no comments in favor of the project.

There were several members of the public that spoke against the project, including:
Steven-Charles Brown, Anthony Martinez, Tyler Legg, Buz Essel, Tony Tangelos, Dave Smith, Robert Meyer

Concerns from the public included:

If the Geotechnical Report was the same as an Environmental Review and if the applicant had gone through proper procedures.

Lack of Access to the Application.

Potential Health issues due to the 5G that could impact wildlife and children.

Property Values decreasing.

If the tower was even necessary, as there was existing coverage for the area.

5G causing Radar Altimeter issues with aircraft.

The tower being in the landing flight path from the East approach, adding that changing the flight path would cause an annoyance to the neighborhood.

The proposed tower being too close to the airport and helipads.

Chair Latham asked Staff to address the concerns of the Public. Mr. Cooper addressed the concerns in turn:

A Geotechnical Report has to do with studying the soil composition, to see if it would be able to support the weight of the tower and a fully loaded fire truck in case of a fire. These are not the same as an Impact Study, but that would come as part of the NEPA process with the Federal Regulations that the Applicant would have to abide by, as stated in the conditions of the SUP.

To address the comment that the application was not accessible, the documents submitted were available online and could be accessed through the website's agenda software.  The only case where a document is not uploaded for public view is when it is submitted late, as was the case with materials supplied by the Public and the FAA letter. Mr. Cooper further detailed where to find the archives used by Navajo County, as well as how to contact Staff if there was an issue with the uploaded documents.

As for the possible health hazards, Staff has not received any peer-reviewed viable evidence that proved that 5G was harmful to health or wildlife. Mr. Cooper stated that the Applicant had a Radio Frequency (RF) Engineer available to speak to the Commission about the health and safety aspects of RF waves.

As far as the information regarding the property value, that was outside of Staff purview, and would be up to the Board of Supervisors to consider.

To address the necessity for the tower, Applicants are required to provide proof of need, and the applicants had submitted an RF study and had an RF Engineer online to address any questions the Commission may have. If there was not a proven need for the tower, it would be within the purview of the Commission to recommend denial, and Board of Supervisors to deny the SUP, per the Zoning Ordinance.

As for how 5G could affect Radar Altimeters, Staff was aware of the statement made by the FAA saying that there was a possibility that some models of altimeters could be affected but noted that there was guidance for adjusting the equipment to make up for the possible interference with extra shielding or by adjusting the settings on the equipment per FAA standards, and the FAA had listed contacts for reporting Altimeter issues.

As far as the Flight Path being affected, Staff has the document from the FAA stating that the tower would not cause issues, and Staff was deferring to Federal recommendation.

Chair Latham addressed the Public, stating that the FAA letter noted that the physical tower was in an acceptable location and did not address 5G.

Commissioner Rogers asked if the Applicant was available to speak. Mr. Cooper Confirmed that he was.

Vice-Chair Smith stated that she wished for an unbiased study as a resource to use as a standard, noting how misinformation was an issue.

Mr. Cooper stated that the recently passed Cell Tower Ordinance Amendment had been researched by an outside source that had not indicated any concerns over new technologies. Mr. Cooper also noted that Staff has not found, nor received, any peer-reviewed studies that proved 5G caused undue harm or health concerns.

Commissioner Rogers asked Staff how many 5G towers were already in the County. Mr. Cooper replied that unfortunately Staff does not have an estimate as some towers were outside of the County's jurisdiction, within the Municipalities, or on Forest Service land and many towers within Navajo County's jurisdiction had been updated.

Commissioner Rogers asked if there was a stipulation the Commission could add that if 5G was found to cause undue health effects that the tower would be removed. Mr. Cooper responded that, if the Commission wished to add a recommendation, it was within the Commission's ability, after consulting with Legal.

Chair Latham noted that while he knew it was not easy to trust the authorities, sometimes it was necessary to have faith that the Public's best interests were at heart.

Commissioner Howe then asked about the proximity of the tower to the helipads and questioned if the Applicant had considered other locations further away from the airport.

Mr. John Katsiris, the Applicant, stated that other locations had been explored within the needed range but stated that the current property owner was the only one that had agreed to lease the land for the tower. Mr. Katsiris further stated that the project had already been registered with the FAA and FCC, and the other ideal locations were owned by the Forest Service from which it was difficult to lease land.

Mr. Katsiris also stated that their Engineer was available to speak to the Commission to explain how the 5G radio frequency worked.

Mr. Steven Kennedy gave a detailed explanation of the differences between harmful ionizing radio waves and non-ionizing radio waves. 5G used non-ionizing radio waves, and the side effects would be generating heat. Mr. Kennedy further detailed how the Federal standards dictated the accepted levels of energy, and how their research across several different agencies, including the FCC, OSHA, and several people from the medical community determined a set limit for the absorption rate of energy or heat from these transmitters. Mr. Kennedy stated that the acceptable limit was 1.4 watts per centimeter squared, over a period of 30 minutes. Mr. Kennedy then explained that this measurement was what was considered safe for a working environment, such as rooftop access to the transmitters, and half of this measurement was what was considered safe for the General Public, noting that holding a cellphone would expose you to half of the limit for the General Public. Mr. Kennedy began to explain what the level of absorption rate would be for the General Public around the tower, explaining that at ground level it would only be 1/10th of the safe limit for the General Public.

Mr. Kennedy had to be cut short on his presentation for time constraints and experienced some technical issues before he could detail any further and rejoined after the discussion continued.

Commissioner Rogers noted that there was not enough viable information to make decisions based on potential health concerns but did wish to find out more about the positioning of the tower in relation to the airport.

Commissioner Rogers then asked the Chair for Mr. Anthony Martinez, an Air Medical Team Supervisor from Air Evac that came to speak in opposition of the project, to address the Commission for more information. Chair Latham allowed this request.

Commissioner Rogers asked if the fact that the neighbors complained about the noise and forced the pilots to only be able to approach and land in certain areas had anything to do with why this tower was an issue for the pilots, and if other options for landing had been considered.

Mr. Martinez stated that the weather patterns around the area made it very difficult to navigate the airspace and still comply with noise abatement, noting that the pilots would receive several complaints if there were any deviations from the approved flight paths.

Commissioner Rogers asked if the abatement was FAA enforced or if it was only accommodating the neighborhood. Mr. Martinez deferred to Mr. Tangelos to answer this question.

Mr. Tony Tangelos, an owner of the Airpark, confirmed that they were accommodating neighbors. Mr. Tangelos explained that there were two reasons the helicopter pilots approached from the east. The FAA's regulations required aircraft to fly a certain height above residences and, given how close the homes were to the runway, it posed a challenge to meet the noise abatement standards set by the FAA. This alone was a challenge, but the second reason for approaching from the East was due to the wind that would come from the South-West and blow North-East, as helicopters had to approach to land flying into the wind and the tower was unfortunately in their path.

Commissioner Booher asked about the average number of flights per day. Mr. Martinez stated that the average was about 1 flight per day.

Commissioner Muder asked what the average height approaching the landing pads would be, citing Bourdon Ranch Road being between the airpark and the tower.

Mr. Tyler Legg, a helicopter pilot for Air Evac that worked with Mr. Martinez approached to answer Commissioner Muder's question, stating the helicopter would be at about 150 feet above ground level.

Commissioner Muder asked if fixed-wing aircraft would be approaching from that direction. Mr. Legg said that planes would have to align with the runway and would not approach from the east, whereas helicopters went straight for the helipads.

Commissioner Booher asked if a quarter of a mile was not enough space to navigate around the tower.

Mr. Legg said that it was possible to maneuver around the tower, but that it would be dangerous to navigate in the dark, and that depth perception at night would make this an especially difficult obstacle.

Commissioner Booher asked what a preferred distance for the tower would be, to which Mr. Legg said half a mile to a mile.

Commissioner Rogers asked if the proposal was, in his opinion, a safety hazard to pilots. Mr. Legg said that it was indeed a hazard, specifically to helicopter pilots, especially at night.

Commissioner Muder asked if Mr. Legg had experienced 5G interference. Mr. Legg stated he had several instances with a particular helicopter in Phoenix where 5G was much more present. He further stated that it would not cause the altimeters to go out, but that the altimeter would give false readings or act erratically.

Commissioner Booher asked what the FAA required regarding dealing with this issue and if there was a workaround. Mr. Legg said their guidance was to simply turn their cellphones' airplane mode on while flying, know how high your landing elevation is, adjust the altimeter settings, and being able to look outside and see where the ground is. Mr. Legg said that the tower would be above the level of the airport, and that could cause issues with judging distance, especially at night when visibility was low, and pilots were not able to see to properly judge distance.

One of the Applicant's representatives, Mr. Graham Chapman, requested to address these concerns. Chair Latham permitted the Applicant to speak.

Mr. Chapman deferred to Mr. Kennedy, the RF Engineer who gave a brief history of the CBAND interference, stating that the various carriers had delayed the release of 5G to ensure the FAA had proper procedures for dealing with altimeter issues. Mr. Kennedy further went on to say that the equipment that was affected by the interference had a defect that make it unable to filter some frequencies, and the issue was easily corrected by adding in shielding, or by replacing the altimeters. Mr. Kennedy noted that it was a mandate by the FAA that all aircraft update their shielding or replaced their altimeters by March of 2024.

With this, Chair Latham closed the public comments.

Commissioner Howe stated that he had some significant concerns about the proposed location, given that the prevailing winds would force the helicopters to approach from the east. Chair Latham agreed.

With there being no further discussion, Commissioner Rogers motioned to recommend Denial of the Special Use Permit to the Board of Supervisors.  Commissioner Booher seconded the motion.

Motion passed unanimously, 9 - 0 .

SUP 23-008 AZ11-172 STALLION WIRELESS COMMUNICATION TOWER, DISTRICT IV: Consideration of a request by Pinnacle Consulting for a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction of a 100' monoelm wireless communication tower and associated facilities in the Linden area. APN#: 409-22-005C, T10N, R21E, S06 of the GSRM. Owner: Ralph E. and Julie A. Forney. Agent: Pinnacle Consulting.

Mr. Tyler Richards, Planner I, presented the item to the Commission. Mr. Richards stated that this was a wireless communications tower in the Linden area, noting the location of the tower in relation to various landmarks and the road. Mr. Richards also shared the site plan, stating that the lease area was 40 by 40 feet, and that as this was not in a platted subdivision, the applicants were able to go above the residential limit of 80 feet and were requesting a 100-foot 5G tower with monoelm stealthing, sharing a side-view of the proposed stealthing.

Mr. Richards shared the simulations provided by the Applicant, and the Applicant's Public Outreach efforts, stating that no one came to their public meeting, and thus did not have any notes to address.  Staff received one comment in opposition regarding the project, stating concern over the beautification of the area, and some health concerns. Mr. Richards then stated that Staff recommended approval of the Special Use Permit, with conditions proposed by Staff.

Commissioner Muder asked about the condition that the site be inspected every 5 years, to which Mr. Richards replied that this condition had been given to previous projects by the Board of Supervisors and would now be added to all subsequent projects.

Commissioner Muder asked Staff if building permits were all that were required for building these towers. Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, responded that the Applicants would still need to complete Federal licensing.

The Applicant, Mr. Graham Chapman from Pinnacle Consulting, then introduced the project to the Commission, stating that this project would provide a great deal of difference in the area's service that would benefit the community. Mr. Chapman further stated that the Elm tree stealthing was chosen as it was their highest quality stealthing and looked best.

Chair Latham opened the Public Comment period.
With no public comments, Chair Latham closed the Public Comment period.

Commissioner Howe had concerns with the stealthing, noting that using a deciduous tree design that was supposed to lose its leaves in the winter being green year-round would look out of place, but that was the only concern he had regarding the project.

With no further comments, Commissioner Booher motioned to recommend approval of the Special Use Permit to the Board of Supervisors. Commissioner Muder seconded the motion.

The motion passed unanimously, 9 - 0 .
