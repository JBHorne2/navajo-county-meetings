ITEM #6 – SP 23-001 BUCK SPRINGS RESORT REVIEW, DISTRICT V: A request for a Site Plan Review to expand a previously approved resort, with associated accommodations, which had included 63 lodging units, to the same use with a total of 64 lodging units (1 additional unit), with a layout change, located on a 19.77-acre parcel. APN# 411-40-018, T08N, R23E, S02. Owner: ET Phone Home, LLC Agent: Laron Turley:

Senior Planner, Cody Cooper, presented the item to the Commission, displaying the area and the site, including existing structures. Further slides showed resolutions for the previous Zone Change, Special Use Permits (SUPs) that had been approved for the site, and the most recently proposed site plan. He further presented the history to the Commission, detailing the original SUP and Zone Change that went through in the 1970's and 1980's. This included the 1985 Board of Supervisors passing a resolution requiring all further development to go through a Site Plan Review process. There was a Zone Change in 1987 to Special Development (SD) zoning, which was finalized in 1988. Mr. Cooper then showed the newly proposed site plan, which adds one additional unit, has an improved grading plan, and the inclusion of several amenities, including: a cafe and bar lounge, a venue for meetings, an outdoor stage, open space for guests, and a dog park. The applicant received will-serve letters from the Pinetop-Lakeside Sanitary District, Ponderosa Water, and Navopache Electric. They also received a letter from the Pinetop Country Club stating that this project meets their CC&Rs and that they both consent to and support the proposed project. Mr. Cooper also noted that Staff had notified the public despite not being required by State law and showed the Commission the area of notification within the standard 300' radius of the project. There had been four inquiries about the project for more information, which Staff provided. Staff received one comment in opposition to the proposed expansion, but the person leaving the comment did not state their reasons. Mr. Cooper then advised that this project would be approved or denied at Commission-level that evening, so it would be a final decision versus a recommendation to the Board of Supervisors. TXT-A 23-001 AMENDMENTS TO ARTICLE IX, NAVAJO COUNTY SUBDIVISION REGULATIONS and ADDITION OF SECTION 9.4 – EXCEPTIONS; PLAT ALTERATIONS: Consideration of Subdivision Regulations amendment and addition of Section 9.4 to Article IX:

Planning Manager, Berrin Nejad, presented this item to the Commission, giving a brief overview of the history. Mrs. Nejad stated that the Navajo County Subdivision Ordinance was originally adopted in 1969, amended once in 2007, and again with a Minor Plat Amendment in 2021. There are several old subdivisions that were platted before 1969 with CC&Rs that specified lots may be split if the zoning is met. However, State law prevents recording a plat without going before the Board of Supervisors for approval and Navajo County's Subdivision Ordinance prevents splitting properties within subdivisions. Mrs. Nejad went on to specify that during the time when splits were not regulated, owners could easily split their properties simply by taking a survey of the split to the Recorder's Office. When the Minor Land Division Ordinance went into effect in 2018, it gave Staff the ability to screen splits that would result in lots that would be unbuildable per the County's Zoning requirements and prevent illegal splits from occurring. This caused issues, as many property owners purchased land with the intent of splitting and were unaware of the regulations as neighbors had split properties, while the splits were unregulated. Eventually, an ad-hoc task force was formed to explore the issue, led by former Public Works Director John Osgood, with several months of research. In the April 2022 Planning and Zoning Commission meeting, a presentation where two different property owners that had been experiencing hardships because of these types of issues spoke to the Commission. It was determined by the Commission that Staff should find a solution. Staff now proposes the amendment to Article Nine of the Navajo County Subdivision Ordinance, the full details of which were provided to the Commission in the Staff Report. With the Text Amendment, there would be a Board of Supervisors approved method in the Subdivision Ordinance to allow for the recording of an altered plat, thus making it legal and allowable for parcels that meet the relevant criteria.

Vice-Chairwoman Smith asked for clarification on the amendments, raising the concern that it was restricting any splits that would result in parcels that were less than one acre in size. Vice-Chairwoman Smith also pointed out that in the new text, the parcels must be accessible by a Navajo County maintained designated roadway. Her question was that within the Heber-Overgaard area there were a handful of larger subdivisions that set aside parcels called tracts, and they were not given lot numbers as they were not intended to be for dwellings, but instead for commercial uses or parks. A number of these tracts were quite large and if they were restricted to the 1 acre minimum, she was concerned about the impact the restriction would have on development. She felt that not having the ability to split into smaller properties would not benefit the community as it restricted options. Mr. Cooper responded and stated that, for the larger tracts, they would not qualify for this because they are specifically parceled out. If they did start splitting tracts, it would imply that they would be splitting into more than five pieces for the larger ones and, any more than that would require the owners to initiate a plat or a re-platting process either way. Ultimately, the tracts would not be impacted positively or negatively, and the intent for the amendment was to allow a splitting process for Subdivisions platted prior to 1969.

Commissioner Rogers mentioned that in the April 2022 meeting, the suggested amendment was only to apply to Subdivisions platted before 1970. He noted that the older subdivisions were not as well planned as some of the more recent ones, such as some that were platted in 1973 which contained proper CC&Rs and asked for confirmation of this. Mr. Cooper confirmed that he was correct.

Mrs. Sylvia Allen approached the Commission to ask for more information, as she is a property owner in a subdivision that was platted in the 1960s. She wanted to clarify the cut-off date, and that subdivisions platted during or after that date would be restricted from splitting their lots. She continued to explain that her lots were a range of 1 to 5 acre lots and did not see a reason to restrict her from splitting her 5 acre lots. Mr. Cooper stated that the amendment would apply to subdivisions platted specifically during or before 1969 and it would be up to members of the public to provide evidence that the intent of the subdivision's CC&Rs was to allow splitting. He stated that they needed to meet the conditions set forth by the amendment to be able to split their lots. Mr. Cooper further noted that her area was one of the many looked at that would qualify and could split down to the 1 acre minimum. She would need HOA approval to split and if there was no active HOA, she would just need to apply for the split with the new process if this amendment was approved.

With no further discussion, Commissioner Booher made a motion to recommend approval of the Text Amendment to the Board of Supervisors. Commissioner Rogers seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously, 7 - 0.

TXT-A 23-002 AMENDMENTS TO ARTICLE 25 – PROHIBITION AGAINST PLACEMENT/MOVEMENT OF MOBILE HOMES 20 YEARS OLD OR OLDER: Consideration of text amendment to Article 25, Section 2517 of the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance, regarding age of mobile home which requires HUD rehabilitation certification:

Planner I, Sondra Starke, presented the item to the Commission, stating that the proposal refers to the age threshold requiring HUD rehabilitation certification for mobile homes, initially put into place by HUD on June 15, 1976. That date became the threshold for requiring mobile homes to be rehabilitated before movement or placement in the unincorporated parts of Navajo County and the Zoning Ordinance currently reflects that HUD indicated age. When Navajo County updated to the 2018 IRC and IBC in June of 2022, it was to reflect the modern safety standards. As a result, Staff noticed a discrepancy between the 1976 and 2018 standards for mobile home safety. HUD rehabilitation certification verifies dwelling safety and promotes safety awareness within communities. Staff decided to look into proposing an amendment to the Zoning Ordinance, to require a 20-year rolling basis rather than having a set date and cleaned up the language within the Ordinance to make the intent clearer. Ms. Starke provided the Commission with the proposed changes. As a side note, when there would be costs associated with rehabilitating a mobile home, owner would have access to apply for the HUD Relocation Fund benefits that can reimburse up to $1500 in rehabilitation costs if the homeowner is at or below the Federal poverty guidelines. When above the area's median income level, certification permitting costs $306 and when below, certification permitting costs $49.  With these findings of fact, Staff recommended approval of the Text Amendment.

Chairman Latham asked if this was to apply to mobile homes that were to be moved, and if the changes would have an effect on established mobile homes.  Ms. Starke stated that it would only come into effect when they were moved or placed. Anything currently existing would not need to be certified.

Commissioner Booher asked what the replacement definition of a mobile home was, if Staff was removing it. Ms. Starke said that they were simply going to remove the definition as they needed to get more information on how they wished to define a mobile home, as there was an odd separation between a manufactured home and a mobile home. Commissioner Booher then asked if this amendment would affect manufactured homes as well. Ms. Starke confirmed that it does.

Commissioner Rogers asked if the HUD classification was mandatory for all, or if it was only for those that wished to be qualified. Ms. Starke explained that the amendment would change the classification from the HUD standard to a Navajo County standard. Any homes that were to be placed or moved in Navajo County that were over 20 years old would be required to be certified as rehabilitated, if the amendment was approved.

With no further discussion from the Commission, Staff, or the public, Commissioner Rogers made a motion to recommend approval of the Text Amendment to the Board of Supervisors. Commissioner Booher seconded the motion.  Motion passed unanimously, 7 - 0.

ZC 22-002 BRIAN A. BRIMHALL, ZONE CHANGE, DISTRICT IV: A request for a Zone Change of a 0.87-acre parcel from A-General (General Zoning District) to C-R (Commercial-Residential) to build an accounting and tax office. The Town did not have any concerns or comments about the project. However, Staff received one comment in opposition of the project from the public, and one inquiry for more information. Access to the property would be off of Juanita, and all setbacks would be using C-R's setback requirements for buildings on the property.

Julie Quesada also spoke to the Commission, giving the same reasons as Mr. Desalmi for her opposition to the project.

With nothing further to discuss,  Chairman   Latham called for a Motion. Commissioner Rogers made a motion to recommend approval of the Zone Change to the Board of Supervisors. Commissioner Murph seconded the motion. Motion passed unanimously,  7 - 0.

SUP 22-010 SUNRISE HEALTH and RECOVERY RESIDENTIAL FACILITY, DISTRICT IV: A request for a Special Use Permit to operate a recovery residential facility for ten (10) residents with 13 staff per week with overlapping shifts. The property is 5 acres with an existing structure that used to be utilized as a nursing home. APN# 409-25-003B, T10N, R21E, S09.   Owner:  Pine Top Holdings, LLC.  Agent: Onkar Bhowra:

Planning Manager, Berrin Nejad, presented the item to the Commission, giving an overview of the project, listing the location, noting its proximity to the schools across the road, and showed the Commission the residential areas around the property. She noted that Sunrise Health and Recovery (SHR) was currently licensed by the State of Arizona and health service would be administered by Dr. Nathan Velez.  The facility would have a maximum of 10 residents, and the length of their stay may vary from one, up to ninety days. The facility currently has parking, including two accessible spaces, and the existing building is currently compliant with Timber-Mesa Fire Department's requirements, including verification of the Emergency Power for an electric fire pump. The applicant provided 11 support letters, which were given to the Commission in their packets.  Mrs. Nejad went over the applicant's supplied materials, including articles, site plans, floor plans, schedules and more, as well as the information for the public meeting the applicant held.

Chairman Latham called for the applicant to comment. The Applicant, Dr. Nate Velez gave the Commission a presentation to introduce themselves, their mission, and their project; Dr. Velez touched on his involvement with Navajo County's Attorney's Office, Navajo County's Health Department, and various State agencies. Dr. Velez then introduced Dr. Onkar Bhowra to the Commission, who also detailed his mission and experience working with the services and detailed the reason that they wanted to provide the service. Mr. Bhowra noted that the facility was already approved as an assisted living facility, and that the uses would be very similar and that the requirements would be the same.

Chairman Latham asked if the facility would be taking Title 36 patients, Dr. Bhowra stated that they would not and that the applicants were paying for this facility out of pocket, not using grant money to fund this project.

Commissioner Booher asked if they would have involuntary admissions. Dr. Velez stated that if patients were ordered there by the courts or a probation officer, the facility might take patients involuntarily, but they would not be taking any psychiatric admissions, nor would they work with a psychiatric capacity in mind.

Commissioner Booher also asked the schools if they had a Resource Officer. Sylvia Allen, a representative of the schools, replied that they did not.  The school would have to come up with the money to hire one, and their budget was already very tight.

With no further questions from the Commission, Chairman Latham opened the floor for public comment.

Representatives from the Sequoia School district provided a petition with over two hundred signatures, and a variety of claims as to how this would negatively impact their ability to serve and protect students and staff members, financially or otherwise. Several concerned parents and school attendees also voiced their concerns, not only for the safety of the school but for the neighborhood in general.

Property owners on Full House Lane voiced concerns over the impact on crime rates this facility would have. All commended the applicant for their mission, stating that they understood the need for this facility, but were unable to support the project given its location.  There were some comments in support of the project as well, stating that the types of patients that would be served at that facility were not violent or criminal in nature, and trying to change for the better.

Commissioner Rogers also made note of this and asked why this location was chosen rather than anywhere else. Dr. Bhowra stated that the reason this location was chosen is that the facility was already improved and fitted with the amenities needed to run a hospital environment, and that the cost of upgrading a different facility made finding somewhere else incredibly difficult for them, as they were paying for this out of pocket and the needed upgrades were detrimentally costly.  Dr. Bhowra further stated that he sympathized with the schools who would be forced to upgrade their security measures, but also noted that most schools are already having to upgrade their security regardless, because of ongoing issues with gun violence.  He noted that the facility's patients were not violent in nature, they were going there to heal, clean up, and then leave to try and integrate into society.  Dr. Bhowra felt that these types of patients would not impact the area or school in any way.

Public Comments to the Commission against the project:

Jerald Lewis, Sylvia Allen, Derek, Kyrie, Amanda & Nicole Luptek, Bill Vallery, Jerry Hunter, Chuck Angle, Konnie Johnson, Amanda Stabenow, Beth Kulish, Kurt Brooks, and Lawrence Meisner.

Safety of the children

Transient traffic

Lindsay Lindstrom, Allison Hephner, Jax Kale, and Alyssa Moffitt.
