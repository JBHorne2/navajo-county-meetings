SP 23-003 SHOW LOW MOUNTAIN RANCH SITE PLAN REVIEW, DISTRICT III: Consideration of a request for a Site Plan Review of Parcels B and C of the Show Low Mountain Ranch Master Development Plan to allow for the development of up to 350 Recreational Vehicle/Park Model spaces on Parcel B and up to 96 Recreational Vehicle/Park Model and/or Manufactured/Modular Home Spaces on Parcel C on portions of what are vacant 63.91 and 233.54-acre parcels.
APN#: 304-57-033A and 304-57-033B, T11N, R22E, S25.
Owner: Show Low Holdings 307, LLC/Universal Show Low Properties, LLC.
Agent: Greg Loper.

Ms. Sondra Starke, Planner I, presented the item to the Commission, and gave a brief overview of the Project. Ms. Starke said that this project had been before the Commission previously as a Master Development Plan Amendment (MDP-A) that had been approved by the Board of Supervisors. Any finalizing of specific site plans for phases would require a Site Plan Review from the Planning and Zoning Commission and would not need to go before the Board of Supervisors again.

The Applicant wished to finalize the site plan for parcels B & C from what was approved for the MDP-A. Parcel B was intended more for recreational vehicle camping uses and would have 350 spaces. Parcel C would have a "build to rent" cottage community with 96 units, and amenities.

The site plans for parcel B & C would include adding buffering to the parcels adjacent to the south side of the project, as well as put in trails and bike paths.
Ms. Starke then went over the vicinity map and requirements from the Staff Report, stating that Staff recommended approval of the Site Plan Review.

There were no questions for Staff from the Commission.

The Applicant's representative, Greg Loper, addressed the Commission and further expanded on the reasons for proposing the site plan, noting that there was a slight delay to hold a public meeting as requested by the Board of Supervisors, to get feedback from the community.

Mr. Loper detailed the results of the public meeting, stating that 65 participants had attended, and that invitations to the legally required 300' area surrounding the project had been sent to a total of 67 property owners. Mr. Loper also said that the majority of the complaints were from adjoining parcels, and the Applicant worked to come to a proper conclusion that would be able to mitigate the worries of the communities, particularly the neighboring farms.

Mr. Loper then listed out the various concerns brought up in the Public Meeting, stating that some of the concerns were out of the applicant's control, such as the Sheriff's response time, etc.

The roadways would be privately maintained and there would be an association that would oversee the maintenance.

Mr. Loper explained that there was an existing contract with the Sanitary district that allowed a maximum of 471 single-family residential units, the equivalent of 635 RV's.

Mr. Loper also noted that the new Parcel D would be updated in a future amendment, and that Parcel E would be 1-acre lots with agricultural use that will blend with the adjacent parcels, including an agreement of acknowledging animal-related smells and noise.

As far as fire protection, will-serve agreements are in place and a fire plan will be reviewed with Timber-Mesa Fire Department.

Mr. Loper also stated that the Applicant was looking into age restricting some portions of the project, but nothing had been determined as-of-yet.

He also touched on Roundup Drive being emergency access only which would be gated with a Knox box or a crash gate.

Landscaping would be done to preserve the natural flora of the area, and it was not intended to wipe the earth bare.

Mr. Loper also noted that Parcel B will be developed in about 5 phases.

Amenities would be added, including pickleball and possible long-term camping staples.

Mr. Loper read out a list of the studies done for the project along with Staff's requirements and finished with a comparison of the original project vs. what it was now.

Chair Latham then opened for Public Comment.

There were no comments in favor.

Comments against the project:

Brenda & Penny Harmon, Emily Junk, Donna Gierst

Complaints of not enough notices.

Too much rock in the area.

Too much traffic.

Noise complaints.

Road issues.

Police Response time in the area.

Night Sky/light pollution.

Equestrian vs. traffic issues.

Sewer issues.

Property/home values will be impacted.

Dilution of national flora.

Storage units being front & center being unsightly.

Public Comments Closed

Mr. Loper addressed the comments, stating:

The storage units were in the appropriate Zoning.

Traffic will be 40% less impacted, and the applicant was looking into what it would take to put in a streetlight.

Sewer agreement is updated with the sanitary district.

There will be no diesels or semis allowed in the project area.

The RV area starts after the commercial area of the project, so views from the road will not be impacted.

With the notification complaints, everyone within the legally required 300' buffer was, 65 people attended the public meeting while 67 were notified, and the applicant had a member of the public post information for the public meeting on the community Facebook page and post fliers in the Community Center.

Chair Latham asked Staff to confirm that the development had already been approved; Mr. Cooper, Planning Manager, confirmed that the project densities and usages had already been approved and the specific layouts of the site plans were what was in front of the Commission that evening. To address the lack of advertisement, Mr. Cooper stated that notification was not required by law for Site Plan Reviews but was done anyway to be transparent with the public.

Commissioner Booher asked about the workforce housing. Mr. Loper replied that the workforce housing is a phrase used for housing that will be satisfying the working class' needs, noting that there was a lack of opportunities in the area and hoped to mitigate that and meet needs.

With no further discussion, Commissioner Booher motioned to approve the Site Plan Review. Commissioner Howe seconded the motion. Motion passed unanimously, 6 - 0 .

TXT-A 23-006 AMENDMENT TO ARTICLE 25 – ACCESSORY BUILDING SETBACKS, AND ACCESSORY DWELLING UNITS: Consideration of a text amendment to Article 25, Section 2503, regarding accessory building setbacks, and Article 25, Section 2520, regarding accessory dwelling units.

Ms. Sondra Starke, Planner I, presented the item to the Commission. Ms. Starke gave an overview of the changes, noting that Code Enforcement and public inquiries brought attention to amendments needed for clarification and to fall into compliance with fire code.

The changes were:

3-foot setbacks for accessory buildings would now be five to be in conformance with fire code.

Park Models would be allowed for use as guest housing to match the Arizona Revised Statues.

Language will be cleaned up to remove "servant/caretaker's quarters" to streamline the Ordinance.

Vice-Chair Smith asked Staff to clarify which setbacks were being considered, and if the required distance between buildings was also being considered. Mr. Cooper, Planning Manager confirmed that it was only the setbacks for the boundaries and that required distance between buildings of five feet was already codified.

Commissioner Booher motioned to recommend approval to the Board of Supervisors. Commissioner Muder seconded. Motion passed unanimously, 6 - 0 .

TXT-A 23-005 AMENDMENT TO ARTICLES 15, 25, AND 30 – SWAP MEETS AND PRIVATE CEMETERIES: Consideration of a text amendment to Article 15, Section 1502 and Article 30, Section 3002 regarding swap meets as well as adding a section to Article 25 and amending Article 30, Section 3002 regarding private cemeteries.
