SUP 23-013 STEP INN INTENSIVE TRANSITIONAL LIVING HOME, DISTRICT V: Consideration of request by Sarah Burnett on behalf of Step Inn LLC to allow for the usage of the existing single-family residence as an institutional residence with a maximum of seven clients in the Lakeside area.  APN # 212-24-057B, T09N, R22E, S23 of the GSRM. Owner : Sarah R Burnett  Agent: Sarah Burnett:

Ms. Sondra Starke, Planner I, presented the item to the Commission. Ms. Starke gave an overview of the project, stating that the project was located in Lakeside.  Ms. Starke further noted the current use of the property as a single-family residence. The proposal would allow for use as an institutional group home for sober living, with a maximum of seven tenants in approximately 1400 square feet, with a 256 square foot addition. Ms. Starke noted the addition was allowed regardless of approval of the project, upon obtaining a permit from the Navajo County Building Department. Ms. Starke then gave further details, including the scope, goals, rules, and policies tenants would have to abide by which were included with the Staff Report. The majority of the land within the vicinity of the area was zoned R-2 (Multifamily Residential), with R1-10 and A-General single-family residential zoning farther outside the neighborhood. Ms. Starke noted the area of notification and the posted notices, and that Staff had received nine comments regarding this project, with two in support and seven against. Given the findings of facts, Staff recommended approval of the project with conditions.

Chair Latham opened for Public Comment.

Comments in Favor:

Greg Harper, Neva Harper, Brian Burnett.

Their Comments:

Worked with Applicant previously and had seen successful reintegration with Applicant's program.

Included testimony from past participants in the program that presented their views.

Stated that this is a much-needed service for the area where there is little to none currently.

Helps enforce long-term success for sobriety by providing a structured environment for the participants. Provides hope for people struggling with their addictions.

Comments Against:

Colleen Ollen, Laurie & Eric Holyoak, Robert Thomas, George & Lisa Hughes, Denise Harmon.

Concerns for the safety of children.

The project would put hardship on neighbors that are fearful of violence or property loss, and who would be liable for these.

Re-experiencing trauma in relation to abuse from addicts relapsing.

Their desired neighborhood does not include a Sober Living facility.

Claims that there were already many facilities that provided this service.

Not wanting to build their home near a facility.

Lack of trust in people struggling with addictions; the relapse rate is too high. Belief that the project was a "cash grab".

There are no transportation lines nearby and did not want addicts walking through their yards.

Road maintenance costs and labor increase, as it's privately owned.

Not wanting "these people" on the mountain.

Chair Latham closed the public comments.

The Chair then asked the Applicant to comment.

Sarah Burnett, the Applicant, spoke to the Commission. Ms. Burnett apologized, noting that the impression of the project's terminology presented the wrong connotations.

Ms. Burnett said this facility would not be an institution, and there would be strict guidelines and criteria to apply, and Step Inn would not be taking tenants that were not already in recovery or have gone through detox. Tenants are not under the influence of drugs or alcohol and are under a mandate to stay that way.

Ms. Burnett stated that the motivation was not money, but she acknowledged that it would still take money to help people. The point of the program was to be kept small and focused. Seven people would be the absolute maximum, but the reality is Step Inn would likely not host more than five.

Ms. Burnett also stated that she started her program because traditional methods were ineffective. Her program would be a hybrid approach that would encompass multiple levels of care from beginning to end.

Commissioner Rogers asked Ms. Burnett about who would be liable for damages, Ms. Burnett said that she would be willing to be responsible and she would hold her tenants responsible as well.

Commissioner Muder asked where Ms. Burnett found tenants. Ms. Burnett replied that she would get individuals recommended from detox centers, the 12 step fellowships, and she also addressed the comment about the road maintenance and traffic, stating that she would be providing transportation to residents' jobs, so there would not be heavy traffic.

Commissioner Muder asked if the tenants would have vehicles. Ms. Burnett said no.

Commissioner Booher asked about the applicant's background experience. Ms. Burnett then stated that she was a certified peer specialist and behavioral health tech operating under provider Dr. Brian K. Merrill for two and a half years, working closely with addicts and alcoholics in treatment and was familiar with unpredictable and dangerous behaviors, Ms. Burnett also stated that she helped manage the women's care facility in Show Low for a time, and that she was currently managing a home facility.

Commissioner Booher asked if any of her tenants were court ordered.  Ms. Burnett stated that she had
court-ordered tenants previously for other projects. This would be the first home she would own, not just manage.

Vice-Chair Smith asked about the time limit on finding a job, if she would evict them if the tenants were unable to find a job in that timeframe. Ms. Burnett stated that she was willing to work with them and make accommodations, that the intention was not to just kick them out for minor infractions, but did have several rules that if broken would result in immediate removal from her program, but had resources for placing them instead of leaving them without options.

Chair Latham stated that he would like to go on record that he deeply disagreed with comments from the public, noting that the statements were fear-based and ignorant to the needs of the community.

Commissioner Rogers asked Staff how long the multi-family zoning was in place. Ms. Starke answered that it had likely been multi-family in the first zoning overlay, which would have been back in the nineteen-seventies.

Commissioner Rogers stated that it had been that way for long enough that anyone that purchased property could reasonably expect a group home project to go in. Ms. Starke confirmed, and listed a number of allowed uses in R-2, such as senior living, orphanages, etc.

Commissioner Rogers said that as a member of this Commission, while the public comments were taken into consideration, it was also part of the Commission's job to look out for the property rights of applicants as well, and that this project was an allowed use within this zoning.

With there being no further business, Commissioner Muder motioned to recommend approval to the Board of Supervisors. Commissioner Rogers seconded the motion. Motion passed, 6 - 1 ( Commissioner Booher , Nay. Commissioner John joined partway through this item.)

SUP 23-014 VERIZON AZ3 COCHISE WIRELESS COMMUNICATION FACILITY, DISTRICT III: Consideration of a request by Vertical Bridge for a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction of a 95' monopole communication tower and associated facilities in the White Mountain Lakes area. APN# : 404-42-013B, T11N, R23E, S31 of the GSRM . Owner: Stanley G. and Carolyn M. Brewer. Agent: Todd Daoust:

Mr. Tyler Richards, Planner I, presented the item to the Commission. Mr. Richards stated that this was a wireless communications tower in the area south of White Mountain Lakes, noting the location of the tower in relation to various landmarks and the road.

Mr. Richards also shared the site plan, a vacant 8-acre RU-1 zoned lot, that was not within a designated residential neighborhood and would only need 1 - 1 setbacks and noted that the applicant was in excess of these setbacks regardless. There would be access for fire trucks that would be made of granite and would have a turn-around that would meet the needs of the fire trucks.

Mr. Richards shared simulated views, and the Applicant's Public Outreach efforts, stating that there were two in attendance at the public meeting, and as their concerns were only about potential health concerns and not toward the land use, and did not have any comments that the Applicant could address. Mr. Richards said that Staff had received two comments with regard to the project, one that was in favor of the project and desired a faster internet connection, and one in opposition, citing health concerns. Mr. Richards then stated that Staff recommended approval of the Special Use Permit, with conditions proposed by Staff.

The Chair opened for Public comments.

Comments in Favor:

Andrew Bakken spoke about the lack of service in the area and hoped that this would increase the services available in the rural areas.

Comments Against:

Jill & Michael Iverson both commented about the health concerns, claiming that it would be weaponized against the public to cause a number of "mutations" due to the microwaves from the tower.

The public comment period was closed.

Applicant Todd Daoust then addressed the Commission with a presentation explaining their reasoning for picking this particular area, and how it would benefit the rural area.

Commissioner Booher motioned to recommend approval to the Board of Supervisors.  Commissioner Muder
seconded. Motion passed unanimously, 7 - 0 .

SUP 23-012 GREATSKY CAMPRANCH RV PARK, DISTRICT III:  Consideration of a request for a Special Use Permit to construct an RV Park with 63 total spaces.  The property is 10.00 acres in size, located south of White Mountain Lake Road and east of Woodside Trail, in the White Mountain Lakes area. APN# 304-22-005, T11N, R22E, S17 of the GSRM.  Owner: FSBC Properties LLC. Agent: Emery Ellsworth:

Commissioner Murph returned for this item.

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, presented the item to the Commission. Mr. Cooper noted the surrounding features of the area. The project was located on the west side of White Mountain Lakes. Directly to the west of this property was a mini-storage facility, to the east was a dog kennel, then to the north and south there were single family residences.

Mr. Cooper then showed the site plan to the Commission, noting the existing facilities on the site and briefly touching on the scope of the project. Mr. Cooper also noted that the applicant had provided preliminary drainage and wastewater reports, which were both included in the Commissioner's packets and online. Staff had received six calls against the project from four different individuals, which touched on lighting, fire restrictions, and one of the comments pertained to when the project was approved for their Zone Change, as a proposed condition of this Special Use Permit had been requested.  However, that could not apply to the Zone Change, and would only be applied to the Special Use Permit that was before them today. Mr. Cooper noted that the Applicant was available for questions.

The Chair opened Public Comments.

Lisa Poste said that she was opposed to the project and stated that she had several civil disputes with the Applicant about her kennel's noise, and his event center's noise.

The Chair then closed the Public Comments.

Chair Latham asked if noise was something Staff had purview over. Mr. Cooper responded that Navajo County does not currently have a noise ordinance, and that it would be a civil dispute.

Commissioner Rogers and Chair Latham asked about the fencing requirements, Mr. Cooper confirmed that the Applicant would be required to install either a solid fence or a wall as outlined in the conditions and would not be able to use a chain-link fence with screening.

The Applicant, Mike Barclay, addressed the Commission and thanked the County and the Commission for their time, stating that he hoped to work through the issues with Ms. Poste.

With there being no further business,
Commissioner Muder motioned to recommend approval to the Board of Supervisors.
Commissioner Murph seconded. Motion passed unanimously,
7 - 0 .
