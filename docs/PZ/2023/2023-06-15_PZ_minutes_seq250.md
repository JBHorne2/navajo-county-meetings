SUP 23-004 PINE ROSE MANUFACTURED HOME PARK, DISTRICT IV:  Consideration of a request by Juan Rosales-Perez for a Special Use Permit to allow for
a two phased project, which consists of a 17 space Manufactured Home Park, including 3 Recreational Vehicle spaces, and fourplex on a 2.83-acre parcel in the Lakeside area.
APN# 212-26-082N, T09N, R22E, S24 of the GSRM. Owner: Juan Rosales-Perez.  Agent: Juan Rosales-Perez.

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, presented the project to the Commission. Mr. Cooper gave a brief overview of the proposed project, noting a bit of the history of the property's code violations that have both been opened as well as closed since September of 2015, and listed the current violations on the site. Mr. Cooper also showed the Commission some photographs Staff had taken of the site at the time of posting, noting that there had not been any significant changes. As such, Staff recommended that there be a condition that all violations be brought into compliance prior to any, and all, permits being issued to the site. Mr. Cooper then went over the area of notification and presented the list of the comments against the project, including one petition provided by the neighbors. Mr. Cooper also noted that the project would fit with the Comprehensive Plan and surrounding area's characteristics as two similar projects were located to the immediate north and east.

Mr. Doug Brimhall, a representative of the applicant, approached the Commission to introduce himself as the Engineer for the project, stating that himself and the applicant completed additional engineering up front for this project to help satisfy Staff's inquiries, one of which was how they would access the property. He also stated that the main ingress/egress would be from Amanda Drive, and that Peterson Drive would be egress-only and for emergency access.

Commissioner Booher asked if there would be a restriction on the age of the manufactured homes. Mr. Brimhall said that all homes would be brought up to Navajo County's Code, but that the applicant had not considered an age restriction.

Mrs. Marilyn Baca, of the public, asked about the size of the parcels. Mr. Brimhall stated that they would vary in size, ranging from the smallest at 36' x 100', to around 51' x 100'. The reason for this variation was to accommodate the existing, permitted dwellings.

The Chairman opened for public comments. Mr. Darton Stradling, Mr. Mike Baldwin, and Ms. Melissa McKinley spoke out against the project. Their complaints are as follows:

That Hi Joe Road's access was being used without permission of the property owner.

That there were issues with the easements, that several utility and private easements were being ignored.

There are flooding issues in the area.

The property was too small to be granted Special Development (SD) for a Manufactured Home Park.

The density of the project is too great for the area. The property is out of compliance and a mess.

RV's are illegal to rent in Navajo County.

The trash containment for the site was too close to the neighboring property. That while the property did have fire hydrants, the flows would not meet the Fire Department's requirements.

The storage of travel trailers and manufactured homes on the site. The applicant was making a nuisance of himself to the neighborhood. The age of the manufactured homes that would be on the property.

The Chairman then asked Staff to address these comments.

Mr. Cooper addressed the comments as follows:

Amanda Drive is a private ingress-egress that would be the main access to the project.

Hi Joe Road does not extend to this property, and is not a factor in this application. The concerns of the easements not being respected is a civil matter and the County would not be involved in the civil dispute. If the access shown to the County was found to be insufficient, the applicant would have to either relinquish their Special Use Permit (SUP) or re-enter the hearing phase as they would have to alter their plans.

For code violations, having an open violation does not preclude you from pursuing or obtaining a SUP, which is why Staff is recommending the condition that all violations be brought into compliance and closed, prior to any permits being issued.

Regarding the concerns of the parcel size, this is not a Special Development project, this is a SUP and that is not relevant to this project.

Density is not an issue as the Navajo County Comprehensive Plan has encouraged this area for high density, as there is more access to utilities and services than outside of the high-density zoning. However, the applicant is required to do a Traffic Study and make improvements, per the findings of the Traffic Study.

The recent meetings with the Fire Department did not indicate any issues with the project as well as the project to the east, and Staff is unaware of any changes, as the plans were given to and approved by the Fire Department, as the applicants have been working with the Department on this for over a year. However, if the applicant did need to put in sprinklers, that would be a part of the Building Permit process and handled in the building phase.

For flooding issues, the applicant is required to have a grading and drainage plan to follow, and they have provided a preliminary plan for the Commission and the public.

No one is disputing the fact that RV's are illegal to rent in Navajo County, the "RV" in question would have to be removed prior to development of the project and would not be allowed in the Manufactured Home spaces.

As far as the trash containment, given that the complainant has stated that it would be 12 feet from his home, and the building setbacks for the parcels are five feet from the property lines, this would be an appropriate place for placement of the trash enclosure.

Commissioner Booher asked for further clarification about the minimum acreage that had been brought up. Mr. Cooper replied that specific acreage requirement falls under Article 17 for SD Zoning and does not apply to this project as the applicant was not pursuing an SD Zone Change.

Commissioner Murph asked for more information about the easements. Mr. Cooper said that it's a public dispute that would have to be handled through the Courts, and Navajo County did not have the authority to be involved in such a dispute.

Commissioner Murph asked if the applicant needed to prove or disprove their access. Mr. Cooper stated that they were unable to prove or disprove private easements, as they were outside of the County's purview.

Vice-Chair Smith asked if it was allowed for the Commission to make proving access a stipulation or condition, to which Mr. Cooper said they could require that as a part of their SUP's stipulations or conditions.

Chairman Latham asked if the SUP was approved, if the applicant would have to come into compliance before any groundbreaking was done. Mr. Cooper confirmed that all code violations had to be cleared before the applicant would be allowed to move forward with any permits or development.

Commissioner Murph asked about the existing manufactured homes, as Mr. Brimhall had indicated that they were staying on the property. Mr. Cooper said the homes would have to be found to meet code, or they would be removed. There are two permitted manufactured homes on the property, and under the Zoning Ordinance an owner can have up to two manufactured homes without becoming a Manufactured Home Park. The third manufactured home is not hooked up to any utilities, and the current use of the property would allow them to store it on the property, as-is. However, if the owner were to use the manufactured home for the project, they would need to bring it into compliance with the Code or remove it prior to any groundbreaking on the property.

Ms. Melissa McKinley stated that Code Enforcement isn't doing their job by coming out and holding the applicant accountable and wanted to know if they were either not going to issue the SUP until he cleaned up the property, or if they were going to give Mr. Rosales the Permit, but that he would not be able to use it until he cleaned it up.

Chairman Latham asked Mr. Cooper to clarify. Mr. Cooper stated that the SUP would be issued, but that the applicant would have to come into compliance prior to utilizing its privileges. If the applicant was found not to comply, he would be stripped of the privileges of the SUP. He further stated that Navajo County does currently have a Code Enforcement Officer that has been working with Mr. Rosales and has been out to the site several times since his employment.

Ms. McKinley asked if the Commission could issue this permit even if everyone was asking them not to. Mr. Cooper reminded her that the current hearing was merely giving a recommendation to the Board of Supervisors and the Commission did not have the final authority to grant or deny a project.

Mrs. Baca asked if these were individual properties or if they were going to be a subdivision and expressed fear that this would become a slum.

Mr. Cooper stated that this would be under one ownership.

Mrs. Baca then asked why the petition was not taken into consideration. Chairman Latham explained this was just a recommendation hearing, but further noted that the petition had more people on it than what was indicated in the surrounding area of notification.

Mr. Baldwin said that everyone on the petition lived within a quarter mile of the project, and questioned the manufactured home being stored on the property as it was on piers.

Mr. Brimhall stated that the petition only mentioned that neighbors were concerned about the traffic, not that they did not desire or were against the project.

Chairman Latham also asked about the traffic study addressing the concerns of the petition. Mr. Brimhall said that it would.

Mr. Cooper also stated the manufactured home in question is not hooked up to any utilities, has no Certificate of Occupancy and no one can legally live in it. Furthermore, prior to this hearing, the manufactured home was on a separate parcel and in compliance with the County, but the applicant had combined the parcels in preparation of pursuing this SUP and unfortunately took it out of compliance as far as being permitted as a livable dwelling, meaning it could only be stored on the property until further permitting had been granted.

Commissioner Murph asked about the manufactured homes, if they would be easy to move or haul away, also asking about the allowance of two manufactured homes and if that third one was a further issue. Mr. Cooper stated that they could all probably be moved easily, and that only two of the three manufactured homes were both permitted and fully installed on the property, and that the Zoning Ordinance allowed for up to two dwellings on the property before becoming a Manufactured Home Park; As the third did not have a Certificate of Occupancy, it was not considered a dwelling and was therefore allowed to be stored there.

Chairman Latham motioned that the Commission recommend approval of the SUP, subject to the conditions recommended by Staff, with the addition of a condition that the easements be verified prior to groundbreaking. Commissioner Murph seconded the motion. Motion passed, 6 - 1 . ( Commissioner Booher )

ZC 23-001 LUXTINY II MANUFACTURED HOME PARK, DISTRICT IV: Consideration of a request by Steve Dedmon for a Zone Change from A-General to R-3 to allow for a 17-space manufactured home park in Lakeside on a 3.48-acre portion of APN# 212-26-119A, 212-26-119C, 212-26-119D, and 212-26-119E, of T09N, R22E, S24 of the GSRM. Owner: Luxtiny LLC. Agent: Steve Dedmon.

Ms. Sondra Starke, Planner I, presented the item to the Commission, stating that this item and the next were linked, but that only the Zone Change was being discussed for this item. Ms. Starke gave a brief overview of the area's features and showed the Commission a site plan of the proposed Park, as well as a zoning map showing the Commission the area's zoning profile. Ms. Starke noted that there is R-3 zoning touching the border of the property, so there was no concern regarding spot zoning. Additionally, the use fit the Comprehensive Plan and the surrounding area. Staff received one public comment against the project that covered several issues, including the development of Hi Joe Road, light pollution, and urbanization.

Mr. Steve Dedmon, the applicant, approached the Commission to introduce himself. He currently owns Luxtiny, which is to the south of this project. The applicant wished to change from A-General zoning to the R-3 zoning to allow for more density.

Commissioner Booher asked if there were age restrictions on the homes. Mr. Dedmon stated that he was now HUD certified and would be building new, custom homes for this community.

Mrs. Marilyn Baca asked about the ingress and egress, if it was off Hi Joe Road, or if it would be coming from another road.

Mr. Jeff Rhyan stated that he was also concerned about Hi Joe Road, as it was not County maintained.

Chairman Latham asked Staff if they would address these comments. Ms. Starke stated that these comments will be addressed in the next item, but that plans were for the roads to be brought up to the County standards. Commissioner Smith asked if they would be County maintained, to which Staff confirmed that they would not.

Commissioner Booher motioned to recommend approval of the Zone Change to the Board of Supervisors, but that there be a condition that the development be given a time limit to begin development.  Commissioner Muder seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously, 7 - 0 .

SUP 23-003 LUXTINY II MANUFACTURED HOME PARK, DISTRICT IV: Consideration of a request by Steve Dedmon for a Special Use Permit to allow for a 17-space manufactured home park in Lakeside on a 3.48-acre portion of APN# 212-26-119A, 212-26-119C, 212-26-119D, and 212-26-119E, of T09N, R22E, S24 of the GSRM. Owner: Luxtiny LLC. Agent: Steve Dedmon.

Ms. Sondra Starke, Planner I, continued the discussion from the last item and explained that if the Zone Change was approved, this Special Use Permit would move forward. Ms. Starke gave the Commission a little more detail about the development and stated that Staff found it followed the Comprehensive Plan and Zoning Ordinance for the proposed use already, save that it needed screening. She also noted that the road would have to be engineered to support the weight of a fully loaded firetruck. (75000 lbs.)

Commissioner Booher asked if Hi Joe Road was a County road or a private road. Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, stated that Hi Joe was a private easement and not maintained by the County.

Mrs. Marilyn Baca asked if the developer could have ingress-egress from Porter Mountain Road, citing how property owners had been struggling to maintain Hi Joe Road but that speeding cars typically ruined their efforts. She further stated that she didn't believe the outlet to Flag Hollow road would be able to handle the increase in traffic the project would bring.

Chairman Latham asked the applicant to address this question.

Mr. Dedmon stated that he had worked on maintaining his portion of Amanda Road for their first project, and that he had made an agreement with the neighboring projects to maintain the road. He also noted that they had started a fund to have Amanda Road paved in the future, and that they had been working with the County Engineer, Mr. Bill Bess, to work toward cost estimates. The plan for this project would follow similar steps for maintenance, if desired by residents. Mr. Dedmon also confirmed that they would be engineering the road to meet the Fire Department's standards.

Commissioner Booher asked about the maintenance, and if the developer's tenants would be involved in maintaining the road. Mr. Dedmon confirmed that the road would be maintained to the boundary of his development.

Mr. Jeff Rhyan stated that he was under the impression that a road doesn't have to be paved to be County maintained.

Ms. Starke addressed this, stating that for the County to maintain a road, it needed to meet a certain level of daily traffic and be brought to that level of maintenance before the County would adopt the roadway into the maintenance system.

Chairman Latham noted that the developer could also form a road improvement district.

Vice-Chair Smith motioned that the Commission recommend approval of the SUP to the Board of Supervisors, Commissioner Booher seconded. The motion passed unanimously, 7 - 0 .

SUP 23-005 AZ3 PENROD WIRELESS COMMUNICATION FACILITY, DISTRICT IV: Consideration of a request by State 48 Development Consulting for a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction of a 110' monopine wireless communication tower and associated facility in the Show Low area. APN# 210-01-009F, T10N, 22E, S33 of the GSRM. Owner: Allen Daniel & Velmarie Foster. Agent: Shannon McCrea.

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, presented the item to the Commission. He showed the Commission some slides of the area, of the style of pole, and some simulated visuals provided by the applicant. Mr. Cooper noted that the applicants would surpass the County's minimum setback requirements at 110%, given the designation of the parcel. Mr. Cooper also detailed the applicants' public outreach information, along with how they addressed the questions the public had about the tower. There were seven letters received from the public in support of the project and the applicant's representative, Alex Crowe, was available for questions.

Mr. Buz Essel spoke to the Commission with concerns over the Zoning Ordinance and asked if there was even a need for the tower. Mr. Essel also asked if Navajo County required an Environmental Assessment, stating that it was required by federal law. Mr. Essel also expressed concern that the law was not being followed regarding call logs proving a need for the tower as they had to be collected over a lengthy period, and that the height of the tower was not being given a Variance in due process.

Commissioner Booher asked if he represented a group of people. Mr. Essel stated that he was representing Concerned Citizens for 5G.

Mr. Nick Schmitz addressed the Commission stating that he was in favor of the project as the service he experienced was terrible, and that property owners had a need for this in his community. Mr. Schmitz also asked if the tower would have both 4 and 5 G capability, and what nearby owners could do to boost the service in the meantime.

Mr. Cooper addressed Mr. Essel's questions, explaining the tower in question was not in a platted subdivision that the Zoning Ordinance recognized as a residential zone and thus did not need a Variance for the height. As for the Environmental Assessment, that would be a requirement of Federal agencies, not the County, and compliance with Federal requirements was a recommended condition of approval.

Ms. Alex Crowe said that the tower would be both 4 and 5 G, and as for coverage for the short-term, she stated that Mr. Schmitz's concerns would be brought to the carrier to see if they could address them for the area.

Commissioner Murph motioned to recommend approval of the Special Use Permit to the Board of Supervisors; Commissioner Booher seconded. The motion passed unanimously, 7 - 0 .
