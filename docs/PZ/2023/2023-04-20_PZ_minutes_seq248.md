COMP 23-001 Update to Navajo County Comprehensive Plan: Consideration of  an update to the Comprehensive Plan of Navajo County:

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, presented the item to the Commission.
The update would include:

A hearing process for proposing major amendments to the Navajo County Comprehensive Plan. Mr. Cooper then gave a brief overview of how that process would function.

Updated population numbers and estimates to reflect the 2020 Census results, as well as more current information and guidance provided from the Arizona Office of Economic Opportunity.

Formatting to make the Comprehensive Plan less complex and improve readability, remove redundancy, and update the goals that have been met or need to be adjusted for clarity.

Mr. Cooper moved on to describing the public outreach Staff had done, a 60-day commitment period that began in January. In total, 15 adjacent jurisdictions had been contacted, and three responded with questions, but had no comments or concerns about the update. Two public meetings had been hosted, one in Holbrook and the other in Show Low. No members of the public attended or provided comments at either meeting. With this information, Staff recommends approval of this update.

There were no comments from the attending public about the Comprehensive Plan.

Commissioner Murph asked where the numbers presented were from. Mr. Cooper clarified that the models were from the Arizona Office of Economic Opportunity, and that they were making projections for growth based on their updated data, as the last models were flawed.

Commissioner Murph continued, stating that even knowing that the projections were not set in stone, they would still influence the County in some way. Mr. Cooper replied that they could, and that while they were going to plan for the current citizens of the County, they also needed to be flexible to account for any changes that may happen in the future.

With no further comments from the Commission or the public, Commissioner Murph made a motion to recommend approval to the Board of Supervisors. Commissioner Rogers seconded the motion. Motion
passed, 8 - 0 .

SUP 23-001 White Mountain Lake Self-Storage - District III:  Consideration of a  request for a Special Use Permit to develop a self-storage facility with four banks of new storage units on a vacant 1.2-acre parcel within the White Mountain Lake area of Show Low:

Ms. Sondra Starke, Planner I, presented the item to the Commission. The request was for a self-storage rental facility on Silver Creek Drive in White Mountain Lakes with four banks of storage. Ms. Starke showed the Commission a map of the surrounding area and listed the nearby landmarks.  She then talked about the features of the project that met development standards such as the ingress and egress, engineering, fire safety requirements, drainage detention, etc.

The zoning for the property is Commercial-Residential (C-R) and does allow storage facilities with a Special Use Permit. Staff had not received any comments from the public either for or against the project. With these findings of fact, Staff recommended approval of the Special Use Permit.

The applicant, Mr. Joseph Holland, addressed the Commission. Mr. Holland said that the community was very much in need of more storage facilities and felt that his project would help meet those demands.

Mr. Ronald Longenbaugh asked the Commission about the growth of the area and if there would be more
commercial uses, as he was under the impression the area was residential. He had no comments either for or against the project.

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, addressed Mr. Longenbaugh's question, showing the Commission and Mr. Longenbaugh the areas that were zoned C-R and stated that it did have the potential for more commercial uses.

With there being no further comments from either the public or the Commission, Commissioner Muder made a motion to recommend approval to the Board. Commissioner Rogers seconded the motion. Motion passed unanimously, 8 - 0 .

SUP 23-002 Kennison Contractor Yard - District IV: Consideration of a  request for a Special Use Permit to expand use of an existing primary dwelling to include indoor storage of contractor's equipment on a 0.44-acre parcel located in Overgaard. APN# 206-27-020, T12N, R17E, S33. Owner: Kennison Brian R & Silvia M Family Revocable Trust. Agent: Brian Kennison:

Ms. Sondra Starke, Planner I, presented the item to the Commission. The property owner, Mr. Brian Kennison was requesting a Special Use Permit (SUP) to add a use to his permitted single-family dwelling as a storage garage for his landscaping business' equipment.

The property is just to the west of Bison Ranch, and the zoning for the property is R1-10. The zoning does allow for contractor's yards, with the condition that they obtain an SUP. Ms. Starke further shared with the Commission a site plan of the project and explained that the permitted structure would have an 800 square foot living quarters inside of it, and then 1600 square feet for equipment storage.

The use is compatible with the surrounding area, as the property is adjacent to C-R (Commercial Residential) zoning along Highway 260. The project has been determined to be compatible with current and future uses and fits in with the Comprehensive Plan. With these findings of fact, Staff recommended approval.

The applicant, Brian Kennison, addressed the Commission and thanked them for their time. He stated that the reason they chose this property was because of the Assessor's web description of the list of allowed uses, one of which was a contractor's yard, with an asterisk attached to it. Mr. Kennison also mentioned that the location was ideal for his business as it was very close to Bison Ranch, which he had contracts with for 18 years to maintain their grounds and roads.

Mr. Kennison had not inquired further than the list of allowed uses and moved his equipment onto the property. Following a complaint, he was informed by Code Enforcement that he was required to obtain a Special Use Permit.

Vice-Chairwoman Smith opened the meeting for public comment.

There were no comments in favor of the project.

Comments against the project:

Sandy Kreston, Jeff Schwoerer, John Schusler, and Kim Johnston all had comments against the project, including:

The yard was currently a mess, with equipment and materials scattered around, and had been that way for two to three years.

Concern over code violations.

The lot did not have a home on it.

The current screening was not sufficient.

The building proposed would not be big enough to hold the equipment.

The business was bringing down property values.

Noise of employees and machinery.

Hours of Operation.

Invasive lighting.

Concern over who would be living on the property.

Concern over harmful chemicals being stored on the property.

Concern that the equipment would damage the roads and the easement used to access the property.

There would be gravel and dirt on the roads because of the equipment that made walking or biking difficult.

Vice-Chairwoman Smith asked Mr. Kennison to address some of these comments.

Mr. Kennison responded:

The building would be arriving next Monday, and groundbreaking would begin in April, shielding the closest neighbor's view from his back deck.

The yard would be screened with trees and landscaping, and the current screening was temporary.

His hours of operation for his business would be from 8:00 AM to 4:00 PM. Most work would be done off-site, with employees only picking up equipment in the morning and returning it by the end of the day.

The easement in question was used by two other commercial businesses.

He would not be storing harmful chemicals or materials.

Mr. Kennison also commented that it was likely he would rent the living quarters to one of his employees, of which he had two.

Current materials on the property were for the construction of the building and he would no longer be storing materials on site once construction was complete.

Any damage to the roads because of the equipment would be repaired by his company, as he was under contract with Bison Ranch to maintain them.

He would not be doing retail business on site.

Repairs would be done inside of the building, not outside. Mr. Kennison also had an off-site mechanic to which would take the equipment.

The structure is permitted by Navajo County.

All the non-compliant equipment will be moved to a different property.

Vice-Chairwoman Smith asked about the easement being blocked. Mr. Kennison replied that the easement back behind the auto body shop towards the Dollar General had been blocked for roughly ten years. Vice-Chairwoman Smith also provided comments about the storing of materials and parking of private vehicles on the easement close to the fire hydrant and requested that they be moved for public safety, to which Mr. Kennison agreed.

Commissioner Rogers asked about any Code Enforcement violations. Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, responded saying that there was an open Code Enforcement violation on the property, which is why Mr. Kennison was applying for this SUP to resolve the issues. The violation was the outdoor storage of contractors' materials, as well as the utilization of the contractors' yard.  Code Enforcement stated that the applicant either needed to acquire an SUP or cease the usage in order to close the open violation.

Commissioner Murph asked for clarification of the need for the SUP process, what was by-right usage, and when the line was drawn to have to apply for an SUP, as the building permit had been issued. Mr. Cooper noted that the hearing process is in place for Staff to be able to ensure that the use fits in with the Comprehensive Plan, and that public interests and safety be considered in a public forum.

Mr. Mischa Larisch, Public Works Deputy Director of Planning & Development Services, approached the Commission to state the reason Mr. Kennison's use was not by-right was because he had outside employees come to the property. Otherwise, if he had only his family running the business, he would be free to store the equipment inside of his building without an SUP.

Commissioner Muder asked if storage containers were allowed in the R1-10 zoning. Mr Cooper replied that they were allowed under certain parameters in the Zoning Ordinance.

There was more discussion regarding fencing, with Commissioner Rogers asking if they could add in a stipulation that he fence the property. Mr. Cooper responded that the Commission could add in that requirement but could only go so far as to require a solid fence, not material types.

Vice-Chairwoman Smith asked Staff to clarify the 12-month sunset clause on the SUP. Mr. Cooper addressed this, stating that the applicant had 12 months to start construction, or the permit would expire.

Commissioner Howe made a motion to recommend approval to the Board, with an addition that a fence be put in per staff recommendation to shield them from the neighbors on the east. Commissioner Rogers seconded the motion. Motion passed, 7 - 1 ( Commissioner Booher ).

SUP-A 23-001 Evergreen Storage - District IV:   Consideration of a  request for a Special Use Permit Amendment from an RV Park and RV storage facility to a two-phase RV, trailer, and mini-storage facility on a vacant 4.30-acre parcel in the Overgaard area.  APN# 206-02-012C, T12N, R17E, S30. Owner: Larry and Dianne Holland. Applicant/Agent: Logan Rogers:

Both Vice-Chairwoman Smith and Commissioner Rogers temporarily recused themselves from the hearing for this item, as they both had a conflict of interest in the project, with Commissioner Rogers being the applicant and Vice-Chairwoman Smith being the representative for the real-estate transaction.

Per the bylaws of the Commission, members needed to elect a Chair Pro Tem to proceed with the meeting and continue.

Commissioner Murph nominated Commissioner Howe as Chair Pro tem; Commissioner Muder seconded the motion. The vote was unanimous, 6 - 0 , for the election of Commissioner Howe as Pro tem.

With this business concluded, Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, presented the item to the Commission, giving them a brief history of the project, stating that this was an amendment to an existing Special Use Permit that had been approved by the Board of Supervisors in October of 2022.

The approval was given to the current landowner, with a different applicant, with the condition that the project be started within twelve (12) months or the permit would expire. This meant that the previous approval was still active. The current applicant was requesting an amendment with their own proposal for the property, which would have two phases. The original approval was for 20 to 25 RV Park sites and 20 storage units.

The amended use proposes that Phase One be 200 self-storage units with 44 RV and trailer storage spaces. Phase two would then remove the RV and trailer storage, add an additional 100 storage units, and add a commercial center. Mr. Cooper showed the area of notification for this amendment, noting that there was no public response either for or against the project. Staff recommended approval of the amendment.

Applicant, Logan Rogers, addressed the Commission, stating that the reason he took on this project was to serve a need for more storage facilities in the community as the current storage facilities in the area are at full capacity and have been so for years. His reason for requesting the two-phase project was to minimize the need to amend another permit, as they were planning for future expansion.

With there being no further comments from the Commission or the public, Commissioner Muder made a motion to recommend approval to the Board. Commissioner Murph seconded the motion. Motion carried unanimously, 6 - 0 .
