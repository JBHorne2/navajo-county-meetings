ITEM #5 –SUP-A 22-001 HASHKNIFE ENERGY CENTER II, DISTRICT III:  Discussion 
and possible action on a request by Hashknife Energy Center II, LLC for a Special Use 
Permit Amendment to allow for the expansion of a previously approved Special Use Permit 
for the building and operatio n of a solar generation and storage facility from 400 MW to 
700 MW and from approximately 3,960 acres to approximately 5,240 acres located on 
APN# 107-01-023, 107-05-031C, 107-05-031D, 110-15-001D, 110-15-008, 110-15-001D, 
& 110-15-001E which are portions of T18N, R19E, S30, 31, & 32, & T17N, R19E, S05, 
06, 09, & 15 of the GSRM.  Owner: Aztec Land Co., LLC & Aztec Land & Cattle Co., Ltd. 
Agent: Hashknife Energy Center II, LLC 
 
Mr. Cooper presented the project to the Commission, noting that this was an amendment 
to a Special Use Permit (S.U.P.) that originally came before the  Commission and Board 
of Supervisors in 2019 and again in 2020 as an S.U.P. Amendment.   The applicant had 
already completed their limited construction, in line with Condition 2 of the pre vious 
S.U.P.s.  Additionally, the applicants had provided Interconnection Agreements, in line 
with Condition 3(h) of the original S.U.P.s.  Finally, annual reports had been supplied to 
Staff, in conjunction with the original Condition 10.  Mr. Cooper provided the Commission 
with some visuals of the full proposed site , along with comparisons for the approved vs. 
the proposed amendment.  The proposal met all applicable requirements of the Zoning 
Ordinance, Comprehensive Plan, and Aztec area plan.  Due to  this, Staff recommended 
approval. 
 
Commissioner Murph asked what plans the project had to deal with fires and if an 
amendment would be needed. 
 
Mr. Cooper advised that legal had looked over the plans and advised that there should be 
no amendments.  That decision was  based off the applicant needing a detailed fire plan 
in place with their original approval. 
 
Commissioner Rogers asked if the  applicants had  completed Phase 1 . Mr. Cooper 
advised it would be best for the applicant to speak on this topic. 
 
Paul Burman s tated that the applicants were working with APS to finish analyzing the 
project and to keep  the project compatible with environmental needs.  There are also 
issues with the speed of global supply chains for technical components , though Mr.

Burman stated that energy projects were quite slow in general.  He felt confident that this 
project was likely to be selected for approval with the Corporation Commission.  
 
Commissioner Rogers then summarized, to clarify, that they do not have a contract with 
APS to sell power and are waiting in line for approval. 
 
Mr. Burman agreed and mentioned, given new policies, they felt confident that they would 
be selected soon.  
 
Chairman Latham asked if the applicant wished to give further comment. 
 
Lisa Phillips, from Invenergy, approached the Commission, asking Mr. Burman if there 
was anything more to add. Mr. Burman noted that he was grateful for the work Staff had 
done. He also touched on Commissioner Murph’s  earlier question about fire safety .  
Invenergy have been proactively working with the Joseph City Fire District to work on an 
out-of-district bill and engaged Fire Chief Rusty DeSpain in creating an agreement in case 
of emergency. 
 
Commissioner Rogers asked for an estimate of when construction would start. 
 
Mr. Burman stated that October 2023 was the goal, but it could begin as soon as February 
2023, depending on when APS contracts are finalized. 
 
Commissioner Murph asked about the Purchase Power Agreement, asking if it needs to 
be in place before they begin constructio n. He also asked about the agreements on file 
and what the difference between these are. 
 
Katie DeSpain, from Invenergy, addressed this, stating that the agreements on file are the 
interconnection agreements with utilities and were part of the conditions set forth for initial 
approval of the project. 
 
Commissioner Murph asked when they think they will have a Power Purchase 
Agreement. 
 
Mr. Burman answered that they  submitted to APS earlier this year, but the selection of 
projects is ongoing. The purchase agreements will be announced later this year, but the 
applicants are hopeful and actively marketing this to APS 
 
Commissioner Rogers asked if there was a need for more power or if there is another 
reason to ask for an expansion. 
 
Mr. Burman said that his team looked at the load forecast  and they estimated a needed 
growth from 1% to 3% would be necessary to be more appealing to APS. He also noted 
that with the existing infrastructure and Cholla eventually  closing they were looking to 
backfill with more green energy.  
 
Chairman Latham called for a motion.  Commissioner Booher made a motion to 
recommend approval to the Board of Supervisors.  Commissioner Rogers seconded the 
motion.  Motion carried 5 – 0 (Commissioner Lister abstaining).

ITEM #6 – SP 23-001 BUCK SPRINGS RESORT REVIEW, DISTRICT V: A request for a Site Plan
Review to expand a previously approved resort, with associated accommodations, which had included 63
lodging units, to the same use with a total of 64 lodging units (1 additional unit), with a layout change,
located on a 19.77-acre parcel. APN# 411-40-018, T08N, R23E, S02. Owner: ET Phone Home, LLC
Agent: Laron Turley.
Background:
Consideration and possible adoption of Resolution 23-01P, approving/denying a Site Plan Review to
expand a previously approved resort, with associated accommodations, which had included 63 lodging
units, to the same use with a total of 64 lodging units (1 additional unit), with a layout change, located
on APN# 411-40-018, which is a portion of Township 08 North, Range 23 East, Section 02 of the Gila
and Salt River Meridian, in the Pinetop area.
The applicant in this case is attempting to expand the existing resort on APN # 411-40-018.  Currently,
there are twenty-four (24) lodging units on this site, though the original resort was approved for
sixty-three (63) units.  With this proposal, the applicant would add a total of forty (40) new cabins,
resulting in a total of sixty-four (64) lodging units on the property.  This would result in an increase of one
(1) lodging unit, with a different layout as compared to the originally approved site plan. 
Due to the property being zoned Special Development (SD) and the original approvals/resolutions for this
site, the applicant is required to conduct Site Plan Review and approval with the Navajo County Planning
and Zoning Commission in order to further develop the property in any manner.
Recommendation: 
Navajo County Planning and Development Staff recommends approval of this Site Plan Review, subject
to conditions.

To whom it may concern, 
 
My name is Laron Turley, and I am the Executive Vice President of Development at LOGE Camps, an 
innovative outdoor recreation hospitality company.  Our goal is to help people find their place by 
making it easy to connect, get out and explore.  LOGE currently operates hotels in the Pacific Northwest

NM and St George, UT, and on the East Coast.   
 
I am writing to introduce an expansion plan for a 19.67-acre parcel in Pinetop, currently known as the 
Buck Springs Resort at 6126 Buck Springs Rd, Pinetop, AZ 95935.  LOGE’s project specific entity, Radify 
Pinetop, LLC, is currently under contract to acquire the site from Buck Springs Resort, LLC.  As part of the 
acquisition and repositioning of the property into a LOGE Camps, we are submitting a site plan for 
approval to expand the current Rental Resort use with a new check-in building that will also serve as a 
small café and bar for hotel guests and the community, a separate meeting building, spa building, and 
restroom facilities building, along with the addition of 40 cabins dispersed throughout the 19.67-acres. 
 
The cabins are approximately 400 square foot “hotel suites” that are prefabricated off-site and then 
placed on a permanent foundation with utility connections.  Each cabin will have ensuite bathrooms and 
be self-contained hotel suites with sleeping area and bathroom.  The concept is to cluster the cabins into 
pods of five cabins to reduce the amount of site disturbance and cluster utility services too.  There will 
be eight pods of five cabins each spread throughout the site to get us to a total of 40 new cabins. 
 
In addition to the expanded buildings mentioned above, LOGE properties feature outdoor fire pits, BBQ 
cooking areas, activity lawns, small stage for live music, on-site trails, bicycle stations, or other outdoor 
recreation amenities.   
 
As an Arizonan native (born and raised in Mesa, AZ), I am very excited to be a part of the future of the 
Buck Springs Resort and brining LOGE Camps to the Pinetop market.  We look forward to meeting during 
our pre-app process.

Mr. Osgood began with explaining that they were bringing this to the commission after receiving 
community feedback regarding subdivision regulations. 
There were people from the public there to also give some testimony about the topic. 
He gave a brief summery of why subdivisions were different from just raw land splits but noted that 
unfortunately the public often became confused by the two. 
The issues first arose when the Minor Land Division Ordinance was approved, as property owners felt 
that their rights were infringed upon, as well as intentions of older subdivisions coming into conflict with 
the Ordinance. 
He wanted to have an open discussion about the various issues that had come up from the community 
and the Board of Supervisors informally to help better serve the community with the commission, as 
well as get their feedback for moving forward with a more formal request. He also mentioned that there 
was a committee that was reviewing this and coming up with solutions for this tricky situation. 
The challenges are: 
• The current Subdivision Ordinance requires a denial of splits within subdivisions. 
• Property owners that had previously planned to split their property for years prior to MLD 
Ordinance going into place and are now unable to move ahead with their plans. 
• Some approved Subdivision’s CC&R’s had envisioned and anticipated lot splits, which is in direct 
conflict with the Subdivision Ordinance. 
• This leads to issues getting building permits as the splits that have been done bring a title 
restriction for these lots that are now out of compliance, as well as out of compliance lots 
unable to be issued permits due to being split prior to the MLD Ordinance. 
• There are issues with an unsustainable amount of lots in some subdivision infrastructures

of text amendment to Article 25, Section 2517 of the Navajo County 
Zoning Ordinance, regarding age of mobile home which requires 
HUD rehabilitation certification. 
 
 
BACKGROUND 
 
The Arizona Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) requires all mobile homes of 
a jurisdiction -indicated age to be certified as rehabilitated in accordance  with current HUD-
established safety standards, in accordance with R4-34-606. The Navajo County Zoning Ordinance 
currently reflects the HUD-indicated age at which mobile homes must obtain certification of 
rehabilitation as having been prior to June 15, 1976 . Navajo County updated to the 2018 IRC/IBC 
on June 22, 2022 to reflect modern safety sta ndards. As a result, the proposed Zoning Ordinance 
Text Amendment attempts to bridge the gap between 1976 standards and 2018 standards for mobile 
home safety assurance upon relocation of older dwellings.  
 
STAFF ANALYSIS 
 
The efforts put forth by Navajo County to promote safety and wellness within the community by 
adopting the 2018 IRC and 2018 IBC have clarified that all development in the community shall be 
held to a regulated and modern standard of quality. In effect, mobile home movement must also be 
done in a regulated manner to ensure that Navajo County is a safe county in which to reside. Navajo 
County has discovered a discrepancy within which mobile homes manufactured between June of 
1976 and the current day that would benefit from HUD certification to verify safe living standards 
and assist homeowners in rehabilitation awareness  are being relocated in their uncertified state s. 
This gap in coverage provides for unsafe homes to remain unsafe, whereas updating the requirement 
to a rolling 20 -year basis would provide for an opportunity to ensure that the mobile homes to be

<!-- page 60 -->
Planning and Zoning Commission                                     January 19, 2023        2 | P a g e  
relocated are safe and habitable by modern standards. 
  
Staff has determined through research that, by continuing to follow the HUD-indicated age marker 
of June 22, 1976, there is a considerable concern with the above discrepancy, which validates interest 
in adjusting the age threshold by a minimum of 20 years on a rolling basis. While researching, Staff 
findings included a section of the Rehabilitation Assistance Packet on the State of Arizona 
Department of Housing website that gives notice to applicants that the local jurisdiction may have 
age requirements that exceed the threshold determined by HUD: 
 
“Please verify the local zoning requirements with the city, town or county BEFORE 
planning to relocate a mobile home. Some local jurisdictions have requirements that 
exceed the state requirements; some local jurisdictions do not allow mobile homes.” 
  
Additionally, Staff found that there is a financial assistance program through the HUD Relocation 
Fund which can reimburse rehabilitation costs  up to $1,500.00 to the homeowner if they are at , or 
below, the current federal poverty guideline set by the United States Department of Health and 
Human Services. Information for this p rogram is available on the HUD website . Fees for 
rehabilitation certification are dependent on income in that there is a discounted rate for homeowners 
under the federal poverty guideline. 
 
  
Based upon the above, Staff is currently recommending that the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance 
be updated to require all mobile homes 20 years old or older to be HUD rehabilitation certified and 
permitted by Navajo County prior to relocation.
