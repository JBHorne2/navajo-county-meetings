TXT-A 23-004 AMENDMENT TO ARTICLE 25 - OUTDOOR LIGHTING:  Consideration of a text amendment to Article 25, Section 2519 of the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance, regarding light pollution, and effective ways to prevent light trespass:

Mr. Tyler Richards, Planner I, presented the item to the Commission. He gave a quick overview of the history of the Lighting Ordinance which had been passed in 1997. Its purpose was to restrict light from projecting into the night sky as it had negative effects on astronomical observations. The proposed amendment was being brought to the Commission to update the Ordinance with new technological advancements in mind, cleaning up the language to be easier to understand and comply with, as well as to include language towards light trespass and light pollution. Staff had worked closely with Code Enforcement, as they currently do not have provisions to regulate complaints of light trespass between neighbors. All existing light fixtures would not need to be updated to comply, and the ordinance would only apply to new dwellings. All residentially zoned, outdoor lighting would be required to be fully shielded in the R1-43, R1-10, R-2 and R-3 zonings. Any property that is zoned RU, or A-General would also have this requirement, if located within a platted subdivision. Any outdoor lighting would need to keep lighting from crossing property boundaries and would not be permitted to affect neighboring properties in adverse ways.

Chair Latham asked Mr. Richards if the proposed shielded lighting was readily available to the public. Mr. Richards confirmed that there were many fully shielded options available commercially.

Chair Latham also asked about fixtures for back yard lighting, like a streetlight or other lighting that would illuminate gardens and such. Mr. Richards stated that any outdoor, residentially zoned, light fixture would be required to have full shielding, and that Staff were trying to focus on not allowing light to travel in a horizontal line onto other neighboring properties.

Commissioner Rogers stated that the Commission and Staff would have to be very careful proceeding with this, as it could potentially cause a limiting factor for residents and contractors, as the lights that are most readily available are not fully shielded and most lights have horizontal lighting.

Chair Latham asked Staff why there was a need to regulate. Mr. Richards stated that there had been several Code Enforcement complaints where neighbors had been shining lights onto each other's houses which was causing issues, and Navajo County did not currently have any provisions in place to prevent or regulate these incidents.

Commissioner Booher asked if light intensity had been considered over the shielding. Mr. Richards stated that Staff had investigated intensity-based regulations, such as a Dark Skies ordinance, but these types of restrictions seemed too restrictive and complex for the broader County regulations. Thus, Staff opted for a simplified solution that prohibited light from crossing onto neighboring properties and would give Code Enforcement an easy solution to bring people back into compliance.

Commissioner Howe stated concern over the proposal being too simplified, noting that it might be better to identify intense direct light, such as with ADOT's regulations regarding the lighting on billboards, as the agency had issues with incorrectly angled lights that caused glare.

Chair Latham asked if the ordinance would prevent hand-held light nuisances or passing cars with high beams. Mr. Richards responded that they had put provisions in the proposed amendment to address such things.

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, also addressed the Commission, stating that Staff had worked closely with Code Enforcement and had taken several things into consideration for this proposal. As things are currently, Navajo County has one Code Enforcement Officer, and sending him out at night to measure lumens was not ideal. The Officer in question had a lot of experience working in Code Enforcement and had previously worked for the City of Phoenix.

Mr. Cooper explained that the proposal would allow our Code Enforcement Officer to cite people if they are directly shining lights into their neighbor's windows or onto neighboring properties. As far as shielding, light can still be directed as needed onto one's own property so long as it's contained within the boundaries, and the intent of the amendment was not to limit visibility or safety. Mr. Cooper further noted that the shielding restrictions would apply to the densely populated zoning districts, mostly within the county islands near Show Low and Pinetop-Lakeside; The trespass restrictions would be County-wide to help with light pollution.

Commissioner Rogers asked if there was a way to make the ordinance complaint-driven, so that if the property owners were responsible, they would be able to have whatever fixtures they wished. Commissioner Rogers noted that this amendment did not prevent property owners who were potentially in violation from simply putting up compliant lighting for show, and then removing it once inspections had been completed. Mr. Cooper stated that the current reality of the situation is that Navajo County is primarily complaint-driven, and until a complaint is received, there is little that the County can do. Code Enforcement complaints go unsolved due to there being no provisions that prevent the violations.

Commissioner Howe asked about using lumens to regulate, agreeing that it should apply to new constructions, as well as questioning when transitioning out streetlights and such, if the utility companies that used high pressure sodium lights and otherwise noncompliant lighting would be notified of new regulations. Mr. Cooper stated that the ordinance was more intended for neighbor-to-neighbor nuisances as most streetlights would likely be within ADOT's Right-of-Way or within Municipalities, with very few areas of Navajo County using lights provided by utility companies. Mr. Cooper also noted that they had a few provisional standards for wattages and light levels for LED's, but it still presented the issue of having to send someone out with a light meter at night.

Commissioner Booher asked if this ordinance was in line with other counties in the area. Mr. Cooper replied that it was, keeping in mind not to be as restrictive as Coconino's Dark Sky ordinance.

Vice-Chair Smith asked if this pertained only to residential areas, not commercial, Mr. Cooper confirmed.

There was more discussion about the feasibility of such an ordinance, with the Commission deciding to recommend an amendment.

Commissioner Rogers motioned to recommend approval of the ordinance, striking the section that pertained to the shielding, but maintaining the section of light trespassing onto adjacent properties. Commissioner Booher seconded. Motion carried unanimously, 8 - 0 .

SUP-A 23-002 HASHKNIFE ENERGY CENTER II, DISTRICT III:  Consideration of a request by Hashknife Energy Center II, LLC for a Special Use Permit to allow for the expansion of a previously approved Special Use Permit for the building and operation of a solar generation and storage facility from approximately 5,240 acres to approximately 5,840 acres, while maintaining the same generation and storage capacity, located on APN# 107-01-023, 107-05-031C, 107-05-031D, 110-15-001D, 110-15-008, 110-15-001E, & 110-15-006A which are portions of T18N, R19E, S30, 31, & 32, & T17N, R19E, S05, 06, 09, 14, & 15 of the GSRM.  Owner: Aztec Land Co., LLC, Aztec Land & Cattle Co., Ltd., and S Bar Land Inc. Agent: Hashknife Energy Center II, LLC:

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, presented the item to the Commission. Mr. Cooper gave a brief overview of the history of the project with the first approval in November of 2019 for a 400-Megawatt (MW) facility with battery storage, with 3,000 acres. Building permits were opened and finalized for construction shortly thereafter, to meet conditions set forth in the approval. The applicant then requested an amendment in 2020 to upgrade to an area of 3,960 acres, which was approved by the Board of Supervisors in June of that year.
Finally, in 2022 the applicants applied for another amendment to upgrade to a 700 MW facility, with an expansion from 3,960 acres to 5,240 acres.

The applicant was now applying to expand the project area to 5,840 acres, to better accommodate their engineer's findings for array placement. The applicant had met other conditions from the original application and subsequent amendments, including supplying Staff with the interconnection agreements for portions of the project that have been approved. The applicants had also provided annual development reports- the most up-to-date report being received just recently.

Mr. Cooper proceeded to share with the Commission a visual of the expansion and noted that Staff had not received any comments either for or against the project.

Chair Latham asked if the storage and generation capacity would change. Mr. Cooper stated that they would not change, and he deferred to the applicant to explain.

Applicant, Paul Burman, approached the Commission and confirmed the added land is needed to accommodate more space, as requested by his team. Mr. Burman stated that in doing their due diligence the applicants had identified additional flood areas to avoid. The applicants had also been working with Arizona Game and Fish and US Fish and Wildlife, both of which had identified more wildlife corridors. A variety of cultural surveys were completed, which found scattered artifacts across the landscape that, while not required to be added to the Historical Register, the applicants still did not wish to disturb. The additional acreage would allow the developer to keep their generation and storage goals while avoiding anything they could potentially impact.

Commissioner Howe asked Mr. Burman to confirm if they were just going for site flexibility and not increasing storage or generation. Mr. Burman confirmed that the developer was not looking to expand the project's footprint as they were comfortable with the 700 MW capacity.

Commissioner Rogers asked Mr. Burman when they would begin project construction. Mr. Burman stated that the applicants would start construction as soon as they received their Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) from Arizona Public Service (APS).

Commissioner Murph asked about the progress with making an agreement with the Joseph City Fire Department. Mr. Burman stated that they are still in the process of working out a service area agreement and it remains the applicants' intent to come into an agreement with the Fire Department.

Commissioner Murph asked Mr. Burman to clarify that the agreement was with them as developers, and not as the landowners. Mr. Burman confirmed this to be the case.

Commissioner Murph spoke on the lack of training the Joseph City Fire Department had for Lithium-Ion battery fires, along with the lack of resources available to them to combat the potential dangers that a Lithium-Ion battery fire could pose, citing the landowners' unwillingness to join the district and shifting the responsibility of funding preparations for a potential disaster to the people of Joseph City.  Commissioner Murph noted that he felt the applicant should be required to join the fire district and felt that Commissioners should complete research on Lithium-Ion battery fires.

Mr. Cooper addressed some of Commissioner Murph's concerns, stating that while the Commission could recommend any sort of condition, the project was outside of the district of the Joseph City Fire Department. Because of this, it would fall under the purview of the State Fire Marshall and the applicants would still be required to provide a Fire Control and Prevention Plan approved by the State Fire Marshal for their battery storage before they could break ground. Mr. Cooper further stated that Legal had also advised Staff not to impose a requirement to join a Fire District upon applicants, with the reason being that if the District was unable to legally annex a property the condition would then be tying the hands of developers.

Chair Latham asked Mr. Cooper to clarify if the previous approval was changed by the decisions made that night. Mr. Cooper said that regardless of what the Commission or Board of Supervisors chose to do with this amendment, the previous approvals would stand.

Commissioner Rogers made a motion to recommend approval. The motion was seconded by  Commissioner Booher. The motion carried unanimously, 8 - 0 .

SUP 23-006 VERIZON AZ3 SW RAINBOW LAKE WIRELESS COMMUNICATION FACILITY, DISTRICT IV: Consideration of a request by Young Design Corp for a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction of a 105' monopine wireless communication tower and associated facilities in the Lakeside area.  APN#  212-26-117A, T09N, R22E, S24 of the GSRM. Owner:  Shumway Storage Family LLLP.  Agent:  Linda Grice:

Ms. Sondra Starke, Planner I, presented the item to the Commission, giving them an overview of the area and noting its proximity to a school. The Zoning for the property was Commercial Residential (C-R). There was currently an approved mini-storage utility facility on the property, and the applicants were proposing to put this wireless communication tower on the northwest corner of the property. The plans included an easement for access off Porter Mountain Road, set on the opposing side to the current access for the Mini Storage facility.

Ms. Starke commented on the ten-foot fence around the perimeter of the tower, stating that it would not cut off the easement. She also showed the Commission the 300-foot Area of Notification and stated that the site was posted. Ms. Starke then went on to describe the zoning of the surrounding areas, stating there was both C-R and A-General zoning, with the proposed use in line with current and future uses in the vicinity of the proposal, along with meeting the current goals of the Comprehensive Plan. With the findings of fact, Staff was recommending approval of the Special Use Permit.

Commissioner Rogers asked if the facility would improve the service in the area. Ms. Starke confirmed that it would and that the need was determined by the company, due to complaints about service.

Commissioner Murph asked about Condition 10, which stated the property owner to be responsible for removal of the tower, as he was under the impression that the developer or the tower owner was the one to remove the decommissioned tower. Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, addressed this and stated it was a typical condition meant to provide the County with a failsafe for removal, clarifying that usually there was a lease agreement between the property owner and the developer or tower owner for removal. Mr. Cooper further stated that the Commission could make an amendment to the proposed condition to include the developer, and/or tower owner as well.

Chair Latham opened for public comment.

Ms. Jill Iveson spoke against the project, complaining about the 300-foot area of notification being too small, as well as citing concerns over 5G causing damaging health issues that could affect children and wildlife.

Chair Latham asked if Staff could address her concerns, Mr. Cooper responded that there had been no peer-reviewed evidence received or reviewed by staff that confirmed these claims, and that the frequencies transmitted by the 5G towers were reviewed and approved by Federal Agencies. Staff had done extensive research into potential harm caused by 5G and have found no verifiable peer-reviewed studies that show adverse effects to health or wildlife. Ms. Starke also noted that the 300' area of notification was a regulation set forth by the State of Arizona in the Revised Statutes.

Commissioner Booher made a motion to recommend approval to the Board of Supervisors, Commissioner Rogers seconded. Motion carried unanimously, 8 - 0 .

SUP 23-007 VERIZON AZ3 CONCHO HWY WIRELESS COMMUNICATIONS FACILITY, DISTRICT III:  Consideration of a request by Young Design Corp for a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction of a 105' self-support lattice communication tower and associated facilities in the Snowflake area. APN#: 403-25-001, T13N, R23E, S09 of the GSRM. Owner: Susan Jacobs. Agent: Young Design Corp:

Ms. Sondra Starke, Planner I, presented this item to the Commission. Ms. Starke showed a graphic of the location and gave an overview of the area, located outside the Town of Snowflake off Concho Highway. Ms. Starke stated that the zoning for the surrounding area and the property was RU-20, which was very rural with an abundance of vacant land. There was an existing tower on the site and while the site is rural it would have a dedicated utility easement off Aztec Road. There would also be an eight-foot fence around the perimeter of the project site.

Chair Latham opened for public comment.

Mr. Jesse Dukes from the public asked if this tower was for Verizon's use only and asked why this project only had an 8-foot fence, as the previous item, which was a similar cell tower, had a 10-foot fence. He cited concerns over local children climbing the fence to get to the tower. Mr. Dukes felt that a 10-foot fence would be a better deterrent as the nature of the chain-link made it harder to climb the higher you got.

Applicant, Linda Grice, addressed these questions, stating that the tower was for Verizon's use, but there was the ability to collocate on this tower with other carriers. Ms. Grice also stated that the fencing was the development standard of the company and that a determined teenager would probably still climb it regardless of an extra two feet, but also said that the applicants were willing to raise the height as required.

Vice-Chair Smith noted that it might be best for the height to remain consistent between sites.

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, also addressed fencing, stating that there was a recommended condition that the fence design would be reviewed and approved by Navajo County Planning & Development. The higher construction also meant there would be a need for the plans to be reviewed by engineers for safety.

With no further discussion, Commissioner Rogers motioned to recommend approval of the Special Use Permit, seconded by Commissioner Booher. Motion carried unanimously, 8 - 0 .
