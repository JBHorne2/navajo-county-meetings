SUP 23-011 US-AZ-5117 SILVER CREEK WIRELESS COMMUNICATION FACILITY, DISTRICT III:
Consideration of request by Destree Development on behalf of Vertical Bridge to allow for the construction of an 80' rusticated monopole wireless communication tower and associated facilities in the White Mountain Lakes area. APN#  304-34-328, T11N, R22E, S24 of the GSRM. Owner: White Mountain Lake Property Owners Assn. Agent: Destree Development for Vertical Bridge:

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, presented the item to the Commission, noting the location of the project next to an existing post office on the property in Edmonds circle. Mr. Cooper noted that the project is within a residential designated area, per the Zoning Ordinance, and shared with the Commission several photo simulations that depicted the proposed location.
The Applicant held a public meeting on July 27th, and shared with the Commission the sign in sheets and went over the comments received, which were as follows:

If the site would have 5g.

Health and safety concerns.

Ensuring the site would be operating within licenses and emissions.

What need there was for the site if there was already coverage.

Why the chosen location.

Why cellular one wouldn't be citing there.

If there were more meetings and how to stay involved.

What the pole would look like as far as stealthing.

What would happen if there were more antennas added or antennas changed in the future.

If the site could cause more power outages in the area.

Why alternate means couldn't be utilized.

If the carrier would have radio frequency measurement insurance.

If the applicant was aware of other proposed sites in the area and why the applicant wouldn't talk to others making proposals.

How far the site would cover as far as range.

Contact terms and process for approval of the site.

The applicant addressed these concerns and provided the Commission with copies in the Commissioners' packets.

Mr. Cooper stated that the tower would be 80 feet but was unable to meet the required 160' setbacks on the southern side of the property, being only 113' from the southern parcel boundary. This would mean the Applicant would be required to obtain a setback waiver granted by the Board of Supervisors.
Commissioner Rogers asked Mr. Cooper to clarify the process for obtaining a setback waiver.  Mr. Cooper explained that the Board of Supervisors added that provision into the Ordinance that the Board of Supervisors could waive the setback requirements for residentially designated properties, at their discretion, with sufficient evidence.

The Applicant, Reg Destree, then spoke to the Commission, giving a brief description of the history of his project, and how there was a need for the site. He showed the before and after coverage maps, detailing why this site was proposed and how the project would benefit the community to the east, as the area currently does not have sufficient coverage. Mr. Destree acknowledged that the site does not conform to all the standards, but he felt that this area was the best option. He also stated that the Applicant's team would be looking to acquire a waiver to the setbacks from the Board of Supervisors.

Commissioner Rogers questioned the Applicant, Reg Destree, about the stealthing for the tower. Mr. Destree replied that any stealthing the Commission or the Board of Supervisors required or preferred could be used but felt that in this area the tree designs would look out of place.

With no further questions from the Commission, The Chair opened for public comment.

Those in favor of the project:

Leah and Joseph Westover.

The comments being:

The cell tower was needed to increase the ability to call emergency services in areas where residents are unable to get a signal.

Those against the project:

Katherine Smith, Victoria Russell, Pari Davidson, Light Dancing, Dave Smith, Tony Tangelos, Kathy & Buz Essel, Ashley Dodds, Robert Meyer, Steven Brown.

The comments being:

White Mountain Lakes Board approval was questionable as many members felt misrepresented, and there was a lack of proper voting procedures.

Lack of access to the application.

Potential health issues due to 5G that could affect wildlife and children.

If the tower was even necessary, as there was existing coverage for the area.

5G causing Radar Altimeter issues with aircraft.

Radiation poisoning to people and wildlife.

The tower being weaponized to turn citizens into "zombies."

The tower would be in the way of the landing flight path from the south accessory airstrip.

Negative effects on property values.

Negative effects on pacemakers and medical devices.

The electric pollution that the 5G would bring into the area.

Spoiling the land with radio waves.

Better technology than 5G is available and should be used instead.

The Chair then closed the public comment period.

Commissioner Booher asked why Staff had recommended denial of the project.  Mr. Cooper clarified that Staff was recommending denial of the project because it does not follow the Comprehensive Plan's goal as far as providing increased setbacks or buffers for conflicting uses such as those for this proposal.  The Board of Supervisors can waive such requirements for this use, which Staff itself does not have the authority to offer.  The recommendation of denial was related to the setback conflicts and the visual obstruction from the tower.

With there being no further business to discuss, Commissioner Booher made a motion to recommend denial to the Board of Supervisors.  Commissioner Rogers seconded the motion.  Motion passed unanimously, 7 – 0 .

SP 23-002 MOGOLLON RV AND SELF STORAGE, DISTRICT IV:  A request for a Site Plan Review to develop a RV and self-storage facility with 40 RV spaces, and 241 storage units on a vacant, 4.94-acre parcel in the Overgaard area. APN#:  206-25-020B, T32N, R17E, S32. Owner: Mogollon RV and Self-Storage, LLC. Agent:  Alton Bostwick:

Mr. Tyler Richards, Planner I, presented the item to the Commission.  Mr. Richards shared several visuals of the property and noted landmarks in the area. There is a restaurant on the north side of the project, and an RV park on the southern side. Mr. Richards then gave a brief overview of the project's history, stating that the BOS approved the Pine Meadows Country Club Master Development Plan in 2007. As time went on with the economic crisis and ownership became fractured, the owners came before the Board of Supervisors for a Zone Change to allow for Commercial-Residential uses on part of the lots. They also applied for a Master Development Plan Amendment to allow development on land that was around the existing golf course.

The Board of Supervisors determined that any further adjustments to the Master Development Plan would require a Site Plan Review and would only need to be reviewed by the Planning & Zoning Commission. Mr. Richards then provided a map of the area, and the site plan of the project.

The property owner intends to pave the driveway up to the property line and will then switch to a cinder driveway, with a keypad entry gate. The applicant will be adding a water main to the property and have a dry standpipe in the center for a hydrant.

There is an existing utility easement along the eastern side of the property. There is existing chain-link fencing on the northern side of the property, and the Applicant will install solid six-foot metal panel fencing on the southern side of the property to shield the RV park to the south. Staff found that this project fits in with the current and future use of the area, meets the goals of the Comprehensive Plan and as such recommended approval of the project.

Mr. Richards then listed out the requirements Staff recommended for approval for the Commission.

The Applicant, Alton Bostwick, was available to answer questions.

Chair Latham asked for clarification on the 12-month requirement if that meant for the Applicant to complete the project or if that was solely for groundbreaking.  Mr. Richards confirmed that the Applicant only needed to break ground within that 12-month time limit.

Commissioner Muder asked about the fencing that was existing was considered "blocked" or if the proposed fencing was going to be different.  Mr. Richards confirmed that the applicant would be using different fencing, and the northern side of the property was also commercial use and did not need the same shielding as that adjacent to a more residential use.  Mr. Richards also confirmed that the new fencing would have to be constructed, noting that anything existing the applicant intended to improve.

Commissioner Booher asked how the project tied into the Country Club, to which Mr. Richards replied that the parcel was removed from the Country Club's plan in 2007 with the approval of the Board of Supervisors.  The owners came together to the Board of Supervisors to request the ability to develop the land, and it was determined by the Board that any further adjustments would have to obtain a Site Plan Review.

Commissioner Booher asked if there would be a dedicated right turn lane for the project. Mr. Alton Bostwick, the Applicant, replied that he had reached out to Arizona Department of Transportation to obtain a Right-of-Way Permit, and ADOT had determined there was no need for a dedicated turn lane.

There were no public comments.

With no further discussion, Commissioner Muder motioned to approve the Site Plan Review.  Commissioner Rogers seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously, 7 – 0 .

MDP-A 23-001 SHOW LOW MOUNTAIN RANCH, DISTRICT III:  Consideration of a request by Greg Loper for a Master Development Plan Amendment from an approved, approximately 508-unit, single-family residential development with commercial uses to be amended to allow for recreational vehicles/park models and manufactured homes for a total of 758 units along with amenities, accessory uses, and commercial uses in the Show Low Mountain Ranch Special Development within the White Mountain Lake area. APN#: 304-57-033A and 304-57-033B, T11N, R22E, S25 of the GSRM. Owner:  Show Low Holdings 307 LLC/Universal Show Low Properties LLC. Agent:  Greg Loper:

Ms. Sondra Starke, Planner I, presented the item to the Commission. She gave a brief overview of the project, noting the location of the project sits northwest of the of Show Low and of south of White Mountain Lake. The project is located approximately 3.79 miles off Highway 77 between Snowflake and Show Low and approximately 3.56 miles off Highway 60.

Ms. Starke then gave an overview of the history, and the areas that would be affected by the amendment.  The amendment would allow for park models and RV use, which would increase the density. The surrounding Zoning Districts are R1-10, RU-1, and C-R, and is buffered on the north, east and west sides by State land.

Ms. Starke then showed the Commission that points of access were proposed in various locations around the perimeter, with crash gates for emergency access on Silver Lake Blvd, connecting to Roundup Drive.

Ms. Starke showed the Commission the proposed uses for the various parcels and areas, noting what the original and approved Master Development plan looked like, where there was a lack of public open space.

Mr. Greg Loper, the Applicant, also addressed the Commission, going over the bigger changes between the approved plan and the amendment. There would be an additional 300-foot buffer on the south side to shield the residences and accommodate natural landscape, there would be a number of proposed green space and parks to help break up the neighborhood, and he also detailed the traffic flow for the northmost parcel, noting that while there was an increase in possible units, there was a decrease in overall lot coverage.  The goal with this amendment was to help alleviate the housing issues, and help bring more tourism to the area, with the forest being so close.

Chair Latham voiced concerns about the northernmost properties, wanting to know if there would be access for residents in the event of a fire.  Mr. Loper stated that he had worked with Staff to find a solution that would find the area and made sure there would be multiple exits on the south side.

Public comments were opened.

Those against the project:

Michael & Dawn Kludt, Leah Westover

Comments and concerns against the project:

Traffic Concerns.

Density.

If there would be age restrictions on who would be allowed to stay.

The use of Roundup Drive, Silver Lake Drive, and Iron Drive being used for more than just emergencies.

How the development will affect farming and animal husbandry.

How the development will affect grazing rights.

The drainage issues of the site.

Sutter Lane not being drivable.

Septic and Sewer access not being viable.

The Chair then closed the public comment period.

With there being no further comments, Commissioner Booher motioned to recommend approval to the Board of Supervisors.  Commissioner Muder seconded the motion.  Motion passed unanimously, 7 – 0 .
