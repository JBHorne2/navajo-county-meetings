SUP 25-003 EZE STORAGE EXPANSION: Consideration of a request by Ironside Engineering & Development, Inc. for a Special Use Permit to expand an existing storage unit facility to an adjacent, 5.15-acre parcel in the Lakeside area. APN#: 212-15-002H, T9N, R22E, S15 of the GSRM. Owner: Somerset Investment Properties, LLC., Agent: Zach Barlow.

Ms. Katherine Wright, Planner II, presented the project to the Commission. Ms. Wright started with an overview of the scope of the project, its location and the vicinity, stating that it was an expansion of the existing EZE storage facility. There will be 10 storage unit buildings of various sizes, two of which will be placed along the perimeter to create fortress-style fencing in addition to six-foot-tall fencing for the remainder of the property. The expansion will connect to the original EZE storage site and add another ingress/egress off of highway 260. The project, when completed with the original EZE Storage, will wrap around two businesses to the south: J&S Patriot Automotive and the Penrod RV & Mobile Homestead. Ms. Wright further explained that travel between the original EZE and the expansion would be available, and there was a potential for some removal of buildings to support traffic flow.

Ms. Wright also noted that the Owner, Mr. Brian Lemmermann was available to answer questions from the Commission.

Mr. Lemmermann approached the Commission to explain a little more about the project, noting that he and surrounding business owners felt it was the best use for the land. The office and original entrance would remain the same, and this was truly just an expansion.

Commissioner Latham announced to the room that while he was a customer of EZE, he was not offered any quid pro quo or discounts, he was not in a conflict of interest.
Commissioner Latham then asked Mr. Lemmermann about the drainage and paving, Mr. Lemmermann replied that they did wish to repave and fix the gate and sign, and they just wished to do all the construction at once rather than in phases.

There were no comments from the public on the project.

With no further questions from the Commission, the Chair entertained a motion.

Commissioner Latham motioned to recommend approval to the Board of Supervisors.
Commissioner Howe seconded.
Motion passed unanimously, 6 - 0 . ( Commissioner John had not yet joined virtually.)

TXT-A 25-001 NAVAJO COUNTY ZONING ORDINANCE UPDATE: Consideration of a Comprehensive Update to the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance.

Commissioner George John attended virtually while this item was presented.

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, presented the item to the Commission. Mr. Cooper gave a detailed summary of the update, stating that there was a need to streamline the Ordinance for developers and the public, and shared some examples of what the Ordinance currently looked like, and Staff's solutions and corrections. Mr. Cooper also shared how Staff had worked to consolidate the articles, and the new format would bring clarity to several things that were not immediately apparent with the current layout of the Ordinance. One such example was how articles had referenced back to previous articles for allowed uses, causing the reader to go through multiple articles for information. This would no longer be the case, and each article would contain all the allowed uses for that zoning.
Mr. Cooper also noted that information on the administrative processes in the Ordinance was scattered through multiple articles and how Staff had brought all the information into one place for convenience and clarity.
They also moved some topics from the general provisions article into their own sections as there was a need to expand upon the information and give it more detail.
Countywide general provisions were modernized, and administration procedures were updated to better serve the public and help with code compliance.

Mr. Cooper also introduced Mrs. Chelle Ewald, Assistant to the County Manager, who assisted with the research and refining of data for the creation of the renewable energy article.

Mr. Cooper went over the various solutions that were considered, the feedback that had been received by the community, and how Staff had worked to incorporate the desires of the people while still following industry standards and providing solutions for developers. The update would not change the existing wind energy generation standards, though it would expand on them, and would add solar standards.

Staff also ensured that requirements aligned with the public's request for a wider area of notification for these large projects, with a one (1) mile buffer.

The update would also introduce the ability to apply for waivers and an administrative option for projects through a Development Agreement process that would be reviewed by the Board of Supervisors.

Staff had a recommendation for an alteration to the language that did not change the intent of the Ordinance, regarding working with Municipalities and Tribal Entities, to change the word consult to coordinate, and Mr. Cooper explained the process for adding that recommendation to the Commission's recommendation.

Mr. Cooper also gave an overview of how they worked to give local Fire Departments and responding agencies clearer authority with Renewable Energy projects and the hearing process in general.

The intent was that the various responding agencies would be able to be more involved with the hearing process and be able to request accommodations for projects to better serve the area and the public (i.e., requesting a cattle guard in place of a crash gate to expedite response time).

For this update's public outreach, Staff received twenty-one (21) comments during the update process, as well as five (5) letters and emails.

Staff also held three listening sessions earlier this year: on March 24th in Show Low, which received twenty-eight (28) comments, March 26th in Heber, which received thirty-one (31) comments, and March 27th in Joseph City, which received twenty-seven (27) comments.

Staff also held three (3) work sessions with the Board of Supervisors, beginning in the previous year 2024 on November 12th, and continuing in 2025 on February 11th and May 13th. There was also a JotForm comment collection forum that was open from March 10th, 2025, through April 18th that received a total of fifty-one (51) comments from the public, which were summarized and incorporated into the update as appropriate by Mrs. Ewald. Seven (7) more comments were received after the posting of the Staff Report, and copies were provided to the Commission. The comments addressed renewable energy, and not the full Ordinance update.

After Mr. Cooper's presentation, the Chair opened for public comments.

Cepand Alizadeh and Augie McMillan both spoke in support of the Ordinance update, particularly the renewable resources section, citing the value it would bring to the County's community and future.

Fae Souders, and Steve Brophy had a few concerns as follows:

Language regarding the fire districts was not clear when referring to the local responding agency, and they requested clarification.

The public was unsure if the new Development Agreement was overly restrictive.

The County is not equipped to handle or manage bonds, or that bonds will become defunct over time, leading to "zombie bond" situations.

Claims that the notification buffers, noise limits, and setbacks are too strict and are unusual to typical industry practice.

Mr. Cooper addressed these concerns, stating that the State Fire Marshal was often the authority having jurisdiction, but there are several local districts that are volunteer that would be responding.

The Development Agreement process would not be very different from the Special Use Permit (SUP) requirements and would have a little more subjectivity with public comments.

Bonds are reviewed by the County Attorney's office, and outside counsel can be brought in for review if needed. The County Attorney's office has also stated that cash bonds are not acceptable, which would eliminate the "zombie bond" issue.

Mr. Cooper then addressed the comments about the requirements for renewable energy projects, stating that the choices were made with public input and safety in mind, and that current standards for wind generation will remain despite comments that they may be overly restrictive, as the standards were easily met by three wind projects.

Commissioner Murph asked for more information about the involvement of the local Fire Departments. Mr. Cooper explained that Staff's goal was to give the responding agencies more flexibility to make requirements by codifying their involvement, and they could work with Navajo County Emergency Management as well.

Commissioner Howe supported the one-mile notification buffer but had some concerns about the outreach for the Tribal Entities, as often there would be notifications that were not addressed correctly or were lost in the shuffle and expressed that there was a need to require proof of outreach.

Vice-Chair Smith asked if there was a way to recommend an addition to the text amendment that would require periodic inspections to ensure compliance into the Ordinance. Mr. Cooper noted that concern was echoed by the Board of Supervisors, who had been requiring annual inspections of SUP's, and they had the prerogative to make a recommendation to add this into the Ordinance, as well as to increase or decrease the time between inspections. Mr. Cooper also noted that Staff normally had these requirements written into the recommendations for projects rather than into the Ordinance. Mr. Cooper also gave a brief overview of the new software that tracked these inspections and would send out notifications to Staff when it was time to review a project.

With no further questions, Chair Muder entertained a motion.

Commissioner Latham motioned to recommend approval to the BOS subject to the recommendations of Staff. Commissioner Howe seconded the motion. Motion passed unanimously, 7 - 0 .
