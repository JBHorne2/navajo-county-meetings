TP 25-001 ROCK CREEK SUBDIVISON TENTATIVE PLAT, DISTRICT III:   Consideration and possible adoption of Resolution 25-01P, approving/denying a Tentative Plat request by Green Elephant Development, LLC., to allow for a 119 manufactured home lots, 25 residential lots, 5 tracts, and accompanying roads and infrastructure on a single, 67.20 acre parcel in the White Mountain Lakes Area.  APN#:  304-21-371Q.  Owner:  Allen Land Development, LLC.  Agent:  Bryant Aplass

Mr. Tyler Richards, Planner II, presented the item to the Commission.

Mr. Richards stated that this was a continuation of the existing approved Master Development Plan (MDP) that had been approved by the Board of Supervisors on December 20th, 2004.
Mr. Richards gave an overview of the history of the project and stated that this Tentative Plat is a continuation of a Master Development Plan Amendment (MDP-A) that was approved by the Board of Supervisors on January 28th, 2025, by Resolution 04-25. Mr. Richards further stated that the project will be on 67.20 acres with a total of 144 residential units, consisting of 25 single-family lots and 119 manufactured home lots.

The units would be a mix of single-family, manufactured homes and multi-family homes and the lots would range in size from one tenth (1/10) to one third (1/3) of an acre with 20-foot (20') front and rear setbacks, and eight-foot (8') side setbacks. The project would have paved roads that would be maintained by a Homeowners Association (HOA) to be established later. Most of the land in the surrounding area is zoned SD, RU-1 or C-R, which has been left vacant or has been developed into either businesses or residences. There will be emergency ingress and egress connecting to the neighboring Juniper Ridge subdivision, and there will also be a north-westernmost cul-de-sac with a loop activation gate as well. Mr. Richards also pointed out some adjacent parcels that were not a part of the project that belonged to a separate subdivision, which would have gated access as well.

The Applicant has received will-serve letters from the White Mountain Lake (WML) Sanitation company, and Voyager at the WML Water Company Inc, Arizona Public Service Company (APS), Timber-Mesa Fire & Medical District and TBS Waste for waste management.

Mr. Richards also stated that the Applicant is requesting a few variances for their Tentative Plat, including to allow for cul-de-sacs longer than 600 feet, to allow for more than 20 homes on a cul-de-sac and to allow for a dead-end street. The Applicant has also requested to delay the geotechnical engineering report that's normally required as a submission for the Final Plat. Staff would be able to make it a condition of approval once they go through the hearing process. Navajo County's Engineering department reviewed these variance requests and found them acceptable and approved them.

Mr. Richards stated that there had not been any comments received about the Tentative Plat.

Staff found the project to align with the goals and requirements of the Comprehensive plan; including, but not limited to, quality design of development of wastewater, water and circulation systems, and the preservation of floodplains and washes for wildlife migrations and preservation. Staff recommends approval of the Tentative Plat.

Mr. Richards noted that Mr. Doug Brimhall, the Applicant's Engineer, was available for questions and comments.

Chair Muder asked Mr. Richards about the requested variance regarding the dead-end street, stating that by his understanding, that would just be a cul-de-sac. Mr. Richards addressed this question, noting that the Subdivision Ordinance does not allow for straight dead-end streets.

The Applicant requested a variance for the dead-end street and a limit on the number of houses on the cul-de-sac because the proposed Passage Drive at the southern end of the project was bordered by State land to the south, and the developers were not able to gain access beyond it. They were also unable to put access in elsewhere due to the creek on the east side, and the golf course on the west.

Mr. Brimhall then addressed the Commission, noting his history of working on the development, how the economic crash in 2008 put a hold on the project, and he had hoped someone would pick the project back up. He further noted how the applicant wished to preserve the neighborhood and the natural environment of the creek, by ensuring that there would be larger single-family lots adjacent to the existing subdivision's larger single-family lots, and as the development continued further away from existing developments, they would have higher density areas to match what is across the golf course at Juniper Ridge. There would also be the manufactured homes, recreational vehicles (RV's) and park models over by the golf course. The applicants have been working to establish a good rapport with the neighboring WML Vistas and Juniper Ridge, and currently at the end of the cul-de-sac to the north they end with a gate to ensure protection for both developments. They would also be willing to remove the gate if the other development preferred it. Mr. Brimhall noted that he would be available to discuss more during the next item, where they wished to go over the first phase of the project.

There were no public comments.

Commissioner Latham motioned to approve the Tentative Plat. Motion was seconded by Vice-Chair Smith . The motion passed unanimously, 6 - 0 .

SP 25-001 ROCK CREEK SUBDIVISION SITE PLAN REVIEW, DISTRICT III:  Consideration of a request for a Site Plan Review of the Rock Creek Subdivision to allow for the development of storage containers, parking, and ten tiny homes on wheels on approximately 9.86 acres of vacant land in the White Mountain Lake area.  APN#: 304-21-371Q. T11N, R22E, S15. Owner: Allen Land Development LLC. Agent: Bryant Aplass.

Ms. Katherine Wright, Planner II, presented the item to the Commission. This project was a continuation of the previous item, to further lock in the site plan for the development. It would include 205 storage containers, 109 parking spots, and 10 tiny homes. There are two new proposed entry points for the project. Staff has not received any comments either for or against the project and are recommending approval of the project.

Ms. Wright further noted that the applicant's engineer, Mr. Doug Brimhall was available for questions.

Both Vice-Chair Smith and Chair Muder had questions regarding the storage and the tiny homes, if they would be permanent residences or if the storage was only for the construction phase, and a question regarding the classification of tiny homes on wheels which was answered by Staff.

Mr. Brimhall stated that the tiny homes were for their employees for the building processes, and so that the commute to work would not be quite so difficult. He further noted that he was unsure of how many of the homes and containers would be built at once. The tiny homes may become permanent cabins at some point in the future.

Vice-Chair Smith asked if the tiny homes would be rentals, Mr. Brimhall stated that he did not believe that was the intent, as there would have been a very different plan to present to the Commission if it was.

Commissioner Muder asked if the storage containers would be permanent. Mr. Brimhall said that the storage units would be permanent site-built fixtures.

There were no public comments.

With there being no further questions, Vice-Chair Smith motioned to approve the Site Plan Review. Commissioner Latham seconded.
Motion passed Unanimously, 6 - 0 .

TXT-A 25-002 NAVAJO COUNTY ASSISTED LIVING HOMES ADMINISTRATIVE PROCEDURES, GUEST HOUSES/ACCESSORY DWELLING UNITS, AND DEFINITIONS UPDATE:  Consideration of an update to Section 1916 (assisted living home administrative procedures), Sections 502, 602, 702, and 1917 (guest houses/accessory dwelling units), and Article 23 (definitions) of the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance.

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, presented the item to the Commission.

Mr. Cooper noted that the last time a Text Amendment was brought before the Commission it was a full Ordinance update, and that while it had been comprehensive, the updates brought before the Commissioner here were mandated by the State of Arizona under the Arizona Revised Statutes (A.R.S.) and could not be completed before the full update. The two major updates brought before the Commission were in response to A.R.S. § 11-820.02 which pertains to Assisted Living Home Procedures, and A.R.S. § 11-810.01, which pertains to Guest Houses and Accessory Dwelling Units (ADU), as well as some updates to the Article 23: Definitions of the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance.

Mr. Cooper then gave a brief background of the Assisted Living Home update, noting that there was an incorrect omission of an administrative procedure in which a deviation from the separation requirement may be allowed in order to have reasonable accommodation with the Fair Housing Act, when a County requires a minimum distance between facilities. Currently, the County requires a minimum separation of one thousand two hundred feet (1200'), which is a typical requirement in the State of Arizona for counties and cities. Mr. Cooper said the amendment would add a subparagraph to the portion of the Zoning Ordinance that this pertains to, ensuring that all provisions of the Fair Housing Act are being followed, and that nothing is considered discriminatory towards the disabled, and that the Ordinance is complete and has the appropriate items for applicants.

Mr. Cooper then addressed the update to accommodate A.R.S. § 11-810.01, which will affect the regulation of ADU's in all counties. Mr. Cooper went over the history of the law, along with giving an overview of the State's requirements for all cities, towns, and counties to go through and adopt procedures within their confines.

The new legislation will be effective on September 26th, 2025, and requires that:

All parcels that allow for a Single-Family Dwelling (SFD) are also allowed one (1) attached ADU, and one (1) detached ADU.

For lots that are one (1) acre or greater, there will be provisions for a minimum of one (1) additional detached ADU.

If there is a failure to adopt the appropriate regulations by January 1st, 2026, the county that failed to adopt these regulations will not be able to impose any regulations on any ADU's within its boundaries- ADU's would be allowed on all lots or parcels zoned for residential use in the county, without limits.

The Statute also dictates what cannot be imposed, Mr. Cooper went over the list for the Commission.

The County may not:

Restrict the use of the ADU's as rentals.

Require a relationship between owners and occupants of a SFD and the occupant of the ADU.

Require additional parking to accommodate for ADU's or require fees in leu of parking. Require aesthetic design standards to match the SFD.

Set restrictions for ADU's that are more restrictive than those required for a SFD in the same Zoning area.

Set rear and side setbacks greater than five feet (5'). (Mr. Cooper noted that this did not apply to front setbacks, however, and that these requirements still met fire code)

Require a restrictive covenant concerning the ADU on a lot or parcel zoned for residential use by a single-family dwelling.

Require improvements on public streets as a condition of allowing ADU's, except where those streets are damaged through the installation and construction of the dwelling unit.

Mr. Cooper then gave a summary of the changes to the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance Staff recommended to accommodate these requirements:

Sections 502 (Single-Family Zoning Districts), 602 (Multi-Family Zoning Districts), and 702 (Commercial-Residential Zoning District) will add a Guest House/ADU as an allowed use.

Section 1917 (Guest Houses/Accessory Dwelling Units) would add provisions for guest houses to be permitted according to law on lots under one (1) acre, and over one (1) acre, remove the conflicting information that have conflict with the new law, remove minimum size requirements from lots and the ADU's, add provisions for the required setback allowance of five feet (5') in the side and rear lot lines, remove additionally required information that is not useful to review of Guest House Applications, and clarify that separate guest house permits are not required for the attached ADU's.

Article 23 (Definitions) will better define "Guest House" to match the new State Law, introduce "Accessory Dwelling Unit" to be defined the same as "Guest House", to prevent confusion, amend the definitions of "Manufactured Home Park" and "Recreational Vehicle Park" to exclude guest houses, as it would trigger additional permitting that might conflict with State Law.

At this time, Commissioner John disconnected momentarily due to technical issues on his end but was able to rejoin. No business was discussed, or decisions were made until his return a few minutes later.

There were no public comments.

Chair Muder asked if this law affected homeowner associations (HOA's), to which Mr. Cooper responded that while they were required to follow State Law, HOA's have their own regulations within a different part of the statutes, and HOA's were not mentioned.
Mr. Cooper further addressed this by stating that the statute only targeted counties and cities and was unsure if the State would pass regulations for HOA's at some later time. Staff has always encouraged applicants to do their due diligence and reach out to any known HOA's when permitting, but ultimately did not have a list of every HOA, CC&R (Covenants, Contracts and Restrictions), or Deed Restriction in the County.

Vice-Chair Smith asked if this legislation would conflict with a property owner's ability to deed restrict their land, Mr. Cooper stated that the statute only stated that the County cannot put restrictions on the land and should not affect property owners and private citizens.

Mr. Cooper also noted that the County could not be involved with enforcing these deed restrictions, and it would be a civil dispute between property owners.

With there being no further discussion, Commissioner Latham motioned to recommend approval to the Board of Supervisors. Vice-Chair Smith seconded.
Motion passed, 5 - 1 . ( Commissioner Murph )
