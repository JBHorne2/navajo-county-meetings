TP 24-001 HIGH PINES ESTATES TENTATIVE PLAT, DISTRICT III/IV:  Consideration of a request for a Tentative Plat to allow for a 100-lot single-family residential subdivision named High Pines Estates. The site is zoned Special Development (SD) on two parcels, which total 93.60-acres. APN#  208-03-002A & 002B, T11N, R18E, S03 & 09 of the GSRM. Owner : High Pines Estates LLC. Agent : Travis Barney.

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, presented the item to the Commission, going over the history of the project, noting that this was a continuation from a tabling from July. Mr. Cooper then detailed a brief overview of the projects size, location, and proximity to surrounding landmarks. Mr. Cooper noted that the item was tabled previously, so the applicant could provide additional documentation from the Forest Service and Arizona Game & Fish at the Commission's request. The applicant did reach out to Arizona Game and Fish and received a document with their recommendations. The applicant has also acquired documented confirmation from the Forest Service to allow improvements to Forest Service Road 332 to be used as an emergency egress.

Mr. Cooper noted that the project was in compliance with the Comprehensive Plan; the project had been submitted and accepted prior to the Subdivision Ordinance update that required lots under one acre requiring a sewer system, and that the new requirements
would not apply to the project.

Mr. Cooper said that Staff received one comment from the public that was not for or against the project, just hoping that the developer could inform purchasers of Arizona's open range laws, and further stated that Staff recommended approval subject to the 24 conditions noted in the Staff Report.

Mr. Doug Brimhall, the Applicant's representative, addressed the Commission, noting that Arizona Game and Fish had an emphasis on a wildlife corridor. The Floodway and Floodplain were to be left undeveloped to meet that need, powerlines will be run underground to keep from disturbing cattle and wildlife, lighting will be mindful so as to not disturb wildlife and trees will be carefully examined before they removed any to encourage nesting, noting that this project was in the burn scar of the Rodeo-Chediski fire.

Vice-Chair Smith opened for public Comment.

Jason Owens, and William Bigler spoke in favor of the project, both speaking to the Developer's willingness to work with them and how this project would help address the housing shortage.

Mr. Bigler also noted that there might be an existing easement that was not discussed that may provide better emergency access to Forest Service Road 332.

Frank Taliaferro, who was not for or against the project, also had additional questions regarding the outlet of the emergency access, noting that the south-west winds may cause issues in the case of a fire, and that the existing easement could provide better access along the northern boundary.

There were no further comments.

Mr. Brimhall explained that in 2014 there was an easement that was granted by the neighboring property owners, but that the family that owned the property at the time preferred that the traffic go along a different route to connect to FSR 332, and were considerate of the neighbor's wishes. With the passage of time, the neighbors sold and split their land, but the easement remained. Mr. Brimhall explained that they would prefer multiple easements, but that they would consider the property owner's wishes as they explored the possibility.

Commissioner Booher asked if there would be an HOA. Mr. Brimhall confirmed, and that the FSR 332 would be maintained by the HOA.

Vice-Chair Smith asked where the accountability was, and how the Country would be involved. Mr. Will Flake, County Engineer stated that there were a lot of roads that were within a public easement that the County did not maintain. Mr. Flake further said that the HOA would be responsible for maintaining the FRS 332, but the County would work with them if the HOA reached out for assistance. Mr. Brimhall added that any complaints would come to the HOA or the Developer, and they would work with the Forest Service directly to address issues.

Vice-Chair Smith asked if the easement would be open at all times, to which Mr. Brimhall confirmed that it would remain open, and both the Vice-Chair and Mr. Brimhall agreed that keeping it open would better serve the residents not to have to deal with a crash gate or
locks in case of an emergency.

Vice-Chair Smith also asked how they were going to prevent residents from abusing the Forest Service land, spoiling the land with ATV's, etc. as the backyards were directly against the forest. Mr. Brimhall stated there would be the HOA to enforce the CC&R's, either through reports or monitoring.

Commissioner Howe asked if Game & Fish had stated that using the wash as a wildlife corridor was acceptable, to which Mr. Cooper replied that the letter from Game & Fish did not say that it was accommodating, and that would have been unusual for them to comment in such a fashion, but that historically washes were acceptable corridors.

Commissioner Howe asked if the HOA and the CC&R's would prohibit parking in or otherwise blocking the corridor. Mr. Brimhall replied that they would not allow for anything to be built in the corridor and that there would be a buffer along it to ensure that fences didn't block the area off.

Vice-Chair Smith voiced her displeasure over the high density, stating that she still had misgivings about the project, but called for a motion.

Commissioner Muder motioned to approve the Tentative Plat. Commissioner Booher  seconded the motion.
Motion Carried, 6 - 2 . ( Commissioner Howe and Vice-Chair Smith .)

SUP 24-007: RED HILLS SOLAR ENERGY GENERATION FACILITY, DISTRICT III:  Consideration of a request by Stellar Red Hills Solar, LLC to allow for the construction and operation of a 1,000MW utility-scale solar energy generation facility and 1,000MW, 4-hour battery energy storage system (BESS) and associated facilities on approximately 9,213 acres of land located northwest of Snowflake, Arizona. APN#: 202-26-002, 202-26-009, 202-27-002, 202-26-001, 202-27-003L, 202-27-003J, 202-50-002, 202-50-003, 202-50-005, 202-50-007, 202-50-009, 202-50-010, 202-50-011, 202-50-012, 202-26-015. Owner: Aztec Land and Cattle Co., Flake Ranch, LLC, Langley NZ Ranches, LLC, Arizona State Land Department. Agent: Ty Utton.

Mr. Tyler Richards, Planner II, presented the item to the Commission, giving an overview of the project, and it's scope by sharing a site map, layout, and its approximate distance from the town of Snowflake. The project will have 7' tall, 3-strand barbed wire fencing around its main utility buildings: the Operations and Maintenance (O&M) facilities and parking, the substation, and Battery Energy Storage System (BESS). The gen-tie line will connect into the Salt River Project (SRP) Sugarloaf Substation.

Mr. Richards further detailed that they had done several studies ahead of time and have identified cultural resources and have plans for if they encounter further cultural resources during development. They will have emergency access for the Fire Department that will be

overseen by Public Works, and they have access from Highway 277 as well. The project meets the goals of the Comprehensive Plan, and Staff recommends approval of the project subject to the 14 conditions in the Staff Report. Mr. Richards also noted that the Applicant

was available to speak and answer questions.

Mr. Tom Galvin, representing the applicant, addressed the Commission. He stated he had done prior research to address past concerns of the Commission and noted that public safety would be the priority in this project. Mr. Galvin then went on to detail the fire safety measures they were taking into consideration, including 24-hour surveillance, self-containing fire suppression in the BESS, an on-site water tank to keep any fires from spreading to the brush and working hands-on with both the State Fire Marshall and the Local Fire Departments in Holbrook and Snowflake, as the project was large enough to reach out to both for first response.

Vice-Chair Smith opened for Public Comment.

There were no comments in favor of the project.

Angie Adkins spoke against the project, citing fire, overheating and smoking panel concerns, contamination of aquifers, local temperatures increasing due to panel surfaces, and radiation from the equipment affecting wildlife.

The public comments were closed.

Melissa Funsten and Mr. Galvin approached to address the comments, reinforcing that Staff was in support of the project and that both himself and Staff had addressed concerns over fire safety. He further introduced Ms. Funsten to address any technical concerns the Commission might have. Ms. Funsten stated that all the BESS have self-contained fire systems and are spaced to not be able to spread. She further explained that the BESS systems are designed to keep the fire inside, and with 24-hour maintenance and surveillance inside the units to mitigate situations that may cause fires.

Commissioner Murph asked if they were going to join or create a fire district, to which Ms. Funsten replied that they have been in communication with both Holbrook and Snowflake fire departments to form agreements, so they were not creating a new fire district.

Commissioner Murph asked why they were reaching out to both, Ms. Funstein explained that due to the size of the project, they had access points from both the southern and northern sides so they were covering as much as they could from either direction.

Commissioner Murph had additional comments about the impact this would have on jobs and voiced his displeasure at not having enough knowledge about this new technology. Mr. Galvin agreed, stating that all this technology was new, and that their goal was to improve the County.

Commissioner Howe asked if the self-contained fire suppression system was water-based. Ms. Funsten explained that they had not finalized the design, but that they were using the Tesla battery system and their recommendations, and it was not water-based. The water tank would be for the suppression of the surrounding brush.

Commissioner Latham asked where the monitors would be located, if they were remote monitoring in another state, or locally. Ms. Funsten stated that there would be on-site monitoring at a safe distance from the BESS units.

With no further comments,
Commissioner Latham motioned to recommend approval to the Board of Supervisors.
Commissioner Muder seconded. Motion passed unanimously,
8 - 0 .

ZC 24-002 LAWRENCE ZONE CHANGE, DISTRICT IV:  Consideration of request by Show Low Development, LLC to allow for the Zone Change from A-General to R1-10 (Single-Family Residential Zoning District 10,000 Square Feet Per Dwelling Unit) on an approximately 39.86-acre parcel located in the Linden area.   APN# : 409-16-001A.   Owner : Show Low Development, LLC.   Agent : Chad Lawrence.

Commissioner Muder recused himself from this item due to a Conflict of Interest.

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, presented the item to the Commission. This Zone change is a distinct and separate process that is tied to the following item, a Tentative Plat. The Zone Change will be moved forward to the Board of Supervisors for final approval, and the Tentative Plat would be a final decision by the Commission. The Navajo County Zoning Ordinance requires that when a subdivision is proposed they do a Zone change to an appropriate zoning. The Developer chose to move forward with R1-10. The project is roughly four and a half miles from the outskirts of Show Low on the west side, heading into the Linden area. This parcel is located south of the Linden Trails subdivision, which is also zoned R1-10, so this would not constitute spot zoning. The Zone Change meets the goals and policies of the Comprehensive Plan, and Mr. Cooper further noted that there were adequately paved roads in the vicinity to allow for a higher density of development. Staff received two comments against the zone change, mainly concerns over the development aspect, and about traffic concerns. Staff does recommend approval of the zone change, as this was appropriate zoning for the area and matched surrounding Zonings.

Ms. Sandra Phillips introduced herself as representative for the project,

Vice-Chair Smith opened for public comment.

There were no comments in favor, Comments Against:

Jerry Ciptak, Ryan & Gretchen Fuller, their comments against the Zone Change were:

Was told previously that the lots would be a minimum lot size of 2 acres and had concerns about R1-10 allowing smaller lots,

The density increasing the run-off of water for the wash,

Overall density ruining the rural feel of the area,

Were told that there were no plans for an HOA,

Septic Issues,

Undesirables/transient activity with short term rentals.

Purchased homes for the rural nature of the area and wanted the land to remain undeveloped.

Mr. Cooper addressed some of these comments, stating that the lot sizes would not change once the Final Plat was approved so there would not be further splitting of the lots, and that R1-10 was a single-family residential use. Mr. Cooper further stated that R1-10 zoning only allowed for one (1) single-family dwelling per lot, so it would not increase density beyond the approved number of lots. Finally, Mr. Cooper stated that A-General Zoning does not have a minimum lot size, so long as they can prove that a standard septic system could be installed on the lot.

Ms. Sandra Phillips, the representative for the Applicant, thanked the Commission for their time and wanted to note that there was similar residential zonings in the surrounding subdivisions.

With no further comments, Commissioner Latham motioned to recommend denial to the Navajo County Board of Supervisors. Commissioner Murph seconded. Motion passed, 6 - 1 , ( Chair Rogers , and Commissioner Mude r recused)

TP 24-002 JACKELOPE TRAILS TENTATIVE PLAT, DISTRICT IV : Consideration of request by Show Low Development, LLC for a Tentative Plat allowing for 41 single family residential lots for common area for pedestrian and equestrian travel as well as drainage, a well and water tank site, and accompanying roads, located on approximately 39.86-acre parcel located in the Linden area.   APN# : 409-16-001A.  Owner : Show Low Development, LLC.  Agent : Chad Lawrence.

Commissioner Muder recused himself from this item due to a Conflict of Interest.

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, presented the item to the Commission. Mr. Cooper noted that this item was tied to the previous item, a Zone Change from A-General to R1-10 Zoning.
Mr. Cooper listed several details that were included on the Final Plat:

The lots would be serviced via septic tanks and alternative septic systems.

There was a proposal for a well and water tank.

There would be a 15-foot trail easement for pedestrian and equestrian use, as well was drainage easements on site.

Inundation and drainage areas were indicated on the Tentative Plat.

There would be paved roads to be maintained by a Homeowners Association (HOA) and would not be dedicated to the County for maintenance.

The Non-vehicular access easement extended around the exterior of the Subdivision.

Mr. Cooper also shared an overview of the area, including nearby landmarks, and detailed the lot sizes for the Tentative Plat:
There were 41 lots proposed, ranging from half-acre lots (0.50) to a little over two acre lots (2.19) with an average of about four-fifths of an acre (0.80), and have five (5) acres of common area. The project was submitted before the implementation of the new Zoning Ordinance regulations requiring subdivisions with lot sizes under one (1) acre requiring a sewer system. However, the submission was not complete or accepted, and the application was not fully completed or accepted until after the Zoning Ordinance requirements had gone into effect.
Based upon these findings of facts, Staff has analyzed and found that there could be damage to the public welfare from this item, as it is not in compliance with the Subdivision Ordinance.  Staff therefore recommends denial of this project.

Ms. Sandra Phillips, the Applicant's Engineer spoke to the Commission, stating that the Landowners approached the project with the knowledge that the Community had enjoyed this open area for a long time, but concluded that it was time to develop the land.  The Applicant has aimed for larger lots, ranging from a half-acre to two acre lots, to maintain the rural feel of the area as well as to blend with surrounding subdivisions.

The Applicant has been mindful of the wash that goes through the property and has done preliminary investigations to ensure that all the lots could be developed and that the conveyance of the wash through the subdivision will meet Navajo County's requirements of ensuring historical entry and exit water flows will be maintained.

Ms. Phillips then confirmed some details that Mr. Cooper had gone over, adding that lighting was not anticipated for this project, save for the entrance sign.  She further noted that they were aware of the difficulties that the subdivision to the north, with their soils unable to meet a percolation rate that would be acceptable for standard septic. Taking this into account, they had prepared research into alternative systems with new technologies that could reduce the nitrogen levels significantly to offset the smaller lots, and so as not to contaminate the wash.
The owners were in contact with a water distribution company to install a large tank and drill a single well that would service the whole subdivision.  Ms. Phillips also noted that even if the zoning were to remain A-General, that there would still be a proposed 39 lots.
With all of this in mind, the Applicant and their Developer felt this was a better addition to the community, having worked to break up the through road, creating an HOA, and adding in more cul-de-sacs.  They were not sure how to address the grazing land, and the existing stock tank at this time.

Mr. Chad Lawrence addressed the Commission. Mr. Lawrence stated that the Landowners wished for the land to be developed in a conscious way that will keep the neighbors in mind, allow wildlife to still traverse the area, have good aesthetics, and be conservative using recycled materials as they developed the homes.  Mr. Lawrence also stated that that there had been several misconceptions about the project that they hoped they could clear up, being usure of where the 2-acre minimum lots and lack of an HOA came from. Mr. Lawrence also stated that they planned on keeping the stock tank.

Mr. J.R. Pierce also addressed the Commission, being the representative of the Landowners. Mr. Pierce gave a brief history of the lot, noting that the land had been in a trust for a very long time and the decision to sell was not an easy one, but they wanted to make sure that the land was well cared for and used for something that would be beneficial to the community as a legacy.

Public Comments:

In Favor:

Mr. Vance Muder, a professional working with the applicant, addressed the Commission, stating that he was working to develop septic systems that would suit the soil and gave a quick overview of the new technology that would allow for the alternative systems to serve such a dense community with little to no impact than a standard system would.

Against:
Gary Townsley and Mrs. Gretchen Fuller spoke against the project, with the following complaints:

Misconduct from the applicant,

Property Disputes,

Overburdening the aquifer,

Causing flood issues with the wash,

Misinformation regarding lot sizes,

Concerns over traffic,

Concerns over pedestrians using the perimeter trail and being in the neighboring back yards,

Too much density for the area.

Mr. Mischa Larisch, Planning & Development Director, addressed the Commission to remind them that the Tentative Plat was not a recommendation to the Board of Supervisors, but was final at the Commission level. Mr. Larisch clarified that Staff was required to recommend denial as the minimum acreage was not being met for the Ordinance requirements, but that the Commission had the prerogative to recommend as they saw fit.

Vice-Chair Smith expressed concerns about setting a precedence for projects to come in and not take the Ordinance into account, though she sympathized with the developers as she did not have an objection to the project.

Mr. Cooper wished to clarify a few points, noting that if left undeveloped a wild cat subdivision was possible without appropriate infrastructure, and that again, A-General had no minimum lot size.

Commissioner Latham asked if this project was denied, if there was a set time limit before the Applicant could resubmit.  Mr. Cooper said that typically there was a 12-month period before resubmitting an amended application.

Vice-Chair Smith asked if the project would be seen by the Board of Supervisors, Mr. Cooper explained that the Tentative Plat was final, but that there would be a much more detailed Final Plat that would have to be approved by the Board of Supervisors before they could break ground.

There was some further discussion that was not under the purview of the Commission, and further clarification of the Tentative Plat process.

With no further discussion, Chair Rogers Motioned to Deny the Tentative Plat. Commissioner Howe seconded.  Motioned passed unanimously, 7 – 0 .  ( Commissioner Muder Recused.)
