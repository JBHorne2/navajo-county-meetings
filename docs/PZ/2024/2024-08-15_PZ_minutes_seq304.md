SUP 24-005 LARK POINT SOLAR, DISTRICT III:  Consideration of a request by Lark Point Solar, LLC, for a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction and operation of a 450MW solar power generation facility, as well as 450MW, battery energy storage system (BESS), and associated facilities on approximately 7,119 acres located southwest of Joseph City, Arizona.  APN# 108-04-001F, 108-04-001E, 108-04-002F, 108-04-002C, 104-04-001C, 110-15-001H, 110-15-002, and 110-15-001E, T17N, R18,19, S01,11,12, S24,25/7, 18, S,19,21,29, S31 of the GSRM.  Owner:  Lark Point Solar, LLC, Aztec East Jeffers, LLC.  Agent:  Julie Dickey.

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, presented the item to the Commission.  Mr. Cooper went over the location of the project in the Joseph City area, sharing with the commission a vicinity map to note its proximity to surrounding landmarks, as well as its overall size. Staff had not received any public comments about the project, and the Applicant's representatives were available to speak.

Ms. Julie Dickey addressed the Commission, thanking them for their time and introduced her team, which were available to answer questions.

The Vice-Chair then opened the public comment period, but there were no comments either for or against the project.

Commissioner Howe asked about the Battery Energy Storage System (BESS), and its set-up. Alyssa Menzel, the Applicant's project developer, addressed this question, explaining that the BESS would be in self-contained shipping containers above ground with the battery cells all stacked in racking systems.  They have already begun speaking with Joseph CIty's Fire District to enter into a service agreement, which would entail training ahead of construction and then throughout the operation of the project for emergency situations with the battery storage units.  The units would be self-contained, and would have a First Responder unit adjacent to the BESS, but collection lines, which are medium voltage electrical lines would run underground.  Ms. Menzel also listed the safety systems, including sensors and cameras that would connect to the First Responder unit so that, if needed, they would be able to see inside the units. They will also be segregated, and there will be an eight (8) to ten (10) foot distance between the units.

Commissioner Booher asked if there would be fire suppression inside the units, and asked for more details on the first response unit. Ms. Menzel explained that all the units had built-in fire suppression, and that the First Responder unit was a staging area where all the controls would be located so that First Responders did not have to approach the batteries in the event of an emergency.

Commissioner Muder asked if there was any other fire suppression on-site, Ms. Menzel stated that they would have a water tank and work with the Joseph City Fire Department for further details.

With no further questions, Commissioner Booher motioned to recommend approval to the Board of Supervisors. Commissioner Muder seconded.
Motion passed unanimously, 6 - 0 .

SUP 24-006 WHITE ANTELOPE WIRELESS COMMUNICATION FACILITY, DISTRICT III: Consideration of a request by SBA Communications for a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction and operation of a 120' multi-carrier, monopole communication tower and associated facilities, located east of Snowflake. APN#: 303-93-003, T13N, R22E, S01 of the GSRM. Owner: Willis Ranch LLC. Agent: Reg Destree.

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, presented the item to the Commission. Mr. Cooper the site plan and general location of the project; The parcel is fifty-one (51) acres and zoned
Commercial-Residential (C-R).

Mr. Cooper shared photo-simulations of what the tower would look like from the closest permitted structure and noted that Staff had received several comments from the public, which were provided to the Commissioners.
The project meets the goals of the comprehensive plan, and Staff recommends approval of the project.

Mr. Cooper also stated that the applicant's agent was available to answer questions, and Mr. Reg Destree introduced himself.

Vice-Chair Smith opened the public comments, there were no comments in support of the project.

Public comments against:

Susan Molloy, Melinda Wilson, Kerry & Kevin Dunn, Gary Gumbel, Karen Perrin, Tom Freeman, Deb Schmeltzer, Marlene Schmidt all spoke against the project, their concerns being:

•  The proximity of the tower is too close to a community of electronically sensitive individuals,
•  Several individuals have moved to the area to be away from electronic pollutants,
•  Health concerns due to sensitivities to pesticides and radio waves,
•  Negative impact to property values, as the community was built specifically to avoid radio wave/electronic pollutants.
•  Concerns about health effects of driving past tower regularly, with question about developing Cheney trail to Clive trail so that sensitive residents could bypass it.
•  Impact of Radio Frequencies on local flora and fauna.

With no further comments, the Vice-Chair closed the public comments.

Commissioner Booher asked Mr. Cooper what the history of the project was, Mr. Cooper stated that there were four houses that were permitted through the Department of Housing.  Mr. Kevin Dunn, from the Public, elaborated further that the housing was managed by the OACAC Housing Assistance program.

Commissioner Booher asked Mr. Cooper what the history of the project was, Mr. Cooper stated that there were four houses that were permitted through the Department of Housing. Mr. Kevin Dunn, from the Public, elaborated further that the housing was managed by the OACAC Housing Assistance program.

Commissioner Booher asked if there was a difference between the chemically sensitive and the electrically sensitive, Mr. Kevin Dunn said there were similarities but were different, and some
residents were sensitive to both.

Mr. Reg Destree addressed the possibility of moving the site further back on the parcel. Mr. Destree stated that a wash that spanned the middle of the parcel hindered moving the site east, and
the Applicant was attempting to stay as far away from the residences to the East as possible while staying within their target area.

Having no further discussion,  Commissioner Booher motioned to recommend approval to the Board of Supervisors.
Commissioner Latham seconded the motion.
Motion passed unanimously,  6 - 0 .
