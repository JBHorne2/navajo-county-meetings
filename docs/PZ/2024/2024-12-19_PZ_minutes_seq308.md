CONSIDERATION OF AN UPDATE TO THE COMPREHENSIVE PLAN OF NAVAJO COUNTY.

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, introduced Ms. Terri Hogan of Logan Simpson to the Commission.  Ms. Hogan thanked the Commission for their time, and gave a detailed report of the progress made for the Comprehensive Plan.  Ms. Hogan began her presentation with going over the main project team, starting with Navajo County's Planning & Development, Logan Simpson as the lead consultant, Kimley Horn to support Transportation and Water Resources, and Elliot D. Pollack & Company to support housing and economic growth.  Ms. Hogan then went over her presentation's agenda, explaining the purpose of the Comprehensive Plan, the process of building and the schedule they used to create the document, the outreach through their Public Participation Plan, and the contents of the plan, detailing what had been added and what had been updated from the previous iteration of the Comprehensive Plan.

Comments from the public were then opened.

Nina Terello, and Lisa Vincenti commented on the Comprehensive Plan Update, with concerns that did not relate to the document itself.

Their concerns:

Allocation of funds for wind farms.

Energy being supplied to the Reservations, as allegedly promised.

Limitations on the number of wind farms allowed in the County

Federal Administration's negative views on wind farms and funding.

Concerns relating to the impact to tourism and harm to nature caused by wind farms.

Current wind farm performance needs public documentation before approving new ones.

Concerns about the approved FDA lighting on the turbines.

Concerns that the power generated would not be used in the County.

Concerns over experimental technology, the County being "guineae pigs" for wind farms.

Comments from the public were then closed.

Chairman Rogers opened up comments from the Commissioners. Commissioner Murph spoke up, stating that he saw nothing in the Comprehensive Plan that addressed possible problems with renewable energy or battery fires. He stated he would like to see this added to the plan before moving forward with it, while also expressing he did not see a realistic promotion of growth in the plan. Mr. Cody Cooper addressed his concerns, stating this is just a guiding document that leads into the Zoning Ordinance which is the next goal. Mr. Cooper advises that the team has adopted a wind energy ordinance and has been instructed by the Board of Supervisors to not only audit said ordinance, but to adopt a solar energy ordinance that will be later audited as well. He went on to clarify that the Staff does not change ordinances while updating the Comprehensive Plan as they do not want to make changes that are in conflict with each other that require further edits.

Commissioner Murph asked if Staff will put together an item that specifically addresses Fire Districts to put before the Board. Mr. Cooper said Staff is limited on what they can do as the Fire Districts are independent boards and the County has no authority over them. Mr. Cooper informed the Commission that in early 2025 an analysis of existing standards and proposed changes, updates and additions for renewable energy will be performed. They are required by law to be advertised as they are public hearings and will come before the Commission and Board of Supervisors.

Commissioner Muder asked if during the audits on the Zoning Ordinance, the concerns Commissioner Murph brought up would be addressed. Mr. Cooper replied that though it was slightly off topic, it could be something that was addressed when the time came. Mr. Cooper went on to say that in the current wind energy ordinance, it does not allow prototype turbines, only established manufactured designs.  Vice Chair Smith asked why the County had to continue to defer to the State Fire Marshal. She expressed interest in setting regulations to be more proactive at a local level. Mr. Cooper explained that that was the exact reason that they wished to approve the Comprehensive Plan, as it was designed to be proactive and encourage Staff to create policy to set regulations. Chair Rogers expressed interest in educating himself and the Commission on energy resources. Mr. Cooper replied that it could be done. Vice Chair Smith echoed Chair Roger's interest in education.

With no further discussion, Chair Roger entertained a motion.
Commissioner Booher motioned to recommend approval to the Board of Supervisors.  Commissioner Muder seconded.
Motion passed 7 - 1 ( Commissioner Murph )

MDP-A 24-002 ROCK CREEK DEVELOPMENT MDP-A, DISTRICT III:  Consideration of a request by Allen Land Development, LLC for a Master Development Plan Amendment for Phases II, III, and IV of White Mountain Lake Vistas, which allowed for a total of 216 single-family lots, to a new Master Development Plan which will allow for 24 single-family lots, 133 manufactured homes, 133 multi-family units, storage and parking facilities, golf course, and undisturbed/recreation area in the White Mountain Lakes area. APN#: 304-21-371Q, T11N, R22E, S15 of the GSRM. Owner: Allen Land Development, LLC.  Agent: Virginia Senior.

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, presented the item to the Commission.  Mr. Cooper shared an overview of the project, located in White Mountain Lakes and just east of Juniper Ridge Resort, and White Mountain Lake Vistas was just north of the project area, and State land to the south of the project. The project area had originally been phases 2, 3, and 4 of the White Mountain Lake Vistas, an existing Master Development plan that had been approved with a total of 300 single family units, with phase 1 having already been completed with 84 of the 300 lots proposed.  Portions of the Juniper Ridge Resort's golf course were interspersed inside the project area, but would not pertain to the project, nor would the project be allowed access to the Golf Courses. The Applicant also wished to dedicate approximately 16 acres as undisturbed boundary areas along the creek bed, which would act as a buffer from a neighboring subdivision. There is also a section dedicated to age restrictions of 55 and older to buffer the existing age-restricted properties in the Vistas and Juniper Ridge.
Mr. Cooper then shared more details showing the Commission the plans the Applicant had for the single family homes, manufactured homes along the Juniper Ridge RV and park model resort.

Mr. Cooper further detailed the concerns that had been brought up during the Public outreach meeting the applicant had held on August 22nd, 2024. Approximately 60 people attended, several did not wish to sign in.

Density was a large concern, but Mr. Cooper was able to point out that the majority of the surrounding area had similar densities, averaging approximately 3.5 units per acre, while the proposed amendment would be at 4.32 units per acre.

Further concerns were the capacity for the water, sewer, emergency response teams, the overall construction and esthetics of the project, the impact on wildlife, concerns over a lack of age restriction on the project due to deed restrictions that were put in place previously, Mr. Cooper paused here to remind the Commission that the County is unable to enforce deed restrictions, and those are privately enforced through the courts, as well as to point out that there were age restriction boundaries proposed.  Further concerns were over the roadway infrastructure, the deviation from the original plan being age restricted and single family only, light pollution, impact to water retention, impact to the lake and lake pollution from the creek, as the original proposal at the public meeting had a belt of designated camping land along the creek, the applicant adjusted their site plan to address the concerns of the public by removing the proposed camping area.  The Applicant also removed seventeen (17) units of Multifamily housing, and added three (3) units to the Manufactured Housing portion to balance it out, reducing the density to address the concerns of the public.

Mr. Cooper further addressed concerns raised, stating that the Applicant had obtained a will-serve letter from Timber Mesa Fire Department for Fire and Medical service, as well as there being the White Mountain Lakes Fire Department. The Applicant also obtained conditional will-serves from the water and sewer company in the community.  Traffic studies would be required for the roads, and if there needed to be improvements made it would be the responsibility of the Developer to address them, and would not impact taxpayers.  Mr. Cooper also noted that all development would fall under the County Lighting Ordinance, so nuisance lighting would not be an issue.  Arizona Department of Game & Fish had been contacted, and Bald Eagle nesting sites were noted north of White Mountain Lake, far enough away from the Project to keep them undisturbed.

Applicant representative Adriana Medina was present to address any questions the Commission might have.

Chair Rogers opened for Public Comment.

Paul Paget, Kathleen Beck, Debi Bismarck, Robert Glumbik, Kathleen Love, Woody Graven Bauer all spoke against the project.

Their concerns:

Development will ruin the view for other subdivisions.

Age restrictions are not fully enforced throughout the project, so "young people" could live there.

Tax increases.

Sewer capacity.

Water capacity.

Lack of growth in the area.

Proposed commercial use on the site.

Other development projects in the area.

Fire escapes becoming clogged with traffic due to density,

Impact to wildlife.

Worries about residents using the golf course.

The public comments were closed.

Commissioner Latham asked what the parameters were for Timber Mesa to grant will-serve letters.  Mr. Cooper explained that most will-serve letters were conditional upon meeting certain standards which the Developer would be responsible for.  Mr. Cooper gave an example to the Commission regarding low pressure, that if the Developer had issues with the current system meeting the required pressures for the Fire Department, they could install additional water storage or pumps to bring it into compliance.

Commissioner Latham then asked at what point they would be required to meet those conditions, Mr. Cooper stated that the Fire Department reviewed all documents that go through at the hearing level all the way to the building phase of the private residence, and are involved.

Commissioner Latham then asked if that applied for sewer, etc.  Mr. Cooper said yes, further noting that the will-serve letters stated that improvements may need to be made before service is given, and the costs would be wholly at the Developer's expense, and the same would be done for Traffic Studies.

Mr. Cooper further iterated that the golf course is not an amenity of the project, and it is fully acknowledged that the course is maintained by Juniper Ridge, and it will not change.

Commissioner Booher asked if there was no development, if that meant that there would not be any improvements to the systems and infrastructure.  Mr. Cooper confirmed that that would typically be the case, as the only other option would be to increase the fees of the existing owners, as there would not be new money coming into the area for improvements.

Commissioner Booher then asked if it was typical for new developers to foot the bill for the improvements to the infrastructure, Mr. Cooper confirmed.

Chair Rogers asked what was currently approved, such as the multi-family and number of lots. Mr. Cooper stated that there were originally four phases and phase one had been developed, with 84 lots. Originally, there would have been a total of 300, so there would have been 216 age-restricted single family units spread across phases two, three, and four. If the MDP-A were to be rejected by the Board of Supervisors, it would revert to the originally approved plan.

Chair Rogers then acknowledged that the loss of habitat and views were out of control of the residents, and unless they bought up all the land there was no way to guarantee that it would remain the same.  He then stated that the density was the highest for this development over all others in the area, and asked if Staff had any further comments about how that affected their approval.

Mr. Cooper gave a brief overview of the history of White Mountain Lakes, stating that it was originally part of White Mountain Lake Vistas, and going back further, it could be considered part of a very old master development plan that was put into place before White Mountain Lakes existed. He then compared Juniper Ridge as a similar Master Development Planned community.  Mr. Cooper then went on to explain the sunset clause on this project, that they would have a certain time to complete a part of the construction, or they would revert to the original plan and strike this plan from the record.

Chair Rogers asked if multi-family zoning was utilized anywhere else in the community. Mr. Cooper replied that there were a few condominiums, a zone change had been approved for a multi-family manufactured home subdivision, but otherwise there were no multi-family zones elsewhere.

Commissioner Latham motioned to recommend approval to the Board of Supervisors.  Commissioner Booher seconded.  Motion passed, 7 - 1 ( Chair Rogers )

TP 24-003 SHOW LOW MOUNTAIN RANCH, VILLAGE B, DISTRICT III: Consideration of a request by Show Low Holdings 307, LLC, and Universal Show Low Properties, LLC, for a Tentative Plat allowing for 350 lots consisting of recreational vehicle spaces, park model homes, and manufactured/modular homes, as well as 28 tracts, and accompanying roads. The request is located on an approximately 175.68-acre parcel located to the south of White Mountain Lakes. APN#: 304-57-033D. Owner: Show Low Holdings 307, LLC and Universal Show Low Properties, LLC. Agent: Greg Loper.

Tyler Richards, Planner II, presented the item to the Commission. Mr. Richards gave a brief overview of the history of Show Low Mountain Ranch, noting that previously the Commission and the Board of Supervisors had approved another portion of the same project as a Master Development Plan Amendment. Mr. Richards said the Applicant, Show Low Holdings LLC, had returned to request a Tentative Plat for Village B, which would start the process of finalizing the plat for this section of the project.

Mr. Richards further described the project, giving details about the main access on Bourdon Ranch Road, with additional emergency access to the Southeast that could connect to Roundup Drive. The Project will have 350 lots with 26 tracts, four of which will be dedicated to private streets, and the rest being dedicated to open space, common area, drainage, and stormwater management. This would total approximately 109.21 acres.

Mr. Richards described the basic details of the lots, including setbacks and sizes. There  was already an on-site water tank and well, and the lots would be served by an onsite water collection system that will be managed and operated by the White Mountain Lake Sanitary District, which had provided a will-serve letter. The roads would be paved and maintained by an HOA.

Mr. Richards then displayed an overview of the plat, showing the plans for the parcel as well as it's current state. Mr. Richards then went on to list how this project aligns with the goals and policies of the Comprehensive Plan, Subdivision and Zoning Ordinance. Staff had not received comments either for or against the project. Staff recommends approval of the project, subject to the fourteen (14) conditions listed in the staff report.

Mr. Richards also wished to remind the Commission that the Tentative Plat was only approved by the Commission, and that the Board of Supervisors would not see this project until the Final Plat stage of the project.

Mr. Greg Loper, the Applicant's representative, addressed the Commission. He reminded the Commission about the Master Development Plan Amendment that had been approved, stating that this was a continuation of that amendment and that this would solidify the next step in the
project.

Mr. Loper also addressed how these projects were funded, as there were comments from the previous project that might also apply to this project. The Commission had concerns over having adequate water pressure, Mr. Loper assured the Commission that they could meet Timber Mesa Fire Department's needs with fire hydrants.

Mr. Loper explained that they did in fact have a contractual Will-Serve from WACO for water that had been made prior to the recession. There is an existing well and pump system that has been repaired and is currently in the process of being dedicated back to WACO.

For wastewater, Mr. Loper stated that they have a formal agreement with the Sanitary District and that there were several upgrades that were needed to proceed. Mr. Loper stated that their project would be assisting these upgrades financially and as new development came into the area it would continue to pay fees to enhance and upgrade the services, be it wastewater, fire, water, etc.

Mr. Loper further went on to share some graphics of the project's intended amenities and open spaces, stating that there would be improvements made to Bourdon Ranch Road. The Projects amenities and improvements would be maintained and served by the HOA going
forward.

The Chair then opened to Public Comment.

Debbie Bismarck approached the Commission, stating that she was a Board Member of the
White Mountain Lake Community Association. She commented that the project would be tying
into their sewer system, that they were already at capacity and couldn't handle another eight
hundred people tying into their system all at once. She expressed interest in wanting to know
when the upgrades were to be done and spoke about the fact that the developers were not
local. (Referring to this and the previous project.)

Public Comments were closed.

Mr. Loper wished to address this, explaining that his project was already helping upgrade and expand the system. The sewer line was installed several years ago along with the pump station. Mr. Loper also wished to point out that he had grown up in Taylor, AZ, and even though he had since moved, he very much still had a soft spot for the area.

Commissioner Muder asked if the well was on the project's property. Mr. Loper confirmed that it was, but that it was across the road from the project, and they were in the process of deeding to a company.

Commissioner Latham wanted clarification about the sewer situation if it was indeed at capacity or not. Mr. Loper stated that they are at capacity and under violation from ADEQ for the discharge from the station, but that it would be an easy fix. Mr. Loper stated that money was currently in escrow for the upgrades needed to meet the needs for the project, and as homes would come online the fees associated with them would further increase the budget and help fund the upgrades.

Chair Rogers asked when they planned to break ground, Mr. Loper replied that their next step would be the Final Plat process, so in approximately two years.

With no further discussion, Commissioner Booher motioned to approve the Tentative Plat.
Commissioner Muder seconded. Motion passed unanimously, 8 - 0 .

TP 24-004 SHOW LOW MOUNTAIN RANCH, VILLAGE D, DISTRICT III: Consideration of a request by Show Low Holdings 307, LLC, and Universal Show Low Properties, LLC, for a Tentative Plat allowing for 189 lots consisting of manufactured/modular housing and recreational vehicle spaces, as well as 22 tracts, with 1 offsite tract for access and public utility easements. This request is located on an approximately 48.52-acre parcel located to the south of White Mountain Lakes. APN#: 304-57-033F. Owner: Show Low Holdings 307, LLC, and Universal Show Low Properties, LLC. Agent: Greg Loper.

Mr. Tyler Richards, Planner II, presented the project to the Commission, noting that it was very similar to the previous project, just a different section of the same development.
Mr. Richards started by describing the 48-acre project, which would have 189 residential units consisting of manufactured/modular housing, and/or Recreational Vehicle spaces. The primary access would be off Bourdon Ranch Road, with emergency access from the southeast connecting to Roundup Drive. The Setbacks would be the same as the previous development, with the lot sizes roughly the same, from .1 to .13 acres each. The project would be managed by WACO, who have provided the will-serve letter for this property, as well as the White Mountain Lake Sanitary District. Mr. Richards went over the features of the project, and that like the last project, Staff had not received any comments in favor or against the project. Staff recommended approval of the Tentative Plat.

With no further questions, The Chair opened for Public Comment.

Debbie Bismarck approached to speak, still voicing concerns about the infrastructure being taken care of.

The public comments were closed.

With no further discussion, Commissioner Muder motioned to approve the Tentative Plat.
Commissioner Latham seconded the motion. Motion passed unanimously, 8 - 0 .
