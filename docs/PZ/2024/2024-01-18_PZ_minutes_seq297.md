TXT-A 24-001 AMENDMENT TO ARTICLE 22 – RECREATIONAL VEHICLE PLACEMENT:  Consideration of text amendment to Article 22, Sections 2202, 2203, and 2207, regarding recreational vehicle placement.

Commissioner Murph returned to the meeting.

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, presented the item to the Commission.  Mr. Cooper explained that the amendment was to clean up the language of the Ordinance; As of now, the language is unclear about whether a Recreational Vehicle (RV) can be stored on fully developed property, and the confusion is causing false reporting to Code Enforcement.  Mr. Cooper noted that this was the most common complaint received by Code Enforcement, as the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance does not allow RV's as permanent dwellings.

TXT-A 24-002 AMENDMENT TO ARTICLE 29 – CITIZEN REVIEW: Consideration of a text amendment to Article 29, Section 2907, regarding citizen review.Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, presented the item to the Commission. Mr. Cooper explained that currently, the Zoning Ordinance only meets compliance with the minimal amount of Citizen Review, which is only required for a Zone Change and gives applicants the option of holding a meeting, sending out notices through the mail, and/or hosting a virtual meeting. This has caused a number of complaints from the public, who feel they were not heard, and their questions not addressed, causing frustration during the hearing process. Staff found it was easiest to strike the current requirements and start fresh by making Citizen Reviews mandatory for all processes. Applicants would be required to host a community meeting for all neighbors within the required 300' area of notification. Applicants would need to give notice 15 days prior to the community meeting and host the meeting prior to coming before the Commission. This would give applicants time to address any concerns, mitigate them, or be able to garner support.

Commissioner Muder asked how Staff's items would be affected by this change. Mr. Cooper said that the Citizen Review would only come into effect when there was a physical change to a property.

Commissioner Latham asked if this was an increased workload for Staff. Mr. Cooper replied that the Community Meetings would be solely on the applicants to host, advertise, and run.

Vice-Chair Smith asked if Staff were attending these meetings. Mr. Cooper stated that unless directed by the Board of Supervisors, they would not attend to separate the County from the Developers.

Vice-Chair Smith questioned if there was a potential for abuse of the system., Mr. Cooper replied that Applicants would have to make a report, and while it wouldn't be a complete deterrent, it might help mitigate any abuse of the system.

Commissioner Muder noted that public meetings had been disputed in the past and asked how they were to tell which party was truthful. Mr. Cooper said that developers would have to provide some evidence, such as recordings, minutes, sign-in sheets with signatures, or otherwise.

Chair Rogers asked what would be done if the public did not respond. Mr. Cooper said that it could happen, but the developers would be required to provide evidence that the notices have been mailed.

Vice-Chair Smith asked what would happen if a project changed hands. Mr. Cooper replied that they would have to host another meeting.

Commissioner Murph asked how this would impact Staff's workload. Mr. Cooper stated the impact should be minimal but would be helpful to address the concerns of the public.
With no further questions or comments from the Commission or the public, Commissioner Muder motioned to recommend approval to the Board of Supervisors. Vice-Chair Smith seconded.
Motion passed unanimously, 8 - 0.

TXT-A 24-003 AMENDMENT TO SUBDIVISION REGULATIONS AND REQUIREMENTS: Consideration of atext amendment to Subdivision Regulations and Requirements, Sections 8.1 and 8.3, regarding street design and sanitary sewage disposal.

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, presented the item to the Commission. He explained that there was not currently a threshold for when a subdivision should have paved roads, or when it is required to have a sewer system. Staff found that with other rural counties in Arizona, the standard often was that platted subdivisions with parcels over five-acres in size would be allowed to have unpaved roads. Also, to align with Arizona Department of Environmental Quality, , subdivisions that have one-acre or smaller parcels should have sewer and subdivisions should allow individual septic systems when all parcels were greater than one-acre.  This standard was also commonly found with other counties in the State.
Mr. Cooper also stated that there have been issues with many platted subdivisions that have parcels under
one-acre that had failed their perc tests and were unable to meet the ADEQ requirements, rendering the lot unusable or cost-prohibitive to develop.

Commissioner Murph asked if this would affect property owners that were selling portions of land, if everyone would be required to pave their driveways. Mr. Cooper clarified that this would only be required of formal platted subdivisions and would not be required of individual property owners.

Chair Rogers asked Mr. Cooper to explain what a developer would do without sewer access. Mr. Cooper stated that the developers would simply have to create larger parcels to accommodate the septic systems, but if the developer wanted to make parcels under the one-acre threshold, they could look at connecting to an existing sewer or work towards creating a treatment facility.

Commissioner Muder , who was a professional in this field, listed a few examples of such facilities.

Chair Rogers asked if this was a problem that they were trying to address, as he could only see this as cost prohibitive.
Mr. Cooper replied that it was an issue, and needed to be addressed moving forward, stating that it was not meant to encourage or discourage developers, but that there needs to be more consideration of the densities that are being put in and ensuring there will be infrastructure to service the communities that are being created.

Commissioner Muder also spoke to this, stating that, in his experience, he could name several current subdivisions with issues with their septic systems where there were failures and work was done improperly. This would end up being an unanticipated problem for homeowners that have purchased these parcels with the intent of building their home only to find that they cannot build because they can't meet the standards set forth by ADEQ, especially when surrounding properties have homes on them.

Commissioner Latham also recalled some previous experiences with connecting a small subdivision that had to be incorporated and brought to a standard to connect to a municipal sewer system.

Commissioner Muder further stated that with his experience in the field of installing septic systems, he could see how this amendment was trying to correct the issues of subdivisions from the 60's and 70's for the future and gave his support.

With no further inquiries from the Commission or the public, Commissioner Latham made a motion to recommend approval to the Board of Supervisors. Vice-Chair Smith seconded the motion.
Motion passed unanimously, 8 - 0.
