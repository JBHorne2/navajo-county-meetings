TXT-A 24-005 AMENDMENT TO ARTICLES 20 AND 25 OF THE NAVAJO COUNTY ZONING ORDINANCE - INSTITUTIONAL RESIDENCES AND OTHER GROUP HOMES:  Consideration of a text amendment to Article 20, Section 2001 and Article 25, Section 2518 of the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance, regarding Institutional Residences and other Group Homes which do not qualify as Group Homes for the Disabled.

Ms. Sondra Starke, Planner I, presented the item to the Commission, stating that the reason they were amending the Ordinance was to provide more guidance and clarify development standards for group homes that do not qualify as group homes for the disabled.  The proposed amendment would benefit Staff, applicants, the Commission and Board as a frame of reference going forward.

Ms. Starke explained that the Special Use Permit (SUP) process would remain the same, and there would be no changes to the requirements for group homes for the disabled.

There were no comments either for or against the item from the public.

With there being no further questions, Commissioner Latham motioned to recommend approval to the Board of Supervisors.  Commissioner Murph seconded the motion.  The motion passed unanimously, 7 - 0 .

COMPREHENSIVE PLAN KICK-OFF MEETING:

Terri Hogan, Project Manager, and McKayla Dunfey, Assistant Project Manager with Logan Simpson Design introduced themselves and their work to begin the process for the Comprehensive Plan update.

Ms. Hogan started by introducing the consultant team that would be involved with the Comprehensive Plan update:

Logan Simpson is the lead consultant:

Bruce Meighen, Project Advisor

Terri Hogan, Project Manager

McKayla Dunfey, Assistant Project Manager

Kimley Horn to support Transportation & Water Resources

Elliot D. Pollack & Company to support Housing & Economic Growth

Navajo County team:

Madhav Mundle, Public Works Director

Mischa Larisch, Deputy Director of Planning & Development,

Cody Cooper, Planning Manager/Project Manager.

Commissioner Booher asked what qualifies someone as a subject-matter expert, Terri responded that there were a lot of things that would qualify someone, including certification, experience, education and vocation.

Ms. Hogan further detailed the purpose of the comprehensive plan and the different elements, topics, public involvement and adoption/re-adoption required by ARS 11-804; Ms. Hogan also stated that there had not been any major changes to the current comprehensive plan since it's adoption in 2004.

She also laid out the Comprehensive Plan's overall hierarchy in the planning process, before listing the topics that would be covered by the study:

Land Use

Environment

Transportation

Open Space

Housing

Recreation

Water Resources

Public Facilities

Energy

Economic Growth

Ms. Hogan then described the Future Land Use map designations, how the Goals and Policies would provide some guidance for future projects, such as Zone Changes, Special Use Permits, Zoning Ordinance interpretations and revisions that would encourage new areas of growth through possible new or different commercial districts, and revisions of development standards.

Ms. Hogan further went on to discuss the project schedule and the timeline for producing the Comprehensive Plan, what they would be looking at for Existing Conditions, through inventory and Analysis and GIS mapping before moving on to the Engagement Strategies they would implement:

Chair Rogers asked how the outreach would be done to the various organizations. Ms. Hogan listed several options: in-person, virtual, phone, etc.

Chair Rogers asked about advertisement plans, Ms. Hogan requested Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, to help clarify and explain the various ways to reach out to the public, including having key people spread the word, attending Show Low Days, E-lists etc. Mr. Cooper said that there has already been some outreach, and that there were plans to also be attending the County Fair.

Chair Rogers asked about how that information was compiled. Ms. Hogan explained that all those top priorities would be sifted through, and the various questionnaires would help forge the plan.

Chair Rogers asked how much public input could influence the Comprehensive Plan. Ms. Hogan explained that the community input was incredibly important, and through the participation a customized plan would be made to better suit the County and be able to guide the community to a better future.

Chair Rogers expressed concern over the aging but vocal community being short-sighted, and potentially having a negative impact that would impede growth. Ms. Hogan assured the chairman that the plan would be balanced.

Commissioner Booher asked about accessibility for technologically challenged members of the public, and requested pen-and-paper options, to which Ms. Hogan ensured that there would be such options available.

Commissioner Murph likewise expressed concern about there being only virtual meetings, wanting to ensure that there would be in-person input. Ms. Hogan confirmed that there would be in-person meetings; She also requested the Commission's input on venues for hosting such meetings.

Chair Rogers disagreed and noted that there were plenty of technologically capable community leaders that would be able to spread the word like wildfire. Mr. Cooper mentioned that there would be a balance between digital and in-person outreach and explained a little more about how the input would impact the Comprehensive Plan.

Ms. Hogan then introduced McKayla Dunfey, Assistant Project Manager, who demonstrated live polling software through Menti Poll. Commissioners, Staff, and Public were all able to participate using smartphones. This closed the presentation, with requests for the Commission to feel free to provide feedback and suggestions.
