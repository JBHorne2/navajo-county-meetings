SUP 24-008 WHITE WHALE PROPERTIES LLC EVENT VENUE SUP, DISTRICT IV: Consideration of request by Zachary Markham to allow for the construction of an event barn, and operation as an event venue for groups up to 125 people on approximately 5.02 acres in the Pinedale area.
APN#: 409-01-008C, T10N, R20E, S20 of the GSRM.
Owner: White Whale Properties, LLC.
Agent: Zachary Markham.

Mr. Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, presented the item to the Commission. Mr. Cooper listed features of the property, noting that the zoning was A-General, and that events would be held on the front lawn of the existing residence and guest house on the property. He then showed the two possible sites for where the event barn would be in the future, as the applicant had not yet decided on where to locate the barn. The property is located approximately four (4) miles from Highway 260 and is directly across from Pinedale Estates.
At the time the Staff Report had been completed, Staff had received sixty-one (61) comments with eight (8) in favor. Mr. Cooper stated that more comments had been received, and the total would be added to the Staff Report that would go before the Board of Supervisors. Staff recommended approval of the Special Use Permit, as it met the relevant goals and policies in the Comprehensive Plan. Mr. Cooper noted that the area was specifically marked for recreation, guest lodgings, resorts, hotels, and tourist-oriented commercial uses.

Commissioner George John attended the meeting online at that point.

The applicant, Mr. Zackary Markham, approached the Commission to speak, introducing his business goals, stating that he had been navigating the relationship with the neighbors, but that often the tenants surprised them and were in the process of mitigating the various issues that had been presented.
Below is a summary of his points:

The event Barn proposed would help contain and mitigate the noise.

A noise curfew has been implemented, with signs asking tenants to be respectful, on top of trying to vet guests.  Automated messages to remind guests to be respectful of the noise levels. These messages are delivered throughout and before the stay.

The neighboring property owner helps keep an eye on things, but an Event Manager will eventually be hired to directly manage events.

Mr. Markham's personal cellphone number had been given out to call in the event of loud guests.

Additional wells will be dug to address the water pressure issues by the Water Company, including installing a 62k gallon holding tank, and these should be done by the end of 2025. Additionally, Mr. Markham was willing to install a holding tank and install booster pumps to fill during off-peak hours to mitigate issues.

It has been communicated to guests that all laws are to be understood and followed. However, as this is a vacation rental, previous tenants have brought in recreational off-road vehicles. Mr. Markham does not rent any such equipment to the tenants.

There is a single fire pit, and no other fires are allowed on the property.

Mr. Markham has acquired a will-serve letter from the local Fire Department.

There were not many venues like this in the County and were not located as close to the forest.

Mr. Markham compared his proposed project to two similar existing venues located in Pinetop.

The Chair then opened public comments, starting with those in Favor:
Erica Markham, Payson Waldriff, Sharae Ellsworth, Michael Waldrif, Timothy Shockey, Janine Shockey

Comments:

The venue will draw more business to the area.

Mr. Markham has been proactive in reaching out to tenants to keep the peace and address complaints.

It has provided jobs for the area, which will also include construction if the project is approved.

It will provide more opportunities for recreation.

Beneficial to local businesses, such as tailors, florists, and bakeries due to weddings.

The Chair opened for comments opposed to the SUP:
Dennis Stinson, Catherine Stinson, Wade Warden, Cindy Hughes, Steve Marzullo, Karen Adams, Dana Mead, Charles Mead, Kayla Kennedy, Leona Bryant.

Comments:

The disturbance from the noise.

Harm to wildlife.

Tenants are disrespectful.

Tenants bring off-road vehicles.

Tenants wander off the property onto private property.

Tenants scare horses with noise, approaching the horses while wandering off the property.

Fire safety concerns.

Alcohol concerns.

Traffic concerns.

Lack of curfew, events keeping neighbors awake late into the night.

Transient concerns over trespassing.

Flooding concerns.

Septic concerns.

Water usage concerns.

Accusations of the applicant not living in the area.

Claims there were other venues that could be used by people desiring to have a wedding in the forest.

The Chair closed the public comments and asked the Commission if there were additional questions.

Commissioner Booher asked what the timeline for completion of the project would be. Mr. Markham gave a few numbers, but ultimately said there would be around seven (7) to nine (9) months for completion.

Commissioner Booher also asked about how many events per year, what would be done for music, and if there would be alcohol served. Mr. Markham explained that there was no intention to sell alcohol, but that would not stop people from bringing alcohol into the venue.  Mr. Markham stated that loudspeakers or music were not allowed outside. Mr. Markham wished to be open from April to October and was unsure about total number of events.

Commissioner Muder asked if he lived there part-time. Mr. Markham confirmed that he did not live there full-time, and it was a part-time residence that was rented while the family was away.
Commissioner Booher asked if the Special Use Permit (SUP) was denied if the Applicant would be able to continue using the property as a short-term rental. Mr. Cooper confirmed that the Applicant would be able to continue using the land as a short-term rental as the County did not regulate short-term rentals, and that a Special Event Permit (SEP) could be acquired for large events.

Commissioner Latham asked if there were noise regulations, Mr. Cooper confirmed that the County did not have a
Noise Ordinance and could not regulate the noise as-is. He noted that the recommended conditions in the Staff Report stated that with the SUP the Applicant would be required to cut all noise at 10:00 PM, the barn would help mitigate the noise, or the applicant would be required to move all parties indoors to one of the existing structures.

Commissioner Latham asked about the Lighting Ordinance and how that would affect the venue. Mr. Cooper stated that any existing structures with lights that existed prior to the implementation of the Lighting Ordinance would be considered legally non-conforming, or "Grandfathered", but that any new construction would be required to adhere to the Lighting Ordinance.

Chair Rogers asked if there was nothing in place to govern or regulate loud noise at that time. Mr. Cooper confirmed that as far as the Ordinance was concerned, there was no law regulating that- but the Sherriff's Office might have something regarding disturbing the peace, but there was nothing in the Ordinance regarding the noise levels.

Chair Rogers noted that by granting an SUP the applicant would be much more restricted. Mr. Cooper confirmed that was correct.
Chair Rogers asked about the County's responsibility toward maintaining the Roadway. Mr. Will Flake, County Engineer, addressed this question, explaining that the County provided maintenance and gave a brief description of a traffic study which would be required by the SUP and what that would address.

Chair Rogers asked about the Comprehensive Plan, wanting clarification about the character area. Mr. Cooper explained that the Comprehensive Plan set various goals and guidelines for different areas of the County to help promote growth in various areas. The area this project was in had been identified as one that would suit recreational use, such as the business Mr. Markham was looking to expand upon. Mr. Cooper added that the Comprehensive Plan was not regulatory and merely a suggested use to help align with the County's goals.

Commissioner Muder asked about the available parking, noting that 25 parking spaces did not seem like it would be enough to accommodate a large wedding party. Mr. Markham said he was able to designate more, noting that he would have strict rules against parking anywhere outside the property.

Chair Rogers asked how he had been proactive in handling the noise and Mr. Markham gave an example: after receiving a complaint via text which he quickly confirmed with his neighbor, he then texted the tenant to ask them to quiet down, resulting in them completely cutting the noise in minutes.

Vice-Chair Smith noted that he couldn't always be glued to his phone to receive complaints, to which Mr. Markham explained he would eventually hire an event manager to be more hands-on with large events and keeping an eye on tenants. He stated that several individuals had already reached out about applying for the position.

Vice-Chair Smith also asked about his emergency plans for evacuation, fires, and emergency services, noting that the remoteness of the area could have grave consequences as help was hours away. Mr. Markham thanked Vice-Chair Smith for her feedback and would include warnings in his language. Mr. Markham noted that parking would be directed by the event manager to help keep things running smoothly in the event of an emergency.
Mr. Cooper stated that Condition 9 of Staff's requirements addresses the parking. The building permitting phase will have everything reviewed by the Fire Department for approval.

Vice-Chair Smith asked if Recreational Vehicles (RVs) had been addressed or considered, Mr. Cooper stated that if the Applicant wanted to include RVs the Applicant would need an entirely new SUP or an SEP that would be reviewed.

With no further questions from the Commission, Commissioner Booher motioned to recommend approval to the Board of Supervisors. Commissioner Howe seconded. Motion passed unanimously, 7 - 0 .

SUP 24-009 HENLEY WIRELESS COMMUNICATION FACILITY, DISTRICT II: Consideration of a request by Pinnacle Consulting Inc. for a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction and operation of a 199'self-support lattice tower and associated facilities, located north of Winslow.
APN#: 103-22-027, T19N, R16E, S07 of the GSRM.
Owner: Gene Hancock.
Agent:  John Katsiris.

Ms. Katherine Wright, Planner II presented the project to the Commission. Ms. Wright went over the details of the project, surrounding landmarks, and nearby zoning which consisted of A-General zoned property with two homes to the east. Ms. Wright also shared the site plan, noting the tower's location in the upper northwest corner of the property and showed the access point to the tower off Morman Trail. Ms. Wright then shared the photo simulations of the tower, noting that there was room for co-location on the tower. Staff had not received any feedback from the public about this tower. The Applicant hosted a public outreach meeting, sending the notifications to the nine (9) surrounding property owners on August 6th, 2024, for the Virtual Public Outreach meeting on August 22nd. There was only one person in attendance, Mr. David Coolidge the Winslow Town Manager, who noted his excitement about there being more infrastructure.

The Applicant was also available to speak, Mr. John Katsiris with Pinnacle Consulting addressed the Commission and confirmed that there would be room on the tower for three carriers. He also noted that the Applicant had an engineer to address any further questions from the Commission.

There were no comments from the public either for or against the tower.

The Commission had no further comments or questions.

Commissioner Booher motioned to recommend approval to the Board of Supervisors.
Commissioner Muder seconded the motion.  Motion passed unanimously,
7 - 0 .
