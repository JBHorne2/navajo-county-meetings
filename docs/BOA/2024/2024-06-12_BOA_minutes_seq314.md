2.

VAR 24-001 – VARIANCE, DISTRICT III:  Discussion and possible action on a request by Eric and Donna Gierst on their approximate 0.98 acre, RU-1 zoned parcel, to allow for a variance, allowing for the encroachment from the required twenty foot (20') front yard setback to a seven foot, nine inch (7'9") front yard setback, on the south of the property, to allow for an additional structure and porch to be connected to the existing building, being utilized as a residence. APN# 304-21-388A, T11N, R22E, S15. Owner: Eric and Donna Gierst. Applicant/Agent: Donna Gierst.

Tyler Richards, Planner II, presented the Variance request to the Board.  Mr. Richards showed where the property was located, within White Mountain Lakes.  Mr. Richards explained that the request was for a Variance from the twenty-foot (20') required front yard setback to seven-feet and nine-inches (7'9") to allow for an addition to the home with a porch.  Mr. Richards then went through property conditions to note any exceptional features.  Mr. Richards explained that there is no minimum property depth in Navajo County, but that the property was one-hundred and ninety feet (190') deep, making it not exceptionally shallow. Mr. Richards noted that the property was irregular in shape, due to having a keyhole shape at the southern portion of the property as well as irregular and non-parallel side and rear lot lines, and that it had an approximate slope percentage of 4.2% from north to south and approximately a 26% grade on the eastern portion, which would be considered exceptional irregular topography.  Mr. Richards then noted that Staff recommended denial of the Variance request, as it did not meet the requirements of the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance, nor State Law for a Variance, due to it appearing to be a matter of preference, as there were other locations on the site to which the addition could be added.

Board Member Larsen asked about the two-foot (2') setback of the carport and if that was legal and permitted.  Mr. Richards responded that this was approved by a previous Planning administration.  Cody Cooper, Planning Manager, noted that this type of approval was typical of the time approved and is considered legally permitted.  Donna Gierst, the applicant for the case, noted that Trent Larson, a previous Planning administrator, had personally visited the site and approved the two-foot (2') setback of the carport.

Board Member Evans asked if the property was surveyed in the preparation of site plans for the property.  Mr. Richards explained that the drawings provided were from a site plan prepared when applying for building permits and not necessarily from a survey.  Mr. Cooper clarified that the site plan was prepared by a professional drafter.

Mrs. Gierst also went on to give her reasons for requesting the variance, stating that it would help with beautification of the neighborhood, and stating the peculiarities of the shape of the lot and grade, along with a private easement was enough to declare a hardship.

Mr. Eric Gierst also provided some examples of hardships, including the location of their propane tank.

There were no further public comments.

Board Member Evans asked about the area, if it had been designated as a side yard.

Mr. Richards stated that he was unable to determine if it was considered a side yard, and Mr. Cooper clarified that if it was a side yard, the zoning setbacks would be even more strict at a thirty-foot (30') setback instead of a twenty-foot (20') setback.

Board Member Evans understood that while the lot did have a peculiar shape and grade, there was room for the addition to be added and not encroach on the setback.

Board Member Larsen also noted that while he was sympathetic to the fact that it would change the look of the entrance, the placement was a matter of preference, and he would not be able to support it.

Vice Chair Evans made a motion to deny the Variance. The motion was seconded by Board Member Larsen .
Motion passed unanimously, 3 - 0 .
