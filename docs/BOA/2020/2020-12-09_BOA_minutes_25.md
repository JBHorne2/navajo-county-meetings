ITEM #2 – VAR 20-003 – VARIANCE, DISTRICT III: Discussion and possible 
action on a request by Steve and Debbie Cavender on their approximately 0.23-
acre, RU-1 zoned parcel to allow for a variance, allowing for the encroachment 
from the ten (10) foot required side yard setback, to a two (2) foot side yard 
setback, on the north of the property, to allow for the construction of a home 
addition on APN 304-30-005, a portion of Township 11 North, Range 22 East, 
Section 15 of the Gila and Salt River Meridian in the White Mountain Lake area. 
 
Mr. Cooper presented the item to the Board, describing the are a of the project 
and notable landmarks, noting the  parcel was 0.23 acres in size,  flag-shaped, 
and having only 19 feet of frontage giving it a  unique shape for a parcel. 
Additionally, the steep slope in the rear area of the lot  makes the applicant feel

this area is unu sable; The applicant feels then that the western portion of  the 
property is the only suitable area upon which to expand structures. 
 
Mr. Cooper then gave some histor y on the lot;  noting that the original CC&R ’s 
called for 8 foot setbacks for the side yards and 20 foot setbacks for the front and 
rear. When the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance went into place, the  zoning in 
the area was assigned as RU -1, however because of the size of the lots, they 
were given the setbacks for R1 -10, as the RU -1 zoning district’s setback 
requirements would  have left most of the lots unbuildable. However, given the 
structures built on the property already, peculiar conditions are not leading to a 
lack of buildability on the parcel. Additionally, as a permit was released with 
appropriate setbacks, there is an opportunity for an addition to occur, though not 
at the size originally requested/desired by the property owners. When 
considering the nature of the hardship , there is concern that this request would 
not preserve the general intent and purpose of the Nava jo County Zoning 
Ordinance, as well as  State law . Staff therefore recommends denial of this 
variance.  Staff noted there was support from neighbors  in favor of t he project, 
including the neighboring property, and from neighbors across the lake ; with a 
multitude of letters and more phone calls voicing their approval. 
 
The chairman called for comments. 
 
Commissioner Smith asked if the garage was encroaching, Cody stated that 
yes, it was, noting that the records indicated that the garage was built in the early 
2000’s. The house however was built i n the ‘90’s. The Garage, as an accessory 
structure, could encroach, however the home itself could not. 
 
Commissioner Hartman asked if there had been i nspections on the addition , 
and if they have not passed the footing inspection, John Osgood confirmed that it 
had several inspections, and they had fa iled one footing inspection  due to 
engineering. 
 
Fred Whittier, the con tractor, said that they had already drywalled in the walls 
and were get ting ready to do the roof when they received a stop order. He tried 
to explain the reasoning behind his ignoring the 10 ’ setback as noted on the 
approved site plan and admitted he did not know that a garage could enc roach 
the setbacks whereas a home could not. 
 
Steve Cavender , the applican t, made his case towar d the variance, trying to 
explain his reasoning for applying , and noted that they have even spoken with 
the adjacent property owner about purchasing the needed space , however they 
did not wish to sell their lo t, nor a portion of it . The reason they wish to move 
forward with a variance is because of concerns about weather , as the roof has 
not been finished.

Eric Gierst from the public stated that he had do ne some research on this, but 
the applicant had not built anything personally, so his ignorance of the setb acks

issue presented by the County not catching this sooner when it would have been

The Chairman asked Commissioner DeSpain what his thoughts were and said

applicant. 
 
Commissioner Hartman also noted that he was a stickler for setting precedent 
and said that he feels the contractor was more at fault, but he felt that the Board 
should support this variance. 
 
Chairman Arendell also said th at there was some level of fault given that they 
had had several inspections, and this should have been caught sooner. 
 
The Chairman called for a motion, and Commissioner J .R. DeSpain made a 
motion to approve the variance.  Commissioner Hartman seconded the motion.  
Motion passed with a vote of 3 to 0.
