Presentation on the Capital Improvement Program for 2025-2029 :
Madhav Mundle stated that they started the Capital Improvement Program last year and that this is the second of a five-year program. He indicated that they are trying to make it better and more inclusive to reflect the needs.  He stated that this presentation is to provide information on how the projects were selected, that there are a few partnership projects, and that one of the partnership projects is bound by an IGA and they are required to have those funds.  He talked about the purpose of the CIP, indicating that it is a roadmap for systematically constructing, upgrading, and modernizing infrastructure; that it is a strategy for achieving goals and objectives for near-term and long-term projects depending on the priorities; and a plan for major capital needs over time. He stated that this is mostly a programming document and not a budget document, indicating that the projects will be in different budget years.  He spoke about the CIP development process, indicating that project prioritization is based on need and available resources to complete the project and that the CIP is reviewed and updated each year by Public Works staff, with the County Engineer leading the process.  He stated that the projects are run on revenue projects, that they are estimated at program levels and the estimates will be tweaked as each fiscal year gets closer.  He indicated that identification of a project doesn't guarantee construction, that projects may get deferred or dropped if the priorities change or the revenue projections fall, and they will prioritize and determine which projects they must deliver.  He spoke about the main criteria used for prioritizing projects, including safety improvement, condition of roadways, drainage improvements, and structure rehabilitation. He reviewed the various projects for Engineering, Flood Control, Highway Maintenance and Fleet Management that are included in the final CIP for the next year, indicating that the document will be uploaded on their website.

Supervisor Seymore shared his appreciation for the time that was put forth to be able to see priorities they are working on and what is needed in the communities.  He indicated that the hopes that upgrading to improve the challenged areas will increase quality of roads in the county.
Supervisor Whitesinger stated that she appreciates the work, and talked about the discussion held regarding some of the projects and being able to better understand how they are prioritized.  She indicated that when thinking about the amount of roadways we have, being able to understand the road structures and the support that is needed is overwhelming.  She stated that she knows that staff have worked hard to make sure they have the appropriate data to be able to make decisions in helping prioritize the projects to best meet the needs of the communities.
Vice-Chair Benally requested additional information on the District One project.
Mr. Mundle stated that there is a pilot project to slowly, based on the priority, start to convert unpaved toad to paved roads by utilizing cold recycled asphalt paving to improve the surface condition of the roadway.

*Consideration and possible approval to discontinue the use of DS200 on-site tabulators and return to only using the central count tabulators :
Bryan Layton stated that they have received a lot of questions and concerns, from citizens, regarding elections, the integrity of the machines, and how the ballots are counted and transported over the past couple of months, that he did a presentation to the Board and that this is a follow-up to the presentation.  He indicated that they have received additional questions regarding the machines, in particular the DS200 model, which are the small tabulators that read and count ballots.  He stated that they have fourteen of the units which are deployed to individual vote centers, that citizens cast their vote on a paper ballot, feed it into the tabulator, that the vote is counted, tabulated and then at the end of the night the results are transferred onto a secure flash drive and delivered, with the paper ballots to central count in Holbrook.  He indicated that there were a lot of questions received regarding this, that they have a handout that can be found on the county website under Elections-Information and FAQs, noting that they are working on making it more searchable and easier to get to.  He stated that there were a lot of very specific technical questions that were received, that he reached out to the vendor, ES&S, that he spoke with their Security Chief and that they helped with responses to a lot of the questions.  He further stated that they have tried to provide as much detail as possible in answering and being responsive to the questions, which can be found on the website.  He summarized the questions that they received regarding the machines, the first being the capability for them to co communicate: do they have modems, can they be compromised or hacked; and second the security of hardware and software: who owns ES&S, where is their servers stored, what third party partners are used and password security.  He shared the information that they found regarding the different questions.  He stated as to the questions regarding the capability to communicate that state statute prohibits modem technology in tabulators and that selling DS200s with modems in Arizona is illegal; that DS200 Tabulators can be ordered with modems for the handful of states that allow for secure modems and software at an additional cost; and that ES&S provides instructions for visual inspection to verify if a unit is equipped with a modem.  He talked about the security or hardware and software, indicating that ES&S is operated in Omaha, NE and there is no foreign investment; that all served are owned, operated and maintained by ES&S in Omaha; that all assemble of the units is done by ES&S in Omaha, computer chips are from Intel and AMD, all products are transported by ES&S drivers and vehicles and all steps in hardware production have chain of custody; all software is written and developed by ES&S staff in Omaha and all steps in software development has a chain of custody; that the DS200s use a highly customized version of Windows 10 which is fundamentally altered by ES&S, it's a proprietary version that can't communicate with or operate anything besides ES&S, it can't run MS Office and will not run .exe or autorun riles, it's can't talk to other operating windows, is not susceptible to "solar wind" attack, can't access the internet and only DS200s with modems can "speak" to other ES&S computers but not access the internet, and DS200 has military-grade encryption tested against "hashbunny" and other attacks; and that the third party companies, including the Department of Homeland Security and academic institutions, are used to test and validate. He Layton reviewed the takeaways from the information received.  He indicated that from the county's perspective the DS200s have sufficient security and have been vetted and approve for use by security personnel.  He spoke about the legal requirements found in the first section of ARS Title 16, that ballots shall be counted by automatic tabulating equipment and ballots may be counted manually only if it becomes impracticable to count all or part of the ballots with tabulating equipment and counties may not independently choose to do so.  He talked about the guides and reference points clarifying that Arizona counties must use tabulators, which include a letter to Mohave County from the Attorney General's Office and a letter to the Navajo County Board of Supervisors from the Secretary of State, which can be found on the website.  He spoke about how Navajo County is in compliance with the statute by using the two machines located at central count.  He stated that the DS200 machines that have been used for onsite tabulation at the vote centers that have the most voter turnout for the past three elections, that they have had some experience using them, that the county is in legal compliance with ARS 16.  He reviewed the options for continuing to use both the DS200 and central count tabulators and for discontinuing the use the DS200 machines and only using the central count machines, noting that both options are in compliance with state statute, and that it doesn't change the voter experience.  He stated that onsite tabulators may make more sense in areas with larger populations but that they have turned out to deliver the efficiencies that they had hoped for.  He indicated that they feel that tabulating all the ballots at election central provides the best environment for observable accountable elections.  He noted that discontinuing use of the DS200 does not preclude staff from coming to the Board in the future and recommend that they be used again.  He stated that would move forward with either decision but that it is staffs preference to simplify the process and use only the central count machines.

Chairman Whiting shared his appreciation for the time on this item.  He indicated that there have been a lot of questions around this and stated that he appreciates staff taking the time to listen to the citizens and learn more about this, as well as the citizens being willing to engaging and providing information that needed to be looked at and considered.  He further stated that he appreciates Mr. Layton validating some of the concerns to be considered if they were to continue to use them.  He indicated that it's his understanding that due to the structure here staff is recommending just using the central tabulators.  He thanked Mr. Layton for the time and work on this, noting that it is important to many community members. He indicated that they wanted to make sure and look at this and consider the options to best serve the people and be within the law.
Supervisor Seymore stated that Mr. Layton reached out to the public, answered questions and researched this thoroughly, noting that they can and can't do certain things.  He stated that by simplifying and moving the process to a more central area that can be observed also simplifies the long term that would have ongoing expense for maintaining and keeping the machines up and that it will make the process more accurate.  He thanked Mr. Layton and staff for the work on this item.
Supervisor Whitesinger thanked Mr. Layton and staff, noting that it was taxing going through all the questions, but that it is important to be able to help the communities and those within the organization to best understand the election and the steps we have taken as a county to ensure we are following every regulation and law and that we are accountable.  She stated that she is grateful to everyone who has taken the time to answer the questions of the community members and going through the process to investigate the best steps for moving forward.  She indicated that it's not something that they take lightly and that it's best to have deliberated on it and determine the best way to help the community members feel confident in the election.  She stated that with every question, if they did not know the answer, somebody reached out to the organization to find the answers and that no question has been taken lightly.
