PUBLIC HEARING:  Consideration and possible adoption of Resolution Number 47-2024 , approving/denying a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction and operation of a 199'self-support lattice tower and associated facilities, located north of Winslow :
A presentation was shown. Katherine Wright stated that this request for a Special Use Permit is for the construction and operation of a 199-foot self-support lattice communication tower by Pinnacle Consulting Inc., located north of Winslow. She indicated that the site spans 372.64 acres, is zoned A General, and the tower will be surrounded by a 50x50 foot lease area with an eight-foot chain-link fence. The proposed tower is situated 199 feet from property boundaries.  She stated that staff recommends approval of the permit, subject to 13 conditions outlined in the staff report. Supervisor Seymore inquired about the height restrictions for cell communication towers, recalling a previous understanding that the limit was 80 feet, with a potential increase to 120 feet.

b.

PUBLIC HEARING:  Consideration and possible adoption of  Resolution Number 48-2024 , approving/denying a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction of an event barn, and operation as an event venue for groups of up to 125 people on approximately 5.02 acres in the Pinedale area :
A presentation was shown.  Tyler Richards stated that this is a request for a Special Use Permit for a property located south of Pinedale, Arizona. He indicated that the request involves constructing a 4,500-square-foot event barn to be used for events like weddings, with parking for up to 125 people.  He stated that the parcel is about 3.7 miles from the heart of Pinedale and is owned by White Whale Properties, LLC, that the proposed site is situated in the southeast corner of the parcel, with nearby vacant land, a subdivision Pinedale Estates, additional residences to the north, and forest land to the south. He indicated that the site plan includes two possible locations for the barn, one being more to the north and the other one is the southeast corner.  Mr. Richards stated that the project aligns with the county's Comprehensive Plan, which encourages small specialty enterprises that support tourism near the National Forest. He stated that the project has appropriate access off Rim Drive, with minimal impact on traffic, as confirmed by a traffic impact statement. He further stated that the Clay Springs Fire Department has also reviewed and approved the project.  He indicated that there have been approximately 63 comments received, with eight in support and the remainder in opposition for concerns including noise, light pollution, fire safety, reckless driving, and trespassing. He stated that Planning and Development has reviewed the concerns and has proposed conditions to mitigate noise and light pollution, including restricting event hours and requiring noisy events to be held inside the barn, however, concerns regarding reckless driving and trespassing are beyond the Planning Department's ability to regulate.  He stated that staff recommends approval of the Special Use Permit, subject to 14 conditions outlined in the report, and that the Planning and Zoning Commission has also recommended approval.
Zach Markham, the owner of White Whale Properties, introduced himself and indicated that the property in question has been used as a short-term rental. He stated that due to a high volume of requests for weddings, they decided to pursue building an event barn. He indicated that the property is zoned as A General, which aligns with the county's goals of supporting tourism near the National Forest.  He acknowledged that there are concerns from some members of the community, particularly regarding noise. He mentioned that they have already taken steps to address this, such as installing signage and sending automated messages to guests, reminding them to be mindful of noise. He indicated that the proposed event barn is intended to keep loud activities indoors, minimizing disturbance to the surrounding community.  He stated that a traffic study was conducted, showing minimal impact to the area. He emphasized that their goal is to be good neighbors and to operate the property in a way that meets county goals while ensuring it does not negatively affect the community. He expressed openness to suggestions for further improvements and clarified that they are not trying to force the project but are seeking ways to best utilize the property.

Chairman Whiting opened the Public Hearing.
Beverly Van Horn expressed concerns about the proposed event venue. She disagreed with Mr. Markham's statement that there are few venues nearby, listing several alternatives within a short distance.  She questioned the need for another venue so close to their rural neighborhood.
Cindy Hughes expressed strong opposition to the special use permit, indicating that the project offers no benefit to the neighborhood, only creating issues such as party noise, traffic dangers, and off-road vehicles speeding through their area. She questioned the project's alignment with the county's comprehensive plan, noting that the property is not adjacent to the national forest as required for recreation area designation. She spoke about concerns with safety, citing potential flash floods affecting access roads and fire risks due to the proposed event barn and activities, including alcohol, smoking, and open fire pits.
Michele Harrel expressed opposition to the special use permit application, highlighting concerns about heavy traffic, road damage, and congestion. She pointed out that the proposal did not address issues like a one-lane bridge in Pinedale and the dirt road's susceptibility to damage from increased traffic. She spoke about concerns regarding air quality due to dust pollution from vehicles and the potential negative impact on property values. She questioned the compatibility of the project with the county's comprehensive plan, stating it would disrupt the neighborhood's character and traditions. She expressed skepticism about the project's long-term intentions, suggesting the owners may expand beyond the stated plan, which she believes could lead to year-round events.
Charles Mead stated that he is opposed to the special use permit application, citing legal concerns and personal objections. He spoke about a legal brief from their HOA law firm, arguing that the property is already in violation of Arizona law, which prohibits vacation rentals from being used as event venues. He stated that even if the county issued the special use permit, the property would still violate state law. He raised concerns about the impact on the community, stating that zoning regulations exist to protect neighborhoods from unwanted businesses. He requested that the board deny the permit to preserve the peaceful nature of their community.
Julie Mead spoke about safety concerns regarding the proposed event venue at the White Whale property. She highlighted the potential dangers posed by the Mortenson Wash, which can become impassable during heavy rains or spring thaw, possibly stranding attendees or requiring rescues. She spoke about concerns with fire hazards, particularly with the combination of large gatherings, alcohol, and smoking outdoors. She noted the lack of a second exit for the neighborhood, relying on a single, narrow exit that could quickly be blocked in the event of a fire. She spoke about increased risks of property damage, loss of life, and difficulty for emergency responders, as well as her personal concerns for her family's safety due to increased traffic and speeding in the area. She requested that the board deny the permit for safety reasons.
Dennis Stinson expressed opposition to the proposed event venue at the White Whale property. He cited concerns about the disruption to the neighborhood, as many residents moved to the area for its quiet, rural environment. He spoke about safety issues, particularly the limited access road, which is the only route in and out of the area for residents, creating a potential bottleneck in case of an emergency. He questioned the feasibility of accommodating 25 cars for 125 guests and raised concerns about the environmental impact of building a barn near the Mortenson Wash, which has experienced significant erosion. He requested that the board to consider the safety and practicality of the proposal, given the area's limited infrastructure.
Catherine Stinson shared her opposition to the proposed event venue, citing concerns about alcohol consumption and safety. She pointed out that while Mr. Markham claimed he would not sell liquor, he did not disclose that a mobile bar was part of the rental package. She spoke of the risk of inebriated guests driving through the area, noting that people already drive aggressively on Rim Drive, which would be exacerbated by large events. She also shared past incidents where wedding guests caused disturbances late at night, keeping neighbors awake. She indicated that converting a residential home into a commercial venue would negatively affect the safety and well-being of the community and requested that the board deny the permit.
Dana Mead expressed concerns about the development of White Whale Properties, particularly regarding Mr. Markham's claim of not being a developer. She stated that Markham is involved in multiple businesses and properties across the country. She shared her frustration with the negative impact the White Whale property has already had on the community, including trash, trespassing, and disturbance to wildlife, as well as the safety risks posed by large events. She requested that the Board consider the history and values of the Pinedale Estates community, urging them to deny the permit to expand White Whale Properties and preserve the peaceful, rural environment.
Kayla Kennedy shared her opposition to the proposed wedding event barn on Zachary Markham's property, which is directly across from her home. She raised concerns about the noise, reckless driving, and trespassing that have already negatively impacted the neighborhood. She indicated that the events, set to occur from noon into the night for six months each year, disrupt the peace of their small residential community. She emphasized the inconsistency in Markham's claims about controlling noise, pointing out that a PA system and mobile bar are still advertised despite his assurances otherwise. She requested that the board to consider the quality of life in the neighborhood, as residents moved to the area for peace and quiet, which is now being compromised. She asked the board to deny the permit, as it would drastically change the character of their community.
Erica Markham co-owner of the property in question, addressed the board to provide context and defend her family's decision to pursue the wedding event venue. She explained that when they purchased the cabin, they never intended for it to be a commercial venue, that the property was initially bought for their family to enjoy, and they had no plans to host weddings, but that after receiving over 100 requests in the first year from couples wanting to marry at their property, they decided to explore the idea. She stated that they are rule-following, responsible business owners with a history of operating vacation rentals in multiple locations. She stated that they are simply trying to meet a demand for wedding venues in the area. She also clarified that they do not intend to have large events every day, but rather a few weddings on weekends, and they believe the project aligns with the needs of the community and the goals of Navajo County. She expressed regret that the situation has caused conflict and hoped that the board would understand their intentions and approve the permit.
Janne Shockey expressed support for the wedding event venue proposal. She stated that she and her family live adjacent to the property and have dealt with issues like a deteriorating road and noise for many years. She indicated that while there have been noise complaints in the area, many of these complaints were mistakenly attributed to the Markham's' property when they were actually from other nearby Airbnb rentals. She acknowledged that there were noise issues during certain weddings but emphasized that the Markham's are trying to address these by building a barn to contain the noise, which would improve the situation. She stated that the road and easement to the property are private, used only by a few families, including the Markham's and Shockey's. She noted that while flooding can sometimes block the road, it has never been an issue for more than a day in the 25 years she has lived there. She stated that she supports the venue, noting that the Markham's are doing their best to address the concerns.
Monica Johnston spoke in support of the Markham's and their proposed wedding event venue. She emphasized the character of Zach and Erica Markham, highlighting their family values, community involvement, kindness to neighbors, and ethical approach to business. She expressed confidence that the Markham's are committed to doing things the right way and improving the community. She also noted that development is part of how communities grow, citing her own family's history in the area. She expressed hope that the venue will allow future generations, including her own children, to celebrate their weddings in the area. She urged the board to approve the Markham's' permit.
Timothy Shockey spoke in support of the Markham's' proposed wedding event venue. He expressed empathy for the concerns raised by Pinedale Estates residents but emphasized that progress and development are part of community growth. He noted that he has owned property in the area since 2000 and specifically chose it for its zoning. He spoke about his role in maintaining a private wash that provides access to the properties in question, stating that it has only been temporarily inaccessible a few times over the past 20 years. He shared his positive experience with the Markham's, describing them as good neighbors who are mindful of the impact their business may have on the community. He expressed support for the venue, stating that it is already zoned appropriately and that no zoning changes are being requested.
There being no additional comments the Public Hearing was closed.

Supervisor Seymore asked legal counsel to clarify whether the property, currently being used for vacation rentals, is in violation of the local Airbnb code. Brandt Clark indicated that they are currently in violation.
Supervisor Seymore indicated that initially the applicants bought this property as a personal space for family enjoyment, after researching the zoning but now they are expanding it to host events and turn it into an Airbnb, which appears to be a change in their original intent, from a private retreat to a revenue-generating business.
Mr. Markham stated that from the beginning, their intent was both personal and commercial, noting that while they bought the property for family use, they always knew they wouldn't be there full-time and planned to use it as a short-term rental when not in use. He indicated that the flexible zoning was one of the reasons they chose the property, as it allowed them to potentially do more with it in the future.
Mr. Markham answered questions of Supervisor Seymore regarding the zoning and use, the number of weddings that would be held, and being a good neighbor.
Supervisor Seymore spoke about concerns with the septic system on the property, particularly its proximity to Mortenson Wash and its potential impact on the community's water supply downstream. He indicated that the current code requires the septic system to be located at least 50 feet from the drainage area and that with ongoing erosion in the area, the septic system could cause contamination, emphasizing the need for a strong study on its impact. He suggested that if the septic system proves problematic, restrictions should be implemented, including the possibility of terminating its use.  He shared concerns that the 25 parking spaces required by the code may not be sufficient to accommodate 125 guests at a wedding venue, especially since guests would be staying for an event, not just quickly coming and going like at a retail store. He suggested that the current parking requirement is shortsighted for such large gatherings and asked if it would be possible to stipulate a higher parking capacity than what the code currently requires.
Mr. Clark stated that they can stipulate higher because of the nature of the building and the types of venue that it would be.
Supervisor Seymore spoke about concerns with alcohol-related risks at the proposed wedding venue, referencing a former police officer's experience with disturbances and safety issues at alcohol-serving events. He pointed out that guests driving on a dirt road after consuming alcohol could create highway safety risks, particularly since there are no nearby transportation options. The speaker asked if the applicant would consider prohibiting alcohol at the venue to address these concerns.
Mr. Markham stated that the venue is currently a short-term rental, where guests can bring alcohol and that for weddings, it would be up to the guests' discretion whether to have alcohol, but that they have not proposed banning alcohol at the venue and do not foresee doing so.
Supervisor Seymore inquired if this could be a proposed stipulation.
Mr. Clark indicated that while the Board could potentially restrict alcohol sales at the wedding venue, banning all alcohol on the premises would likely be beyond the Board's authority and difficult to enforce. He spoke of the challenges in regulating individual behavior, such as guests bringing their own alcohol, and recommended considering denial of the special use permit as an alternative to managing alcohol restrictions.
Supervisor Seymore inquired whether the property owners had posted signs or included information in their brochures about being respectful and friendly to the community, particularly in regard to maintaining a curfew or quiet hours, indicating that Airbnb properties sometimes become like "frat houses" instead of respectful, residential spaces.
Mr. Markham stated that they have laminated flyers posted by every exit, instructing guests to keep noise to a minimum and respect neighbors. He indicated that the signs specify that no speakers are allowed outside after 6:00 p.m. and that he provided his contact information during a community meeting, to address concerns directly.  He stated that when noise complaints have arisen, they've responded quickly, contacted the guests, and the guests have been respectful in lowering the noise levels.
Supervisor Seymore spoke about the peaceful reputation of the property but expressed concern about the potential disruption to the tranquility of Pinedale Estates due to the natural amplification of noise from the venue.  He noted that many nearby properties have deed restrictions and belong to an HOA. He spoke about the history of Pinedale, where efforts to address community needs, such as moving the cemetery, have been ongoing. He questioned whether allowing a wedding venue would undermine the community's peace, especially when the county recently invested in restoring a community center that could host weddings.  He inquired of the applicant about their level of involvement in the Pinedale community, specifically regarding local events like the St. Patrick's Day celebration, Chili Cook-Off, and other community activities. He inquired if the applicant had helped or contributed to these events.
Mr. Markham indicated that they haven't yet participated in local Pinedale events but expressed a desire to contribute in the future. He spoke of an idea to donate a portion of the revenue from an event at their property to support the volunteer fire department, recognizing the potential need for their services. He indicated that the property is currently used as a rental, and the applicant usually visits during slower periods when fewer events are scheduled.
Melissa Buckley stated that she received the applicants PowerPoint.
Chairman Whiting indicated that he would like to see the PowerPoint.
A presentation was shown. Mr. Markham provided a detailed comparison of their property with other wedding venues in Navajo County, highlighting the larger acreage and distance from residential areas. He spoke about their commitment to minimizing noise, including limiting outdoor activities and loud music after 10 p.m. and the proposal of using an indoor barn for events. He addressed concerns about traffic, water, and fire safety, noting that a local water project would resolve water pressure issues by 2025, and proposed using portable restroom facilities to mitigate septic concerns. He spoke about their fire safety protocols, limiting fire hazards to a designated fire pit. He expressed their willingness to work with local authorities to ensure compliance with regulations and address any community concerns.
Chairman Whiting spoke about concerns with fire safety, particularly regarding the fire pit on the property, emphasizing the importance of communicating fire restrictions during high-risk periods, such as red flag warnings. He inquired how the applicant would inform guests about fire restrictions, acknowledging the need for proper communication to ensure safety during times when open fires are prohibited.
Mr. Markham described their fire safety protocol, including clear communication with guests about fire restrictions, noting that they have two indoor fireplaces and one outdoor fire pit, and guests are informed that they may not be allowed to use fires during periods of fire restrictions. He stated that they are vigilant about staying updated on fire bans through alerts and a neighbor who is a former firefighter.
Brandt Clark answered questions of Chairman Whiting , clarifying that the venue, if operating as a commercial wedding venue, would be in violation of Arizona Revised Statutes governing short-term rentals, as it requires a special use permit for such activities. He indicated that if the property is being used for weddings without the permit, it would not be compliant with the regulations, which distinguish between residential and commercial uses.
Chairman Whiting spoke about the parking concerns, with Supervisor Seymour raising the issue of the adequacy of the 25 parking spaces currently provided. He inquired of staff if they had any concerns regarding the number of parking spaces.
Cody Cooper stated that they do not currently have concerns about the parking spaces, as they meet the requirements set by both the local ordinance and the IBC, which generally dictates one parking space per five guests. He acknowledged the possibility of needing more parking spaces to accommodate additional needs, such as caterers. He indicated that while the current parking guidelines may be slightly outdated, the existing number of spaces is generally sufficient, though an increase in parking would not be opposed.
Supervisor Whitesinger addressed the traffic study requirement, which is one of the conditions for granting the special permit and inquired of staff whether the current traffic study has been reviewed and if there is a clear understanding of what it entails.
Mr. Cooper indicated that the traffic study was reviewed by both their team and Public Works. He noted that a finalized traffic study is required before moving forward with construction, that the preliminary study has been reviewed, but a final, accepted study will be necessary, especially if there are event limitations or other conditions.
Supervisor Seymore noted that the barn is approximately 4,200 square feet and requested that the applicant describe the barn and how it will be built.
Mr. Markham stated that the barn will be built using pre-made log kits, designed for event spaces like this, and described as being similar to putting together a large-scale "Lincoln Log" structure. He indicated that the building will be a large, open hall, with bathrooms located on the west side, accessible from the exterior and that it will be both air-conditioned and heated to ensure a comfortable environment for events, addressing concerns about temperature control during hot weather.
Supervisor Seymore spoke about the conditions expressing concerns about the number of events allowed at the venue, suggesting a limit of 12 events per year (from April 1st to September 30th) to minimize disruption for nearby residents. He further spoke about the importance of regular review and compliance, proposing that the special use permit be reviewed annually rather than granted for five years. He inquired what would trigger non-compliance, suggesting a set number of complaints or violations, such as exceeding the event limit, before the permit is reconsidered or revoked.
Mr. Clark recommended against specifying a set number of violations or complaints that would trigger non-compliance, indicating that the special use permit be reviewed annually by the Board of Supervisors would be enough. He stated that this would allow for a more flexible approach, considering both egregious violations and minor issues, noting that the decision to renew the permit should be based on whether the venue continues to be in compliance and serves the best interest of the health, safety, and general welfare of the surrounding area.
Supervisor Seymore spoke about additional restrictions, indicating that there will be no parking on the main access road and that there will need to be at least 40 parking spaces designated, that 10:00 p.m. would be an appropriate curfew, that there will be no loud noise outdoors, and that there be no porta-potties. He spoke about setting a precedent for similar venues in other areas, which could lead to complications in regulating and managing such developments.
Mr. Cooper indicated that porta-potties are typically allowed for one-time events but not as a long-term solution for facilities, but that for permanent venues, like the proposed one, the preferred solution is to have a compliant septic system or sewer connection. He noted that the septic system must meet requirements based on the building's layout and expected number of users. He indicated that the use of porta-potties could be optional but is not a required solution for compliance.
Chairman Whiting reviewed the new stipulations and inquired if staff has any issues with the new proposed conditions.
Mr. Cooper stated that staff does not have any concerns with the additions, noting that they are all enforceable through a special use permit.
Mr. Clark inquired if the annual review would be done by staff or come before the Board each year.
Supervisor Seymore stated that he wants it to come before the Board.
William Flake suggested that they set a minimum of 40 parking spaces and have the traffic study assess whether additional spaces are necessary, based on the venue's needs.
Chairman Whiting inquired if the applicant has any concerns with the additional conditions.
Mr. Markham expressed a willingness to comply with the conditions but voiced concerns about the potential for numerous complaints, even minor ones, which could make it difficult to operate the venue if it were to be shut down based on complaints that may not be substantiated.
Brandt Clark clarified that if complaints arise about the property, the revocation of the special use permit would be at the discretion of the Board, that the process would follow standard protocol, where complaints are reviewed by the Code Enforcement Department, and if violations are found, the revocation process could be initiated. He indicated that the proposed annual review by the Board would ensure oversight, without imposing undue burden on the property owner and that the review would allow the Board to assess the situation proactively, rather than waiting for complaints to trigger action.
Mr. Markham spoke about investment and requested that after a year the Board consider making it the five-year review.
Mr. Clark stated that it would be at the discretion of the Board to change that.
Supervisor Seymore spoke about the importance of being a good neighbor and expressed the goal of compromising between the property owner and the concerned residents. He acknowledged that while the situation may not be ideal, the aim is to find a balanced solution that respects both the property's use and the residents' concerns.
Mr. Markham raised a concern regarding the property being used simultaneously as a vacation rental and for events, noting that this could lead to confusion with complaints, particularly around noise and disturbances, and inquired how he could clarify if a complaint was due to renters and not an event.
Chairman Whiting spoke about the importance of mitigating the issues for both uses.
Supervisor Whitesinger expressed appreciation for the public input, acknowledging the validity of concerns shared by community members. She spoke about the importance of finding a balance between property owners' rights to use their land and addressing community concerns. She thanked the public for their engagement and emphasized the value of being good neighbors in the rural community of Navajo County.
Chairman Whiting acknowledged the efforts of Supervisor Seymour, noting that he personally visited the site, reviewed materials, and looked for ways to understand all perspectives to find a solution. He thanked staff for developing stipulations to address, providing a way to ensure the intent of all parties is met. He encouraged both parties to work together as good neighbors, with a clear intent to create a positive experience for the community. He shared his appreciation for the public engagement and feedback, noting the community's strong care for one another.
Supervisor Seymore thanked the constituents for their organized and well-articulated input, noting that their points were heard and appreciated.  He indicated that the approval would be on a trial basis, and if successful, it could lead to harmony moving forward.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to adopt Resolution Number 48-2024 , approving a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction of an event barn, and operation as an event venue for groups of up to 125 people on approximately 5.02 acres in the Pinedale area, with the 14 conditions stated by staff, with modification to #14 requiring an annual review with the Board of Supervisors, modification to #6 that the time period be changed to April 1-September 30 and that no more than 12 venue events be held during that period, that a traffic study be completed, a study on the permitting of a septic tank to be in compliance and with sufficient setbacks, that there be a minimum of 40 parking spaces and be limited in with the traffic study and platted out to show that and that site #2 would be preferred location for the venue, motion seconded by  Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
