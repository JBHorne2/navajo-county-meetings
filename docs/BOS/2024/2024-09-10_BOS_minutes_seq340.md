PLANNING & DEVELOPMENT SERVICES :

a.

Presentation regarding the ongoing update to the Navajo County Comprehensive Plan, including upcoming dates and events as the update moves toward the hearing process :
Cody Cooper reported on Comprehensive Plan, noting that it is a guide for development in the County for the next 20-30 years and that they are statutorily required to review it every 10 years.  He stated that the main purpose is to help with conversation of resources, money usage, and the health, safety and welfare, which is also guided through many of their processes.  He spoke about planning hierarchy, noting that the comprehensive plan is a strong foundational document, then the next step would be zoning and corridor plans, then the regulator documents such as the zoning ordinance and other ordinances in the zoning and corridor plan phase, next is the site plans and then the general plans and certificate of occupancy.  He stated that as far as plan contents, based on our population, Navajo County is only required to cover two elements in the plan as required by state law, land use and transportation, but that due to the growth and needs, and wanting to be forward thinking, they have put in ten topics, noting that water and wastewater are combined, but are two topics.  He indicated that 10 topics is a lot to have the public and developers to read through, so they have combined them into three main themes that are like uses.  He spoke about the community questionnaire that was put out at events and on the comprehensive plan website, noting that they received 135 responses and indicated that the questionnaire has been shut down so they can start to bring the ideas together to guide the plan update.  He spoke about the responses that were received, noting that some of the items are conflicting in nature, which is natural within this type of planning and that there has to be some give and take with the conflicts.  He stated that it is very important to continue encouraging community feedback so they can refine the balance and figure out where the scales need to be in the end, noting that this process is an organized way for residents to share thoughts and perspective that will help to directly craft and influence the document that will guide the county decisions on future projects.  He stated that the document is the communities document, and their input will guide what is adopted.  He spoke about the community interviews that have been done with some major stakeholders, the community events that have been held as well as the ones that are coming up.  He stated that just through the events they have engaged with over 900 community members so far.  He spoke about next steps, indicating they will have the 60-day review as required by state law, and will have to giving the plan to adjacent counties that physical touch Navajo County, any of the cities within the county boundaries and the local and regionalized agencies; and the public will be able to review the plan as well.  He stated that they will schedule open houses during review period, noting that there will be one October 3, 2024, from 6:00 to 8:00 p.m. at the Navajo County Governmental Complex and another one at the Show Low City Hall, October 4, 2024, from 11:00 a.m. to 1:00 pm.  He indicated that their hope is to have members of the public come in so they can discuss the items with them and get feedback.  He stated that after that the next step would be the Planning and Zoning Commission Hearing for recommendation of the Comprehensive Plan, which they anticipate doing on December 12, 2024, and the final event would be the Board of Supervisors Meeting for potential adoption, on January 14, 2025.
Supervisor Seymore thanked Mr. Cooper and the team for the efforts put forth in preparing this.  He stated that there is a lot of work that goes into this and that it's the foundation for planning for the county.  He encouraged citizens to get out and be part of the process.  He indicated that it's a very hard process, but that once established that they will be able to go through every 10 years to make changes.  He indicated that it is a foundational piece of planning that is very important to the community.
Supervisor Whitesinger thanked Mr. Cooper and the team.  She stated that it's a great effort to go into community and being able to reach out to so many members of the public to get input.  She stated that she appreciates that they expanded outside of just the land use and transportation to get a comprehensive perspective of how those other topics impact the community.  She thanked them for getting the community involved.
Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Cooper and the team for getting out to engage with the public to make sure that their voices are being heard.  He inquired how they will get the information out to the public for the open houses.
Mr. Cooper spoke about the different ways that they are putting information out, including on their website, the comprehensive plan website, doing a press brief and working with Alicia Deets for social media blasts.
Chairman Whiting inquired if it is their intent to talk with people and get feedback about the county's role for topics such as renewables and cell towers, what can be done inside their authority.
Mr. Cooper stated that they always want to engage, educate and discuss with the public about the roles of staff, the Board of Supervisors and the community members.  He indicated that the comprehensive plan will go through and guide some of their organized documents and how that will assist them prior to coming before the Board.  He indicated that cell towers, that they do see it as a large topic, that for cell towers they are looking at the ordinance that has been developed but that it's largely regulated federally through the Telecommunications Act of 1996.  He indicated that for cell towers the health and safety aspect is not done through the county. He indicated that the county is limited on what they can review for cell towers, that it is generally the setbacks and height and that through federal regulations those have to be considered reasonable, or the ordinance will be stricken as not legal and could result in court issues.  He stated that the health and safety aspect is fully regulated by the Federal Communications Commission and that the county has no oversite and is not allowed to have oversite in those cases.
Chairman Whiting spoke about having sympathy for those citizens that do not wanting to have near the sites near them due to health concerns but noted that they don't have the authorize to address that.  He inquired if the Board could refer them to the correct agency.
Mr. Cooper talked about getting a fact sheets together to explain the counties involvement through the process and what they can oversee and how the public can reach out to the other agencies for the areas that each organization oversees.
Chairman Whiting and Mr. Cooper spoke about the different agencies involved in the different processes.
Chairman Whiting shared his appreciation to Mr. Cooper and requested that the Board be kept informed.  He shared his appreciation for the public outreach.

WORK SESSION :
At 10:55 a.m. All Supervisors were present with staff.

Madhav Mundle discussed the implementation of a Payment Management Program to address the significant financial constraints in maintaining 720 miles of roadways. The program, developed by consultants, uses data-driven analytics to prioritize road maintenance, focusing on cost-effective strategies like slurry seals and chip seals. The program aims to maximize the use of the $2.5 million annual budget, with a 10-year plan to prioritize road segments. Supervisor Whiting emphasized the need for a county-wide approach to road maintenance, while Fern Benally highlighted the challenges of maintaining roads in Navajo areas and requested better communication and support from Public Works. Mr. Mundle also covered the development of a Construction and Design Standards and Specification Manual to ensure consistent and safe construction practices.

Mischa Larish discussed the permit and code enforcement service enhancements. The previous permitting system was a paper-based, in-person process with limited management tools. The new online citizen portal provides a digital, web-based process with improved tracking and management capabilities. The citizen portal allows users to submit building permits, access county road information, report code violations, and schedule inspections online 24/7. This streamlines the process and provides better transparency. The new permitting system includes comprehensive workflow tracking and reporting tools that allow staff to better manage and prioritize inspections and plan reviews. This has helped reduce review times. The code enforcement process has been enhanced with the addition of a hearing officer. This has provided more tools to address code violations, with 64 citations issued so far. The hearing officer process has resulted in 15 properties coming into compliance and 18 working towards compliance. The new systems and processes have improved customer service, transparency, and efficiency in the permitting and code enforcement functions. Staff highlighted the importance of encouraging residents to utilize the online citizen portal to take advantage of these enhancements.

Supervisor Seymore expressed strong support for the new permitting system and code enforcement enhancements. He noted that the data-driven, transparent approach has been well-received by contractors and has helped expedite the permitting process, which is critical given the seasonal nature of construction in the region. Supervisor Weisinger appreciated the consistency and equity the new systems bring, ensuring all residents and developers are treated the same. She highlighted the importance of clear communication of timelines and expectations, which the new processes facilitate. Chairman Whiting acknowledged the frustrations expressed by Supervisor Benally regarding Navajo roads and offered to support her in advocating for partnerships and funding opportunities at the state and federal level, if she can provide more information on the specific needs and strategies. Overall, the Supervisors were very supportive of the improvements made to the permitting and code enforcement functions. They recognized the value of the data-driven, transparent approach in providing better service to residents and developers, while also creating a framework for consistent, equitable decision-making.

Rochelle Lacapa gave a presentation on Guidelines for Successful Transportation Partnerships. Ms. Lacapa covered Characteristics of Successful Partnership projects: 1. Budget falls within the $100,000 procurement threshold or is within the county's approved budget capacity; 2. Clearly defined scope of work, including cost estimates and documentation; 3. Achievable projects within the procurement timelines, with projects over $100,000 requiring an earlier start; Eligible Transportation Partnership Projects: 1. Public roadway projects, including gravel, culverts, storm drains, roadway signing and striping. 2. Surface transportation-related projects on public roadways, such as box culverts, low-flow crossings, and impact attenuation devices; Ineligible Transportation Partnership Projects: 1. Utility work, private driveways, non-public roadways, assistance for funeral services, fuel tank purchases, and equipment/materials not exclusively used for transportation. Ms. Lacapa further described the types of non-construction partnership projects which can be highly visible projects with immediate impact and lower visibility projects with long timelines. Ms. Lacapa gave information on grant opportunities focusing on how the County can support tribal entities as co-applicants for AZ SMART grants by providing letters of support for grant applications to demonstrate partnership. Lastly, Ms. Lacapa discussed Intergovernmental Agreements, indicating that all transportation partnership projects require an IGA with the lead agency, which can include the Navajo Nation Department of Transportation, tribal transportation departments, ADOT, and incorporated cities/towns. The presentation emphasized the importance of building strong relationships with partner entities, clear communication, and aligning projects with the county's budget and procurement timelines to ensure successful transportation partnerships.

Supervisor Whitesinger expressed that the presentation provided a helpful framework and template for understanding the process and requirements around transportation partnerships. She noted that this clarity will be valuable in communicating with partners, especially when there are changes in elected officials or staff. Supervisor Seymore commented that the visual aids and information presented were very useful, as they help establish a common understanding of how the county can legally and effectively engage in these partnerships. He appreciated the delineation between high-visibility, immediate impact projects versus longer-term, lower-visibility initiatives. Chairman Whiting acknowledged the importance of the relationships and communication emphasized in the presentation. He offered to be supportive and echo the county's voice when Supervisors are advocating for partnerships and funding opportunities with state and federal entities, if provided more information on specific needs and strategies. Overall, the Supervisors seemed to appreciate the comprehensive framework outlined in the presentation. They recognized the value in having a clear, standardized approach to transportation partnerships that balances the county's obligations, budget constraints, and the need for visible, impactful projects for constituents. The Supervisors expressed a desire to utilize this framework moving forward in their discussions and collaborations with partner agencies.
