a.

Consideration of a Proclamation, proclaiming August 14, 2024, as "Navajo Code Talkers Day" :
Supervisor Alberto Peshlakai stated that Navajo Code Talkers Day will take place on August 14, 2024. He indicated that a custom within the Navajo Nation is that they recognize the day as a Tribal holiday and that all of their offices will be closed.  He stated that District II is the home to 4 of the original Navajo Code Talkers, as well as further up north.  He shared his gratitude for their assistance when the U.S. Government called upon them to provide an undecipherable code during the war.  He read the proposed proclamation.

9:04 a.m. Dawnafe Whitesinger, Supervisor District V, present.

Supervisor Whitesinger shared her gratitude to Supervisor Peshlakai for presenting this item.  She stated that she is grateful to observe the heroic men who served our county and nation in those across the Navajo Nation who stand with pride knowing that the men fought for our country.

Supervisor Peshlakai made a motion  to approve a Proclamation, proclaiming August 14, 2024, as "Navajo Code Talkers Day", motion seconded by  Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

TREASURER :

a.

Consideration and approval of the following items: Receipt of the Annual Report of Collections and Delinquencies in accordance with A.R.S. §42-18002; Settlement of Accounts; and Request for Authorization to Invest Funds Pursuant to A.R.S. §35-327(G) :
Kari Lopez shared her appreciation to her staff for everything that they do on a daily basis and the level of support they give everyone that comes to their office.  She stated that they are the most kind and supportive staff and that they are always willing to help the taxpayers and anyone in the county.  A presentation was shown.  Ms. Lopez spoke about the real property tax roll corrections and collections in accordance with ARS 42-18002, indicating that they collected approximately ninety million, leaving approximately two million in uncollected 2023 real property taxes, but that they are still collecting on the outstanding taxes.  She stated that if she would have included the unsecured into the levy amount is over 94 million and the outstanding taxes as of June 30, 2024, would've been 2.5 million in outstanding taxes that they are still collecting today.  She spoke about the numbers for each taxing entity.  She stated that they collected approximately 7.5 million for the 2023 real property taxes, leaving an outstanding balance to be collected of approximately $189,000.  She spoke about the County investments, indicating, as required by ARS 35-327(G), she requested authorization to invest and reinvest county school and special district funds as necessary for the 2024-2025 fiscal year.  She stated that she would use the same objectives when making investments, safety before liquidity before yield.  She indicated that as of June 30, 2024, the county had approximately $400 million invested in short-term investments and securities and that the previous fiscal year they had over $360 million invested. She spoke about the interest earned on investments, indicating that of June 30, 2024, they have earned over 12 million, and combining savings and securities, the previous fiscal year interest earned was a little over $7.5 million.  She noted that interest bounced back after COVID, that significant gains were made over time and that Navajo County has never made as much interest.  She stated that all securities are reviewed each year by the State Auditor's Office with no findings, that the portfolio is safe, it's liquid with high yield and in compliance with Arizona Revised Statutes.  She indicated that liquidity is a major part of knowing what's happening in the county for cash flow needs and that they keep investments until they mature.  She stated that they always have enough case on hand to meet the needs of the county, including the schools and the fire districts.  She spoke about the Treasurer's Processing Fund, indicating that according to ARS 11-495(C), the County Treasurer shall annually submit to the Board of Supervisors the amount of anticipated revenues.  She stated that the fund revenues for FY24-25 are estimated to be $10,000 based on the estimated sale of tax lien certificates of $1 million, noting that the calculation comes from the real property outstanding collection amounts.  She requested approval of the annual report of collections and delinquencies in accordance with ARS 42-18002 and settlement of the accounts with approval to invest and reinvest funds pursuant to ARS 35-327(G).

Supervisor Seymore thanked Ms. Lopez for the great job she has done, indicating the investments over the past couple of years have been very meaningful.  He shared his appreciation for her stewardship, indicating that the residences appreciate it as well.  He thanked the Treasurer's Office staff for the great job that they do.
Supervisor Whitesinger shared her appreciation to Ms. Lopez and her staff for the work that they do to serve the communities.
Chairman Whiting thanked Ms. Lopez and her staff for the work they do and for the report.  He shared his appreciation for the level of communication that they have.

Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion  to approve Receipt of the Annual Report of Collections and Delinquencies in accordance with A.R.S. §42-18002; Settlement of Accounts; and Request for Authorization to Invest Funds Pursuant to A.R.S. §35-327(G), motion seconded by  Supervisor Seymore .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Consideration and possible adoption of Resolution Number 34-2024 , extinguishing a 30-foot public ingress and egress easement over two properties in Section 15, Township 11 North, Range 19 East of the Gila and Salt River Base and Meridian Recorded on Fee#2018-9280 :
Bryan Cook stated that Resolution 34-2024 is for extinguishment of a 30-foot ingress and egress over two properties.  He spoke about the location noting that the easement was established and dedicated to the public, but the current property owner and applicant wish to extinguish the public easement.  He stated that in different surveys another easement was granted for the public and that the current access is no longer needed.  He noted that no properties are land locked and that there was no homeowners' association to work with.  He stated that the public easement was originally established as part of a minor land subdivision, that there have been many ideas for the property, that it has been recombined and is no longer needed.  He stated that notification was sent to all property owners within a three-hundred-foot radius.  He indicated that Navapache contacted them regarding interest in the utility easement and that the utility easement is not affected by the extinguishment.  He stated that no additional responses have been received other than the support from the two current property owners.  He requested approval for the extinguishment of the public easement.

Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Cook for working with them on this and finding a resolution that was workable.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to adopt  Resolution Number 34-2024 , extinguishing a 30-foot public ingress and egress easement over two properties in Section 15, Township 11 North, Range 19 East of the Gila and Salt River Base and Meridian Recorded on Fee#2018-9280, motion seconded by  Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Consideration of approval of an Intergovernmental Agreement with the State of Arizona, Department of Transportation for Redhead St; Crandell Rd-Capps Rd & Porter St; 5th Ave to 2nd Ave, sidewalk and bicycle path improvement design :
Madhav Mundle stated that this IGA is between Arizona Department of Transportation and Navajo County.  He indicated that Navajo County has received funding through AZ Smart Funds for a project in Heber-Overgaard, which is in District IV.  He stated that the scope of the project is to design sidewalks and multi-use paths adjacent to two of the schools, that it will not be for all of the streets, and they will talk with school district to see which are most suitable streets.  He indicated that ADOT will administer design of the project, that the County intends to administer the construction of the project, and that they will pursue a federal grant for the construction of the site.  He stated that the grant amount if $170,000 and per the agreement $30,000 will be Project Administration Fee by ADOT since they will administer the project on behalf of the County.  He stated that staff recommends approval.

Supervisor Seymore thanked Mr. Mundle and staff for reaching out for the grant.  He stated that it will be good a great asset for the community.  He requested that staff double check if the street is Redhead or Reidhead.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to approve an Intergovernmental Agreement with the State of Arizona, Department of Transportation for Redhead St; Crandell Rd-Capps Rd & Porter St; 5th Ave to 2nd Ave, sidewalk and bicycle path improvement design, motion seconded by  Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

NAVAJO COUNTY FLOOD CONTROL DISTRICT: Board of Directors Session :

REGULAR AGENDA :

a.
Consideration of approval of a Professional Services contract with JE Fuller Hydrology & Geomorphology for Navajo County ALERT System Maintenance and Improvements for 2024 in the amount of $140,000.00 :
Julianna Davis stated that the Flood Control District is asking for approval for the Navajo County Alert System Maintenance and Improvement for 2024 in the amount of $140,000.  She indicated that the County participates in statewide rain gage system called the alert system, which provides an early warning system for flooding potential.  She stated that each of the thirty gages include the measuring apparatus, sources of power, transmitter and antenna.  She indicated that this also includes the Winslow Levee siren.  She advised that the scope of work includes the routine preventative maintenance for the alert system, supplies, base station server upgrades and data acquisition software deployment, as well as repairs at the dam for replacing the cable and conduit for the gate sensor.  She stated that staff recommends approval.

Chairman Whiting shared his appreciation for the work on this, indicating that it is important to the citizens and provides awareness of the flow of water and how things are going.

Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion  to approve a Professional Services contract with JE Fuller Hydrology & Geomorphology for Navajo County ALERT System Maintenance and Improvements for 2024 in the amount of $140,000.00, motion seconded by  Vice-Chair Benally .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Chairman Whiting read the opening script for Call to the Public.
Donna Shurwin, Pinetop, spoke about elections and requested an agenda item or a debriefing with Byan Layton, the Supervisors and Elections staff to assess what took place in the Primary Election.  She stated that she worked as a Poll Worker in Whiteriver, that there were several things that did not run smoothly, and that she feels there is a gap between the Supervisors and staff.  She stated that citizens that work in the polls dedicate a lot of time to be there and that it takes a lot.  She indicated that they have brought many issues to the Board, that the Board has listened and that there have been a few changes, such as removing the tabulating machines from the voting centers, but that there is still a lot of room for error.  She spoke about the drop box in Whiteriver, indicating that the ballots were picked up by a helper from the Recorder's Office but that there was no chain of custody, that there was supposed to be a person from each major party present plus a third person to hand it off to and that there were no cameras for the drop box.  She stated that there were people that were told that they had already voted even though the people said they had not voted, which has happened previously.  She indicated that the mail-in votes are falling into the wrong hands.  She stated that a debriefing would be beneficial to those that are concerned from both parties.  She indicated that they feel there are things that need to be addressed and that when it is not being addressed, they feel disrespected and that their voice does not matter.  She spoke about the Elections Procedure Manuel, indicating that it does not follow the statute, that it is embellished, that things have been added to it and that the manual is not correct.  She stated that she was present for the electronic adjudication the Saturday following the election.
Belinda Quog, Show Low, stated that she agrees with the request for a debriefing session.  She reported on observances she made at the Pinon polling location.  She indicated that when they were setting up the night before elections, they found an election programming zip drive in one of the express voting machines and that the Inspector took it and pocketed the drive.  She stated that there was electioneering going on over the seventy-five-foot line with loudspeakers and that it was very disruptive.  She talked about new voters, who wanted to register, being persuaded to vote for one particular party, which is not ethical or legal.  She indicated that the poll pad had voter as a Democrat and that they insisted that they were registered as an Independent, that he didn't vote in the PPE because he didn't want to change his party affiliation and that he was told to re-register.  She stated that another voter noticed that their date of birth was changed and their address from ten years ago was active, but that when they voted at the PPE all the information had been correct.  She stated that the poll workers were told by a county representative that the provision votes would not count.  She indicated that over 60 voters were told they had already voted at the poll pads, that some of them had traveled a long way and that they were confused because they knew where their ballot was at home and that most of them were middle-aged and educated.  She stated that there was a report from Indian Wells that 30 – 35 percent of voters were turned away due to being told that they had already voted.  She spoke about previously addressing the Board regarding issues of voters being turned away during the PPE, noting that it doesn't seem possible that the volume of people
