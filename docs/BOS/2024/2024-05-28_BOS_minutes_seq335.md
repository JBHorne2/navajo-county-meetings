Presentation/Summary of Navajo County's Fiscal Year 2023 Annual Financial Report, Report on Internal Control and on Compliance, and Federal Single Audit Report :
Jayson Vowell introduced the auditors, Don Bohart and Nikolas
Rabayda, and stated that as required by statute, the auditors are required to come to the Board and present the financial status of the financial reporting.
A presentation was shown. Don Bohart, Audit Manager on the Fiscal Year 2023 audits, spoke about the Auditor General's Office and what they do.  He stated that they will be discussing three required annual reports, the Annual Comprehensive Financial Report, the Report on Internal Control and Compliance and the Federal Single Audit Report.  He indicated that for the first report, the County's Annual Comprehensive Financial Report, where the audit objective is to obtain reasonable assurance about the financial statements as a whole are free from material misstatements due to fraud or error and to issue a report that includes their opinion, for FY23 they have reported an unmodified or clean opinion, which means the County's financial statements are reliable.  He stated the second report on Internal Control and Compliance is where they report any findings and recommendations results from the audit of the County's Financial Statements, indicating that three findings were reported, which they will review in detail.  He indicated that the third report, the Federal Single Aduit Report, includes their assessment of the County's compliance with federal program revenues, the requirements over each federal program, and the County's schedule of federal expenditures, they reported one finding, which will be discussed in detail.  He stated that reasonable assurance is a high but not absolute level of assurance, that the audits are performed following governmental auditing standards and federal uniform guidance but that there's no guarantee that they will always detect material misstatements or non-compliance.  He indicated that there was no fraud identified in the FY23 reports of the County.  He spoke about the highlights that were included, including the County's largest primary revenue source and how the County used the money over the past ten years.   He reviewed the main sources of revenue, including federal and state grants and programs, shared state sales taxes, county sales taxes and county property taxes and spoke about the changes in those over the past five years.  He further reviewed the four primary expenses, including general government, public safety, highways and streets and health and welfare and talked about the changes in those over the past five years.  He stated that the County's overall net position increased by $10.9 million is FY23, noting that not all of the net position is spendable at the end of FY23 due to investments in capital assets and restrictions for specific uses such as highways/streets and medical costs, resulting in a negative or deficit unrestricted balance due to the county's pension and OPEB obligations.  He spoke about the highlights for the federal expenditures for the past five years, indicating that most are from the Department of Treasury and Department of Health & Human Services as a result of COVID grants and that while the county continues to receive some money associated with COVID it is substantially less that prior years.
Nicholas Rabayda noted that the County has corrected four of the reported findings from the previous fiscal year and that the County's FY22 initial financial statement submitted for audit consideration contained a material misstatement but that they didn't identify any material misstatements during the FY23 audit.  He stated that in the report on Internal Controls and Compliance, they reported three financial statement findings in the report as follows.  The first finding was regarding deficiencies in the County's purchasing fuel card process, and an unallowable alcoholic beverage purchase, which has since been reimbursed.  He reviewed the recommendations including that the county ensure that all employees follow county policies by improving its procedures over purchasing card fuel purchases, conduct an in-depth review of employees prior to fuel purchases, and follow existing county policy which requires the purchasing card approver to review all transactions and investigate and report misuse.  He stated that the County concurs with the finding and plans to implement the recommendations by June 30, 2024.  The second finding identified deficiencies in the county process for risk management and documenting its information technology risks, which includes identifying, analyzing and respond to the county-wide information or IT risks and not including classifying and inventory sensitive information that may need stronger access and security controls.  He reviewed the recommendation including a plan for where to allocate resources and where to implement critical controls, perform an annual entity-wide IT risk assessment that includes evaluating and documenting risks and safeguards, and evaluate and manage the risks of holding sensitive information by classifying and inventorying information.  The third finding identified deficiencies in the County's control over its systems and data.  He stated that they recommend that the county continues to implement comprehensive IT policies and procedures and develop process to ensure the procedures being constantly followed to restrict access to its IT system and manage system configurations and changes.  He stated that the County concurs with both of those findings and plans to fully correct them by June 30, 2025.  As to the Single Audit Report, he stated that they audited four federal programs, and indicated that as to one of the programs they reported a federal compliance finding related to WIOA.  He reviewed the finding, stating that the department failed to ensure that it spent the required twenty percent of program dollars that were earmarked to provide in school and out of school youth paid and unpaid work experience during the period of April 2021 to June 2023. He stated that they recommendation is that the department spend no less than the required twenty percent of the youth activities money on providing youth with paid and unpaid work experience, implement strategies to recruit and retain in-school and out-of-school youth, monitor both the county and its subrecipients paid and unpaid work experience throughout the award period and adjust spending to meet the earmarked requirement if work experience participation is lower than expected.
Mr. Vowell stated that Navajo County was the first to issue their financial report for FY23 and that out of fifteen counties only two counties made the deadline.  He stated that the financial report is a consolidated financial report that is submitted to the Government Finance Officers Association for their Certificate of Achievement Program, indicating that they have done this consistently over the years and been awarded that.   He shared his appreciation to the Auditors General Office for being able to work with them, indicating that it is helpful for being able to identify opportunities for improvement.
Chairman Whiting thanked the Auditors for the partnership and talked about the importance of having the audit.  He referred to the comment made regarding fraud not being found but indicated that the findings are to help prevent mis happenings from being able to occur.  He spoke of the importance for the continued teamwork and collaboration to try and find solutions so that they can make sure they are doing everything correctly and that it sounds like there are plans in place to make sure it continues to move forward in a positive way.

Presentation regarding the current wildfire conditions, restrictions status and preparedness information :
A presentation was shown. Catrina Jenkins stated that they use the outlooks each year as a tool and talked about the outlook over the next few months, indicating that wildfire is always a threat when you live in the wildland urban interface.  She stated that the White Mountain Fire Restrictions Coordination Group is made up on coordinating agencies from various levels across the region, that there are subject matter experts on the committee that evaluated data-based information each week and the science provided data provides the information of whether or not they will go into fire restrictions.  She spoke about the Ready, Set, Go program, indicating that Ready, Set, Go is the language all fifteen counties have adopted that goes into preparedness information as well as evacuations.  She stated that you can be prepared by having your alert system sign up completed, having your property Firewise and defensible space made and that your communications, evacuation, and financial emergency plans are up to date.  She indicated that everybody needing to be signed up for the Navajo County Sheriffs Office app and the Ready Navajo County Alert System, indicating that these are areas where you can get vetted official information about incidents and emergencies in and around Navajo County.  She talked about the Set stage, indicating that this is when there is something going on in your neighborhood and you are paying attention to the vetted sources of information, that you have your kit to your door or vehicle and if extra time is necessary, you could leave the area if you think there will be an evacuation.  She stated if an evacuation is necessary, they send the Go message, indicating that you want to leave immediately because you may not get a second alert and that it's important to follow the instructions received when you get this alert.  She stated that if someone chooses not to leave, they will not be allowed to leave the property for any reason and that emergency services may not be available.   She spoke about how most wildfires are created by humans and the ways to prevent starting a wildfire, such as using an ashtray when smoking, not dragging chains, maintaining the undercarriage of your vehicle, and putting out campfires.  She spoke about creating a defensible space by cleaning out getters and rooftops and putting reflective numbers on your house.
Chief Deputy Brian Swanty stated that this is a non-stop process each year.  He talked about stated that Ms. Jenkins does a great job each year getting everyone organized and scheduling the weekly meetings to discuss fire restrictions and that they have a fantastic team when it comes to wildfire season.  He stated that communication that is relayed to the public is critical and that many people get bad information that is not vetted, that there is a Public Information Group that shares information on different sites and recommended that citizens get the information from a vetted source.
Assistant Chief Conrad Loney, with Timber Mesa Fire, talked about the area that Timber Mesa covers and how many residents they service.  He stated that they have a strong relationship and collaborative efforts with local partners.  He stated that wildland fire still remains the number one threat to the life and property of the people in the White Mountains.  He talked about not becoming complacent and challenged community members to engage in proactive efforts to be prepared.  He talked about being Firewise, and about having a defensible area around your house.  He stated that they will be holding the White Mountain Communities Annual Firewise Block Party on Saturday, July 13, 2024, from 10:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m. at the Blue Ridge Elementary School.
Fire Marshall JD Pepper, Division Chief Fire Marshall Timber Mesa Fire District, shared his appreciation to Navajo County Emergency Management, Navajo County Sheriff's Office and Planning and Zoning and talked about the cooperation efforts with each of the departments.  He stated that his job is to assess threats to the public and communities and to respond to and mitigate those threats.  He stated that they recently conducted some Basal Scoring Tests, which is a way or scoring the density of woody biomass, within the City of Show Low and talked about the results as well as mitigation efforts that can be taken.
Chief Randy Chevalier, with Timber Mesa, thanked the board for support from the County on a regular basis.  He stated that wildfires are a growing problem and that everyone is going to have to band together to figure out how to solve it.  He about wildfire risks in their area and risks that occur at citizens houses that create challenges for emergency response teams.
Supervisor Seymore spoke about how everyone has been effected by fire risks and increase in insurance premiums due to the fire risks.  He stated that we each need to do what we can because of the amount it costs.  He stated that Emergency Management does have grants to help mitigate citizens property, so it is not all out of pocket.
Ms. Jenkins stated that the Grant Coordinator in her office takes care of the Health Forest Initiative Grants, that they go out and do an assessment and that citizens can get on the grant list if they choose to and that homeowners can be reimbursed a percentage of the cost, they pay to have mitigation work done.

Chief Chevalier talked about being Firewise approved and still having insurance rates go up or dropped.  He indicated that there is a statewide coalition across the state to work on finding a solution, indicating that it won't be a short-term fix, that it will be a long process.  He stated that you still need to make the effort to be fire wise and to help your neighbors as well.
Supervisor Whitesinger thanked them for their work on the initiative.  She stated that the partnership is important and thanked them for investing their time.
