Presentation and update on the Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment Program (BEAD) Challenge Process :
Grant Evans stated that in January the Board approved a contract with High Street to help with the BEAD challenge process.  He indicated that the consultants will present on what they have been doing to date as well as some of the things moving forward.  He stated that they have been doing a lot of mapping as well as data analysis and that they will show some of the results from that.  He indicated that they have been working with the State Broadband Office and the ACA over the past couple of months to make sure this process aligns with the way the state is going to execute their program.  He stated that they will cover in detail what is critical to the success of the public outreach campaign that will launch today.  He further stated that there are approximately 44,000 residences in Navajo County, off Tribal land, that are currently broadband serviceable locations and that approximately 37,000 are not eligible to receive any of the funding but that the challenge process provides an opportunity to take a deeper look into those areas and see if there are inaccuracies or overstatement on the service level those residents are getting.  He indicated that they will be sending postcards to the 37,000 residences and that public outreach is important to get the word to all residents to be watching for the postcards and get educated about the program.  He stated that they will be doing a press release and some social media campaigns starting today to get the word out.  He advised that they do have a BEAD Task Force in Navajo County, which consists of the partner Cities and Towns and NPC.  He talked about the outreach that they have done to date.
Mark Eggy and Amy Link, with Highstreet, appearing virtual, showed a presentation.  Mr. Eggy stated that the Federal Government is making an investment in broadband infrastructure with the goal of connecting every location in America to high-speed high quality broadband internet.  He talked about the goal to maximize the broadband in Navajo County, indicating that they want to make sure every eligible location is identified.  He stated that there is approximately a billion dollars in funding for Arizona that will be allocated to locations in priority locations that are unserved, underserved and community institutions.  He stated that there are approximately 44,000 locations that can and should be connected, that 7,369 are currently eligible for funding through the program and the remaining 37,000 are considered ineligible.  He further stated that according to the national maps 32,000 of those are considered to have access to high-speed internet and that the additional 5,000 that are covered under the Rural Digital Opportunity grant funding program that should establish high-speed access in the coming years.  He indicated that there are four providers doing business in Navajo County currently that are considered to meet federal standards for providing qualifying high-speed high quality broadband internet, that some areas have a choice between providers but that most locations only have one option.  He stated that through this process part of their job is to make sure those who don't have a choice are still getting good service.  He reviewed all the maps for different areas talking about the locations that show they are covered, those that are not covered and those that are not included due to being funded by other grants.  He talked about anchor institutions being identified to make sure they are eligible for future funding.  He talked about the challenge process, stating that the process allows an opportunity to correct any inaccuracies in the State map.  He further talked about the different kind of challenges: enforceable commitment, which is areas covered by another grant; availability, which is when the provider reports that high speed internet is available but the consumer is unable to subscribe; speed, which is when consumers are able to subscribe but are unable to obtain the advertised speeds and community anchor institutions, which are missing entities that match state  CAI definition.  He stated that with the community institutions any that are missing from the map can be added to statewide funding so that in the final stages of the grom they would be eligible if there is money left after all of the unserved and underserved locations are connected.
Ms. Link talked about the outreach and engagement, indicating that they will be sending out postcards to 37,000 locations to verify they have the high-speed internet or that they are able to receive it.  She talked about postcards, stating that you can either scan the QR code or go to the website.  She indicated that they want everyone on the same website and that from there it will direct everyone according to the type of challenge to make it as easy as possible.  She talked about the website, the information that it will provide, and the options provided depending on the type of challenge that applies to the location.  She stated that for the speed challenge they will have to take the test three times on different days to make sure it's an accurate representation of the performance.  She talked about the need to get the word out, the press release that will be done, the social media posts, and advised that the post cards will start arriving about a week from today.  She stated that you don't have to have a post card to respond, that you can just go to the website.  She reviewed the timeline for the challenge process, indicating that they need all of the information to be collected by May 15, 2024.  She stated that they would like to have everyone start as soon as possible due to having the three-day time period for doing the speed challenge.  She further stated that they hope to be able to track where they get the responses from so they can do some additional follow-up for the areas with a low response.
Supervisor Whitesinger inquired if the website is active now and how those who are having issues with internet would be able to access the website?
Mr. Evans stated that the website is live.  He further stated that if the areas where they are having issues to connect are already known, they are already eligible for funding, but that if their property is being overstated, they will need a speed test to correct that and that the speed test will have to be taken at the address to validate it.
A discussion was held regarding how to get people to participate in the challenge process that has internet but that it is slow or limited.
Vice-Chairwoman Benally inquired if this is just for those with internet, indicating that in the northern part of District One there are individuals without internet or are in blackout areas.
Mr. Evans stated that would be part of the availability challenge, indicating if you have been denied service or don't have service at all you can post that evidence on the website.
Supervisor Whitesinger inquired what the participation of tribes looks like.
Mr. Evans stated that he doesn't know how the tribes are handling the process, that they are eligible for funding, and that the tribal communities can navigate to the state broadband office to take the speed test or availability challenges.

Cody Cooper stated that last year Planning and Development went out for an RFP for a full update of their comprehensive plan, indicating that they have only had limited updates since 2004.  He further stated that Logan Simpson Design was selected, and he talked about the work they have been doing to date.
Terry Hogan, Project Manager with Logan Simpson Design, talked the Consultant team consisting of individuals from Logan Simpson as the Lead Consultant, Kimley Horn to support Transportation and Water Resources and Elliot D. Pollack and Company to support Housing and Economic Growth and the Navajo County team.  She stated that Comprehensive Plans are required for all Arizona counties according to Title 11 and that they are required to have certain elements and topics as well as public involvement and that they must be adopted or readopted every ten years.  She indicated that the Navajo County plan is in conformance with statute since it has been readopted over the years, but that it hasn't had any major updates since the original adoption in 2004.  She stated that the purpose of the plan is to guide the adjusted harmonious development of the area pursuant to present and future needs, to conserve natural resources of the county, ensure efficient expenditure of public monies and to promote public health, safety, and welfare.  She reviewed the planning hierarchy pyramid, stating that the comprehensive plan is the foundation of all planning tools and that as you go up the pyramid you get more specific in your regulations or plans.  She talked about the topics that will be covered, stating that the statute requires all comprehensive plans include foundational elements of land use and transportation and that as population increases over 125,000 there are more required elements.  She talked about the future land use map that the county currently has, stating that it shows future land use designations of what the community has aspired for the area to look like in the future.  She further talked about the process for determining goals and policies and the implementation plan.  She reviewed the project schedule, indicating that it is a twelve-month process.  She stated that the public engagement process started in February and will go through December to allow for the community input of whether they are hitting the mark or not.  She indicated that at the end of the adopting plan process there will be a sixty-day review where they will send the draft plan to everyone that has been involved in the process for comments and then they will come back through process prior to going to the Board for final adoption.  She talked about where they are in the process currently and the work that they are currently doing.  She reviewed the engagement strategies for public participation, which is required by statute, indicating that they will have opportunity for written comment, public hearings with effective notes and open discussions, communications, dialogues, and consideration of the public comments. She talked about the project branding, indicating that they are following the style guide.  She further talked about the Comprehensive Plan Commission, which consists of entities from the federal level, the state level and different county entities and departments, noting that they will meet three times during the process.  She stated that they will be doing community meetings or community interviews, which will be one on ones or focus groups, stating that they work working on getting contacts for different entities and that if there is any interest in being a part of this, they can contact Cody Cooper.  She indicated that they want as much input as possible for this plan and that they will be holding different meetings.  She stated that they will be providing updates to the Planning Commission and the Board through the process.  She indicated that the project website is up and talked about the information that will be on the website, as well as other outreach strategies.  She reviewed the next steps and what will be done over the next several months.
