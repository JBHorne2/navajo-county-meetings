Consideration of award of Contract No. B23-09-021 for the Navajo County Complex Remodel to Pointe Companies, Inc and to authorize change order authority to the County Manager for 10% of the contract amount, and authorize the County Manager to execute the necessary documents :

Including the restrooms, kitchen, and employee breakroom.  He indicated that there were three challenges identified that they wanted to address, including difficult customer access to services, the current lack of entry restrictions, the lack of training space and the aging of restroom facilities.  He talked about the objectives of the project, including simplify and improve customer access to the Recorder/Voter Registration, the Assessor, the Treasurer, and the Board of Supervisors/Clerk of the Board; improve safety and security by strategically locating new badge access doors and access points to restrict access; create a more functional training space and remodel/update restrooms to improve functionality and include ADA compliance.  He reviewed the floor plan, addressing the areas where changes will be made for customer access, the badge access points that will be added, the training space, the breakroom, and the remodeled restrooms.  He talked about the budget for the project, indicating that $150,000 are from the APRA allocation, $150,000 General Funds and $211,500 with come from the Local Assistance Consistency Fund.  He reviewed the projected schedule, stating that this is a six-month contract.  He talked about aging and deteriorating sewer pipes and indicated that they will be doing a separate contract for a cured-in-place pipe lining, which will allow them to not have to cut up the floor and will be done at the same time.  He talked about the post construction item that will need to be completed but are not be in this contract, including securing counters, refresh the training space, breakroom, and kitchen area, indicating that it will mostly likely be done inhouse.  He stated that they received one bid for this project, that it is a local contractor, and that staff is recommending approval of the item.

Consideration of an Intergovernmental Agreement with the State of Arizona, Department of Transportation for Concho Highway, Golden Horse Ln - White Antelope Rd Pavement Rehabilitation :
Madhav Mundle stated that the HURF Exchange Program was created by the State Legislature in 1997, that it had been put on hold and that the program is now back.  He indicated that this program authorizes ADOT to exchange state highway funds for federal surface transportation funds, which allows local governments to utilize HURF funds and build projects faster without out needing to comply with time consuming and expensive federal requirements.  He stated that the program is offered at ADOT's discretion, that Navajo County is eligible for it in 2024, so Public Works applied for it.  He stated that the funds will be applied to a District Three project for payment preservation on Concho Highway.  He talked about the location of the project, indicating that it will be approximately three miles, and that they will get a little over seven-hundred-fifty-five thousand dollars as part of the HURF Exchange and that they will also be using some of the Navajo County HURF funds to make the project happen.  He stated that staff recommends approval.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and possible adoption of  Resolution Number 10-2024 , approving/denying text amendment to Article 22, Sections 2202, 2203, and 2207, regarding recreational vehicle placement :
A presentation was shown. Cody Cooper stated that this is a proposed amendment to the Recreational Vehicle Placement section of the Zoning Ordinance.  He indicated that this is not an amendment to the actual placement but to clarify short term occupancy and storage versus general placement, due to reports from Code Enforcement regarding complaints.  He reviewed the proposed changes and stated that this will hopefully help for those that have multi-family properties from being reported for storing their RV's.  He stated that staff, as well as the Planning and Zoning Commission, recommend approval. He indicated that staff have not received any comments from the public.
Chairman Whiting indicated that this is not changing anything, that it's just clarifying.
Mr. Cooper stated that it's just clarify the language by adding definitions and clarifying which zones are appropriate.
Supervisor Seymore inquired if this is storage or living in an RV.
Mr. Cooper stated that the short-term occupancy is staying in an RV, which is once per calendar year for a period not to exceed thirty-days, which is already in the ordinance, but that they are clarifying that to say within the multifamily you can't do short term occupancy, but you can do the storage.  He stated that it's a little of both, but just clarifying which one is allowed in what area.
A discussion was held regarding the different situations that would be acceptable for the proposed ordinance.
Supervisor Whitesinger talked about how developing appropriate language to help define specific usage is really important.  She thanked the Department for taking the steps to specifically state some of the clarifications.
Mr. Cooper stated that they appreciate the support the Board has had with code compliance so they're able to work with staff to help refine the ordinances to make it easier for the public to understand.

Chairman Whiting opened the meeting for public hearing.  There being no comments, the public hearing was closed.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to adopt  Resolution Number 10-2024 , approving text amendment to Article 22, Sections 2202, 2203, and 2207, regarding recreational vehicle placement, motion seconded by  Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

2.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and possible adoption of  Resolution Number 11-2024 , approving/denying text amendment to Article 29, Section 2907, regarding citizen review :
A presentation was shown. Cody Cooper stated that this section is on citizen review.  He talked about the history, indicating that the statutes require that when there is a zone change that a citizen review process occur, that Navajo County does require applicants to mail out or host a meeting to notify the public ahead of a hearing but that the statute is limited to a zone change.  He indicated that there are a number of items that come through that are not zone changes, such as special use permits or plats, that the public want to be informed and heard on these items.  He indicated they are recommending to strike the existing citizen review and put in a process that would apply to all items that go to the Planning and Zoning Commission and then come before the Board.  He indicated that this would allow full openness and disclosure to the community regarding the projects prior to the Commission and Board meetings to see if the applicant could work with the public or staff to resolve some of the concerns prior to the meetings.   He stated that this would require a public meeting for the applicant to listen to the concerns of the citizens and talked about the requirements for the meetings.  He stated that staff works to take care of the concerns for public health and safety, but they want to expand upon that is possible with this process.  He addressed instances where the applicant could apply for a waiver.  He stated that staff recommends approval of the item and that the Planning & Zoning Commission unanimously recommend approval.
Supervisor Seymore inquired if they would be responsible for extra costs for the extra notification.
Mr. Cooper stated that the property owner would be responsible for the mailings, but that staff would be able to help with generating a list.
Supervisor Seymore indicated that he feels this is a good process by trying to address some of the potential issues and that it's proactive.

Chairman Whiting opened the meeting for public hearing.  There being no comments, the public hearing was closed.

Chairman Whiting inquired if this would increase the length of the process.
Mr. Cooper indicated that not necessarily and talked about the meetings that they already have.  He indicated that they would advise the applicant of the process at first approach.  He stated that it can be worked into the current timelines without extending the process.

Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion  to adopt  Resolution Number 11-2024 , approving text amendment to Article 29, Section 2907, regarding citizen review, motion seconded by  Supervisor Seymore .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

BUDGET WORK SESSION : Pursuant to ARS § 38-431.02 and ARS § 38-431(3), notice is hereby given to the members of the Navajo County Board of Supervisors and to the Public that a quorum of the Board of Supervisors may be present for a work session immediately following the Board of Supervisors Agenda in the Board Chambers where no official action will be taken :
Bryan Layton thanked the Board for their time today. He stated that next week staff will start budget meetings with Department Directors and Elected Officials and want to make sure we are working toward the same goals.
Jayson Vowell started the presentation by addressing the overall budget philosophy indicating that we need a balanced budget required by law, we need to have a structural balance which is making sure our recurring revenues meet or exceed our recurring expenses, and that we grow and maintain adequate reserves by having a cautious approach to growing programs and identifying one time revenues fund one time projects.
Mr. Vowell reviewed the General Fund balance. He spoke about the General Fund balance in 2014 vs today. He indicated that we finally got healthy about 2021. He spoke about carryover cash from projects that did not get completed.
Mr. Vowell went on to talk about the major budget impacts and accomplishments that have been made. He discussed the approval of the Jail District sales tax in January 2021 and also the implementation of the CARES Act funding and ARPA funding which brought in $8 million for Public Health and Public Safety in fiscal year 21 and 22. He stated that the 2020 jump was due to COVID relief. Mr. Vowell explained the growth in sales tax, indicating that people had money in their pockets from the stimulus. He stated that we have seen the growth in sales tax and continue to be responsible for those dollars. He further stated that we have continued to reduce and stabilize the property tax rate. Mr. Vowell spoke about the refinancing of the PSPRS Pension and indicated a pension contingency reserve fund of $2 million. Mr. Vowell discussed flexibility transfers from the library district and flood control district and how we were able to eliminate those. He also discussed 100% ALTCS and ACHHHS have been returned to the general fund. He stated that we used to take 50% from the general fund for those programs.
Mr. Vowell spoke about the employee benefit reserve funds. He indicated that we were able to take a million and a half from the reserve fund. He further indicated that we have not had an increase for the last 7-8 years for our employees' health care. Mr. Vowell further discussed another benefit to our employees was the market increases to employee compensation. He stated that we have been able to be competitive within the market.
Mr. Vowell went on to discuss the Property Tax rates. He spoke about FY 15, 16, 17 and then in FY20 we were able to keep that flat.
Mr. Vowell gave a property tax breakdown on the dollar. The general ones that everyone pays for is School District, Fire District and NPC. He stated that the General Fund is about 8%.
Mr. Vowell discussed the PSPRS Pension Refinancing and stated that in October of 2022 we decided to refinance our PSPRS. He stated that our debt service payment is about $1.2 million dollars. He spoke about how this has helped us and stated that we will stay our cost and budget certainty at $1.2 million dollars without the worry of it going up.
Bryan Layton spoke about the history of employee compensation. He indicated that since 2018, we have been able to give anniversary step increases, longevity adjustments, performance pay, market adjustments and COLA increases. He spoke about the minimum wage increase and provided some examples, such as a Deputy Sheriff Cadet who was hired in January 2021 making $42,742 and three years later a Deputy Sheriff II making $59,761; a Road Maintenance Equipment Operator I who made $14.81 an hour in December of 2021 is currently making $20.33 an hour as an Equipment Operator II; and an Intern in Public Works making $10.50 an hour hired in the summer of 2018, went full time in May 2019 making $40,438 and is currently a Division Manager making $76,502.
Jayson Vowell spoke about the anticipated challenges. He spoke about the housing of inmates. He stated that we were at 50 inmates and now we are at 30. He stated that the Sheriff's office depends on that money. He indicated that we see those inmate numbers turn down due to Hopi building a new jail. He indicated that the contract could go away. Mr. Vowell stated that jail medical services are right around $2 million dollars and will continue to be an expense. Mr. Vowell indicated that of course inflation will continue to go up and that we have seen a huge jump in both State Shared Sales Tax and County sales Tax.
Mr. Vowell presented available budget tools. He stated that should sales tax revenues start declining or if there are significant state cost shifts that we can't adapt to that we do have several tools or levers at our disposal to help us absorb new costs or provide relief until revenues improve. He spoke about the reduction of county cost of county contribution to healthcare with no impact to service. He stated that we can freeze or slow capital replacements and vacancies. He further stated that we can freeze or slow one-time projects and payments. He stated that we could also spend down some financial reserves.
Mr. Layton discussed the existing and proposed fiscal year 24/25 budget priorities: those being broadband, the Taylor Road Yard, the Holbrook Complex, the Heber remodel, and the south county expansion. He further spoke of the big budget priorities being on the radar is employee compensation, exploring opportunities for GIS, and other Board of Supervisors priorities that we would like to hear more about from the Board of Supervisors.
Mr. Layton discussed the options for employee compensation, indicating that COLA, performance pay, and one-time salary increases are all options. Supervisor Peshlakai asked about employees that are capped out at their salary range and how these options affect those employees.
Mr. Layton indicated that they should not be capped, that everyone gets COLA because it takes all the ranges and salaries and moves it up.  He stated that with performance pay you may not get an increase, but a onetime pay, depending where you are in the steps.
Supervisor Peshlakai spoke about a specific employee who had dual responsibilities, did receive two increases because of that, but was told that they weren't eligible to receive the additional COLA increase because of the previous increases.  He stated that they were offered employment elsewhere with additional incentives, which they took, but that they would have stayed because of the work environment with the Department and that the Department wanted to keep the employee because of their productivity.
Mr. Layton stated that this should have come to Administrations attention to be reevaluated.
Supervisor Seymore stated that he would like to see more benefits as part of compensation, like maybe offering three or four more vacation days, that doesn't cost anymore out of pocket, but that could be built in as a park to be used for vacation, grieving days, or maternity leave.
Supervisor Whitesinger inquired if a daycare facility could fall into the same scenario, indicating if there was a group of parents or caregivers that wished to forego performance pay or something to that effect.  She stated that it would support the group of employees looking at childcare.
Mr. Layton stated that it is very important and that they have looked into a daycare facility but that the cost to establish a center would be approximately 2.5 to 3 million dollars.  He indicated that there's a workgroup at NACo that is trying to address that in different ways, such as encouraging smaller units like at home daycares. He stated that he will reach out to that organization and look into it further.
Mr. Layton stated that GIS is basic map geography that you can layer and that it could be used for just about anything.  He stated that it's a really great tool to assist in planning, deploying resources and providing information to the public.  He talked about the high-level maps and indicating that most organizations share this for their zoning and that it's much more useful for developers, miners, and homeowners.
Bryan spoke of the desired zoning map vs how we currently use GIS, indicating that we don't have enough control points so the maps are not detailed and can be confusing.  He stated that they would like to bring in a consultant company who would come in and establish control points that are in more detail than we currently have and that it would take a couple of months to do. He indicated that they would like to do an RFQ to see what companies are available in the area and that they are estimating four hundred to five hundred thousand dollars. He stated that this is the first step to create a basis of network control points that we can work off of and then the sky is the limit. He indicated that we are not providing wrong information now, that it's just not detailed and that with this they could provide excellent detailed information on the website, which will help save on staff time due to calls regarding the current information.  He stated that this is a tool that we need to start using but that we are not set up to use it yet.
Mr. Layton talked about a recommended approach that he would like to look at, due to slowing growth in revenues, potential loss of revenues and potential for State cost shifts, indicating that it severely restricts the budget capacity to add new positions.  He stated that if a department has received grants that will displace some general fund dollars and keep it flat, they would work with them to be creative and find a solution.   He stated that with the capacity they do have, they would recommend prioritizing employee compensation.  He indicated that there is fund capacity which would allow for some one-time projects and if need be, they have some budget leverage to slow expenses.
Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Layton for the data regarding what is happening with the revenues and for the recap on how we are at where we are at this time.  He recognized the Department Directors and Elected Officials that are involved in the budget conservations, indicating that how those are done have changed over the years to try and find the best way to productively engage and find solutions for what works best.  He indicated that the current process that is in place seems to work well.  He stated that it has been his experience that during the budget meetings both sides try to credibly find solutions. He stated that they come in knowing what the realities are but that both sides work to see what came be done to accomplish what the departments are asking.  He indicated that it has already been vetted through and both sides try to find a way forward, which he feels is healthier.  He talked about previous years when they would come in and make a pitch and the Board would have to decide who gets what, which created winners and losers and that it was not a good relationship with the Departments and the Board of Supervisors.  He stated that the way it's done now, the intended outcome is to find a way to have a balanced budget and to meaningfully partner with each of the Department Directors and Elected Officials on ways forward.
Mr. Layton stated that they try to make things work and that communication with the Department Directors and Elected Officials has been key, before coming before the Board.
Supervisor Whiting stated that he trusts our team, that their interests are heard and that they want to be able to reward or compensate the employees who are going above and beyond. He stated that Brad Carlyon does a great job of talking about some of the responsibilities that he has and the pressure he feels to take care of the obligations and make sure that those needs are met.  He stated that it seems like it is working better now, that they are trying to partner and find solution, whereas the other process didn't allow for some of those conversations to occur.  He indicated that he wants to support them, that they have to be balanced, that the conversation has to be credible and that they have to look at it as a whole due to the responsibility for the services that have to be provided.  He stated that the feedback that they get from the upcoming budget meetings is important and appreciated.  He indicated that he hopes it has moved to a place where there is trust and where they can try and find the best pathway forward to give the departments what they need and what they have been asked for by citizens, but that they have to do that within the budget responsibilities.
Supervisor Whitesinger stated that this is a good approach, that each department knows the needs within their departments.  She further stated that for the Board to make the assumption that they know what is needed in each department is unreal and that having those conversations is important. She indicated that having a variety of capacity that could potentially meet our needs is important when looking at long term.   She talked about when looking at potential budget shortfalls years down the road, and the departments are aware of that, having these conversations and changing the relationships is really important and has seemed to become more positive. She stated that the past year budget cycle was a positive conversation in trying to best understand the organization and the department's needs.  She shared her appreciation for being able to be thoughtful, rather than being restrictive.
Mr. Layton stated that the Department Directors and Elected Officials have been fantastic and indicated that we wouldn't be where we are financially right now without their cooperation and diligence.  He further stated that they try to be creative in finding ways to make things happen.
Supervisor Peshlakai stated that the Detention Center in Hopi should be in operation by next month, that he has been part of some of their internal meetings, and that they are planning on doing their opening in March 2024.  He stated that with the presentation his only question was regarding the compensation for employees, and that he didn't know until after the fact the situation with the employee that he spoke of previously.
Supervisor Seymore stated that the greatest resources are the employees and the assets that are in the buildings.  He stated that we need to build a bigger, better stronger, and that things will be better.  He further stated that we need to always have a vision, to make sure that we know what that vision is and that we continue to look at that vision.  He indicated that we had the opportunity to make a difference with one-time funds and that we are hoping that the broadband is something that goes forward since we won't have those funds again, that we don't have access but that we have surplus.  He stated that we shouldn't put ourselves in the corner, that they shouldn't be opposed to raising a tax, if it maintains the standard.  He indicated that there are sometimes costs that are associated with standards for safety, protection and services that are provided.  He stated that they need to stay abreast of the employees because everybody's competing for good sources of employees.  He indicated that he will advocate for the compensation package to always compensate those for the services that they do.
Mr. Layton stated that these are the kind of parameters that they hoped for in this discussion and that they will work to find new solutions.  He indicated that with the Boards support these are the parameters that they can work with.
Supervisor Whiting stated that he liked the comments regarding being creative to find solutions that meet the needs of the employees.  He stated that you see it a lot in the workplace, that pay matters, but that ultimately culture matters and that people want to enjoy their experience.  He stated that he would love to see what other organizations are doing that are creative, whether it's talking about additional days off or looking at solutions for daycare needs. He stated that his experience is that the team is always innovating and looking, and he encouraged that they look at what other counties are doing to make their employees feel valued and enjoy their experience.
