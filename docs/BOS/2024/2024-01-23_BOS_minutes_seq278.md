a.

Consideration of award of Contract B23-09-029 for the Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment Program (BEAD) Challenge to High Street Consulting Group, LLC and to authorize change order authority to the County Manager for 10% of the contract amount, and authorize the County Manager to execute the necessary documents :
Grant Evans stated that the BEAD program is a new program, a one-time appropriation for broadband expansion and that it was created by the current administration in infrastructure to serve the unserved and underserved properties, businesses, and residents across the country.  He talked about the definition of unserved and underserved as defined by the FCC and indicated that the areas that have been identified as unserved or underserved are eligible for the funding.  He stated that each state will oversee their own funding which is overseen at the federal level by NTIA, that as part of the process each state is required to administer a challenge process and that the process is only open to local governments, nonprofits and internet providers to review the data and challenge, where necessary, to add in properties that should be eligible for the BEAD funding that are not currently identified.  Mr. Evans indicated that the ACA is overseeing the program for Arizona, that last October they issued their guidance for how the program will work and that around that time Navajo County formed a BEAD Task Force to capitalize on the middle mile construction program and the BEAD funds.  He stated that the Task Force is recommending that a consultant be hired for the BEAD Challenge Process.  He indicated that the goal of this contract is to assure that all properties in Navajo County are identified and accounted for except for the Reservation properties, due to not having the authority or jurisdiction to carry out the challenge process on the reservation so that will have to be led by the tribal authorities.  Mr. Evans spoke about the process and timeline, indicating that it will start March 1 through April.  He stated that they are asking for approval today so that the consultant can get started with gathering and analyzing data, as well as doing a public awareness campaign so that citizens can be aware and be active participants.  Mr. Evans talked about the partnerships, stating that the Task Force consists of all of the cities and towns within Navajo County and NPC and that they have all verbally agreed to a financial commitment, as well as from APS and SRP. He thanked Rochelle Lacapa for her assistance through the collaborations and relationships she has. He talked about a Utilities Grant Program, for communities affected by the coal plant closures, that Ken Maruyama has submitted for, indicating that they anticipate to received twenty-five-thousand dollars from that.  He stated that they have approximately $105,000 total in anticipated partner and grant contributions for this project. He talked about working with the Task Force and partners to discuss additional funding.  He talked about the RFQ process, indicating that they received seven proposals, and that High Street Consulting Group was the highest scoring respondent.  He stated that staff is recommending approval.
Supervisor Whitesinger inquired approximately how many people this will serve?
Mr. Evans stated that they have approximately twenty thousand addresses that they will be targeting and talked about the importance of the community outreach to make sure that the properties do speed tests or provide documentation that they are not offered the services that are reported.
Supervisor Seymore thanked Mr. Evans for his work and for gathering all the partnerships for this project.
Supervisor Whitesinger thanked Mr. Evans, Ms. Lacapa and all those that have helped with this project. She talked about how we sometimes take broadband for granted when we have it available but indicated that there are those who don't have the accessibility and may need it for education, medical or other purposes.

Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion  to approve award of Contract B23-09-029 for the Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment Program (BEAD) Challenge to High Street Consulting Group, LLC and to authorize change order authority to the County Manager for 10% of the contract amount and authorize the County Manager to execute the necessary documents, motion seconded by  Supervisor Seymore . Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Presentation to inform on most recent Fatality Review data :
A presentation was shown.  Alyssa Lemmon stated that she has been coordinating the county fatality reviews for the past four years, stating that the fatality reviews are conducted because many deaths and preventable.  She talked about the process that they use for the fatality review, indicating that from the nineteen child deaths in Navajo County, sixty-three percent could have been prevented.  She talked about the recommendations for prevention, indicating that they found four main categories where actions could be taken to prevent death, including pregnancy strategies, parenting strategies, school and community involvement and general service utilization.  She indicated that many of the recommendations in the categories are considered education focused, that at the Health District they have several programs providing education services identified in the reviews and talked about some of the different services including mental health for both adults and youth.  She talked about overdose deaths, indicating that they reviewed twenty-nine of the fifty-three deaths from 2021, that they are not reviewing the deaths that occur on tribal land or where tribal land is listed as the primary residence due to difficulties in receiving records from tribal entities on child deaths and the program being minimally staffed.  She talked about some of the common factors in overdose fatalities, including incarceration history, adverse childhood experience and use of more than one substance and the recommendations including, improved support, enhanced training for first responders, improve care coordination until release from incarceration and establishing emergency department discharge protocols and education.  She talked about services offered through the Health District including free Narcan/education, established community hotline and certified staff for Peer Support Specialists and ACE trainers. She stated for the Suicide Mortality, they did eighteen of forty-three reviews, indicating that for the same reasons mentioned previously they did not review tribal deaths.  She talked about common factors, including adverse childhood experience, some form of crisis within two weeks of the death, suicidal feelings, thoughts, intentions or plans within a month of the death, signs/symptoms of behavioral health challenges or alcohol consumption prior to death.  She further talked about the recommendations including increased access to mental health services, crisis response teams, improved support for childhood traumas, social connection, improved training for healthcare and criminal justice staff.  She talked about services offered by the Health District, including certified staff as mental health first aid and ACE trainers, mental health first aid and ACE classes, free gun locks and a veterans resource guide. She talked about barriers that have been identified to the review process and community concerns.  She reviewed ways that people can provide support, indicating people can participate with the community health improvement plan workgroups and listen to those with lived experiences and in recovery.  She stated that the annual Fatality Review Reports can be found on the county website.
Supervisor Seymore indicated that the data is great information, indicating that unless you know where you're at you don't know where to make improvements.  He talked about the Reaching Rural program bringing things before the Board that can help.  He talked about how he reached out to the schools to be able to share information to help break some of the barriers through education. He thanked Ms. Lemmons and her team for the work they have done on this.
Supervisor Whiting shared his appreciation for the presentation and the hard work they have done.  He spoke about the importance of being aware of this topic.  He indicated that it is not always that people are unwilling but that there are a lot of misconceptions and that hopefully we can have the hard conversations and look for solutions moving forward.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to return to regular session, motion seconded by  Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

PUBLIC HEARING:  Consideration and possible adoption of Resolution Number 03-2024, approving/denying a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction of a 105' monopole communication tower and associated facilities located north of the Winslow area :
Tyler Richards stated that this is for a cellphone tower north of Winslow.  He talked about the location for the proposed tower and indicated that it is zoned A General.  He reviewed the site plan, indicating that it will be a one-hundred-five-foot tower, that the setbacks are in excess of the ordinance requirements, that they are planning on having a twelve-foot access road for emergency vehicles, there will be a ten-foot fence surrounding the facility and that the equipment will sit on a five-foot platform.   He stated that the applicant did hold a community meeting, but that there was nobody in attendance at the meeting.  He reviewed the staff analysis, indicating that it has been properly noticed and posted, that it complies with the Zoning Ordinance and meets several of the goals of the Comprehensive Plan, that it has been determined to be compatible with current and likely future uses and that there are no minimal adverse effects on public health.  He stated that staff has not received any public comments either in favor or against the project.  He further stated that staff is recommending approval and that the Planning and Zoning Commission unanimously recommended approval at their December 14, 2023, meeting, subject to the conditions provided.
The applicant (appearing virtual) did not wish to speak.

Chairman Whiting opened the public hearing, there being no comments, the public hearing was closed.

Supervisor Seymore talked about the requirements for the tower height having been eight-foot and one-hundred-foot and requested clarification regarding the proposed tower being recommended for one-hundred-five-feet.
Mr. Richards stated that the maximum height requirement for a non-residentially zoned tower can go up to one-hundred-ninety-nine-feet.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to adopt  Resolution Number 03-2024, approving a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction of a 105' monopole communication tower and associated facilities located north of the Winslow area, motion seconded by  Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

PUBLIC HEARING:  Consideration and possible adoption of  Resolution Number 04-2024 , approving/denying text amendment to Article 15, Section 1502 and Article 30, Section 3002 regarding swap meets :
Sondra Starkey stated that this proposed text amendment for swap meets is due to several code enforcement concerns as well as inquiries from the general public.  She stated that this amendment would be to include a definition for swap meets, indicating that in their research they found several jurisdictions include auctions as well, and that this would be to have in in the zoning ordinance as a permanent use for a property.  She stated that in the staff report she attached track changes for Article 15 and 30 showing the definitions and usages.  She stated that staff, as well as the Planning and Zoning Commission, recommend approval.

Chairman Whiting opened the public hearing, there being no comments, the public hearing was closed.

3.

PUBLIC HEARING:  Consideration and possible adoption of  Resolution Number 05-2024 , approving/denying text amendment to Article 25, Section 2503, regarding accessory building setbacks, and Article 25, Section 2520, regarding accessory dwelling units :
Sondra Starkey stated that this is for a text amendment after finding a couple of discrepancies between code enforcement inquiries, as well as inquiries from the public. She stated that the first amendment they are proposing is to update the three-foot accessory building setback to a five-foot setback, which falls in line with fire code.  She stated that the second amendment they are proposing is that zoning districts, which currently allow for accessory dwellings, to also allow for park models as guest houses, which will comply with recent changes to the statute.  She indicated that the third amendment they are proposing is the removal of the remaining reference to servant's quarters, stating that it was resolved in a previous amendment that updated it to guest houses instead of caretaker's quarters.  She stated that staff, as well as the Planning and Zoning Commission, recommend approval.

Chairman Whiting opened the public hearing, there being no comments, the public hearing was closed.

Supervisor Seymore requested clarification on if a modular unit or park model in a site-built subdivision could be added as a second home.
Ms. Starkey stated that it would be dependent on the master development plan and the CCNR's that were established at the time. She provided some different examples.  She stated that it would still need to be under seventy percent of the square footage of the primary home, since that is a requirement for secondary dwellings or guest houses.
Supervisor Seymore inquired if a permit would be required.
Ms. Starkey stated that it would be required not only a guest house permit, but also a building permit.
Supervisor Seymore talked about keeping the neighbors looking nice and not allowing them to become downgraded by allowing park models to come into areas where everything has been site built.
Ms. Starke stated that it doesn't change the developments were guest houses wouldn't be allowed that it just in the zoning districts where they are already allowed.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to adopt  Resolution Number 05-2024 , approving text amendment to Article 25, Section 2503, regarding accessory building setbacks, and Article 25, Section 2520, regarding accessory dwelling units, motion seconded by  Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

4.

PUBLIC HEARING:  Consideration and possible adoption of Resolution Number 06-2024 , approving/denying text amendment to Article 25, Section 2517 of the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance, regarding age of mobile homes which require rehabilitation verification :
Sondra Starkey stated that this text amendment, indicating that last year Planning had put through a zoning ordinance update that required a twenty-year rolling basis for rehabilitation certification for manufactured and mobile homes twenty-years or older and that this would be an update to that.  She indicated that, in working with HUD, they do not require a rehabilitation certification that extends past 1976, so they are requesting the text amendment to reflect that any type of verification that the county would need to go through the building department.  She stated staff is recommending approval.

Chairman Whiting opened the public hearing, there being no comments, the public hearing was closed.

Supervisor Seymore discussed the item regarding older homes and stated that he appreciates the work that staff has done on this.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to adopt  Resolution Number 06-2024 , approving text amendment to Article 25, Section 2517 of the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance, regarding age of mobile homes which require rehabilitation verification, motion seconded by  Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
