b.

Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 41-2024 , authorizing the Clerk of the Board to administratively approve uniform video service licenses pursuant to A.R.S. §11-1914 :
Jason Moore addressed the item, indicating that the Resolution would authorize the Clerk of the Board to approve the uniform video service license agreements for Cable One, now known as Sparklight. He stated that in 2019 the Legislature adopted a comprehensive statutory scheme to govern cable companies, which is found in ARS 11-1901 to 11-1952, noting that previously the Board would've approved the agreement and that it would have had all terms needed in it requiring notification and repair of roads whenever the needed to dig them up when they were installing cable.  He stated that the new statute forbids the Board from voting on or taking official action, indicating that the Board can't approve the license agreement and that it needs to be delegated somewhere else.  He advised that the statute doesn't specifically say it, but that the application is supposed to be filed with the Clerk of the Board and that he thinks by implication it's the Clerk of the Board that approves it, although it could be delegated somewhere else.  He suggested granting a one-year license instead of the requested ten years, indicating that a more comprehensive resolution or Ordinance that governs cable companies.  He stated that they have been research what other counties are doing, noting that they will come back to the Board with a resolution that will govern the process, which will ensure that future agreements can adequately address necessary terms, typically included in franchise agreements, which the new statute restricts. He indicated that staff is requesting that the Board approve the Resolution and delegate their authority to the Clerk of the Board to grant the license agreement due to there being a limited amount of time to act under the statute after the application comes in.
Chairman Whiting stated that he wanted to clarify that there needs to be an updated ordinance but that due to time constraints with the application a decision needs to be made sooner, that previously the Board would have made the decision but that under the new statute they can no longer do that, so they are advising that the Clerk be designated until the ordinance is updated.
Mr. Moore indicated that was correct.
Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to adopt  Resolution Number 41-2024 , authorizing the Clerk of the Board to administratively approve uniform video service licenses pursuant to A.R.S. §11-1914, motion seconded by  Vice-Chair Benally .  Motion passed 4-0, with Supervisor Peshlakai being absent.

b.

Presentation on FY2024 Navajo County grant development & management performance :
A presentation was shown.  Ken Maruyama, the Strategic Initiative Manager in the Finance Department, shared his experiences with Navajo County over the past three years. He spoke about the importance of grants in providing financial resources for community programs and initiatives, while also acknowledging their complexity.
He indicated that in fiscal year 2023, the county spent $8.1 million in grant awards, and for fiscal year 2024, he has calculated grant awards totaling $13.3 million. He stated that each department manages its own grants without a centralized monitoring system. He stated that he was involved in 15 grant development projects over the last fiscal year, that 8 grant awards were made and approved by the Board, that there are 6 on the way and 1 application that did not get approved.  He stated that grants support county programs and services in different areas and that he will have some of the departments share about their experience to help everyone better understand how the grants benefit the County and the communities.
Sgt. Alden Whipple spoke about the significant efforts of the Sheriff's Office over the past year in securing funding for various projects, thanks to collaboration with county members, especially Mr. Maruyama. He indicated that the level of opportunity is unprecedented and assured that the funding will be utilized effectively. He stated that the resources will enhance jail support, patrols, undercover investigations, and equipment improvements.  He indicated that the key advancements include upgraded communication systems for local police departments, an increase in radio towers for better public safety communications, and the establishment of a fiber optic link between the Navajo County dispatch center and the Show Low Police Department that allows for seamless dispatching from either location. He stated that the new command post which can be used for search and rescue operations and various county events, making it a valuable resource for the agency.
Lynda Young provided an overview of grants received through the criminal justice system in Navajo County.  She stated that they have $1.9 million in Overdose Prevention and Engagement Grants, noting that the program connects high-risk defendants to immediate intervention and treatment.  She indicated that there have been over 60 graduates who are overcoming addiction and reintegrating into the community, noting that one graduate transitioned from addiction to pursuing higher education and gaining full custody of his children.  She stated that they have $950,000 in a Veteran's Treatment Court Grant, which expands existing services for veterans, providing specialized treatment and fostering community through shared experiences. She noted that they do have an existing Veterans Court.  She indicated that the program connects veterans to vital resources and improves their outcomes.  She stated that the Victim Services Grants total $339,000, that they are the only Victim Services Agency, that they assisted over 5,000 victims last year and provided over 70,000 services. She indicated that the funds support court advocacy, crisis response, financial assistance, temporary housing, and counseling. She spoke about a specific case of a 16-year-old sex trafficking victim, highlighting the comprehensive support and medical care provided to ensure her safety and recovery.
Madhav Mundle spoke about the importance of grants for Public Works, particularly given that the gas tax rate of 18 cents was set in 1991, which is insufficient due to inflation. He outlined efforts to secure funding to enhance infrastructure in Navajo County, highlighting over $2 million in grants received in the past year.  He reviewed the grants and projects, including the RAISE Grant, which is a planning grant that helps fund a match for a major project aimed at improving connectivity between business and recreational centers in South County; the grant for the sidewalk and multi-use path around the schools in Heber, which will improve safety and accessibility; the grant for the drainage and bridge improvements in the White Mountain Lakes area; the CDBG grant and the Transportation or Alternative Grant for the Joseph City Sidewalk Improvement project; and the Off System Bridge Grant for the Silver Creek Bridge at Shumway Road. He spoke about the challenges of navigating different federal requirements for these grants but emphasized their crucial role in maintaining and improving the county's infrastructure.
Grant Evans stated that the County was awarded a competitive broadband grant from the US Treasury, which is administered by the Arizona Commerce Authority, totaling $9.7 million, noting that it is nearly 10% of the state's $100 million allocation for the program. He stated that the funding supports a countywide middle-mile fiber optic infrastructure project, currently under construction, which has already received approximately $3 million in reimbursements.  He indicated that the project will serve as an economic growth engine for the county, establishing an open-access network that enhances competition and service offerings, and that it will provide reliable and redundant connections for public safety services.  He stated that the total project cost is estimated at $20 million, with $9 million match from ARPA funds, making it largely funded by grants.
Janelle Linn stated that Public Health relies heavily on grants, which are essential for the department's operations. She indicated that currently, there are 29 active grants, with a peak of around 60 in the past. She further indicated that managing these grants requires diligence to ensure accountability and meet funding deliverables.  She reviewed the different Divisions within the Health Department that benefit from grants, including, the Clinical Division Grants that support cancer screenings for uninsured women, provides free immunizations for adults and children, offers STD and HIV counseling and treatment and conducts TB testing and treatment; the Environmental Health Grants that sustain a registered sanitarian position and pursues national program standards for additional funding; the WIC Grant that serves 2,500 to 3,000 citizens monthly, providing supplemental food for pregnant women, young children, and infants; the Education and Outreach Division Grants that fund fatality review teams to analyze deaths and develop prevention programs, supports opioid initiatives, including Narcan distribution and community education, provides mental health first aid training, promotes youth health education in schools, and funds tobacco cessation programs and youth advocacy coalitions; the Injury Prevention Funding that allows them to issue free car seats and safe sleep packs for low-income families and to offer CPR courses; the Public Health Improvement Grant that funded a community health assessment and improvement plan; and the Workforce Development Grant that supports a finance assistant position and enables technical assistance from federal agencies.  She stated that the grants are vital for sustaining and enhancing public health services in the community.
Mr. Maruyama talked about the grants that benefit the Emergency Management Department indicating that they help with coordination and communication with a wide range of community organizations and ensures the county is prepared for natural disasters, as well as helping to provide funds for equipment, supplies and planning.
Mr. Maruyama provided an update of the ARPA program, indicating that the Board of Supervisors allocated $1.3 million to support 12 community projects, that currently nine projects have been completed, while three are still in progress. He stated that $1 million has been disbursed, and the County Oversight Committee continues to support the success of these projects.
Mr. Evans spoke about the progress of the County projects, indicating that 12 projects have been allocated a total of $18.7 million, that the broadband project is 30% complete and accounts for nearly half of the ARPA funds; that the Code Enforcement Facility is set to begin construction next month and that the County complex rehabilitation is essentially complete, with only final touches remaining.  He stated that there are several other projects at various stages, including the webcasting software and improvements to the countywide public safety communications radio system.
Mr. Maruyama spoke about the Community Development Program, noting that last year the Board approved $500,000 in CDBG funds to support three community projects, all of which are currently in progress. He stated that the County is collaborating with NACo and the Arizona Department of Housing for the completion of the project in compliance with federal regulations.  He indicated that while the County has been successful in grant development and management, there is always room for improvement. He stated that he would suggest that a SWOT analysis be conducted from a strategic planning perspective.  He spoke about the importance of teamwork in developing and managing grants, expressing gratitude for the leadership and efforts of county employees and community stakeholders.
Supervisor Seymore expressed gratitude for Mr. Maruyama's leadership and expertise in grant writing, noting that he is among the best in the Western United States. He indicated that thanks to his efforts, the county has secured millions of dollars in funding that significantly benefits their programs. He acknowledged the contributions of the entire team involved in the grant processes, emphasizing their collective importance in securing these essential funds.
Supervisor Whitesinger expressed gratitude to the team for their collaborative efforts and the value of leveraging resources.  She highlighted the importance of utilizing professional knowledge that exists within the organization to impact citizens positively and finding grants that address community needs. She shared her appreciation for everyone's contributions and commitment to enhancing the community and providing support to the communities.
Vice-Chair Benally expressed gratitude to the team for their efforts in securing grants that help stretch county dollars.  She thanked Mr. Maruyama for the work he has done.
Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Maruyama for his leadership, highlighting the significant improvements in grant success since he joined. He acknowledged the collaborative work of the team and emphasized the positive impact of working together in pursuing these opportunities.

A presentation was shown.  Tyler Richards stated that this is for a special use permit for a new solar farm located about four miles southwest of Joseph City, encompassing eight parcels totaling approximately 7,119 acres. He indicated that the project, owned by Aztec Landing Cattle Company and Aztec East Jeffers, includes solar panels, a battery storage system with a capacity of 450 megawatts, an operations and maintenance building, and associated infrastructure like storage warehouses and access roads.  He stated that the site will feature solar modules and cabling for energy conversion, surrounded by a six-foot chain-link fence and that the layout includes designated areas for the solar panels and access points.  He indicated that the project aligns with local land use goals, includes wildlife assessments, and plans for cultural resource management.  He noted that it has access from Territorial Road and McCaw Road, with public works reviewing for safety.  He indicated that the project aims to utilize existing infrastructure from the nearby Cholla Power Plant.  He stated that there have been no public comments against the project and that the Planning and Zoning Commission recommends approval with the 16 conditions listed in the staff report.  He indicated that the applicant is present for any questions.
Chairman Whiting opened the public hearing.  There being no comments, the public hearing was closed.
Chairman Whiting noted that in July, a press release announced a temporary pause on new projects but that this project was already in the works.  He inquired if this is the last project that was already in the works.
Mr. Richards stated that this is the second to last project that was already in the works and that the last project will be brought to the Board in November.
Chairman Whiting inquired as to the location of the project in the Joseph City area, if the landowner is present and if the landowner is in support of this project.  He further inquired if any comments have been received as this has moved through the process.
Mr. Richards stated that the project is located southwest of Joseph City and that the landowner is present and that they do support the project. He stated that no public comments have been received for or against the project.
Chairman Whiting inquired what the process entails to this point.
Mr. Richards stated that the application process involves several steps, including concept meetings, a meeting with Planning staff to review the request and guide them through the process, a pre-application meeting with all relevant County departments, a community outreach meeting that are within 300 feet in the vicinity of the project, and they go before the Planning and Zoning Commission before coming to the Board for final approval.  He stated that the Planning and Zoning Commission has recommended approval unanimously.
Chairman Whiting indicated that it is his understanding that although the applicant has to come before the Board for approval, they still have to get a contract with a utility that would approve the project and that they have to get approval to access the grid as well.
Mr. Richards stated that the project must also secure a power agreement with a utility, or they won't be able to move forward.
Alyssa Menzel, Applicant/Developer with Long Road Energy, indicating that they are required to obtain a permit from the Arizona Corporation Commission, which requires a significant amount of public outreach, open houses and community meetings.
Chairman Whiting inquired if the interest for these projects is due to the utilities putting out an RFP for power to put on the grid.
Ms. Menzel stated that, APS, the local utility releases an RFP annually to evaluate various renewable energy projects.  She indicated that they select projects based on their development stage, economic factors, and size. She stated that there is significant interest in the area, with many projects currently undergoing interconnection studies, largely due to the retirement of APS.
Supervisor Seymore inquired as to the decommission plan approval.  He further inquired if a bond will be posted for the amount of the decommissioning.
Ms. Menzel stated that they are required to submit a decommissioning plan to the County, as well as the private landowner.  She indicated that a bond will be posted.
Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to adopt Resolution 43-2024 , approving a request by Lark Point Solar, LLC, for a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction and operation of a 450MW solar power generation facility, as well as 450MW, battery energy storage system (BESS), and associated facilities on approximately 7,119 acres located southwest of Joseph City, Arizona, motion seconded by  Vice-Chair Benally .  Motion passed 4-0, with Supervisor Peshlakai being absent.

Consideration of a Land Purchase Agreement between Michael C. Burdick, Sr. and Carol D. Burdick (Sellers) and Navajo County, Arizona (Buyer) for Flood Control in the Wagon Wheel Area, Navajo County, Arizona :
Donald Perkins stated that this item is for Assessor's Parcel Number 212-04-071, owned by Michael and Carol Dick.  He indicated that it has known flooding issues and is located within an unmapped FEMA floodplain. He stated that both parties have agreed that the Navajo County Flood Control District will pay $10,000 to acquire this 0.47-acre property.  He indicated that the Flood Control team is exploring various options to address potential flooding concerns in the Pine View Summer Home Sites subdivision, and that the parcel will be incorporated into a future flood control project aimed at addressing flooding concerns in that area.
Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion  to approve a Land Purchase Agreement between Michael C. Burdick, Sr. and Carol D. Burdick (Sellers) and Navajo County, Arizona (Buyer) for Flood Control in the Wagon Wheel Area, Navajo County, Arizona, motion seconded by  Supervisor Seymore .  Motion passed 4-0, with Supervisor Peshlakai being absent.
Vice-Chair Benally made a motion  to return to regular session, motion seconded by  Supervisor Seymore .  Motion passed 4-0, with Supervisor Peshlakai being absent.

CALL TO THE PUBLIC :
Donna Shurwin, Pinetop, expressed concerns about election procedures, particularly during the primary election. She highlighted issues with the ballot curing process, noting that representatives from major political parties were often absent when ballots were being reviewed, which she believes violates legal requirements. She urged for better adherence to these laws, with the general election.  She indicated that the County has spent over $100,000 on cameras for drop boxes, stating that many are non-functional and lack nighttime visibility, raising security concerns. She pointed out that some election centers did not submit end-of-day reports, leaving questions about the chain of custody for ballots. She emphasized the need for accountability and better service to constituents.
Chairman Whiting directed staff to get a response for her questions.
Celia Laughlin expressed frustration with local officials, emphasizing a lack of trust in government actions and decisions. She indicated that citizens should have more authority and called for accountability regarding taxes, land use for turbines, health impacts from 5G towers, and election oversight.  She demanded a hand-count audit of all paper ballots in the upcoming general election to ensure transparency and accuracy, asserting that local officials should prioritize the will of the people over federal or state government influence. She indicated that failure to secure elections could result in the removal of those in office.
