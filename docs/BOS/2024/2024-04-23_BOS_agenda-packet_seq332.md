SHERIFF: 
 
a. Consideration of a proclamation declaring the week beginning April 14th
through April 20th, 2024, as "National Telecommunicators Week" in Navajo
County, Arizona :
Sheriff David Clouse introduced Caitlyn Montoya, the Lead Dispatcher,
indicating that she does a phenomenal job representing her staff and the
profession.  He stated that she has worked hard for the members of her
team to be awarded benefits that include them as first responders,
indicating that it is the dispatchers voice that keeps many of the deputies
safe.  He talked about how the dispatchers always know their location,
know the type of situation they are in, and that if further assistance is
needed, they are there for you.  He stated that it is comforting to have them
and that they couldn’t do this type of work without their dedication and
service.  He stated that when there is a critical incident, a natural disaster
or serious law enforcement call Ms. Montoya and her team come in and
ensure that they have the communication needed and that they don’t go
without that service.  He indicated that last month the dispatchers received
3850 administration calls or non-emergency calls and further received
1398 9-1-1 emergency calls, stating that they have to generate those calls,
find the resource, and dispatch the call and then stay of the line to assist
with giving CPR over the line or giving advice to keep people safe.  He
read the proposed proclamation.  Sheriff Clouse stated that the Navajo
County Sheriff’s Office could not run without our telecommunicators and
requested approval of the proclamation. 
Supervisor Seymore stated that this is fantastic, indicating that the
dispatchers are their voice, their eyes, and their comfort. He stated that the
dispatch centers are important and a big key to the safety in our
communities.  He thanked Ms. Montoya and her team for all they do.
Supervisor Whitesinger stated that having the proclamations come
forward reminds us of some of the things that we take for granted.  She

BOARD OF EQUALIZATION:
 
a. Discussion and action regarding a Petition for Review of Proposed
Correction Real Property APN 202-18-003Q; Swearing in of Assessor,
Appraisers and Petitioners:
Cammy Darris, Assessor and Joseph Holland, Petitioner, were sworn in by
Melissa Buckley. 
Joseph Holland stated that he recalls a proposition several years ago that
said the value couldn’t be grown more than five percent year to year.  He
indicated that the assessed value on his property has gone up over
twenty-five percent last year and again this year, so he asked for a review. 
He talked about some comparable properties in the area and stated that
the Assessor’s Office indicated that they don’t consider it comparable due
to foundation issues.  He indicated that due to the huge growth in the
assessed value be understand it to be ground to be reassessed.  He stated
that there was a minor land adjustment between his and neighboring
properties that dealt almost exclusively with the road due to not wanting to
be landlocked.  He indicated that he is asking for a review, indicating the
higher assessment was not warranted and that it’s not keeping with the five
percent increase that was supposed to be the historical increase.  He
talked about the amount it cost to build his home in 2019, indicating that he
understands that the prices have increased but that he thinks the assessed
value is high.     
Cammy Darris indicated that the home is 5,054 square feet on 1.84 acres
located in Snowflake on Crestview Drive. She stated that in Mr. Hollands
petition he indicated that the value can’t increase more than five percent
year to year and that the boundary adjustment that was requested only
affected a small area and does not justify the change in assessment as
required by statute.  She indicated that she set up a meeting with the
Petitioner but received no response, so they answered the petition. She
spoke about the change being $485, indicating that the land value was
65,302 and went to 65,787 with the improvement value staying the same. 
She stated that the limited property value, which is used for tax purposes,
stayed the same. She indicated that the limited value is what is capped at
5% but that the full cash value is synonymous with market and can go up
depending on what the market does.  She talked about the comparables
that were provided and stated that she provided the statuses for the limited
cash value and full cash value, as well as the information regarding the
cracks in the other home.  She requested that the FCV remain at $789,633
and the LV at $399,893.
Ms. Darris answered questions from the Board discussing the statutes,

MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING 
 
THIS MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING (MOU) between the Navajo County Government 
(NCG), a political subdivision of the State of Arizona, and Community Bridges, Inc. (CBI), an 
Arizona non-profit corporation, is made and entered into effective March 31st, 2024. (Effective 
Date). 
 
WHEREAS Navajo County Government designated CBI as a provider of peer support services 
within the Navajo County Sheriff’s Office Detention Center (ODC). 
 
WHEREAS CBI and Navajo County Government recognize the mutual benefit of a collaborative 
relationship for the benefit of CBI’s and Navajo County Sheriff’s Office (NCSO) mission to 
provide peer support services to eligible individuals in custody at the Navajo County ODC 
(Peers).  
 
WHEREAS CBI has been awarded funding through the State Opioid Response Grant to provide, 
among other things, the services described in this MOU and CBI has an obligation to expend the 
grant funds on such projects. 
 
UNDERSTANDING 
 
NOW THEREFORE, in consideration of the foregoing and for other good and valuable 
consideration, the adequacy, receipt and sufficiency of which are hereby acknowledged, the 
parties share the following understanding: 
 
1. CBI ASSURANCES: CBI assures NCSO that it can: 
a. Identify specific qualified personnel who will be assigned for the provision of peer 
support services at the Navajo County Sheriff’s Office Detention Center (Providers). 
b. Provide the NCSO with identification of assigned Providers and the desired 
rotational schedule for Providers and for ultimate security clearance by NCSO. 
c. Require Providers to maintain the cleanliness and safe conditions of the ODC. CBI 
will advise all Providers to comply with all rules, regulations and policies of the ODC. 
Providers will complete a volunteer/vender safety and security course before 
placement within the ODC. 
d. Ensure that Providers maintain current professional licensure if required by the State 
of Arizona and provide verification of any required licenses to NCSO upon request.  
e. Assume clinical responsibility for control of Providers. 
f. Data sharing of success and failures, and other requested data as permitted by State 
and Federal law.
