CALL TO THE PUBLIC :
RaNae Rowles, 400 Freedom Lane, Taylor, talked about cleaning up the voter rolls, indicating that between December and March she submitted over three hundred names of voters who had not voted in the past ten years.  She stated that Navajo County is attempting to maintain their voter rolls properly but that the processes are inadequate to identify and remove records involving data errors, name changes or confirming that voters have died.  She talked about the different categories where names were removed, including in-state duplication registrations, voter registrations in another state, and deceased voters.  She stated that the county relies on return mail to indicate voters no longer live at the address, indicating some voters don't receive the mail and that mail is often thrown away instead of returned.  She recommended that the county obtain a legal option on whether they can implement a process for defaulting voters to inactive unless they contact the county to notify them that the voter is still living at the registration address.  She talked about a report given to the Recorder and Election Departments regarding significant gaps in the vulnerable maintenance process and that since that time many records have been removed due to research by citizens who have reported the issues to the county.  She stated that this is not a sustainable approach to correcting the records and that they need the county to advocate for changes in the system, so they don't continue to add faulty records with no viable way to remove them.  She talked about a Supreme Court ruling from 2018 where a voter status could be changed to inactive if the voter did not reply to a confirmation letter or postcard after failing to vote in several election cycles.  She stated that the National Voter Registration Act requires states to conduct a general program that makes a reasonable effort to remove names of voters who are ineligible due to death or change in residence and indicated that the county's current system doesn't appear to be working.  She requested that the county review the information that has been provided and to petition the Legislature and Secretary of State to implement a more proactive approach to voter roll maintenance so that the registration can be cleaned up.

Elections Presentation :
Chairman Whiting stated that there have been a lot of questions about elections and that there were some concerns around it.  He indicated that they wanted to see what all of the questions and concerns of the citizens were and then have staff share what the Election process is and incorporate the questions from citizens into that.  He stated that they asked the Republican and Democrat party chairs to take the questions that were received through the website had the party chairs take all of the questions submitted so that the questions could be consolidated and incorporated into the presentation.  He indicated that the intent was to hear the voice of the citizens so that a presentation could be put together to help everyone understand the processes. He stated that they appreciate the comments shared today in the Call to the Public.
A presentation was shown.  Bryan Layton stated that there is a lot that goes into elections which are governed by the State statute and the Elections Procedures Manuel.  He indicated that there are more details than can be covered today but that they took the questions asked and that they will be sharing what they learned.  He stated that they cannot address what happens in other jurisdictions and can only speak to the Navajo County processes.  He indicated that they do have a lot of information that is being provided in the handouts and that it will be placed on the website, under Elections, for everyone to review.  Mr. Layton talked about the three main settings for ballots, indicating that there are the vote centers, where you cast your vote and it's tallied; then it moves onto election central, where the votes are counted; and then it moves onto the Secretary of State, where it's aggregated, and then results are provided to the public.  He reviewed the detailed steps for each process.  He stated that first they print the ballots, that there are many different ballot styles depending on where people live and what they vote for, indicating that during a presidential election that are over 400 different unique ballot styles. He stated that once they are printed, they are counted, boxed up, sent to the vote centers where they are inspected, counted, and made ready for voters.  He indicated that when a voter comes in, they show their ID, are checked in to the system and once they are checked in and verified they receive a slip of paper showing that they are a registered voter, identified for their correct ballot style, provided the ballot where they can then vote and then they place it in the vote box which is locked up and secured inside the vote center or if there is a tabulator machine on site they can tally it on site.  He stated that all of the different votes have one thing is common that they are all paper driver, that in Navajo County they do not use any electronic or digital ballots.  He stated that in Navajo County you can vote at any vote center, that you don't have to vote within your precinct.  He stated that with over 400 ballot style, if you go to a precinct that is far from your home it won't be likely that they have a pre-printed ballot for your style, so they would give you a blank ballot, direct you to an express vote machine, they will put in what ballot style is needed, then it will print out your results that you will then put in the vote box or the tabulator machine.  He indicated that the express vote machine is just a printer, that the paper is what is counted, nothing from the machine is counted and that it also services the ADA accessibility requirement in each of the sites.  He stated that the ballots are then transported by deputies back to the Election Central warehouse, where they are counted, put together, packaged, and sent to the Secretary of State.  He stated that the Saturday after the election they are required by law to do a hand count of the paper ballots.  He indicated that there are a lot of questions regarding the integrity of that process, and he reviewed the checks and balances involved, indicating that first there is a chain of custody that's followed for every step of the process, second there is physical security with the tamper resistant seals and seals with barcodes that feeds into the chain of custody for the ballots and equipment, third the ballots returned to the elections warehouse must be reconciled so the number of ballots sent must equal the number of ballots returned including the unused and spoiled ballots, and forth the number of voters checked in needs to equal the number of votes that they got back.  He talked about the second section regarding mail in ballots, indicating that the right to vote by mail is set by State statute and can't be changed by the Board of Supervisors, that it is not an option or legal authority that the Board has. He reviewed the process, indicating that the ballots are printed, mailed to those on the Early Active Early Voting list, and that they received the mailed envelope with a colored envelope, affidavit envelope and the actual ballot inside.  He stated that once received the individual will complete the ballot, put it in the verification envelope and sign it, place it in the colored return envelope.  He stated that it has a unique barcode that identifies the specific voter and that they can return it by USPS or drop it into an official ballot drop box.  He indicated that there were several comments regarding having more camera monitoring at the drop boxes, that they do have it for some but that they have received some grant funding for additional and that they are currently looking at what equipment would work best to install on the ballot drop boxes.  He stated that when the ballot is returned the Recorder scans them to make sure that it is a valid voter, that it is the item that was sent by Navajo County, that they check and verify the signature, and once verified the ballot is opened, counted, sent to the Secretary of State and then a hand count audit is done the Saturday following the election.  He reviewed the checks and balances for this process, indicating, first that the ballot/envelope is not forwardable, second for physical security the drop box is medal and locked, ballots are picked up by two people one being from each party and a chain of custody, third the colored envelope has a barcode specific to each voter, forth the ballot must be signed and the signatures are verified against the voters signature of file, and fifth the voter is checked in on the system to prevent the person from voting again.  Mr. Layton addressed the ballot security, indicating that the Election Central building is highly restricted with only select Elections, IT, Sheriff's Office, and Facilities staff having access, that there is a security system that records entry in and out of the building, that processes involving ballots and counting verification is done by two people and that on election nights everything is streamed live.  He talked about the cybersecurity that they have in place, indicating that they have monthly meetings with local cybersecurity professionals up to the national level that help them become aware of emerging threats.  He talked about the different equipment, including the poll pad which has special software and is used to check in and verify voters before voting, that it is done in real time, that each voting center has a hotspot that has a masked Wi-Fi connection, that the Wi-Fi hotspot is encrypted and then it's encrypted to a local file and that it does not go to the Secretary of State.  He stated that the Secretary of State will only accept information from Navajo County through a dedicated gateway computer, which is a secure line, and will not accept information from any other source of transmission.  He talked about the express voting machines, indicating that it provides ADA accessibility that is required by law, the onsite tabulators that are counting machines used to tally the votes, and the central count machines, stating that they do not have external connections.  He indicated that data isn't transmitted over Wi-Fi, that they use two types of flash drives.  He stated that one is proprietary, that it is unique to some of the equipment, that it has a special coding on it and will not work on just any computer and that they are used to load different new ballot styles for each election onto the machines.  He further stated that they also have regular flash drivers that are scanned and cleared by IT, that they are brand new and are used to take information that is aggregated and put it on the elections computer and the gateway's computer just to make sure that there are no connections or transmissions, so they know exactly what is going onto the machine.  He talked about transmitting the data, indicating that first the results are put all together on a dedicated machine, that the computer is not on the county network or any other connection, that it is then put on the gateway computer that has a secure connection to the Secretary of State, that it has never been connected to the internet and cant connect to anything besides the Secretary of State secure portal, which is extremely firewalled and that only one person has access to log into that computer. He stated that once everything has been sent a report from the Secretary of State is downloaded to verify what they received.  He reviewed the checks and balances for the machines, indicating that first a logic and accuracy test is done pre-election and that the political parties not only witness but help with the test and that it must pass with one hundred percent accuracy, second is the physical security where they box and seal the equipment, third is the hand count audit that is done the Saturday after the election where they take votes from one random vote center that had an on-site tabulator, they run them through and count them and it must pass with one hundred percent accuracy and then forth they do a post-election logic and accuracy test where they have multiple batches of different types of ballots that are run through and must pass with a hundred percent accuracy every time.  He talked about the Voter Registration Lists, indicating that there are a lot of federal and state restrictions on how people can be removed and how it's maintained.  He stated that the three main ways to be removed are first when they receive a download from that State for a death, second, they received information from the courts if you're a convicted felon or found mentally incapacitated, and third an address change which is received from the post office or the voter, and that they also have a system that operates in thirty-one other states and the District of Columbia that helps them.  He stated that it's a manual process, that they receive reports and have to make the changes manually.  He talked about the questions related to the number of registered voters exceeding the number of voting age adults recorded in the census, indicating that there are a combination of factors that come into play, such as the census taking place during COVID, which was very difficult, and they believe that Navajo County was under counted, there being a large number of part-time residents or snowbirds that are seasonal homeowners that may be registered to vote in Navajo County but may have been counted elsewhere in the census and there being seventeen year olds on the voting list, which is legal and in the state statute that if you are seventeen turning eighteen prior to the next election you can register to vote, which reflects Navajo County being proactive in getting out to the schools to educate the seniors on the importance of voting and being involved in the communities.  He talked about the process for being able to remove inactive voters that have not voted in two general election cycles.  He spoke about the hand count, indicating that Mohave County did look into it last year and that some of their takeaways were, first that there would need to be a lot more people involved with could lead to confidentiality risk and that confidentiality is required by law, second, timing due to the strict timelines it would require a lot of additional staff to be hired to support the effort, third, was accuracy indicating that there was an error rate of 5.4% with the 850 ballots and forth, was a concern with logistics and cost with finding somewhere big enough to accommodate the number of people it would take and the amount that would be spent indicating that Mohave County estimated it would cost as much as $1.1 million to do a full hand count.  He stated that currently we don't experience concerns with confidentiality, or meeting state deadlines, that we have witnessed and audited one hundred percent accuracy and that we don't have one million dollars in discretionary revenue available in the budget.  He talked about looking into how Navajo County can better advertise the need for help during the elections.  He talked about where the authorities and jurisdictions are for a lot of the questions asked, indicating that most of the authority comes from the state.  He summarized all of the information that was provided, indicating that Navajo County only uses paper ballots, ballots are hand counted to audit results after the elections, that there is on-demand verification of mail-in ballots and at vote centers to prevent voting twice, that checks and balances exist to identify attempts to stuff the ballot boxes or the drop boxes, that the Secretary of State will only accept vote results from Navajo County from our dedicated and secured line portal computer, that the right to vote by mail is set by statute and can't be changed by the Board, that voter registration lists are a constant work in progress that is manual and tough with all of the regulations, that for a hand count the experience of others shows that it would take longer, introduce errors and have higher costs, that the Board has the responsibility to carry out elections but has very little discretion or authority granted by statute to alter how elections are covered, that in Navajo County there are check, balances and securities in place so that ballots are counted and reported correctly, and that we have great people who are professionals and trying their best to conduct elections with integrity and support our communities.
Supervisor Seymore stated that he appreciates the time that staff has taken on this to work through the processes.  He talked about some of the Legislative people that attend their CSA meeting and talked about election integrity.  He thanked the citizens that provided input today.  He talked about working with the citizens to have transparency, indicating that they are here to make it fair and honest the best they can but that they are also regulated.  He talked about voter integrity and everyone and doing their part.  He challenged those present to encourage people to vote and not that their vote doesn't count, indicating that in Navajo County everyone's vote is being counted.
Supervisor Whitesinger shared her appreciation to those that expressed their concerns, indicating that when the public comes and participates it is important.  She stated that it opens an avenue for conservations and being able to answer the questions.  She shared her gratitude to Mr. Layton and staff for the presentation and for answering the questions of the public.  She talked about the importance of protecting the rights of voters.  She shared her appreciation to all those who assist with elections to make sure that every vote is counted.
Vice-Chairwoman Benally thanked Mr. Layton for the presentation.  She spoke about the constituents on the Navajo Nation having problems voting due to transportation, weather and road issues and indicated that the mail in ballots and drop boxes are important for them being able to vote, that they don't want them to be disenfranchised and that they want all of them to have an opportunity to vote when it comes to elections.
Supervisor Peshlakai thanked Mr. Layton and staff for putting together the presentation.  He stated that he has been voting since he was eighteen and talked about voting by paper ballot until transitioning to the express machine.  He spoke about the details presented regarding mail-in ballots and being able to vote in any vote center in Navajo County, indicating that it is a positive thing, especially for the Navajo Nation so that people can go to a vote center rather than having to report back to their Chapters.  He stated that seeing the process from start to finish has been an eye opener knowing how much security there is and that he has faith in our elections team as well as the poll workers.  He talked about his experience when voting during the Presidential Preference Election.  He talked about the security measures with Navajo County Elections compared to those on the Navajo Nation.
Chairman Whiting inquired how all is present for the pre-election equipment audit?
Mr. Layton stated that it is Election staff, a representative from the Democrat and Republican parties, Law Enforcement, and individuals from the State.
Chairman Whiting inquired if the equipment is opened up for them to look inside during the audit?
Mr. Layton stated that he does not believe it is opened. He stated that they create a sample ballot deck and run it through in different combinations approximately ten different times to make sure they are getting consistent results.
Chairman Whiting inquired what happens with it after the audit if it is sealed?
Mr. Layton stated that it is sealed, that they have the physical production of the elections building, that within there the equipment is boxed, sealed to identify tampering, and then it's behind lock and key until election day.
Chairman Whiting inquired who all is present for the post-election audit?
Mr. Layton stated that the Saturday after the election they do the hand count and that they are required to have the same individuals present, representatives from the Democrat and Republican parties, Elections staff and Law Enforcement.
Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Layton for the information.  He spoke about the information shared related to Mohave County, indicating that there was some good information shared on that relating to the cost prospective and the confidentiality.  He requested that Mr. Layton review the information provided regarding there being no concern with Navajo County not being connected to the internet.
Mr. Layton talked about the Secretary of State only receiving vote data from the county through a specific source stating that the Secretary of State is completely firewalled and will only accept information from one source from Navajo County.  He stated that they have the seals and that the connections are either through a USB or a hardline connection, that there is no Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or electronic transmission.
Chairman Whiting inquired as to the authority for mail-in ballots being controlled by the State and that it is not something that the Board can change.
Mr. Layton stated that the Board of Supervisors has no authority to alter whether or not they can do mail-in ballots, that it is controlled by the State, indicating that it is one way to provide ADA accessibility to certain voters and that it's in state statute.
