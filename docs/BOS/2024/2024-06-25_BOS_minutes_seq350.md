PUBLIC HEARING:  Consideration and adoption of the amended ordinance, Ordinance Number 03-11, amending Section 2 to increase the Justice Court Staffing Fee from $30 to $48 effective July 1, 2024 :
Jason Vowell stated that during the budget process they go through and analyze the County's funds and accounting system.  He indicated that during that process it came to their attention that the Ordinance for the Justice Court Staffing Fee has not been updated since 2012, and that according to the Ordinance this should have been reviewed annually, but that this has not been done.   He stated that as part of the budgeting process they look at the activity and the revenue that is received in the fund to help offset some of the costs that the County incurs for doing the service, which supports eight clerical and attorney positions throughout the County.  He indicated that the proposed increase from $30.00 to $48.00 is significant, but that it has not been increased since 2012, and that it should have been done year over year incrementally.  He spoke about the process that they used to determine the increase, indicating that they have used the same process for other increases when looking at inflationary measures.  He stated that the increase still doesn't cover the entire cost, that it isn't meant to cover entire cost, and that they do transfer from the general fund to support this and that even after the proposed increase the general fund will cover approximately 40%.  He indicated that he reviewed the numbers from 2012 and that at that time the general fund supported between eight and then percent and that the general fund is taking on more of the burden year after year.  He stated that staff is recommending adoption of the ordinance to increase the fee and that going forward they will take a more active approach at looking at it annually.

Vice-Chair Benally opened the meeting for public comments.
Melissa Buckley stated that there were two emails received from Former Supervisor Jerry Brownlow and Former Constable Ryan Reinhold and that copies have been provided to the Board.  Ms. Buckley read the comments from both individuals regarding their concerns about the proposed increase of fees.
A member of the public inquired what the historical increases were prior to this per year.
Mr. Vowell stated that he doesn't have the exact amount but that it was not as much of as increase as the amount being recommended.
Brad Carlyon clarified that his office did not ask for the increase and that he was unaware that this helped fund his office.
There being no more comments, the public hearing was closed.

Mr. Vowell answered questions of the Board regarding the functions and activities that are supported with the fund, the number of positions it impacts, how the amount of the proposed increase was determined, and finding a possible compromise.
Supervisor Seymore stated that there are many things in the budget they do to try and make sure that things are cost effective and the activities going forward can pay for themselves.  He indicated that there have also been some costs that were appropriated from the State and that the County will be sharing some increase of those, that have to do with court costs.    He indicated that he is okay with the increase and spoke of the reasons that the costs are assessed.  He stated that this should have been looked at sooner but that they are trying to bring it up to date.
Brad Carlyon spoke about the types of cases that the County Attorney's Office handles in the Justice Courts.  He indicated that the cost also assists the Public Defender's Office.  He further indicated that he is not sure how it will impact impartiality of the Justice Court's due to the routinely imposing mandatory fines, fees and surcharges.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to adopt the amended ordinance, Ordinance Number 03-11, amending Section 2 to increase the Justice Court Staffing Fee from $30 to $45 effective July 1, 2024, and that the ordinance be reviewed annually, motion seconded by  Supervisor Peshlakai .  Motion passed 4-0 with Chairman Whiting being absent.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and adoption of Ordinance Number 01-24 , repealing all other ordinances concerning animal care and control and adopting this comprehensive Ordinance :
Rhonda Krouse, Code Enforcement Division Manager, stated that she oversees three programs, one of them being Animal Program, and that Brandon Damron is the Program Manager that oversees the day-to-day operations.  A presentation was shown.  Ms. Krouse indicated that in reviewing the programs ordinances and contracts the team determined that they needed to update the terminology, definitions and standardization of the ordinance. She further indicated that there were multiple ordinances in the program that they were able to combine into one, that they were able to align them more with the Arizona Revised Statutes and to reflect some of the issues that they are currently experiencing.  She stated that the number one goal of the Animal Care and Control Program is to educate the public and to provide awareness on the issues that they run into.  She spoke about the proposed modifications and additions to the ordinances, including adding definitions for aggressive, attack, County Enforcement Agent, and Cruelty; adding new ordinances for keeping of wild or exotic animals, keeping of horses, defined quarantine animals/area, Hearing Officer, and added penalties from Arizona Revised Statutes.  She talked about the proposed modifications to the fee schedule, noting that they are requesting a moderate increase due to inflation which affects market forces for dog food, gas, equipment and vehicle maintenance.  She stated that calls for dogs at large are approximately sixty percent of their volume and often leads to conflicts between neighbors and that they anticipate the fee will play a significant part in reducing the issues.  She further stated that with the kennel permits will assist with hoarding issues and the overcrowding in the shelters, indicating that the ones in our area are all at capacity at this time.
Supervisor Whitesinger inquired as to the dog at large, if it is for a dog running in the community or due to the conflict with community members, noting that there is a difference in the amount based on the location.
Ms. Krouse indicated that it is state law and that they govern the unincorporated areas. She stated that for the unincorporated areas they have to be on a leash, be a working dog or have to be under voice control.  She indicated that the municipalities have their own ordinances.

Vice-Chair Benally opened the meeting for public comments.
Ken Kato inquired about, based on a previous incident, what ordinances are there for dogs maiming or killing a citizen.
Brad Carlyon stated that there are crime statutes for those issues.
Ms. Krouse stated that they can only site for civil issues, not criminal.
Louise Legett, Snowflake, stated that she has a friend with farm dogs and that people will go by and harass the dogs while they are working, which causes them to growl.  She indicated that there is right to farm laws that protect them and inquired if the right to farm laws are incorporated in this ordinance.  She stated that her friend has had trouble with the courts, but her dogs are doing their job.
Ms. Krouse stated that you have to be in voice control of your animal, or they have to be working dogs, which is covered.
There being no additional comments, the public hearing was closed.
Supervisor Seymore inquired if this ordinance will be stronger than the ordinances that the municipalities have.
Ms. Krouse indicated that it would not.  She stated that they looked at other ordinances within Navajo County and that they tried to keep it neutral and that she doesn't feel that they have trumped any of the other ordinances.

Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion  to adopt  Ordinance Number 01-24 , repealing all other ordinances concerning animal care and control and adopting this comprehensive Ordinance, motion seconded by  Supervisor Seymore .  Motion passed 4-0 with Chairman Whiting being absent.

PUBLIC HEARING:  Consideration and adoption of  Ordinance Number 02-2024,  approving the removal of motor vehicles, trailers, and storage containers from County-maintained rights-of-way during snow season and for the enforcement of parking restrictions by issuance of citations and/or towing :
Cody Cooper stated that this is for a snow winter parking ordinance that would be in effect from November 1 to April 1.  He stated that the intent of this ordinance is to prevent the parking of vehicles, trailers, and storage containers within the county maintained right of way during typical snow season, noting that during snow season the vehicles can delay or prevent the ability to maintain and do snow removal on roadways and that this would offer a mechanism for law enforcement to tow for the offense.  He spoke about the fines that could be imposed for the offenses, indicating that it is discretionary and that they will see if it inhibits the maintenance.  He stated that he worked with Public Works and Sheriff's Office staff on the language that everyone would be comfortable with, but also to make sure that staff are able to do safe and timely maintenance of the roads during winter snow season.  He indicated that staff has not received any comments regarding this ordinance.

Vice-Chair Benally opened the meeting for public comments.  There being no comments, the public hearing was closed.

Supervisor Whitesinger inquired if this is specific to snow season and not for other types of complaints for parking.
Mr. Cooper stated that this is specific to the normal snow season, from November to April.

Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion  to adopt  Ordinance Number 02-2024,  approving the removal of motor vehicles, trailers, and storage containers from County-maintained rights-of-way during snow season and for the enforcement of parking restrictions by issuance of citations and/or towing, motion seconded by  Supervisor Peshlakai .  Motion passed 4-0 with Chairman Whiting being absent.

Vice-Chair Benally stated that comments are limited to three minutes per person, that the Board can't dialogue, discuss, consider or decide any issues raised during Call to the Public but can respond to criticism or may direct staff to review an item.  She stated that professional decorum is expected, that civil demeanor needs to be maintained, including respect of others, and that speakers who choose to violate the guidelines will be given a warning and then asked to leave if the conduct continues.  She further stated that speakers are asked to refrain from repeating comments or questions already shared, noting that residents are welcome to share additional concerns with the Board of Supervisors outside of Call to the Public by contacting the Clerk of the Board.
Danielle Earl, Linden, spoke about the I-40 Trade Port Corridor, noting that she is speaking not only as a resident of Navajo County, but on behalf of the residents of Winslow who would be affected by this.  She stated that she is providing documents for the Board to review, including fourteen letters from residents, the parcel summary report and unofficial warranty deeds for the nine properties owned by Atlas Global Development Group, Inc.   She requested that the Board not approve the annexation of the properties to the City of Winslow.  She spoke about the development agreement that is on the Winslow City Councils agenda.  She indicated as part of the trade port plan they want to have a bypass route diverting truck traffic from I-40 toward Highway 87, which is the stated reasoning for the application of the Raised Grant.  She indicated that Atlas Global has been buying property in the area in hopes of developing an industrial mega site, noting that they describe the existing site character as primarily vacant land with interspersed residential parcels.  She indicated that the future of the area is to build hotels, hangers, light industrial commerce park and multi family housing, but that the area would need to be annexed to the city and zoned commercial to fulfill the plan.  She further indicated that the issue is the interspersed residential parcels standing in the way, that the residents of those parcels are protesting the annexation and the types of companies they're trying to attract, because they will lose their rights and property to further the agenda of the project.  She requested that the property rights of the owners be protected, and that the property not be annexed to the city.

She talked about having security for the drop boxes.  She stated that they should have a backup hand count in place for upcoming elections.
