Memorandum of Understanding
Between
Flagstaff Initiative Against Trafficking (FIAT); a program of Northland Family Help Center
and
Navajo County Attorney’s Office/Victim Services
This Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) sets the terms, responsibilities and basis for Navajo
County Attorney’s Office/Victim Services as a member organization of FIAT, which will entail
collaboration and coordination of efforts to address sex and labor exploitation in Flagstaff,
Arizona. If requested, inclusion of and service to neighboring communities in Northern Arizona
as part of the activities outlined in this memorandum will be allowable.
Background
It is an understatement to say that sex and labor exploitation crimes, also referred to as human
trafficking, are highly complex and difficult to address. Since September of 2018, over 418
unduplicated adult and 50 juvenile victims of human trafficking have been served by providers in
the Flagstaff area. Due to limited participation in data collection and the lack of a coordinated
procedure for tabulating juvenile victimization, these figures represent a narrow snapshot of the
true rates of exploitation in the region. In alignment with other Arizona communities and current
research, it is highly probable that juvenile victims account for the majority of those subjected to
exploitation crimes, yet they currently remain under-identified, and therefore, underserved within
the region. Anecdotal evidence provided by youth-serving professionals affirms this theory.
The impact of trauma on an individual stemming from human trafficking victimization is equally
as complex, and yields ripple effects across generations, relationships, and the collective
wellness of the Flagstaff community. As a result, victims and survivors of exploitation have high
levels of needs that are unique to their lived experience, which will diverge from the needs of
non-trafficked victims of sexual and intimate partner violence. Due to the unique circumstances
of trafficked individuals, serving this population and preventing future victimization requires an
approach that is simultaneously trauma- and evidence-informed, multi-disciplinary, and
coordinated.
Absent a strong response from service providers, the criminal justice system, local leadership,
and the community, victims will continue to be under-identified, leading to rising rates of
exploitation. As has occurred in other regions of Arizona, criminal activities frequently
committed in tandem with human trafficking crimes also have the potential to increase, creating
concerns for public safety beyond exploitation. Further, law enforcement measures to curtail
these crimes and the generation of high-quality investigations, combined with a strong
commitment from local authorities to aggressively prosecute offenders, is generally accepted
within the anti-trafficking field to be the linchpin necessary to reducing rates of sex and labor
exploitation. In alignment with best practices, support and coordination between criminal justice
entities and service providers should, therefore, be proactive and based on a shared victim-
centered and trauma-informed approach adapted to the unique roles of all entities in addressing
human trafficking crimes.

Inclusion Criteria:
 An individual is considered to be a confirmed victim of human trafficking under the
following circumstances:
 The individual answers yes to any of the established human trafficking questions
asked by staff.
 The results from an administered human trafficking screening tool indicate that it
is more likely than not that the individual is a victim of human trafficking.
 The individual discloses on their own that they are a victim of human trafficking,
exploitation, sex trafficking, forced prostitution, or labor trafficking.
 An individual is considered to be a suspected victim of human trafficking under the
following circumstances:
 They answer in the affirmative to the established human trafficking questions
asked by staff, yet they maintain they are not a victim of human/sex/labor
trafficking.

is more likely than not that the individual is a victim of human trafficking, yet
they maintain they are not a victim of human/sex/labor trafficking.
 The individual discloses on their own that they are a victim with reference to any
combination of the following terminology: human trafficking, exploitation, sex
trafficking, forced prostitution, or labor trafficking yet later they maintain they are
not a victim of human/sex/labor trafficking.
 Based on the display of indicators of exploitation and/or the utilization of

professional opinion of Navajo County Attorney’s Office/Victim Services staff
that the individual is a suspected victim of human trafficking.

Director for 10% of the contract amount, and authorize the Public Works Director to execute the
necessary documents.
Background:
This project is a pavement rehabilitation project.  The project is located on Concho Highway (County
#5020) in Navajo County, Arizona. The project begins at approximate Milepost (MP) 3.3 and extends
east to approximately MP 6.3.  The work consists of 3 inches of mill and fill of asphalt paving and
improvements of 14 existing turnouts.  These treatments will extend the life of the pavement by
correcting surface distresses such as cracking, raveling, and minor rutting and protecting the underlying
pavement structure. The primary purpose of this Project is to improve the roadway condition and safety
of the traveling public.
Sealed bids were received by the Board of Supervisors at the Navajo County Governmental Center,
Holbrook, Arizona until 4:00 PM M.S.T., May 9, 2024, in strict accordance with the specifications on file
in the office of the Navajo County Department of Public Works. No bids were accepted after 4:00 PM
M.S.T. The bids were publicly opened and read aloud at 4:15 PM. Three bids were received and Sunland
Asphalt & Construction, LLC. was the lowest responsible, responsive bidder.
If approved, a no-cost change order changing the project name from Concho Highway Pavement
Preservation Contract Number B24-02-015, will be changed to Concho Highway; Golden Horse Ln -
White Antelope Rd, Pavement Rehabilitation Contract Number B24-02-015 to accurately reflect the
project's limit and scope of work.
Recommendation: 
Staff is recommending award of Contract B24-02-015 Concho Highway Pavement Preservation to
Sunland Asphalt & Construction, LLC.
