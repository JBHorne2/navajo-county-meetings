CALL TO THE PUBLIC:
Celia Laughlin, 2169 Hashknife, Overgaard, stated that she is a PC for her
Precinct.  She indicated that there are many concerned citizens in the county and
state.  She requested that an Election Integrity Task Force agenda item be added
to the next meeting and that the meeting be in person.  She requested that the
machines be removed, that elections go back to paper ballots and that the ballots
from the 2022 Election be preserved until they can be investigated and approved. 
She talked about voters questioning the validity of elections and indicated that a
task force to examine election processes and procedures would help restore
confidence.  She talked about the reasons for requesting a task force.  She
addressed the requirement for the voting locations to have a Wi-Fi unit that can
communicate with the Navajo County Elections Office and indicated that it provides
a backdoor for hackers.  She talked about concerns with the drop boxes not having
24-hour video surveillance.  She inquired if the GOP and Democratic Chairman will
obtain lists of all members of their party that will be working at the polling stations
and Election Office before, during and after election day.  She indicated that there
are problems and inefficiencies throughout the Eric Systems, which is still used.
She reiterated her request that a Task Force be added to the next agenda, that the
machines be removed, that paper ballots be returned and that the 2022 ballots be
preserved until an investigation by the task force can be conducted.
Chairman Whiting thanked Ms. Laughlin for her comments and requested that
she share her information with Melissa Buckley.
Supervisor Seymore talked about considering having a public forum or an item
on a future agenda.
Steve Carbajal, 650 N. Penrod Rd., Show Low, addressed the Supervisors
attending the meetings in person.  He talked about the Pinetop Town Hall that
Supervisor Whitesinger held, and that Supervisor Seymore and Recorder Michael
Sample attended.  He addressed comments about them not knowing how the
voting process takes place in county.  He requested that an agenda item be put on
a future meeting to discuss how voting system works in Navajo County and that the
Elections Department explain the process, indicating that they will have more
public at meeting to help them understand as well.
Chairman Whiting requested that Mr. Carbajal share his information with Ms.
Buckley.

LIBRARY DISTRICT: Board of Directors Session: 
 
REGULAR AGENDA: 
Katy Pearson (appearing virtually) stated most libraries have sales annually but
have a lot of books to dispose of after.  She indicated that the thrift stores and used
bookstores don’t have the capacity to accept donations as large as the one that the
district would need to do annually.  She stated that they have made every effort to
dispose of the books without throwing them away but that they still have a large
amount left every year.  She stated that the district recommends holding an annual
book sale to dispose of the books which will still benefit the libraries, indicating that
the sale proceeds would go back into purchasing more library materials as well as
services for the district.  She stated that they are requesting that it be done
annually to keep the funds and books in the district due to running out of storage
capacity at the district office for everything not circulating through the libraries
anymore.  She indicated that they have a tentative schedule with the Fairgrounds
to hold it April 22-26, 2024, from two to seven, that they will have 2 staff members
manning it at all times, and that they are offering the books at $5 a bag, stating that
it will be whatever they can fit in a bag.  She indicated that the proceeds will be
used on the volunteer libraries first and then the other libraries in the district.  She
stated that they would also like to hold a community expo and invite other
departments and local businesses, indicating that they could possibly have press
coverage.
Supervisor Seymore made a motion to approve Navajo County Library District
holding an annual book sale for books discarded by the district and its member
libraries, motion seconded by Vice-Chairwoman Benally.  Vote unanimous
approving the motion.

Section 2521 – Wireless Communication Facilities 
Purpose 
The purpose of this Section is to establish rules and regulations for the siting of wireless communications 
facilities.  The goals of this Section are to provide for the development of wireless communication services 
County-wide by:  
1. Considering the public health, safety, and welfare. 
 
2. Considering impact on adjacent properties. 
 
3. Encouraging configuration which minimizes additional visual impact through proper siting, design, 
landscape, and camouflage techniques. 
 
4. Considering historical and environmentally sensitive areas. 
 
5. Encouraging the collocation of facilities. 
 
Definitions 
As used herein, capitalized terms shall have the following meanings: 
1. Alternative Tower Structure: Vertical components not generally designed for use as antenna support 
structures including, but not limited to, structures such as church steeples, ballpark light poles, and 
water towers. 
 
2. Antenna: Any exterior device for transmitting and receiving wireless communication mounted on a 
tower, alternative tower structure, building, or structure and used for transmitting and receiving 
wireless communication for a fee to more than one (1) customer at one time.  
 
3. Antenna, Attached: An antenna mounted on the exterior of an existing building, silo, smokestack, 
water tower, utility or power pole, existing wireless communication tower, or an alternative support 
structure. 
 
4. Antenna, Concealed (stealth): An antenna with a support structure that screens or camouflages the 
presence of antennas and/or towers from public view, in a manner appropriate to the site’s context and 
surrounding environment.  Examples of concealed antennas include, but are not limited to, manmade 
trees, clock towers, flagpoles that do not exceed ten feet (10’) above the maximum building height, 
light structures, steeples, water tanks, and architectural façade and parapet features.

SECTION 1. Article 25 is hereby amended as follows: 
 
Section 2521 – Wireless Communication Facilities 
Purpose 
The purpose of this Section is to establish rules and regulations for the siting of wireless 
communications facilities.  The goals of this Section are to provide for the development of 
wireless communication services County-wide by:  
1. Considering the public health, safety, and welfare. 
 
2. Considering impact on adjacent properties. 
 
3. Encouraging configuration which minimizes additional visual impact through proper 
siting, design, landscape, and camouflage techniques. 
 
4. Considering historical and environmentally sensitive areas. 
 
5. Encouraging the collocation of facilities. 
 
Definitions

<!-- page 109 -->
As used herein, capitalized terms shall have the following meanings: 
1. Alternative Tower Structure: Vertical components not generally designed for use as 
antenna support structu res including, but not limited to, structures such as church 
steeples, ballpark light poles, and water towers. 
 
2. Antenna: Any exterior device for transmitting and receiving wireless communication 
mounted on a tower, alternative tower structure, building, or structure and used for 
transmitting and receiving wireless communication for a fee to more than one (1) 
customer at one time.  
 
3. Antenna, Attached: An antenna mounted on the exterior of an existing building, silo, 
smokestack, water tower, utility or power pole, existing wireless communication tower, 
or an alternative support structure. 
 
4. Antenna, Concealed (stealth):  An antenna with a support structure that screens or 
camouflages the presence of antennas and/or towers from public view, in a manner 
appropriate to the site’s context and surrounding environment.  Examples of concealed 
antennas include, but are not limite d to, manmade trees, clock towers, flagpoles that do 
not exceed ten feet (10’) above the maximum building height, light structures, steeples, 
water tanks, and architectural façade and parapet features. 
 
5. Certification: A written statement of the fact to be certified, made under oath by the 
applicant or licensed professional working for the applicant and notarized. 
 
6. Collocation: Use by two (2) or more wireless communication providers located on the 
same tower or alternative tower structure. 
 
7. Commercial Covera ge: A single FCC licensee’s network of wireless communications 
facilities providing a level of service to all areas of the community which, when fully 
developed, will permit viable commercial operation. 
 
8. FAA: The Federal Aviation Administration. 
 
9. Facility, Existing: A wireless communication facility in active use and for which a 
building permit has been properly issued and has not expired before the effective date of 
this Section. 
 
10. Facility, New: A wireless communications facility proposed to be located where a facility 
does not currently exist. 
 
11. FCC: The Federal Communications Commission.

<!-- page 110 -->
12. Height: The distance from the finished grade at the antenna tower base to the highest 
point of the tower. Overall tower height includes the base pad, mounting structures,  and 
panel antennae, but excludes lightning rods and whip antennae. 
 
13. Non-Residential: A property contained within the RU -5, RU-10, RU-10, RU-20, IND-1, 
or IND -2 zoning districts. Additionally, a property contained within the RU -1, A -
General, or Commercial -Residential zoning districts when not within a platted 
subdivision. 
 
14. Residential: A property contained within the R1 -43, R1-10, R-2, or R-3 zoning districts.  
Additionally, a property contained within the RU -1, A -General, or Commercial -
Residential zoning districts when within a platted subdivision. 
 
15. Tower, Lattice: A self -support structure, erected on the ground, which consists of cross -
bracing of structural steel to support antennae and related equipment. 
 
16. Tower, Monopole: A self -support structure, with a single shaft of wood, steel, or 
concrete, and a platform for antenna arrayed at the top known as a "top hat." 
 
17. Wireless Communication: Any techn ology for transmitting communication through the 
air. 
 
18. Wireless Communication Facility: Any combination of one (1) or more antennae, towers, 
and/or structures or equipment used for the transmission of wireless communication. 
 
19. Wireless Communication Provide r: Any FCC licensed service provider for Navajo 
County and any supplier of wireless communication facilities for those providers. 
 
20. Use, Permanent: The active, daily use of antennae for the commercial transmission and 
receipt of wireless communication intended at the time of its installation and approved to 
be actively used for a permanent basis. 
 
21. Use, Temporary: The active, daily use of antennae for the commercial transmission and 
receipt of wireless communication intended at the time of its installation an d approved to 
be actively used for a specific period of time. 
 
22. User, Single: A single dwelling or a single business.

All wireless communications facilities requiring a Special Use Permit shall be subject to this 
Section except those used by a single user and not otherwise restricted within that Zoning 
District, including, but not limited to, radio and devices necessary for the use of a subscription to 
a commercial wireless provider service such as wireless internet and satellite TV.

The following provisions are applicable to all wireless communication requests requiring a 
Special Use Permit: 
1. Principal or Accessory Use: Antennas and towers may be considered either principal or 
accessory uses to the principal use of the property. 
 
2. Lot Size: For purposes of determining whether the installation of a tower or antenna 
complies with the applicable zoning district regulations, even though the antennas or 
towers may be located on a separately leased portion of the lot, the zoning district 
requirements of the entire overall lot shall control requirements, including, but not limited 
to, setbacks, lot-coverage percentages, and other such requirements. 
 
3. Wireless Communications Facility Characteristics: 
 
a. Wireless communication facilitie s including the tower structure, antennae, and 
related electrical and mechanical equipment, shall use materials, colors, textures, 
screening, and landscaping to minimize their visual impact on the natural and 
surrounding setting, unless subject to applicable standards of the FAA. 
 
b. Towers shall not be lighted, unless required by the FAA or other applicable 
authority. If lighting is required by the FAA or other authorities, all effort shall be 
taken to minimize light pollution. 
 
c. All wireless communication fac ilities shall maintain compliance with applicable 
State or local building codes under which they were constructed and any 
regulations of the FAA, the FCC, and any other federal government agency with 
the authority to regulate them or their components. Shou ld applicable federal

communications facilities that are not in compliance shall be removed at the 
owner's expense if not brought into compliance within thirty (30) days after 
written demand by Navajo County. 
 
d. The following setback requirements shall apply to all towers within the 
appropriate classification: 
 
i. Residential: Towers must be set back 2 feet from any lot line for each 1 
foot in height of the tower, including tower and antenna attachments. 
 
ii. Non-Residential: Towers must be set back 1 foot from any lot line for 
each 1 foot in height of the tower, including tower and antenna 
attachments.

facility, including the base, platform, and attached antennae shall not 
exceed one hundred ninety-nine feet (199’) above grade.

(199’) in height are prohibited unless collocating on an existing structure (other 
than a wireless communication facility tower) and unless there is a plan to 
mitigate interference with military and commercial aircraft, disruption of avian 
migration and nesting, and to preserve nighttime skies for astronomical

requirements. 
 
h. Facilities that are located on existing or replaced streetlights, traffic signal poles, 
or electrical utility poles are exempt from any setback requirements. 
 
i. Setback and separation distances shall be applicable irrespective of municipal and 
County jurisdictional boundaries. 
 
j. No signs shall be permitted at a wireless communications facility with the 
exception of a single, one (1) square foot sign for each carrier, to provide 
emergency contact numbers.

height restrictions, within the applicable Zoning District shall apply to wireless 
communication facilities.

not apply to generators used in emergency situations should the regular po wer

Performance Criteria

facilities. 
 
5. Concealed/Stealth antennas  
 
6. New towers/facilities suitable for collocating multiple providers. 
 
7. New towers/facilities under ninety-nine feet (99’).

New Proposed Wireless Facility 
 Located on Parcel 208-18-003J | This parcel is Zoned as A- General Commercial and is 49.48 acres
 Designed as a Monopole
 Located within a 50’x50’ enclosure surrounded by an 8’ Chain-link fence Compound
 Overall height of 199’ (195’ Top of Steel, with a 4’ Lightning Rod)
 Allows for co-location and is capable of hosting multiple wireless carriers

<!-- page 281 -->
Background 
 11 community members were invited to attend a community meeting which  
was held on November 8th, 2023. There was one attendee who spoke in favor 
of the proposed WCF . 
 The Navajo County Planning and Development Services Staff has 
recommended approval of the Special Use Permit.
 The Planning and Zoning Commission took place on February 15th, 2024, 
where approval of the Special Use Permit was recommended to the Navajo 
County Board of Supervisors.
 One community member spoke in favor of the proposed wireless 
communications facility at the Planning and Zoning Commission because it 
would improve E911 service in the area.

<!-- page 282 -->
Least Intrusive Means 
 After conducting a thorough review of each parcel within the desired 
radius and within Navajo County jurisdiction, it was determined that 
the selected parcel, zoned A-General (General Commercial), best 
suited requirements as noted in the Zoning Ordinance.
 Vertical Bridge has identified a coverage gap in this area and has 
determined that a new wireless communications facility is needed at 
this location with this height (199’) to remedy that gap.
 The subject parcel is bordered by other A-General and unspecified 
zoned parcels. There are primarily rural residential properties and 
grazing land along the North, East, and West sides and USFS owned 
land from the South. The proposed location is near the Southeast 
corner and approximately 1,553' South of Hwy 260.

Setbacks 
 Setback distance between the wireless communication facility 
and the (2) nearest property lines are 199’-10”& 459'-11" from 
the center of the tower.
 The parcel is approximately 49.48 acres, which provides 
sufficient space for the tower to meet the required setbacks and 
fall radius as required by Navajo County
 No existing towers or structures are located within the 
geographic area required to meet the initial Anchor tenant’s 
capacity and coverage requirements 
 Site will not only benefit nearby existing properties, but will also 
benefit visitors and emergency services

Co-location
 The Navajo County Zoning Ordinance encourages collocation which is defined as "Use 
by two (2) or more wireless communication providers located on the same tower or 
alternative tower structure”.
 Vertical Bridge has designed the proposed WCF with an overall height of 199’ to allow 
up to four wireless carriers to collocate, thereby reducing the need for future towers in 
the area
 As shown on the previous elevation slides, the RADs for the Anchor tenant and three 
additional carriers are listed below: 
 Proposed Anchor Tenant: 190’ RAD
 Future Tenant: 178’ RAD
 Future Tenant: 166’ RAD 
 Future Tenant: 154’ RAD
