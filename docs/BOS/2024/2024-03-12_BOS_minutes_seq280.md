CALL TO THE PUBLIC :
Celia Laughlin, 2169 Hashknife, Overgaard, stated that she is a PC for her Precinct.  She indicated that there are many concerned citizens in the county and state.  She requested that an Election Integrity Task Force agenda item be added to the next meeting and that the meeting be in person.  She requested that the machines be removed, that elections go back to paper ballots and that the ballots from the 2022 Election be preserved until they can be investigated and approved.  She talked about voters questioning the validity of elections and indicated that a task force to examine election processes and procedures would help restore confidence.  She talked about the reasons for requesting a task force.  She addressed the requirement for the voting locations to have a Wi-Fi unit that can communicate with the Navajo County Elections Office and indicated that it provides a backdoor for hackers.  She talked about concerns with the drop boxes not having 24-hour video surveillance.  She inquired if the GOP and Democratic Chairman will obtain lists of all members of their party that will be working at the polling stations and Election Office before, during and after election day.  She indicated that there are problems and inefficiencies throughout the Eric Systems, which is still used.  She reiterated her request that a Task Force be added to the next agenda, that the machines be removed, that paper ballots be returned and that the 2022 ballots be preserved until an investigation by the task force can be conducted.
Chairman Whiting thanked Ms. Laughlin for her comments and requested that she share her information with Melissa Buckley.
Supervisor Seymore talked about considering having a public forum or an item on a future agenda.
Steve Carbajal, 650 N. Penrod Rd., Show Low, addressed the Supervisors attending the meetings in person.  He talked about the Pinetop Town Hall that Supervisor Whitesinger held, and that Supervisor Seymore and Recorder Michael Sample attended.  He addressed comments about them not knowing how the voting process takes place in county.  He requested that an agenda item be put on a future meeting to discuss how voting system works in Navajo County and that the Elections Department explain the process, indicating that they will have more public at meeting to help them understand as well.
Chairman Whiting requested that Mr. Carbajal share his information with Ms. Buckley.

LIBRARY DISTRICT : Board of Directors Session :

REGULAR AGENDA :
Katy Pearson (appearing virtually) stated most libraries have sales annually but have a lot of books to dispose of after.  She indicated that the thrift stores and used bookstores don't have the capacity to accept donations as large as the one that the district would need to do annually.  She stated that they have made every effort to dispose of the books without throwing them away but that they still have a large amount left every year.  She stated that the district recommends holding an annual book sale to dispose of the books which will still benefit the libraries, indicating that the sale proceeds would go back into purchasing more library materials as well as services for the district.  She stated that they are requesting that it be done annually to keep the funds and books in the district due to running out of storage capacity at the district office for everything not circulating through the libraries anymore.  She indicated that they have a tentative schedule with the Fairgrounds to hold it April 22-26, 2024, from two to seven, that they will have 2 staff members manning it at all times, and that they are offering the books at $5 a bag, stating that it will be whatever they can fit in a bag.  She indicated that the proceeds will be used on the volunteer libraries first and then the other libraries in the district.  She stated that they would also like to hold a community expo and invite other departments and local businesses, indicating that they could possibly have press coverage.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to approve Navajo County Library District holding an annual book sale for books discarded by the district and its member libraries, motion seconded by  Vice-Chairwoman Benally .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Supervisor Peshlakai made a motion  to return to regular session, motion seconded by  Supervisor Seymore .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES :

a.

Presentation on Economic Development Activities :
Chris Pasterz (appearing virtually), Economic Development Director for Navajo County and Executive Director for Real AZ, introduced himself, stating that he would welcome the opportunity to present to any districts or communities that are interested.  A presentation was shown.  He talked about the Real AZ economic development priorities, indicating that there were three priorities determined by Real AZ, as follows: Priority #1 – Coal Community Transition, Industry Recruitment, and Job Creation/Workforce Development; Priority #2 Strategic Planning and Data Collection and Branding; and Priority #3 Broadband.  He further talked about the Economic Development Director duties, indicating that it has been important to meet the members and communities across the region and build those relationships.  He stated that that they need to engage the different communities in a way that they see fit and that fits with their community values.  He addressed some of the projects for the different priorities, such as participating in the BRECC (Building Resilient Economies in Coal Communities) project for the power plant closures/coal community transition.  He talked about some of the wins they have had for different priorities, such as the RV Park that will be in Winslow and the Real AZ website, which has available industrial and commercial sites for sale throughout the region and the Brownfields grant.  He talked about the Federal Funding Summits that have been held.  He shared his appreciation to the Northern Arizona Workforce Development Board for their work with workforce and job creation.  He talked about the Workforce Housing Needs Assessment Report that they have provided to each jurisdiction.  He addressed the Broadband projects for Navajo County and Apache County.  He provided his contact information and indicated that he can present to anyone that is interested.
Supervisor Seymore thanked Mr. Pasterz for his involvement and for reaching out to the communities.  He stated that this will not only benefit the communities now but, in the future, and he indicated that he appreciates the work Mr. Pasterz is doing.
Vice-Chairwoman Benally thanked Mr. Pasterz for work he is doing, stating that many of the communities in District One have attended and are interested in Coal Community grants.  She talked about attending the Kayenta Township Meeting last night and stated that she is thankful for the NPC project to assist with the locals going to college and that it will be a big benefit to the area to have the new campus.  She stated that she is looking forward to the projects that are coming up.
Supervisor Whitesinger thanked Mr. Pasterz for the work he is doing in the region.  She talked about challenges and impacts for the region with the coal industry closures and stated that being able to partner with communities and the region will allow growth in the county.  She indicated that work and conversations are important and that she is grateful for conversations.  She talked about being able to recognize the opportunity for economic growth.  She addressed discussions regarding the impact on the budget with the plant closures, indicating that it will have a significant impact, but that being able to look to the future is a positive thing.  She thanked him for the information and the work that is being done.
Supervisor Peshlakai thanked Mr. Pasterz for the information.  He talked about the impact on the communities and schools with the plant closures.  He addressed being able to work together and finding new opportunities for growth that will help the communities.
Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Pasterz for work he is doing and for the conversations that he continues to have with the with Board and the communities to keep everyone working together.

PUBLIC WORKS :

a.

Consideration of approving the Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) between the White Mountain Apache Tribe Division of Transportation and Navajo County for assistance in road projects :
Madhav Mundle (appearing virtually) stated that Navajo County and the White Mountain Apache Tribe, Division of Transportation, have had a long-standing MOA, which works as an intergovernmental agreement to work on transportation related projects.  He stated that the MOA is past due and that the proposed MOA has not changed but only had a few modifications to stay current with the contact information.  He stated that staff recommends approval.
Supervisor Whitesinger clarified that the MOA's are in place for all the tribal communities and townships.  She stated that the partnerships that are created throughout the communities are fundamental for growth and capacity building throughout each township and tribal community.  She thanked Mr. Mundle for the work done on this item.  She stated that the tribal communities have separate funding support, and that Navajo County supports in some of the conversations and being able to provide some of work in the communities.  She indicated that the conversations have been ongoing for many years.

Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion  to approve the Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) between the White Mountain Apache Tribe Division of Transportation and Navajo County for assistance in road projects, motion seconded by  Supervisor Seymore .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

b.

Consideration of an Intergovernmental Agreement between the State of Arizona and Navajo County for federal Transportation Alternatives Grant for a sidewalk project in the Joseph City area :
Madhav Mundle (appearing virtually) stated that this is a sidewalk improvement multi-use pathway project in Joseph City.  He stated that the Board previously approved CDBG funds for this project, that they also applied for a transportation alternative grant, which is a federal grant distributed through ADOT.  He stated that the grant is only 5.7%.  He indicated that in addition to the CDBG and this grant, they anticipate that there may be some HURF funds that will be used from the County as well.  He stated that staff recommends approval.

Supervisor Peshlakai made a motion  to approve an Intergovernmental Agreement between the State of Arizona and Navajo County for federal Transportation Alternatives Grant for a sidewalk project in the Joseph City area, motion seconded by  Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE: Report from County Manager, County Attorney and Board members :
Supervisor Seymore reported on the opportunity he had to go to Washington, D.C. to testify before the House of Natural Resources and Federal Land Subcommittee on a bill that would convey federal land to Navajo County and Apache County that would impact the cemeteries in Pinedale and Alpine.  He stated that it was a great opportunity and that they have been working on this for many years.  He stated that he hopes to work with some kind of resolution in future to address the issues raised during the Call to the Public, with either an agenda item or public forum regarding how the county does their elections.  He thanked everyone for the work they do and indicated that he hopes everyone has a wonderful spring and Easter season.
Supervisor Peshlakai reported on preparing to assist in the community clean ups in his district that are coming as part of Earth Day.  He stated that they had another successful District Two Community Meeting which was held at the Hardrock Chapter and that they are looking forward to going into another Chapter location next month.  He indicated that as far as projects in District Two, everything is going well, and he thanked staff for all of the assistance with helping move things forward.  He indicated that he is grateful for the staff's continued support.
Supervisor Whitesinger thanked those who provided support at townhall that they hosted in Pinetop-Lakeside, indicating that there was a good response.  She indicated that there were a lot of questions regarding the upcoming elections, a lot of discussion points on what it looks like and information for the public on elections department.  She stated that they will continue to provide information to the public on elections and how the county prepares for elections.  She stated that she participated in a meet and greet meet, where they met the Secretary of Interior, Deb Haaland.  She talked about the great job that Rochelle Lacapa does serving as the Chair of the Fort Apache Heritage Foundation and where she serves as one of the Board of Directors.  She addressed the upcoming White Mountain Regional Transportation Meeting, where she serves as Chair, that includes Navajo County, Apache County, and surrounding townships, indicating that she is grateful to be a part of those conversations.  She extended an invitation for all of the communities to attend the meeting.  She thanked the Navajo County team for their support in helping get the committee going.
Chairman Whiting recognized Supervisor Seymore for his leadership in testifying in Washington last week, indicating that this is an issue that they have worked on for a while and that it is important to the citizens in Navajo County and Apache County.  He thanked everyone that support him.  He acknowledged the citizens that came and talked about voter integrity, stating that it is important to continue to evaluate and review the process and make sure it's transparent and working for the citizens.  He shared his appreciation to the Board for allowing him to be able to work with a lot of the forestry industry, indicating that there have been a lot of ups and downs.  He acknowledged Brad Worsley on his letter to the editor regarding some of the good news that's been happening, stating that Mr. Worsley acknowledged him for his continued involvement, and that he feels it represents all of the board and that what they are doing matters.  He indicated that the White Mountain Stewardship has doubled the previous amount of work done and indicated that it is not without constant monitoring, support, and meeting with the stakeholders.  He thanked everyone for their efforts and indicated that it's important to keep leadership at the local, state, and federal level aware of what's happening in the Arizona Forests.  He stated that they are working on doing a tour in May to bring some of the leaders together and help them understand what's currently happening in the forest and some of the issues that are being faced.
