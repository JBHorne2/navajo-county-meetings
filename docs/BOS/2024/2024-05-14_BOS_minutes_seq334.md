Mark Thomas, 5871 Bobcat Drive.
Chairman Whiting inquired if Mr. Thomas had new information to provide.
Mr. Thomas indicated that he did.  He spoke about the presentation at the last meeting, indicating that for the most part it was pretty much by the book and exactly what you would think should happen, but in reality, that's not what is taking place. He stated that it appears that the county knows little about the process of the cost of hand counting ballots, indicating that an estimated 1.1 million was mentioned by the county personnel and spoke about being able to demonstrate, if asked, how a hand count could have occurred for 65,000 voters for about $300,000 or less. Mr. Thomas stated that the situation in Mohave County was incorrectly addressed in last month's presentation, indicating that Mohave County had chosen to hand count, but the Attorney General Kris Mayes and Secretary of State Adrian Fontes threatened criminal charges and fines and that the Arizona legislator is currently seeking a censure of the Attorney General as she has no legal standing to do so. He further stated that she misled the county or scared them when there is no legislation or language that a county cannot choose to hand count. He stated that the Supervisors took a loyal oath of office and have the duty to stand up for the people and that they demand that the Board uphold their oath.

Donna Shurwin, 1207 S. Forest Lane, Pinetop.
Chairman Whiting inquired if Ms. Shurwin had anything new to share.
Ms. Shurwin stated that she did.  She indicated that she wanted to make sure that the county knew that Missouri, in 2022, conducted a hand count of one of their actual counties and compared to the rest of the state, when the counting was done, they were 20 minutes behind the tabulating machines from other counties and after they had proof that the hand count could be done in a timely fashion they created and published a free 150-page manual for hand counting, which the Navajo County could obtain, and that in between now and the July 30th, when the first primary election happens, it could be taken into consideration. She inquired how the county advertises for poll workers, indicating that the website says election board members and she asked how do people know where to look when they want to apply to be a poll worker? She inquired if the county website could be changed and make it so that people can, on a search, find where they can apply to be a poll worker? She stated that the drop boxes, according to the Arizona Elections Manual, are supposed to be manned by two people 24 hours a day, but that now they are putting money towards buying cameras, but that after reading a little deeper into the election manual they discovered that it actually states it has to be manually watched 24 hours a day.  She stated that now the camera idea is obsolete idea because they need people to be able to man every drop box in the county, with two people, one from each party.  She inquired how that would be done and stated that they need to get rid of the drop boxes.  She stated that according to a friend that works in the office, that for the 2022 election 24,000 people mailed in ballots and that it takes one person a full day to count 300 ballots, which if you divide that into 24,000 it would equal eighty days, she inquired how that would be done in a timely fashion for signature verification, indicating that it's not doable. She stated that they need to clean things up. She stated that the Board is there servants and that in the pledge it says indivisible but that we are divided and that she doesn't know why there's such a holdback and the county doesn't seem to want to do what they are asking.  She stated that previous to getting machines in 2015 they had to hand counted.  She indicated that there's a backups provision in the manual in our election training for if the electricity were to go down, they can do everything manually. She inquired why they don't do that, indicating that they need to get rid of the machines and do a manual count.

Rick Burris, 2883 Silverdollar St., Overgaard, spoke about the discrepancy between the Whiteriver Voting Center and Central Elections at the County from the recount that occurred on December 9, 2022.  He stated that a recount of election day ballots from the DS200 from the Whiteriver Vote Center and the recount at the Central in Holbrook  an error reported from Whiteriver Vote Center, zip drive numbers along with ballots not counted of the DS200 did not match the recount at Central in Holbrook, indicating that the zip drive counted 855 ballots, that the DS200 went down at the voting location and there was no power, resulting in 145 ballots not counted by the DS200 and the 145 ballots were received at central in a bag for recount. PUBLIC HEARING:  Consideration and possible adoption of  Resolution Number 17-2024 , approving/denying a text amendment to Article 20, Section 2001 and Article 25, Section 2518 of the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance, regarding Institutional Residences and other Group Homes which do not qualify as Group Homes for the Disabled :
A presentation was shown. Cody Cooper discussed the item, indicating that staff was instructed to look at the group home policies to see if any updates were necessary, that they have investigated it, done some research and that they are bringing forward recommendations that they have found through the research.  He stated that this is for group homes, including institutional residences, that would not qualify through the group homes for the disabled.  He indicated that they wanted to create recommendations that are clear and concrete so that the public and developers coming in fully understand what's expected for these type of group homes.  Mr. Cooper stated that a special use permit would still be required, but only in the multi-family or commercial residence.  He reviewed the changes that are being recommended, including separation requirements, occupancy limits, exterior appearance, parking, compliance with building, environmental health and fire safety, and state licensing for the facilities.  He stated that the Navajo County Planning and Zoning Commission did unanimously recommend approval and that staff is also recommending approval.  He indicated that they have not received any public comments, through hearings or phone calls.

Chairman Whiting opened the meeting for public comments.  There being no comments, the public hearing was closed.

Supervisor Whitesinger requested clarification regarding the difference with the number of residents allowed for the different zoning areas.
Mr. Cooper addressed the differences between the multi-family neighborhoods and the commercial residential neighborhoods.  He stated that they want to make sure that there is adequate space for the residents so that they are getting proper treatment while there.
Supervisor Whitesinger inquired if they articulate the square footage for the rooms that allow two occupants or if it's just given.
Mr. Cooper stated that they haven't articulated any minimum square footage, that it is something that they can look at and consider.  He spoke about different considerations that they would look at on a case-by-case basis to make sure it's appropriate.
Supervisor Seymore shared his appreciation to Mr. Cooper and his team for the work done on this item.  He stated that they have done a great job and he spoke about them also encompassing parking to make it more appealing to neighbors.

Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion  to adopt  Resolution Number 17-2024 , approving a text amendment to Article 20, Section 2001 and Article 25, Section 2518 of the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance, regarding Institutional Residences and other Group Homes which do not qualify as Group Homes for the Disabled, motion seconded by  Supervisor Seymore .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion  to enter into the Navajo County Flood Control District Board of Directors Session, motion seconded by  Supervisor Peshlakai .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
