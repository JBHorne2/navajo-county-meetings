Broadband Update : Glenn Kephart thanked the Board for their support in moving the Broadband contract to EX2.  He stated that they meet with EX2 last week to discuss the project and their plans for the project.  He indicated that initial pricing should be received in about a week for the one hundred thirty-two miles of broadband and that they are looking at approximately eighteen months to complete the project.  He stated that they want to do underground as much as possible, that it could cost a little more but that there wouldn't be pole maintenance fees, but that they will continue to discuss the options.  He further stated that they have $19.7 million between the Arizona Commerce Authority (ACA) grant and the American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds, which is around the amount they believe the project will cost.  He indicated that on the grant agreement with the ACA, that was approved at last board meeting, they have received information that the Governor's Office is wanting to add some language from Federal standpoint so it may be revised which could cause a little delay and that it will have to go back before the Board if it is revised.
Ken Maruyama talked about the possible revision to the grant agreement and indicated that he hasn't heard back from ACA on the timeframe for getting the agreement completed.
Mr. Kephart stated that they met with EX2 on Thursday and then on Friday they had a meeting with a last mile service provider, Webcom, and Ben Blink from the Governor's Office.  He indicated that they are interested in Navajo County, that they have a plan to serve about thirty percent of the unserved in Arizona and that they are looking at five counties to go after grant money for last mile services.  He further indicated that they have already done a lot of work, looked at the needs and demographics and they are putting together a plan that they would submit next year to be able to provide last mile services.
Grant Evans talked about the work that EX2 will be doing.  He stated that the goal is to go before the Board at the November 8, 2022, meeting for approval of the design and construction contract.  He indicated that they are negotiating for design, construction, operation, maintenance, and commercialization of the network.
Mr. Kephart talked about how fortunate Navajo County is being able to bring Mr. Evans on with his knowledge.
Supervisor Seymore inquired what the next biggest hurdle would be for starting work with the broadband project?
Mr. Evans stated that permitting review times and permitting from other agencies would be the biggest critical item.
Supervisor Whiting inquired if they are confident with the November date to present to the Board?
Mr. Kephart indicated that they may not be able to execute the whole contract with EX2 prior to receiving the modified contract from the ACA with the modifications from the Governor's Office, but that they believe they will be able to negotiate a contract prior to the November meeting.

Opioid Funding :  Janelle Linn provided an update on the opioid settlement.  She stated that the opioid epidemic has quickly become one of the most significant public health crises ever and that even though the opioid deaths from over-prescribing opioids and heroin have decreased, the problem overall has really increased.  She further stated that drug addiction is a complex and massive health disorder characterized by a chronic and relapsing nature and that relapses are part of the recovery process.  She indicated that in 2018 the Health and Human Services came out with a five-point strategy to combat the crisis, focusing on better prevention, treatment, and recovery services, removing barriers to high quality care for substance abuse users, better data on the epidemics particular to local jurisdictions and impacts of COVID-19 on issues and better targeting of overdose reversing drugs and embracing a range of intervention, including reductions in current resources. She indicated that opioid deaths increased by thirty percent during the pandemic.  She talked about how telehealth and virtual health options did expand, which has been a benefit moving forward.  She indicated that they are now focusing on primary prevention, harm reduction, evidence-based treatment, and recovery support.  She talked about the guidelines followed when funding usually comes in but indicated that this is not the same, seeing how it was a lawsuit award, and addressed the guiding principles that John Hopkins University Bloomberg School of Public Health came up with to allow the funds responsibly and quickly.  She stated that they also came up with indicators to assess the readiness of the agencies to receive the funds from the settlement.  She talked about the agreement and the approach to getting ahead of the problem.  She indicated that it is important to use evidence-based and population health-driven processes, that they treat it as a public health issue and not criminal justice issues, that they determine the correct partners and correct metrics to be used, that they have a baseline date to ensure measurability, and that they have improvement goals, objectives, and a timeline.  She stated that the need to determine long term goals of what they want to fundamentally change and consider ultimate goals.  She talked about the steering committee and consortium groups that will be needed, as well as partners, both tribal and non-tribal.  She indicated that John Hopkins put out a primer with examples of evidence-based projects that can be tailored to different communities, which they will take into consideration.  She talked about some of the things that the Health Department is already doing and indicated that it's their responsibility to provide tools and resources to ensure that those suffering from substance abuse disorder can find solutions so that they can live long and productive lives.
Chairwoman Whitesinger inquired as to specific numbers for the county.
Ms. Linn stated that there were ninety-three last year and that they were able to successfully intervene and save about fifty percent of those lives.  She indicated that the law enforcement, health professionals and some community members carry Narcan, which has helped save lives.  She further indicated that they have three different reports that she can provide.  She stated that last year in Navajo County hospitalizations cost the county over forty-five thousand dollars.
Glenn Kephart talked about the importance of this item. He further talked about how it's Ms. Linn's responsibility to get everything together and then provide the information to the Board.   He stated that this is the recommendation for the process, that they are not asking for a decision to be made today, but if there are any suggestions for changes, the Board can let them know.

White Mountain Lakes County Recreation Improvement District Update :  Rochelle Lacapa addressed the potential timeline for the implementation of the new governance structure for White Mountain Lakes County Recreation Improvement District.  She indicated that it became a law on September 24, 2022, and that the new law allows the Board of Supervisors to convene a public hearing and determine if there is a need for a new governance structure and then appoint a Board of Directors for the District.  She indicated that this would allow for them to have an elected Board instead of the Board of Supervisors serving as the Board of Directors.  She talked about the steps that need to be taken for them to have a governing board, the requirements to be on the Board, the application process, the manual and the timeline.
Supervisor Whiting addressed the requirements for being able to be on the Board.
Chairwoman Whitesinger inquired when they would have to run, since the first board will be appointed.
Ms. Lacapa stated that this Board will have to decide if it should be a three- or five-member board and addressed the timeline for the elected positions.
Chairwoman Whitesinger stated that the decisions that are made should be made to impact them in a positive way and long term.
Ms. Lacapa addressed the legislation regarding the restrictions and authority that the Board of Supervisors will have over the governing body for the first 4 years.
Supervisor Whiting thanked the team for the work that they have done with getting this done.  He talked about doing a training on open meeting law for the Districts and indicated that they want the District to succeed and be successful. He addressed having a five-member board verses three.  Chairwoman Whitesinger stated that the training should include the Roberts Rule of Order as well.  She thanked Supervisor Whiting for his work on this.
