SHERIFF'S OFFICE :

a.

Consideration of a Proclamation designating the week of May 15, 2022, to May 21, 2022, as "National Police Week" in Navajo County, Arizona, and urging all citizens to show their support to these dedicated professionals :  Sheriff David Clouse addressed the Board indicating that National Police Week is recognized in May.  He talked about the history of the proclamation.  He stated that on May 15, 2002 there were over one hundred names added to the US Memorial Wall in Washington, DC which marks 23,229 fallen officers in our nation and that there were twenty-six names added to the Arizona Memorial Wall in Phoenix on May 2, 2022 which marks 388 fallen officers in Arizona.  He indicated that May 15 is the day that is recognized to mourn the loss and honor the fallen officers.  He read the last paragraph of the proposed proclamation. He thanked the Board and the citizens of Navajo County for their support.  He talked about the support and gratitude that has been shown throughout the County for those who work in Public Safety over the past week.  He talked about the dangerous positions that the officers are placed in daily.

Vice-Chairman Peshlakai stated that they are honored to join in recognition of law enforcement officers and stated that they appreciate their service.
Supervisor Benally talked about the importance of Public Safety Officers and the dangers that they are placed in daily.  She thanked them for the services that they provide.
Supervisor Whiting thanked the Sheriff's Office for all that they do and indicated that they appreciate them.

Supervisor Whiting made a motion  to approve a Proclamation designating the week of May 15, 2022, to May 21, 2022, as "National Police Week" in Navajo County, Arizona, and urging all citizens to show their support to these dedicated professionals, motion seconded by  Supervisor Benally .  Motion passed 3-0 with Chairwoman Whitesinger and Supervisor Seymore being absent for the vote.

REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and approval of the amended Ordinance,  Ordinance Number 01-19  to clarify actions that are or are not permissible regarding outdoor fires :  Catrina Jenkins addressed the Board regarding the correction that needs to be made on Ordinance 01-19.

Vice-Chairman Peshlakai opened the meeting for public comments.  There being no comments, the Public Hearing was closed.

2.

Update on Fire Restrictions :  Catrina Jenkins stated that they do weekly meetings for the White Mountain Fire Restrictions Coordinating Group every Monday and that the voting members decided that the region will enter into Stage 2 Fire Restrictions on May 26, which includes the areas of Navajo County, Apache County, the Forest Service, BLM State Lands, Town of Pinetop-Lakeside and City of Show Low.
Chief Swanty addressed the Board regarding the Stage 2 Restrictions and the differences from Stage 1.  He stated that any burn permits that were issued have been terminated and that they are now null and void.  He indicated that they will be taking an aggressive stance on the restrictions moving into the holiday weekend.

Supervisor Benally inquired as to the approvals of the firework permits in the Consent Agenda with the new restrictions going into place and how the permits are contradictory with the new restrictions.
Ms. Jenkins addressed the Board regarding the firework permits, indicating that they work with the entities/agencies that submitted those permits, that those permits are supervised by fire and law enforcement officials and that if it is not safe they will not proceed with those firework shows.

Supervisor Whiting made a motion  to return to regular session, motion seconded by  Supervisor Benally .  Motion passed 3-0 with Chairwoman Whitesinger and Supervisor Seymore being absent for the vote.

Consideration of Task Agreement Number 13 to the Intergovernmental Agreement between the Hopi Tribe and Navajo County that authorizes funding assistance for the Division of Transportation to provide asphalt material and tires :  John Osgood addressed the Board regarding the item, stating that approval of this agreement will continue a successful partnership between the County and the Hopi Tribe for supportive road, infrastructure, maintenance and repair of public paved and non-paved road in District Two.  He stated that the source of funding is the Highway User Revenue Funds and that the fiscal impact of the item is $53,000.  Mr. Osgood further stated that staff recommends approval.
