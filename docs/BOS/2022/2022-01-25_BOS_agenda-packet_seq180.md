Submitted For: David Clouse, Sheriff 
Submitted By: Kimberley Willis, Sheriff's Office Finance Manager
Department: Sheriff 
Presented By: Mutualink, Sheriff David Clouse,
Lt. Alden Whipple
Subject:
Presentation regarding upcoming School Safety Interoperability project.
Background: 
The Navajo County Sheriff's Office is the recipient of funds from the Arizona State Treasurer's
Office relating to SB1823, Section 89, for the school safety program.  SB1823 Section 89 dictates
that of the amount appropriated to the school safety program line item shall be deposited in the
school safety interoperability fund established by section 41-1733, ARS, directing $150,000 to the
Navajo County Sheriff's Office. 
The monies appropriated may be spend for an interoperability communication system that: 1)
enables the deployment of secure, multimedia data communications system to a user base
consisting of public safety agencies. 2) provides a communications solution environment that
allows for identifying system users' identity, location, and operational status during an incident 3)
is capable of being deployed to end users on existing communication assets owned by
participating entities 4) allows participating entity to maintain discretionary real-time control of
all commnications assets 5) encrypts all media communications 6) ensures staff privacy 7) Is
United States Dept of Homeland Security certified qualified antiterrorism technology and 8) is
compatible with federal emergency management agency interoperable gateway systems for
disaster communications.
The Sheriff's Office will receive 4 quarterly electronic deposits beginning in November 2021.

Background:
The American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) of 2021 provided direct payments to state, tribal and local
governments to help address the negative economic impacts associated with the COVID19
pandemic.  The US Treasury provided detailed guidelines for the proper use of these funds and
the Navajo County Board of Supervisors created its own Guiding Principles for the use of ARPA
funds.  These Guiding Principles were used to create a project proposal vetting process (provided
as an attachment) which was then used to evaluate project proposals received by local government
partner entities, tribal entities, non-profit organizations, and staff. 
 
Proposal: Staff proposes a funding package of $21.1 M which allocates $18.7 M for county-wide
projects, $1.35 M for community partnerships, and $1 M for project management and
contingency.  This funding package leverages another $18.1 M of contributions and partnerships
from other sources for a total expected project value of $42.1 M for the communities of Navajo
County.     
 
Recommendation: 
Staff recommends approval of the proposed funding package for the ARPA projects.

Motion before the Board:
Consideration of abatement of real property APN 206-06-039 and authorizing the County
Attorney to initiate Civil enforcement (complaint for injunctive relief) in Justice Court or Superior
Court. 
Background:
The property at 1940 SR 260 - APN 206-06-039, zoned C-R, is in violation of Navajo County
Zoning Ordinance Section 2203.  Specifically, Section 2203 (2) states " An RV shall not be
placed in the following zones: I-1, I-2, C-R or Multi-Family Residential, except in an approved
RV Park or as may be permitted under Article 28 hereof."  Further, Section 2203 (3) states " No
RV shall be placed on a permanent basis."  The following is a summary of the facts and timeline
of the violation: 
The Navajo County Building and Zoning process allows for the temporary placement of
one RV on a property when a building permit has been issued while a dwelling is being
constructed on the property.
Two RV's and other structures have been on the property since at least June 2020. 
Navajo County is in receipt of multiple complaints about the code violation at the property
The property owner submitted for a septic permit on February 22, 2021, which is one of the
first steps toward obtaining a building permit.  The permit was denied because the proposed
septic system was within a wash area that conveys stormwater from an area greater than 20
acres, in violation of ADEQ regulations.
Since at least March, 2021 Navajo County Public Works staff has notified the property
owner that an Engineer licensed in the State of Arizona will be required to design the septic
system to conform to ADEQ requirements, and that our engineering staff would work with
their engineer to find a solution if possible.
The property was issued a deadline of August 27, 2021 to provide the engineered septic
design.
On September 9, 2021 Public Works staff received notification from the property owner's
engineer that they would be "stepping away" from the project "indefinitely." 
In subsequent discussions with the property owner, it was revealed that financial concerns
and other issues were preventing their moving ahead in the building permit process.  
Since the property owner(s) continue to live on the property in non-compliance of the County's

<!-- page 81 -->
zoning ordinance as outlined above, resolution may be best achieved through the judicial system
where the people involved can pursue their case.

Instructions  
 
At the first verification visit after the effective date of the 2013 CRS Coordinator's Manual, ONLY LINES 6 AND 13 NEED TO BE 
COMPLETED. These lines form the baseline data about the number of buildings and area of the SFHA for when the table is completed as 
part of the next annual recertification. The "period covered" entered in line 16 is the date that lines 6 and 13 are first completed.  
At all subsequent annual recertifications and cycle verification visits, the entire table is completed. The information in lines 6 and 13 from the 
last report is transferred to lines 1 and 10 in the next report.  
 
Instructions for the Columns  
 
Column A numbers are for the SFHA (the A and V Zones shown on the Flood Insurance Rate Map) (FIRM)). Use the FIRM currently in effect, 
not a draft or pending revision.  
Column B is completed only if the community receives CRS credit for regulating floodplain development outside the SFHA under Activity 410 
(Floodplain Mapping) or Activity 430 (Higher Regulatory Standards).  
Column C numbers help relate what happens in the floodplain to what is happening in the rest of the community.  
Enter "0" if there are no numbers to report for this period. Do not leave a cell blank. Do not fill in the shaded boxes.  
 
Instructions for the Lines  
 
Lines 1-7 deal with buildings.  
o   Section 301.a of the CRS Coordinator's Manual defines what constitutes a "building" and lists examples of structures that are not 
counted as "buildings" by the CRS.  
o   Section 302.a of the CRS Coordinator's Manual describes how the CRS counts buildings. For example, accessory structures are not 
counted.  
o   As noted in Section 302.a, to determine building counts, communities may use any method that yields reasonably good estimates of 
the number of buildings. Examples of acceptable methods are listed in Section 302.a. Precision is less important for large numbers. For 
example, the impact of the numbers will not change much if there are 10,000 buildings or 10,100 buildings.  
o   If a building is out of the SFHA, but in a parcel that is partly in the SFHA, it is not counted in column A --In the SFHA.  
o   In line 14, note how the building counts were obtained or estimated. Use the comments area, if needed.  
 
Line 4 refers to map revisions. These include physical map revisions, Letters of Map Revision (LOMR), and Letters of Map Amendment 
(LOMA). If a building is removed from the SFHA by FEMA through a LOMA, but the community still administers its floodplain management 
regulations on the property, the building should not be included in the line 4 count in column A --In the SFHA. However, communities that still 
regulate areas removed by LOMAs can receive credit under Activities 410 or 430. If the community is receiving such credit, the building 
should be counted under column B --In a regulated floodplain outside the SFHA.  
 
Line 7 is for the total number of buildings that were substantially improved plus the number of buildings that were substantially damaged 
during the period covered. 
 
Lines 10 -13 deal with areas.  
o   These areas are based on the areas shown on the community's FIRM including LOMRs or LOMAs. Section 403.b discusses those 
portions of the SFHA that are subtracted from the area of the SFHA to calculate the community's aSFHA used in credit calculations. 
o   Section 403.e of the CRS Coordinator's Manual discusses calculating areas for CRS purposes.  
o   Section 403.e notes that communities “should not spend an inordinate amount of time measuring areas.” As with buildings, 
communities may use any method that yields reasonably good estimates. Examples of acceptable approaches are listed in Section 403.e.  
o   Line 13 asks for the current acreage of the SFHA. The best source for this number is a GIS layer that shows the SFHA. If the 
community does not have GIS, the county, regional agency, or state NFIP mapping office may have SFHA layers and may be able to 
provide the data. If the community has a relatively recent FIRM, the study contractor or consulting engineer may have the data.  
o   In line 15, note how the area calculations were obtained or estimated. Use the comments area, if needed.  
 
Lines 17 -19 are voluntary, if the numbers are readily available.  
o   Line 17 includes replacing an existing manufactured home with a new one. The newly placed manufactured home is counted as a new, 
post-FIRM, building.  
o   The total of lines 17 -19 should equal the value entered in line 2.
