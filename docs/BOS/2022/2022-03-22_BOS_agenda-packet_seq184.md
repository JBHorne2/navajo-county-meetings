Title: Request for ARRF funds
Submitted By: Maxine Tyler, Executive Assistant
Department: County Attorney
Motion before the Board:
White Mountain Safe House is requesting ARRF funds in the amount of $1250.00 from the
County Attorney's ARRF Funds
Background:
White Mountain Safe House is requesting ARRF Funds.  These funds are utilized to provide
necessities for survivors of domestic violence.  These funds are used to relocate families that are
in a dangerous situation as well as provide necessities that they have had to leave behind.  An
example is birth certificates, social security cards, and driver's licenses.

Motion before the Board:
Application for Treasurer's Deed for the Board of Supervisors on behalf of the State 
Background:
APPROVAL BY THE BOARD PER ARS 42-18261, wherein it reads: If a lien is not redeemed at
any time beginning five years after the lien is assigned to this state, the county board of
supervisors, acting on behalf of this state, may apply for and receive a treasurer's deed to the
property under this article.
Title searches must be performed on each parcel and each owner must be served with a certified
notice. If redemption is not made by law before July 31, 2022, the parcels will then be deeded to
the State of Arizona and turned over to the Board of Supervisors for auction.

Motion before the Board:
Gila River Indian Casino Grant Application for Timber Mesa Fire and Medical District to provide
a second set of firefighting personal protective gear to 34 firefighters to reduce their risk of
exposure to cancer causing chemicals 
Background:
In recent years, research has shown a distinct and real link between increased rates of cancer in
firefighter and structural firefighting.  Today, cancer is one of the most serious health challenges
facing firefighters.  Timber mesa has been working to implement recommended, “best practices”
and steps to reduce the exposure of its personnel to harmful carcinogens and chemicals.  One of
the most important steps that can be taken to protect firefighters is to provide a second set of
turnouts or structural firefighting gear to each individual.  This allows firefighters the ability to
exchange contaminated gear for clean gear, immediately after an incident and then have clean
gear to operate in if called to additional incidents, while the contaminated equipment is being
decontaminated.  Research has proved that contaminated gear is a critical component to how
firefighters are exposed to dangerous substances.  While this has been a strategic goal of TMFMD
for the past 3 years, the cost of providing the additional equipment has kept this goal from being
accomplished.  Currently, 65% of TMFMD’s firefighters have 2 sets of turnout gear.  The funding
provided by this grant would allow for the remaining 35% to receive the needed PPE.  Should this
funding be provided, the plan would be for TMFMD to purchase the equipment and disseminate it
to those in need upon receipt of the gear.

assisting The County Emergency Operations personnel in the deployment of large scale training exercises
that include area law enforcement and fire agencies and assists The County with annual Point of Distribution 
(POD) exercises, to name a few examples. TMFMD enjoys a robust relationship with Navajo County and with 
all governments listed above and all our other regional partners.  
3. Describe the proposed project, objectives, and your plan to implement.
In recent years, research has shown a distinct and real link between increased rates of cancer in 
firefighter and structural firefighting. Today, cancer is one of the most serious health challenges facing 
firefighters.  Timber mesa has been working to implement recommended, “best practices” and steps to 
reduce the exposure of its personnel to harmful carcinogens and chemicals.  One of the most important steps 
that can be taken to protect firefighters is to provide a second set of turnouts or structural firefighting gear 
to each individual.  This allows firefighters the ability to exchange contaminated gear for clean gear, 
immediately after an incident and then have clean gear to operate in if called to additional incidents, while 
the contaminated equipment is being decontaminated.  Research has proved that contaminated gear is a 
critical component to how firefighters are exposed to dangerous substances.  While this has been a strategic 
goal of TMFMD for the past 3 years, the cost of providing the additional equipment has kept this goal from 
being accomplished.  Currently, 65% of TMFMD’s firefighters have 2 sets of turnout gear.  The funding 
provided by this grant would allow for the remaining 35% to receive the needed PPE.  Should this funding be 
provided, the plan would be for TMFMD to purchase the equipment and disseminate it to those in need upon 
receipt of the gear.

5. Identify the needs/problems to be addressed, target population and number of people to be served by the 
project.
The problem needing to be addressed in the issue of cancer in the fire service.  According to the CDC 
and the Fire Fighter Cancer Support Network, “Cancer is the most dangerous threat to firefighter health and 
safety today” and “Cancer caused 70% of the line-of-duty deaths of career firefighters in 2016”.  This is a very 
real and serious issue that has only in recent years started to be understood.  Currently, TMFMD has been 
impacted with at least one firefighter battling cancer that is believed to have been caused as a result of 
structural firefighting.

The most significant risk for firefighters happens at structure fires.  Today’s fires have been proven to 
produce a significant number of carcinogens and other toxic and dangerous chemicals as a product of 
combustion.  Firefighters enter these environments to save lifes and property, and, in the process, their 
personal protective equipment (PPE) become contaminated with these chemicals in the form of soot.  
Firefighters are then forced to keep this gear through the remainder of their work cycle, until it can be 
cleaned, when there is no longer the potential that the gear will be needed.  This can be several days at 
times.  In the event of another incident requiring the use of that PPE, the firefighters are forced to use the 
contaminated equipment again.  The project being proposed would address this issue by allowing firefighters 
to have a clean, uncontaminated set of PPE to use, after an incident while the contaminated set is being 
decontaminated.  The benefit of this cannot be overstated and provides potentially lifesaving relief from the 
requirement to use contaminated PPE.    
The target population of this project is the public safety personnel of TMFMD, their immediate families 
and the communities of Show Low, Linden, Lakeside and White Mountain Lakes that relay upon the 
firefighters of TMFMD for protection.  While this project would immediately serve 31 firefighters and the 
families of those personnel, the roughly 34,000 people living in the communities listed above are also being 
served by having a safe and healthy work force to response to them when requested. In addition the 
immidate communities served by TMFMD, there are multiple other agencies and communities that rely upon 
TMFMD for automatic and mutal aid, especially for response to structure fires.  These communities would 
also benefit from have a healthy work force to assist them.

Title: Amendments to Navajo County Building Division Addenda and Additions 
Submitted For: John Osgood, Public Works Director 
Submitted By: John Osgood, Public Works Director
Department: Public Works
Presented By: John Osgood
Motion before the Board:
Consideration of Resolution Number 9-22, adopting the 2018 International Building Code and
International Residential Code and Amending the County's Addenda and Additions.
Background:
The Navajo County Building Division has been using the 2015 version of the International
Building Code (IBC) and International Residential Code (IRC) for building construction,
permitting and inspection since June 2019. Our neighboring communities of Coconino
County, the City of Show Low and the Town of Snowflake have adopted the 2018 IBC
and IRC. Understanding that the 2015 version is out of sync with the Insurance Services
Office's (ISO) recommended six-year cycle; that expected benefits from up-to-date
building codes are safer buildings, less damage, and lower insured losses from
catastrophes; and that most jurisdictions throughout the state have adopted their codes on a
six-year/even year schedule (e.g. 2006, 2012, 2018), the Navajo County Building Division
recognizes that aligning our IBC and IRC schedules with neighboring jurisdictions and those
throughout the state will best serve our customers, businesses and property owners who perform
work in Navajo County as well as in other areas of the region and state. 
The changes from the 2015 to 2018 versions of the IBC and IRC are relatively minor, pertaining
primarily to fire safety; staff does not anticipate significant concern from or impact on the
commercial and residential building communities. Existing projects currently in the system will be
reviewed and inspected under the 2015 codes.  Staff recommends that once the 2018 codes are
adopted that we allow a ninety-day transition period between adoption and implementation, so the
building community has time to adjust, plan accordingly and confer with our staff on any issues
and questions.    
Staff will provide a brief presentation at the March 22nd meeting and answer questions.
Recommendation: 
Approve the Resolution.
