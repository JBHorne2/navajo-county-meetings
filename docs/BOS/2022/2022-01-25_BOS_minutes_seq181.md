b.

Presentation regarding upcoming School Safety Interoperability project :  Sheriff Clouse addressed the item.  He talked about the Legislative Bill ran last year by Representative Payne for a school safety interoperability project, where four counties would run a pilot program for school safety working with technology and that he volunteered to be one of pilot counties.  He stated that in 2020 the Sheriff's Office put together a 5-year strategic plan and that school safety was one of the things that they deemed to be important.  He further stated that in some areas they don't have many deputies, so they are trying to find ways to get more security in those communities.  He indicated that Lt. Whipple, who does a lot of the technology updates for the Department, and Deputy Chief Tucker volunteered to take on the project.
Lt. Whipple addressed the item and stated that they have been working on this project for past several months looking at options to determine what route would be best.  He indicated that they are requesting permission to purchase the Mutuallink product due to it being the only product that met the criteria that the State Legislature was asking for.  He stated that it is an excellent product and will make schools and the communities safer, enhance safety, give them the ability to respond quicker, and to be able to see what the potential emergency is.  He further stated that it will give the schools a direct link to the dispatch centers.  Chrissie Coon, with Mutuallink addressed the board and indicated that she has been working with several Sheriff's Offices throughout the State.  A presentation was shown.  She addressed the key points that were in the Legislation Bill and indicated that this is a multimedia network between schools and public safety that would expediate notification to public safety when there is an incident and allow the closest public safety agency to respond.  She talked about the mutual aid channels that would allow for data such as pictures and video to be shared.  She addressed the criteria regarding privacy and indicated that they channels are not open access.  She stated that they are also working with some of the rural inoperability programs and that any agency with the program can communicate with each other for incidents that are cross jurisdiction.  She advised that the program for Navajo County will be LNK360 which will connect police, fire, medical and local community partners.  She talked about the three goals of the program which are to expedite notification into the 911 centers; to create a 2-way push to talk communication with 911 and to bring clarity to the situation by allow them to share pictures and video.  She talked about the application being available to teachers and staff on their cell phones which would allow them to activate a lockdown, active assailant, shelter in place, evacuation or any other type of emergency situations.
Supervisor Seymore inquired how many people at the school would have access to the application, if it would be all teachers or if it would just be at the discretion of the schools?
Ms. Coon indicated that it would be available to all of the staff. She talked about what information would be sent to the dispatch center once it is activated and about what public safety entities would be able to be added to the system.  She stated that they work with the schools and public safety to get information such as the schools emergency operations plan, a map of the school and any evacuation plans so that they can preprogram and automate the information and it's available in real time.  She stated that they run interoperability through Verizon and the FirstNet systems.
Chairwoman Whitesinger inquired if a school has to request to have this program or how it's determined what schools would participate?
Ms. Coon stated that they go out and meet with the schools to do demonstrations on what the system does and how it will work with Public Safety.  She further stated that the biggest concern is that the system has to be FERPA and HIPPA compliant, which their system is.  She indicated that anything shared is recorded at the school or law enforcement side, but as a third party they don't record any of the communications, so they are able to protect the privacy of students and staff.
Lt. Whipple indicated that they have already started to talk to the schools, that so far it has been received well and the schools are looking forward to being able to work with the program.
Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked everyone for the information and the presentation.

Consideration and approval of the proposed funding package for the American Rescue Plan Act projects :  Bryan Layton addressed the item, indicating that this is an opportunity for States, Tribes and Local Governments to respond to the COVID pandemic and respond to some outstanding needs.  A presentation was shown.  He stated that the County was allotted 21.5 million dollars and that all of the other government entities in our region, including cities/town and the Tribes also received an allocation.  He talked about the potential partnership with the other entities and stated that it is a great opportunity for everyone involved.  He indicated that this is federal money, therefore there are a lot of guidelines and that the money must be spent by 2024 and any capital projects must be completed by 2026.  He stated that ten million is a standard deduction for negative economic impact and lost revenues and that the remaining $11.5 million is for projects to responded to pandemic, economic impacts and outstanding infrastructure needs such as broadband, water and sewer.   He indicated that Navajo County didn't see a loss during COVID that there was an influx of people, building and development, which has highlighted the potential for economic development and has helped shape the proposals.  He talked about with there being so many needs and requests how they worked to prioritize and allocate the projects for this once in a lifetime opportunity.  He further talked about the purpose statement that was created to guide how to fund projects that would transform the county and help the communities for years to come and the guiding principles for those projects which would need to be affordable, stainable, allow leverage of other funding sources and partnerships and benefit the region.  He addressed the evaluation process that includes the qualification from the treasury and the guiding principles and stated that projects were submitted by staff, partner agencies and non-profits.  He stated that some ideas didn't qualify even though they were great ideas and that those were put in another project list to use other means and that those projects will be brought to the Board at a later time.  He indicated that the projects were put into three categories which are county wide improvements; community partnerships and execution/fulfillment.  He reviewed the projects for each category and talked about the next steps, which include regular reports to the Board of Supervisors.  He indicated that the plan detailed project timelines need to be ready to go by the end of 2022 in order to complete all projects by 2024.  He stated that if there are any funds that have not been used by will come back to the Board to determine what priorities they would like the funds contributed to.
Supervisor Seymore indicated that the Board has had a lot of discussion regarding this, that other Counties have reached out regarding our plan and have said that it helped them with their process.  He stated that he thinks this will make a difference in our county for years to come.  He thanked staff for all of the work they have done and indicated that he is looking forward to working with community partners to make this successful.
Supervisor Benally thanked her communities for following through with their project requests and talked about the projects that the ARPA funding will help with.  She stated that she appreciates the County Team for their work.
Vice-Chairman Peshlakai thanked the ARPA Management Team for their work and the presentation.  He inquired if those who submitted for projects that are not being funded will be notified.
Mr. Layton indicated that he will be following up with them and offer guidance and support for their projects if they can.

Glenn Kephart thanked the Board for their leadership, indicated that it has been a long road but that they made it to this point on time.  He thanked the ARPA Team that came together and the dedication on the efforts.

REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

COVID-19 Update :  Janelle Linn stated that last week there were 901 new cases reported to public health, which is the highest number of cases reported since the start of the pandemic for non-tribal cases.  She indicated that the average age of the cases was 37 years old and that they are seeing a lot more cases in younger people.  She stated that fifty-one of the cases reported last week had been vaccinated, although few had a booster dose.  She advised that there were six hospitalizations, with the average age being 59 years old and only one of those were vaccinated.  She further advised that there were eleven deaths last week, with the average age being 66, that they none of them were vaccinated and that there have been six deaths reported this week.  She stated that for the month of January there have been over 2,500 cases, which is almost double from December.  She indicated that the Delta and Omicron strains are circulating heavily in our communities, that there have been some flu cases and a few reports of co-infection.  She stated that they are asking people to be aware of their symptoms and to stay home if they are feeling sick because it's not worth the chance of passing it to someone who could be at risk.  Supervisor Benally inquired what the vaccination percentage rate is for Navajo County and if that percentage includes Navajo Reservation?
Ms. Linn stated that off tribal land the vaccination rate is still under fifty percent and that for the Navajo Nation they are reporting at above seventy-five percent which is up to date with a booster.  She advised that the CDC has done away with the term fully vaccinated replaced it with up to date for a vaccination which mean that you have all recommended doses including boosters.  She indicated that they are still seeing an increase with case counts, which is to be expected with the Omicron because they anti-bodies from the vaccine and from previous strains are not seeming to be as effective against Omicron, but that the symptoms are not as bad.  She stated that the hospitals are still reporting some cases with the Delta variant.
Supervisor Whiting thanked Ms. Linn and her team, indicating that it hasn't been easy, that they are doing great job on focusing on facts and support what is going on.  He stated that he notices the efforts they are putting forward and that he appreciates the updates and the information provided but especially the work that Ms. Linn and her team are doing.

b.

Consideration of abatement of real property APN 206-06-039 and authorizing the County Attorney to initiate Civil enforcement (complaint for injunctive relief) in Justice Court or Superior Court :  John Osgood addressed the item and indicated that staff has been working for 19 months with property owner to either secure a building permit to build a dwelling or to vacant the property as required by the counties zoning ordinance.  He stated that after multiple written notices, a notice of violation letter by the County Attorney's Office, speaking at length with Environmental Engineer representing the property owner and after multiple conversations with property owner and there being no response, it is apparent that legal action through the Court system is appropriate.  A presentation was shown.  Mr. Osgood talked about the timeline of the incidents, the zoning for the property and he reviewed a summary of the violations, indicating that there are two RV's and some other structures were reported on the property in June 2020 and that they have received multiple complaints about the code violations on the property since then.  He stated that the property owner submitted for a septic permit in February 2021 but that it was denied due to the proposed septic system being in a wash area that conveys storm water from an area greater than twenty acres which is in violation of ADEQ regulations.  He indicated that since March 2021 Public Works staff has notified the property owner that an engineer licensed in the State of Arizona would be required to design a septic system that comply with ADEQ requirements and that a Public Works Engineer would work with their engineer to work on a solution.  He stated that they were given a deadline of August 27, 2021 and that on September 9, 2021 that Public Works received notification from the property owner that they would not be working on the project indefinitely.  He further stated that the Sheriff's Office served the property owner with a notice of violation from the County Attorney's Office on October 5, 2021 listing the violations and requiring that the property owners contact the County Attorney's Office by October 15, 2021 to discuss resolution of the violations but that they have not contacted the County's Attorney's Office to date.  He reviewed the current conditions of the property since last week, indicating that an additional RV has been added to the property over the last couple of months.  Pictures of the violation were shown.  He stated that Public Works has exhausted any options they have to get owner to comply.   He indicated that if the Board approves the item that Public Works staff intends to reach out one last time prior to proceedings with legal action.
Supervisor Seymore stated that he doesn't like to do abatements, that he prefers to give the property owners an opportunity but that this is a very visible corridor in a mostly commercial area, that it's a deterrent to anyone wanting to build next to them, that there are a lot of businesses in the area and that if this is allowed to continue others will begin to do the same thing.  He stated that he feels they have been very fair and that he supports this action to move forward.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion to approve the abatement of real property APN 206-06-039 and authorizing the County Attorney to initiate Civil enforcement (complaint for injunctive relief) in Justice Court or Superior Court, after one additional attempt for the property owner to comply, motion seconded by Vice-Chairman Peshlakai .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
