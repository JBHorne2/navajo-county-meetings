PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration of Resolution Number 36 - 2022 , approving/denying establishment of a separate governance structure for the White Mountain Lakes County Recreation Improvement District :
Rochelle Lacapa addressed the item.  A presentation was shown.  She stated that the district is a special taxing district, that currently the Board of Supervisors serves as the Board of Directors for the District and that the duties and authorities are the responsibility of the Board of Supervisors.  She indicated that over the past several years the residents within the district have requested more local control over the taxing district and would like to elect a separate Board of Directors from those who are qualified electors within the district, manage the day-to-day operations and oversee the financials.  She stated that as a result of the feedback the County has worked with Representative Walter Blackman on HB 2067 which would allow the Board of Supervisors to establish a new/separate revenue governance structure for the district.  She advised that on March 30, 2022, the Governor sign HB 2067 into law and the general effective date was September 24, 2022.  She talked about the proposed implementation timeline and indicated that, if approved, the applications would be available tomorrow. She further talked about the application process, the required qualifications, and additional information that is being requested.  She reviewed the handbook for the county improvement districts that addressed the duties and responsibilities, key information to state statutes and state agencies that would be helpful.  She stated that staff is recommending adoption of the resolution for a 5-member Board of Directors.

Chairwoman Whitesinger opened the meeting for public comments.
Kathy Henderson Essel, 1770 Edwina Place, White Mountain Lakes, thanked the Board for helping with the process.  She indicated that there would have been more present today but that several people were helping with Elections.  She requested clarity on how Board of Directors would communicate with the Board of Supervisors, stating that they hope to work together as a team.  She talked about how in the past information for the district has gone through the management team, how that it is becoming problematic, and requested more of an open communication with the staff at the county that will be helping to establish the Board.
Jessica Delaney, 1987 Ridgeway Dr., Show Low, stated that she was a former employee for the district, that she is a very involved member of the community and that she is offering her complete support to the adoption of the resolution.  She thanked Representative Blackman and Supervisor Whiting for all of the work done on the bill.  She stated that she has lived in White Mountain Lakes community for approximately six years and that she remembers all of the work done over the years.  She addressed the previous Administrator and issues with the new Administrator.  She talked about the lake being a huge part of the community.  She thanked Rochelle Lacapa for all of the work that she has done and the communication she has provided.  She addressed a couple of concerns she has with parts of the handbook.
Supervisor Whiting thanked Ms. Delaney for coming up and sharing her concerns.
Jacklin McKinley, 8648 Cochise Circle, thanked the Board for the opportunity of allowing community to have a Board of Directors.  She talked about the difficulties with getting information as a community.  She stated that she is in full support of this.  She addressed concerns that she has with the handbook.
Chairwoman Whitesinger stated that Rochelle Lacapa will address the concerns brought up regarding the handbook.
Ms. McKinley inquired where they will be able to get applications.
Ms. Lacapa addressed where the applications will be available.
Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked those who spoke.
Supervisor Whiting addressed the concerns that were addressed during public comments.
Ms. Lacapa addressed the concerns with the handbook and the language from the legislature.

Chairwoman Whitesinger closed the public hearing.
Supervisor Whiting stated that Brandt Clark has worked for several years to find ways to help, as well as Melissa Buckley, Glenn Kephart, Rochelle Lacapa and many others have worked to find solutions for the concerns that were brought to them.  He thanked Representative Blackman for helping to get the law changed.  He thanked the citizens for their involvement in the community and being engaged.

Supervisor Whiting made a motion  to approve Resolution Number 36 - 2022, approving establishment of a separate governance structure for the White Mountain Lakes County Recreation Improvement District, motion seconded by  Supervisor Seymore .

SHERIFF'S OFFICE :

a.

Consideration of expansion and continuation of the current contract with Mutualink, Inc. :
Sheriff Clouse stated that this item is for school safety.  He talked about strategic plan that the Sheriff's Office has established, with part of it being proactive in school safety.  He talked about Navajo County volunteering for pilot program for mutual link last year, that they were one of four counties that did that and that this past year the legislature expanded the program for three more years to all schools. He talked about the schools that were involved previously and indicated that now it will be expanded to include all schools.  He stated that it is a great project, that the schools Superintendents have responded well and worked well with the Sheriff's Office.  He thanked Lt. Whipple and his team for the work that they have done on this project and indicated that everyone has worked hard on this from the IT Department to the Officers and the Schools.
Lt. Whipple addressed the item.  A presentation was shown. He stated that when this first started, they were looking at the rural areas and that this year, after seeing how well the implementation went, they are wanting to include every school in the state.  He addressed the project benefits from last year to the new contract.  He talked about the licensing, what is included for the schools and what law enforcement has.
Supervisor Seymore inquired as to the training curve if it a hard process to learn.
Lt. Whipple addressed the training, indicating that the use of computer training is more in-depth but that would be the dispatcher mostly.  He walked through the process from the schools, the Sheriff's Office, and responders for that area.  He indicated that they can share live video feeds from the school, images, messaging and push to talk.  He stated that the Mobile application is very user friendly.
Supervisor Whiting thanked them for the work they have done on this.
Lt. Whipple stated that Navajo County is the one expanding the most for the project and that many are just doing one school district at a time.  He stated that due to the additional funding they are able to really expand it to all schools.  He indicated that setting up the license/users is easy.
Chairwoman Whitesinger talked about public safety limitations and the ability to respond being challenging so the partnership to notify those in the surrounding areas is great.  She talked about the preparation prospect for the schools.  She indicated that she appreciates the work done on this to help the schools.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to approve an expansion and continuation of the current contract with Mutualink, Inc., motion seconded by  Supervisor Whiting .  The motion passed 4-0 with Supervisor Benally being absent.
