ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES :

a.

Consideration and adoption of the proposed Tentative Budget for Fiscal Year 2022-23 for Navajo County :  Jayson Vowell addressed the item, stating that the budget is a result of a lot of meetings with Departments, Elected Officials and Staff.  A presentation was shown.  Mr. Vowell stated that with the budget they forecasted future growth, estimated expenses and identified the needs within the County with the goal of bringing the best services to the communities and residents.  He further stated that the first thing to having a balanced budget is to identify the available resources and make sure that they equal the projected expenditures,  second is looking at financial reserves and have a goal of healthy reserves to meet short term and emergency funding for future opportunities and third is operations for providing high quality services to the residents.  He talked about the AA rating that the County received during the PSPRS bond refunding, stating that it is an excellent rating for the County.  He addressed the ARPA funding that has been received.  He reviewed the proposed budget for FY 2022-23, highlighting things such as lowering property tax to achieve a flat levy, proposed changes to the pay structure, creating a pension reserve fund, responding to increased demand and investing in capital projects.  He reviewed the expenditures and revenues.  He stated that the budget is balanced and that the available resources equal the budgeted expenditures.  He talked about projected revenues, such as, state shared sales tax and the primary property tax for the County, noting that last year we were the 4 th lowest property tax in the State.  He further talked about recommended changes to the general fund expenditures.  He reviewed the operating budget, indicating that the County is in the business of providing services and that sixty-seven percent of the general fund goes to personnel and ERE costs.  He talked about the Special Districts and the taxes that collected and distributed to those districts. He addressed the special revenue funds and reasons for some increases in that fund.
Supervisor Whiting thanked Mr. Vowell for the work done.  He stated that
he appreciates how everyone worked together to find solutions to move this process forward.
Supervisor Seymore thanked staff for the work done on this and indicated he hopes the citizens recognize the work done. He stated that he appreciates everyone who has had input and worked on the budget.  He further stated that he hopes that it will meet the needs of the people.
Supervisor Benally thanked Mr. Vowell for the work done on the budget and the work done to present this item.
Chairwoman Whitesinger talked about the work done on getting to this point.  She talked about the positive points with this budget compared to the tough times that they have been through in the past.  She stated that she is grateful for all who contributed to the budget.
Mr. Vowell thanked the Board for their leadership and guidance.  He thanked Glenn Kephart, Bryan Layton and Eric Scott for their contribution and indicated that it is a team effort.

Supervisor Whiting made a motion  to adopt the proposed Tentative Budget for Fiscal Year 2022-23 for Navajo County, motion seconded by  Vice-Chairman Peshlakai .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 17-22 , to apply for the U.S. Department of Justice comprehensive opioid, stimulant, and substance abuse site based program grant : Brad Carlyon addressed the item, indicating that this is a grant opportunity from the Arizona Offices of the Court, offered to rural counties to try to address the opioid epidemic and resulting overdoses.  He stated that Navajo County has tracked the deaths, addressed the percentages over the past couple of years and the increase of opioid deaths.  He indicated that they don't track the number of overdoses that don't result in death but that he sees that number from the criminal complaints that they receive.  He talked the data for the history of those who use opioids.  He stated that it's a problem that costs all taxpayers, from health and behavioral health costs and the court costs.  He talked about specialty courts that have been created to address these issues.  He stated that this grant is to get more programs to the rural areas.  He talked about the team that came together to work on the program that Navajo County has come up with to use this grant.  He stated that the goal is to help them, keep them out of court and get them back to being a productive citizen.  He indicated that there can be thirty people in the program at one time with ten leaving and entering the program each month.  He stated that it's a 3-year grants and that they will come out to help set up a plan unique to our needs. He talked about the training that will be provided.  He further talked about the costs that will be associated with the program.  He stated that at the end of the grant, if they find that the program is effective, they would come back before the Board to request funding to keep the program ongoing. He addressed some concerns that Supervisor Seymore had brought up regarding the cost of personnel.
Supervisor Whiting thanked Mr. Carlyon and indicated that he thinks this program is much needed.  He inquired as to additional costs involved.
Mr. Carlyon and Ken Maruyama addressed the cost involved during the grant period.
Supervisor Seymore thanked everyone for their work on this item.  He addressed his concerns with starting a new program and the costs involved.
Mr. Carlyon addressed the additional costs and the costs saving that will come from the program.
Supervisor Seymore stated that he likes how this compliments the mental health program that is already being implemented.
Supervisor Benally thanked those who worked on this to help those with addictions.
Chairwoman Whitesinger talked about the dynamics of the country and how fentanyl is impacting our nation.  She talked about how this is impacting our local communities and the direct impact that it has on family, friends and neighbors and the importance of being able to change for those individuals struggling with addiction.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to adopt Resolution Number 17-22, to apply for the U.S. Department of Justice comprehensive opioid, stimulant, and substance abuse site-based program grant, motion seconded by  Vice-Chairman Peshlakai .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
