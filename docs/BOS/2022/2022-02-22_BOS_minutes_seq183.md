REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

COVID-19 Update :  Janelle Linn stated that there is good news, they have only had 152 new cases reported over the last week which is down approximately 120 cases from the prior week and that of the 152 cases thirty-eight had at least one dose of vaccine. She stated that they still have some concerns with the hospital and mortality rates, that they are staying the same even with the case counts being lower, due to the Delta variant still circulating and many people still catching it and getting very sick.  She advised that they had 14 hospitalizations last week and the average age was 66 and only three of those had started their vaccine series.  She further advised that they had 26 school related cases.  She stated that they had eleven deaths with the average age being 77 years old and that none of them had started their vaccine series.  She indicated that they are still pushing that the vaccine helps prevent serious disease.  She reported that 49% of the cases last week were under the age of forty, that they are still seeing a lot of spread of infection in younger people, but the older population are the ones getting sick. She stated that they are reassigning some of the surge COVID staff, that they still have grant for a least another year and that they are using them to reach out to the previous cases to see how many have lingering symptoms and long term impacts so they can prepare for the public health planning for the coming months and years and local health care needs.  Ms. Linn thanked the Board and Administrators for the support provided to them over the past couple of years.

Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked Ms. Linn for the update.  She stated that our communities have been impacted and hearing that the numbers are down is good news.
Supervisor Seymore thanked Ms. Linn for the report and the job she and her staff have done.  He stated that we can't take for granted the important lessons learned and the work done has helped saved lives.

2.

Consideration of the Customer Agreement between Motorola Solutions, Inc. and the Animal Care & Control division for WatchGuard Video body-worn camera for a projected amount of $9,882.00 with a monthly payment of $159 for 5-years :  Rhonda Krouse addressed the Board regarding the item, indicating that they are requesting WatchGuard body worn cameras for the Animal Control Officers, which are the same cameras used by the Sheriff's Department.  She advised that the total cost is $9,882.00 with a monthly payment of $159.00 for five years.  She stated that there are three benefits to having the cameras, officer safety, training opportunities and lower costs.
Supervisor Seymore inquired if this is for one camera.
Ms. Krouse stated that it will be for three cameras.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to approve the Customer Agreement between Motorola Solutions, Inc. and the Animal Care & Control division for WatchGuard Video body-worn camera for a projected amount of $9,882.00 with a monthly payment of $159 for 5-years, motion seconded by  Supervisor Whiting .

Brad Carlyon stated that this is a need for the Department but that there are additional costs for IT storage and to the County Attorney's Office for editing any videos that will be provided.  He indicated that he is not sure of the exact costs due to attorney time spent, which many of the cases are done by contract attorney's due to them being misdemeanors, for redacting victim information and the software needed to be able to edit.  He stated that this isn't just here it is for all law enforcement offices.
Rhonda Krouse indicated that the storage is through Spillman in the Sheriff's Office, that they will be able to redact the videos on their side and that they will be training with the Sheriff's Office in May.
Mr. Carlyon stated that they will have to still review the video to make sure all victim information is redacted.
Supervisor Seymore inquired if this will help with the processing of cases or if there will be an advantage point.
Mr. Carlyon stated that there may be some impact to it, that they may be able to save some trial time but that they will spend more time in pretrial preparation by having to review the videos.  He addressed the laws that are being put in place regarding the videos and the policies in the different departments.  He addressed the pros and cons of the video and audio recordings.
Supervisor Whiting thanked Mr. Carlyon for all of the hard work his teams does.  He addressed the redaction and storage issues and inquired if the redaction is included in the monthly fee.
Mr. Carlyon addressed the additional costs both short term and long term.
Ms. Krouse further addressed the need for the videos and the safety of the officers.

Vote was unanimous approving the motion.

Vice-Chairman Peshlakai made a motion  to return to regular session, motion seconded by
