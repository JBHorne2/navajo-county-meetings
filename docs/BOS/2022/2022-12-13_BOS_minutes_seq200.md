PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration of a Liquor License from Jason Gilmore for The Gilmore Group located at 6662 State Route 260, Show Low, AZ:  Melissa Buckley stated that they received a liquor license for a beer and wine bar.  She indicated that the Treasurer and Health Department stated that the application meets their requirements, and that the Sheriff has submitted his approval.  She advised that Planning and Zoning has responded that the application does not meet the requirements for their department due to the location not currently having a permitted structure on the property.  She stated that staff is recommending denial until the applicant has met the requirements of the department.  Mr. Gilmore addressed the Board indicating that they purchased the land and are waiting on the outcome of the liquor license prior to a structure being put on the property.  He stated that he spoke with the Arizona Department of Liquor License and Control and that they have told him there is not a requirement to have a building in place prior to a license being granted.  He further stated that he left a message for the Planning and Zoning Department asking them to reconsider but that he has not heard back from them.
Cody Cooper addressed the Board stating that with Planning and Development they are looking for the public health, welfare, and safety for any type of use or proposed use and that it is difficult for their department to say if something will be safe for public use when it doesn't exist, and they haven't received an application.  He indicated that the department spoke to Mr. Gilmore on November 17, 2022, then followed up with an email on November 21, 2022, letting him know the position of the department and that there was no further outreach due to their position remaining the same.
Mischa Larisch addressed the Board regarding concerns with Mr. Gilmore wanting to construct a building out of a storage unit/shipping container and wanting to verify that it would be safe and up to code.  He indicated that they have concerns with a liquor license being issued for a vacant piece of land.  He stated that they would be okay if the Board put conditions on the license issuance that he get a proper permitted structure prior to conducting any business at the location.
Chairwoman Whitesinger inquired if the Board recommended denial if Mr. Gilmore would be able to apply for a liquor license in the future.
Mr. Gilmore stated that after the Department of Liquor receives the recommendation from the Board, they will make the final determination on the issuance of the license and that he anticipates that the Department of Liquor will overturn this Boards recommendation if it is denial.

Chairwoman Whitesinger opened the meeting for public comments.  There being no public comments, the public meeting was closed.

Supervisor Seymore indicated that he agrees with the recommendation from Planning and Zoning and inquired if there is any legal obligation, one way or the other, that a building would need to be in placed prior to the license being issued.
Brad Carlyon indicated that the Liquor Board will not care if there is a building in place but that if it is granted, he will still need to come back and get his various building and business permits.  He stated that the recommendation for conditions to be put in place has no weight with the Liquor Board.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to approve a Liquor License from Jason Gilmore for The Gilmore Group located at 6662 State Route 260, Show Low, AZ, with a contingency that the applicant has built a building that has been approved and meets all codes prior to opening the business; motion seconded by  Supervisor Whiting .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

PUBLIC HEARING : Acknowledgment of potential projects presented during the 1st public hearing for 2023 Navajo County CDBG funds:
Isabel Rollins addressed the Board, indicating that this is the first of two public hearings for the County to receive the Community Development Block Grant  funds next year.  She indicated that this hearing is to hear about potential projects for the County to fund and at the second hearing the Board will rank the projects and select up to three to be funded.  A presentation was shown.  She provided the background for the CDBG, indicating that it is a HUD, small cities program that provides funds for housing and community development in rural Arizona.  She advised that for projects to qualify for funding they need to either benefit at least 5% low to moderate income people, to aid in the prevention or elimination of slums or blight or the project must solve an urgent need health hazard.  She stated that there are certain groups that automatically meet the criteria for projects, such as adults sixty-five and over, adults with severe disabilities, ADA accessibility improvement projects, homeless, victims of domestic violence, persons who are literate, persons living with AIDS and migrant farmworkers, but that the project would need to exclusively fund those group of people to qualify without income proof.  She talked about the amount of money they are expecting, indicating that it is expected to be approximately $500,000 to Navajo County, that the amounts are estimated based off the figures from last year and that the final figures should come out between February and April.  She stated that there is also fifteen percent of funds are set aside for a competitive round that is announced each year, that the county would be able to apply for if they wanted.  She indicated there are a wide variety of projects that can be funded, and she reviewed the possible types of projects.  She talked about previous projects done in Navajo County.  She addressed the schedule for the proposed projects.  She stated that the pre-application form needs to be filled out and submitted to Ken Maruyama by December 20, 2022. She indicated that if other funds are needed for a proposed project, the funds need to be committed by the end of the year.  She stated that at the January 24, 2023, Board of Supervisors Meeting the projects will be ranked and the top 1-3 will be chosen for fundings.  She advised that the application will be submitted to the Department of Housing by June 1, 2023, that they will review then and that the grant award(s) will be October or November 2023.  She talked about the responsibility of the County to select projects, indicating that any project that they select must be brought up at today's hearing and that up to three can be selected.
Chairwoman Whitesinger opened the meeting for public comments.
Ken Maruyama addressed the Board, indicating that he developed a list of potential projects that are part of the Board packet.  He stated that the Town of Taylor has withdraw two of their proposed projects, that the City of Holbrook has expressed interest in applying for funding, but that he doesn't have any information on the project and that the City of Pinetop/Lakeside has requested funding for the Youth Centers roof replacement.
Celeste Hall with Rim Country Senior Center, talked about their project to build a 30 x 50 thrift store that would be a benefit to the facility and the community by allowing people to take stuff that would end up at the landfill or thrown out to the store for repurposing and to be able to sell to those who can't afford new.  She stated that they also need to have the buildings for the Senior Center monitored for fire safety, replace the chairs at the Senior Center due to the current ones  they have being seventeen years old and replace a copier.  She stated that it would care for and benefit the elderly and talked about all that offer for the seniors and the community.
Allison Hephner with Meals on Wheels Arizona White Mountains stated that their project focuses on transportation.  She talked about the significant impact that COVID had on the elderly due to increased isolation and lack of connections.  She further talked about the transportation issues for the elderly with being in a rural area.  She stated that their project would be to purchase a van that would be used to transport both their disabled population and to be able to bring others to the center to allow them to have connections.  She stated that they plan to pilot this and then work in partner with the other senior centers.
Melina Skillman with the Town of Pinetop/Lakeside addressed the Board regarding their project for the Yellowjacket Youth Center, indicating that the roof needs to be repaired.  She stated that the Nexus Coalition does a lot in that location to serve the population.  Vicky Solomon with Nexus Coalition addressed the board regarding the project and stated that they hold meetings, training, activities, and a Jr. Leadership Academy at the location as well.  She indicated that the Bikers Against Child Abuse also hold functions at the location and that the Recovery Works holds family support group there.
Roger Brown with the White Mountain Coalition Against Homelessness addressed the Board regarding their project for building ten tiny homes on some property that they purchased in Show Low.  He indicated that it would be a small community for the homeless.  He talked about how they help those who are homeless, indicating that it would be temporary transitional housing while they are in the program.
Mike Nielson, President of the Holbrook Senior Citizens Association addressed the Board indicated that they are asking to replace a van that would be wheelchair accessible, due to their previous van being in a wreak.  He stated that it would assist the senior citizens in Holbrook with different needs.
Brett Starns with Navajo County addressed the Board regarding the project of the Clay Springs Heritage Foundation, indicating that the roof needs to be replaced.  Michelle Brewer and Eleanor Malcolm addressed Board regarding the different ways that the community uses the building, stating that there are many projects needed to be done, but roof needs to be replaced prior to anything else being done.
Lisa Robertson with the City of Show addressed the Board regarding the downtown redevelopment project which would continue the city's efforts to improve the downtown area, particularly regarding flooding.  She talked about the anticipated projects that would be done to help with the flooding.
Joseph Jarvis with the Town of Taylor addressed the Board regarding their project improvements to Foothills Blvd. including paving and repairing portions of the road.
Janelle Linn addressed the Board regarding supporting any local partners who submit projects for increased capacity for animal care and control.  She indicated that they would serve and benefit all communities.
