Presentation/Summary of County's Fiscal Year 2021 Annual Financial Report, Report on Internal Control and on Compliance, and Federal Single Audit Report :
Jason Vowell introduced Taryn Stangle, who is an Audit Manager and Lindsay Perry, the Auditor General with the Arizona Auditor General's Office.
Ms. Perry provided some background on the Auditor General's Office indicating that it is a legislative agency made up on nonpartisan staff and that they report to the Joint Legislative Audit Committee, which is a bipartisan group with five members from the Senate and give members from the House.  She stated that they provide specific recommendations to improve the operations and programs of state and local government.  A presentation was shown.  She indicated that they follow several standards, including governmental auditing standards created by the United States Comptroller and outline several requirements for audit reports, professional qualifications for auditors, auditor ethics, independence, objectivity, professional judgment, qualifications, and an audit organizations quality control.  She addressed the statutory change that requires that the audit findings be presented to the Board at a regular meeting within ninety days of the audit being completed.  She reported as to the findings on the County's Annual Financial Report, indicating that for fiscal year 2021 they found the report to be a clean report meaning that the financial statements are reliable; as to the Internal Control and Compliance Report, which includes things like deficiencies in internal control, fraud or abuse and non-compliance with laws, regulations, contracts and agreements, she indicated that they had four item which they would discuss further and as to the Federal Single Audit Report, which is an assessment of the county's compliance with federal program requirements, internal control over compliance with the federal programs and the counties schedule federal expenditures, indicating that they found two items, which will be further discussed.  She talked about the audit report highlights, which addressed the largest primary revenue sources and expenditures over the last 10 years, brief information about key findings and recommendations, links to current and prior audit reports and user guides.  She stated that some key information is the county's four main sources of revenue consist of federal and state grants and programs, state shared sales taxes, county property taxes and county sales taxes, indicating that over the past 10 years these revenues have remained fairly consistent with the exception of federal and state grants.  She stated that the primary expense purposes consist of general government, public safety, highways and streets, and health and welfare, indicating that all expenses have increased slightly except for the general government expenditures that has a sharp increase in prior years.  She indicated that the overall net position increased by $16.6 million, meaning that the revenues were higher than the expenses and that the total net position for the county increased by $70.5 million.
Ms. Stangle stated that the county did improve its internal policies and procedures for purchasing cards.  She reviewed the four findings from their report, the county's responses and the recommendations regarding control and compliance, first being deficiencies in the financial statement preparation process which delayed the issuance of the financial statements and expenditure limitation report past the statutory deadline, second, that the county paid nearly $20,000 for employees purchasing car fuel without verifying that the fuel was used in company vehicles, third, the deficiencies in the process for managing and documenting information technology risks, and forth, deficiencies and controls over the IT systems and data.  She addressed the findings, county's responses, and recommendations in the Single Audit Report, indicating that first finding with the WIOA youth program, the county failed to ensure it spent twenty percent of the program dollars in schools and out of school youth with paid and unpaid work experiences from April 2019 to June 2021, and second that the Public Health Services Department did not review for accuracy eleven of the twelve required reports before submitting them. She advised that they will follow up on the findings and corrective action during the fiscal year 2022 audit.  She thanked the Board for allowing them to present and thanked the County's staff for their cooperation on the audit.
Supervisor Seymore thanked them for the presentation and report.  He indicated that they look forward to making the corrections and making improvements going forward.
Mr. Vowell stated that their number one goal was to get the clean audit opinion.  He noted that there were no identified areas of mismanagement or misappropriation of funds, which they are proud of.  He stated that they do expect to be awarded a certificate of achievement for excellent in financial reporting which is the highest award that local governments can receive.  A presentation was shown.  He addressed the findings from the audit report and the changes that have been or will be implemented.
Supervisor Whiting stated that he appreciates the Auditor General's Office for the work on this.  He shared his appreciation to the Navajo County Team for looking at ways to be in compliance.
Supervisor Seymore thanked Mr. Vowell and his staff for the work that they put into the audits, stating that he knows that a lot of improvements have been made.
Supervisor Benally thanked them for all the work that they do and their accountability.
Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked the Auditor Generals Office for the presentation and for their investment in the organization.  She stated that it's important to understand the standings in the organization and how we can improve.  She thanked Mr. Vowell and his team for their investment and for findings way that we can improve and better serve the communities.

NAVAJO COUNTY PUBLIC HEALTH SERVICES DISTRICT: Board of Directors Session :

REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

Consideration for approval of the amended food code ordinance, ordinance PHSD 01-09 to adopt the 2017 Food Code in Navajo County and update the associated fee schedule for food and environmental health activity :
Rhonda Krouse introduced the staff in the Environmental Health Division.  A presentation was shown.  She reviewed the data from 2021, indicating that there are approximately 588 fixed establishments, that last year they were able to complete at least one inspection for each of the establishments, 1351 inspections per the food code, 173 mobile vendor inspections, 47 pre-openings that consist of fixed establishments, new restaurants, and new vendors and 75 complaints from the public which range from foodborne illnesses to bed bugs in hotels to algae growth.   She stated that their goal this year is to get all inspections in the food code done, which is approximately 1944 inspections.  She talked about the history of environmental health, including the executive orders that came with COVID.  She talked about the importance of the Environmental Health Division with doing the inspections.  She stated that forty-five million people have a foodborne illness yearly, that 128,000 are hospitalized, that there are three thousand deaths and that it costs anywhere from ten billion to eight-three billion dollars a year.  She talked about the food code, indicating that every four years the USDA, CDC, industrial personnel and consumers discuss the food code during a conference to determine any changes that should be made and then then make recommendations to the FDA, who will determine if those changes are accurate.  After the changes have been made they send it to the states who review them and determine whether or not to adopt them and then they are sent to the counties to determine what they want to do.  She stated that our Ordinance has not been updated since 2009 and that they are using the 1999 food code, but that several counties have adopted the 2017 food code, which is the new code that has been published.  She indicated that they are still reviewing the 2021 code and that it will not be available for the states until late 2022 or possibly 2023.
Kathryn Mathewson addressed the Board regarding the changes in the 1999 code and 2017 code, indicating that it identifies the different types of establishments, the temperatures for hot holdings and the different types of violations.  She stated that there were seventeen different changes, and most were language changes.
Supervisor Seymore inquired if any of the changes would be a great cost for the business.
Ms. Mathewson indicated that there would be no changes in the equipment, but that Navajo County is the only county in the state that does not have a food manager ordinance or food handler ordinance but with the new ordinance they are asking that it be added which would be a cost to the organization providing the food handler card.
Supervisor Whiting inquired how much they currently collect from fees annually and how much it would go to.
Ms. Krouse indicated that it is approximately $280,000 and that they are anticipating approximately $320,000.
Supervisor Whiting inquired what the additional money will be used for.
Ms. Krouse addressed how the money will help with their budget.
Supervisor Whiting inquired how they are partnering with the businesses to prepare them for some of the changes.
Ms. Krouse talked about how they will be doing public service announcements, letting the businesses know when they are doing inspections, sending out correspondence.
Supervisor Whiting thanked them for having worked on a strategy to be able to communicate with the business.
Ms. Krouse reviewed the amendment changes that they are proposing.  She stated that they have published this in the paper and that they have not received any comments.
Supervisory Seymore stated that he appreciates the work done on this.
Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked them for the presentation and indicated that there is a great amount of effort put into ensuring the safety of the public.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to approve the amended food code ordinance, ordinance PHSD 01-09 to adopt the 2017 Food Code in Navajo County and update the associated fee schedule for food and environmental health activity, motion seconded by  Supervisor Benally .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
