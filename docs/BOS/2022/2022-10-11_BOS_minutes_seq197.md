Bryan Layton, Assistant County Manager; Glenn Kephart, County Manager; Brad Carlyon, County Attorney; Brandt Clark, Deputy County Attorney (appearing via Zoom); Melissa Buckley, Clerk of the Board

CALL TO THE PUBLIC :
Roxanne Perguson, Navajo County Attorney's Office, thanked the Board for wearing purple in support of Domestic Violence Awareness Month.  She addressed the statistics for the services provided each year to victims of domestic violence and for the Crisis Response Team.  She addressed the events that have happened and talked about the events that will be happening this month, indicating that the money raised during the events goes to a crisis response emergency fund to help with victim's needs.
Becki Colon, Navajo County Attorney's Office, talked about the poker run event that will take place on October 15, 2022.  She indicated that it will be a run that helps raise awareness and money to help support victims.
Ms. Perguson introduced her staff.
Catrina Jenkins, Navajo County Emergency Management, updated the Board on the railroad closure on State Route 77.  She stated that it will begin on August 22, 2022, at 1:00 p.m. and will last through October 25, 2022.  She indicated that it will be a hard closure and that First Responders will be allowed to cross the tracks but only emergency vehicles that are responding to emergency situations.  She stated that BNSF will have trains coming through at a restricted speed of five miles per hour.  She further stated that there will be a pedestrian crossing and people will be allowed through between trains.  She indicated that this is a joint project between ADOT and BNSF. She advised that she has flyers for anyone who is interested and that it has maps of the alternate routes.
Bill Bess, Navajo County Public Works, talked about the restrictions and how they will handle local traffic.  He talked about the challenges that they will face with the amount of traffic that comes through on a daily basis.
Ms. Jenkins continued to address the closure and the need for the repair.  She stated that the repairs are supposed to last for fifteen years.
Mr. Bess stated that ADOT will have some equipment for road maintenance during that time staged by the north on the Public Works parking lot.
Chairwoman Whitesinger requested that the information be shared with the public as soon as possible.
Ashley Dodds, 8601 Silver Creek Drive, Show Low, addressed the Board regarding HB2607.  She inquired of the names and political affiliation for those who initiated the Bill and what will determine who is qualified to be appointed to the initial Board of Directors by the Board of Supervisors.  She addressed Section C38-14 stating that if the Board lacks a quorum for more than thirty day the Board of Supervisors may revoke the authority of the appointed Board of Directors and indicated that she feels that this should be extend for a period of three months.  She addressed the taxes and the budget for District.  She inquired if the appointed Clerk position for the Board of Directors will be a paid position.  She further inquired if the reimburse to the County for services as part of the annual budget would include the Sheriff, insurance, roads, fire and lifeguards.  She indicated that she spoke with Senator Rogers regarding this and that she is wanting a follow up on the questions.
Glenn Kephart indicated that Rochelle Lacapa will get in contact with Ms. Dodds to answer the questions.
Cindy Dodds, 8435 Silver Creek Drive, addressed the Board regarding the White Lakes Community.  She stated that she doesn't have Facebook or social media and that she is not being information with what's going on in the community.  She stated that there needs to be more information provided.  She talked about the Car Circle not being used due to weeds and the disc golf not having a proper set-up and that it is not being used.  She stated that she feels like money is being wasted or not used.  She stated that she has not seen any improvements to the lake for the past two years.  She indicated that she went to meeting and that they talked about the new furniture and computers but that see has not seen anything else done. She stated that they have kayaks and inquired who is paying insurance for those if there was an accident.  She talked about the lake being shut down due to an algae problem and indicated that she had to find out through grape vine to knows what was going on. She stated that they need to have more signage some people will know what's going on.  She indicated that she is not sure if the lake has been opened yet and that there has to be a better way to let people know what's going on.
Supervisor Whiting directed that Ms. Dodds talk to Mark Honberger to address the issues.
Steven Slayton, 4422 Larkspur Road, Show Low, addressed the Board about voter integrity.  He stated that he put in a request for the Cast Vote Ballots for 2020, that the response was that the County was that they don't have to keep them, or they are not available and indicated that they are in the programs in the machines.  He talked about the number of Counties that have provided them and the number that has had problems with the votes, indicating that it is very concerning.  He stated that he put in a FOIA request and sent a letter to Supervisor Seymore and the Recorder but indicated that he hasn't heard back.  He addressed the funds received from Facebook and indicated that it hasn't been authorized by the State Legislature.  He talked about handing out criminal victim report forms to citizens so that they can report any voting issues they have to the Sheriff.
Jacklin McKinley, 8468 Cochise Circle, addressed the Board regarding notices of the Community Meetings for White Mountain Lakes and indicated that they do their best to attend the meetings and be involved in the community.  She stated that for the past twelve to thirteen years only a hand full of people are making decisions and that they feel like they are not being heard.  She further stated that the community meetings are lacked, that notices are not put up until twenty-four hours prior to the meeting, that they find out about things after the fact, that the lake isn't being used due to the lack of being taken care of or the community having input.  She addressed the algae issue and stated that it was serious.  She requested that the Board work with them and keep them more informed.  She addressed the appointment of the Community Board, indicating that she hopes it will be local residents and that there should be five Board Members. She indicated that the important meetings take place in winter when approximately fifty percent of population is not available.  She requested that they be more informative and let them know when the meetings will happen.
Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked everyone who spoke during Call to the Public and informing the Board of their concerns.

COUNTY ATTORNEY :

a.

Consideration of a grant agreement between the U.S. Department of Justice and Navajo County to accept $1.3 million from the Bureau of Justice Assistance (BJA) Comprehensive Opioid, Stimulant, and Substance Abuse Site-based Program (COSSAP) for Navajo County Rapid Response Program for Overdose Prevention Project, and authorizes the Finance Director to sign the grant agreement in JustGrant :
Brad Carlyon addressed the Board regarding the item, stating that he previously reviewed the program details when they came before the Board to get permission to apply for the grant.  He stated that the grant is to identify those coming into the criminal justice system, mainly the jail, that are high risk for opioid overdose and help get them treatment and assistance to keep them alive. He indicated that the grant is for a two-year period and that there are a number of partners that were involved in the grant, including Sheriff Clouse, the jail and Community Bridges, Inc., as well as others.  He requested that the Board approve the item.
Supervisor Seymore thanked Mr. Carlyon and the Team for the effort that they have put forth working on the item and indicated that he hopes this can make a difference.
Mr. Carlyon thanked Ken Maruyama and Dawn Wilson for the work they have done on the grant.

Supervisor Whiting made a motion  to approve a grant agreement between the U.S. Department of Justice and Navajo County to accept $1.3 million from the Bureau of Justice Assistance (BJA) Comprehensive Opioid, Stimulant, and Substance Abuse Site-based Program (COSSAP) for Navajo County Rapid Response Program for Overdose Prevention Project and authorizes the Finance Director to sign the grant agreement in JustGrant, motion seconded by  Supervisor Seymore .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked everyone who has worked on this item and stated that she knows that it will have a positive impact on many citizens, not only those affected by opioids, but families and other citizens who may be affected as well.

SHERIFF :

a.

Consideration of a donation from Navajo County for the construction of the Navajo County Peace Office Memorial :
David Clouse thanked the County Attorney's Office for the work and efforts on Domestic Violence Awareness.  Sheriff Clouse introduced John Jarrett and indicated that he will talk about the Peace Officer Memorial.
John Jarrett talked about the Memorial, indicating that construction has started by Show Low City Hall.  He talked about the history with law enforcement.  He further talked about the memorial that is in Show Low and how it's old, faded and out of date.  He stated that people need to know the sacrifice and what they do to protect our communities.  He indicated that he had many ideas, moving it, cleaning it, bringing it up to date or re-doing it.  He talked about the meetings that were held and the planning that has taken place.  He further talked about the construction, the funds that are needed as well as the funds that have been raised.  He indicated that he has tried to keep everyone informed.  He talked about why the memorial is located in Show Low, the criteria for an officer to be memorialized and the names of the fourteen Officers that will initially be on the memorial.  He indicated that they are looking into other names that may be placed on the memorial.
Supervisor Whiting stated that they appreciate them being here and presenting.  He talked about the emotions from being close to those who serve in law enforcement.  He stated that he appreciates the time that has been put into this and getting participation from the region.  He inquired who all in the region has participated in the memorial.
Mr. Jarrett talked about different organizations through the region that have donated to the project.  He stated that he is open to any ideas and thoughts that the Board may have. He talked about the donations that have been received and funds spent to date.
Supervisor Whiting inquired if looking at doing a yearly recognition.
Ms. Jarrett talked about some of the different dates that they hold vigils or events to recognize fallen officers.  He stated that the planning isn't there yet as to what will be done in the future.
Supervisor Seymore shared his appreciation for what has been done with working on this, stating that memorializing a name or event can be difficult and that it needs to be meaningful.  He talked about the greatness of when the monument was originally done and indicated that the names that are on there is very respectful for them and their families.  He stated that he is thankful to those who have already stepped up.
Mr. Jarrett thanked all the Navajo County staff that has been involved.
Supervisor Benally inquired as to the names that she spoke to Sheriff Clouse about being included with the initial names being placed on the memorial.
Mr. Jarrett indicated that they will be included if were within Navajo County.
Supervisor Peshlakai thanked them for the work done on the memorial.  He inquired if the funds would be a combination from the general fund and the Sheriffs fund.
Sheriff Clouse indicated that the request is for $25,000, that the Sheriff's Office is giving $12,500 from the anti-racketeering funds that are used for giving back to the communities through drug prevention projects, education, and law enforcement activities and that they are requesting that the Board approve donation of $12,500.
Glenn Kephart stated that the County has capacity from the capital and funding of compacity for this.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion to approve a donation from Navajo County for the construction of the Navajo County Peace Office Memorial; motion seconded by Vice-Chairman Peshlakai .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

Consideration of a Cooperative Agreement for Animal Impoundment Services between Navajo County Public Health Services District and Pet Allies :
Rhonda Krouse addressed the Board regarding Pet Allies, Inc. indicating that it is a non-profit organization, located in Show Low, that they operate at a 96% save rate meaning it is a no kill shelter and that they offer low-cost spaying and neutering.    She stated that they had a new shelter built in 2021.  She talked about the proposed contract and indicated that she believes that it will build a better relationship and help with some of the animal issues throughout Navajo County.  She stated that the shelter is a great place and a welcoming environment.
Supervisor Seymore talked about what a great organization it is.
Chairwoman Whitesinger inquired how they are notified about the animals.  Ms. Krouse stated that they use dispatch and Spillman to get notification to the Officers.  She stated that she doesn't have the data prior to starting but since starting with Spillman last year have had 1272 calls, which is an average of approximately 100-135 calls a month.

Supervisor Whiting made a motion  to approve a Cooperative Agreement for Animal Impoundment Services between Navajo County Public Health Services District and Pet Allies, motion seconded by  Supervisor Seymore .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

WHITE MOUNTAIN LAKES COUNTY RECREATION IMPROVEMENT DISTRICT: Board of Directors Session :

REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

Quarterly Report and Update :
Mark Honberger provided an update on the Labor Day Holiday, indicating that there was low visitation, but that they had a safe summer overall.  He talked about the water quality update for the and indicated that warnings that were posted limiting swimming for three weeks to allow the test results to come back but that the lake wasn't closed for boating.  He stated that they had algae water and the basic lake water tested, indicating that the algae water was toxic meaning that it is harmful to be ingested and could be irritating to people with sensitive skin but that the basic lake water didn't have any visual allergy and was pristine.  He stated that the best place to find information for the lake is on the website or Facebook page.  He provided an update on the water levels, indicating that it is currently at three feet above the zero mark, with the zero mark being at twenty-four feet, and he talked about the fluctuating levels.  He stated that they repainted the boat ramp and talked about the boat slip rentals, kayak rentals and concessions.  He indicated that they currently have 655 followers on Facebook, with forty-eight being new followers.  He talked the Bass Tournament that was held at the Lake.  He addressed the community feedback, talking about how they communicate with the public and reach out to the community.
Supervisor Whiting thanked for providing an update and continuing to provide outreach to the community.  He indicated that they want to be open as best as possible to make sure everyone knows what is going on.  He stated that he would be interested to see if anyone comes forward with solutions on how to better reach out to the community.

Supervisor Whiting made a motion  to return to regular session, motion seconded by  Vice-Chairman Peshlakai .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
