BOARD OF SUPERVISORS 
NAVAJO COUNTY, ARIZONA 
October 24, 2005 
 
Kayenta Town Hall, Kayenta Justice Court and Peabody Coal Mine – Time:  9:17 a.m. (DST) 
 
PRESENT:  Jerry Brownlow, Chairman; Percy Deal, Vice-Chairman; David Tenney, Member; Jesse 
Thompson, Member; J.R. DeSpain, Member; James G. Jayne, County Manager; Darlene Fraley, Deputy 
Clerk of the Board.  
 
Mr. Deal welcomed those present and led the Pledge of Allegiance and the Invocation. 
 
Bill Cly of the Kayenta Township and Albert Bailey of the Chapter welcomed all present advising that 
Kayenta is a unique community in that it is its own government and encouraged sharing experience that the 
County has access to as well as supporting the Township when possible.  Mr. Bailey introduced Chapter 
personnel and reviewed the history of the Township.  He advised that Kayenta collects a sales tax and does 
not look to a central government for everything while using government support to leverage funds.   
 
Mr. Brownlow introduced the Board of Supervisors a nd all elected officials in the audience:  Judge 
Nielson, Judge Wing, Judge Ruechel, Judge Widmaier, Judge Bradley, Judge Hatch, Superintendent of 
Schools AJ Brown, Clerk of Courts Juanita Mann, and Constable Claw.  He recognized Holbrook City 
Manager David Newlin, Highway Patrolmen, and C ounty staff from the Sh eriff’s Office, Human 
Resources, Public Works, Building and Grounds, Administration and the Board of Supervisors. 
 
KAYENTA AIRPORT PLANNING PROJECT: Bill Cly introduced the Kayenta Township staff.  He 
reported the existing airport in Kayenta is not in good condition.  The new one will have hangars and be 
north of the existing site with an anticipated completion date of 2006.  In response to questions from Mr. 
Newlin, Mr. Cly stated that the airplanes using the airport use general aviation fuel and due to concerns 
regarding large planes tearing up the runway, the plane passenger limit is about 20.  Mr. Deal and Mr. Cly 
discussed the difficulties experienced when seeking financial assistance from the State and the FAA.  Mr. 
Deal advised other areas such as Homeland Secu rity, emergency management agencies and fire 
departments experience these same difficulties.  Mr. Bailey advised that the airport must be upgraded as it 
must be usable in the event of medical emergencies.  He reported Kayenta Township is asking for  
financial support from the County. 
 
SCHOOL BUS ROUTE MAINTENANCE:   Kayenta Unified Superintendent of Schools, Mr. 
Allsbrooks, welcomed all Navajo County representa tives.  He recounted the agreement between the 
County and the Kayenta schools regarding road mainte nance of school bus routes  wherein the district 
accepted responsibility for the maintenance.  He reported that a recent legal opinion advises that schools 
can not maintain roads, that this is a federal, state and county responsibility.  He stated that agreements for 
the bus routes must be between the BIA and the County and he is willing to facilitate the agreement.  He 
reported Kayenta has 2500 students and transports 1500 of them with 32 bus routes, half of which are not 
paved.  Mr. Allsbrooks reported the district is willing to house the equipment and would consider a lease; 
he said it could become a sub station to house equipment to be used on roads other than bus routes.  He 
reported that Kayenta School District has made considerable academic progress and is the first reservation 
district to reach performance plus academic rating.  All schools in the district made Adequate Yearly 
Progress and a major criteria of this performance measure is attendance.  He stressed the need to take the 
measures necessary to maintain the school bus routes. Mr. Deal expressed his concern regarding this issue 
and discussed the need for the BIA to cooperate with the County to assist in addressing the issue.  Mr.

BIA ROADS: There were no BIA representatives present to address this topic. 
 
DRUG COURT PRESENTATION:  Presiding Judge Dale Nielson reported that the judicial system will 
initiate a Drug Court in Navajo County in January. He outlined the membership of the drug court who will 
come together on a weekly basis with those who must participate in a Drug Court as a condition of their 
probation.  He explained this is an 18 month program designed to break methamphetamine addiction 
through weekly meetings, drug tests, participation in  AA or NA meetings, community service, specific 
incentives and if necessary because of noncompliance, sanctions that may include jail time.  The Court did 
not receive a requested grant but in recognition of the growing problem of meth in the County the courts 
made a commitment to fund this project that has a good success rate in other areas of the country.  Judge 
Nielson will be the only judge participating in the drug court initially.  He encouraged Kayenta to allow 
the implementation of such a program and advised that  grant funding is critical to future success.  Mr. 
Newlin offered the City of Holbrook as an employment opportunity for those who are going through the 
program.  Judge Wing reported that the growing meth problems in the County are impacting families when 
children must be removed from the homes and gave an example of a veteran CPS worker whose morale is 
suffering from this growing problem.  Asa Begay reported that he does contract work for the Department 
of Justice and reviews Drug Court proposals.  He stated that a major grant success component is the effort 
of cooperation between the County that borders the Nation with the Nation.  He said reviewers look for 
sustainability, capacity, skills and knowledge.  He a dvised that personnel is available for technical 
assistance in developing a grant proposal.  Mr. Thompson reported that at one time the Drug Court was 
available on the Navajo Nation.  He thanked Judge Nielson for reaching out to the Native American 
community and encouraged the establishment of such a program. 
 
RESOURCE AVAILABILITY—FIRE:   Constable Claw advised that he volunteers on the Kayenta fire 
department and they are seeking funds from the County for equipment for the fire department as matching 
funds for a grant.  He said they are currently using equipment borrowed from the Navajo Nation and are 
requesting assistance from Navajo, Apache and Coconino Counties.  Donna Leno reported that she works 
for the Indian Health Services seeking grants and is using this experience to assist in obtaining grants for 
the Fire Department.  She said their department is an all volunteer department, covering 4,000 square miles 
in three counties in Arizona and San Juan County in Utah. She advised that the Kayenta Fire Department is 
the only emergency response team within 150 miles and it takes an average of 20-30 minutes to respond in
