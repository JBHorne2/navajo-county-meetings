BOARD OF SUPERVISORS 
NAVAJO COUNTY, ARIZONA 
  
Special Meeting 
January 11, 2005 
  
Supervisors' Chambers, Holbrook, Arizona-Time 10:15 a.m. 
  
PRESENT: Present in Chambers: Chairman Jerry  Brownlow; Vice-Chairman Percy Deal; 
Members Jesse Thompson and David Tenney; County Manager James G. Jayne; Deputy 
County Attorney Lance Payette; Board Secretary June Foard. 
  
UPDATE ON CURRENT NORTHE RN ARIZONA DECLARATION OF 
EMERGENCY: Larry Dunagan reported that the D eclaration of Emergency now includes 
Apache County.  He indicated that the declaration still stands as origina lly declared with the 
amendments to include Navajo, Apache and Gila  County.  Mr. Dunagan asked if there were 
any new information that came from meeting with the Navajo Nation.  He reported that, at this 
time, there is no direction from the Governor’s office on how to proceed.   He reported that 
they are looking at the BIA roads on the reservation for funds to repair those roads.  BIA 
representatives reported that they will have an assessment team go out to assess the conditions 
and damage to the roads on the Navajo Reservation for cost es timates. That is being done at 
the Tribal and Federal level. The use of State funds to repair the Federal and BIA roads on the 
reservations cannot be reimbursed to Navajo County by the State.  The County has the option 
of using other funds.  Mr. Thompson reported that the Hopi BIA was working almost 24 hours 
per day and was assisting with the assessments . Discussion was held on  funds available for 
help and re-imbursement.  Questions were raised as to whether reimbursements could be made 
by the State for Federal roads.  Lance Payette reported that there could be no reimbursements 
from the State for those Federal roads as only Federal monies could be used. 
  
Larry Dunagan reported there ha ve been temporary repairs done to the levee.  There were 
10,000 additional sandbags received from the State.  The information 593 line is now 
operational for residents in Winslow to call for updates on the levee.   
  
Mr. Tom Hieb reported on the repairs to the levee.    He reported that the emergency repairs 
were done when the levee bre ached on December 31, 2004.  Since then there were several 
things done.  Experts were brought in to look at the breach and decide what to do to make the 
repair for short term and long term.  The experts were pleased with the temporary repairs.  Mr. 
Hieb reported they will be get ting some recommendations for futu re repairs of the levee.  Mr. 
Hieb reported the storm had been downgraded which should help the situation.  He added they 
will continue to monitor the levee for additio nal cracks and  there were equipment crews on 
standby.  Discussion was held on funds for the temporary repairs.   
  
Mr. Menlove reported that there were some materials donated by AP S and those materials 
were very much appreciated.  Mr. DeSpain requested a letter be prepared to thank APS for the 
materials.

It was reported that there have been 12,000 to 15,000 sandbags placed on the levee.  The bags 
have been placed 2 to 3 feet high in the breach area to ensure that water would not go over the 
levee.  Jack Brooks pointed out the areas that were of the most  concern.  Chairman Brownlow 
asked if the railroad crossing was in danger and Tom Hieb reported that there was not much 
danger at this time.  Mr. Thom pson expressed his appreciation of everyone’s efforts to help 
with this emergency. 
  
Mr. Parsons reported on costs of  the repairs to date.    Mr. DeSpain asked how much of the 
$200,000 amount would be refundable.  Larry Duna gan reported the State Legislature could 
increase that amount at the Governor’s request. 
  
Mr. Jayne stated that he would li ke to thank all the county sta ff for their assistance with the 
emergency and advised that Public Works sta ff was working on letters to thank the various 
entities. 
  
Several tribal entities were in attendance to present information regarding concerns for their 
area and for residents in outlying and remote areas.  Mr. Herb Begay  reported that tribal 
officials were assessing the areas for residents that needed water, food, medicines, coal and 
other necessities. Problems with roads on the reservation were discussed.  Mr. Begay reported 
that school bus routes are a priority as it is im possible to get some of the children to school at 
this time.  Mr. Harry Begay  addressed the board regarding his area of the reservation.  Mr. 
Begay presented pictures taken on January 5, 2005 of road damage. Mr. Deal reported that 
there has been more damage due to rain and snow since the photos were taken.  Mr. Fred Tso, 
Transportation Director for several school districts, addressed the board regarding some of the 
school routes.  He reported that about 2,500 miles per day were traveled by school buses.  He 
indicated that most of the routes were  impassable and unsafe at this time.  Mr. Tso reported 
that many of the culverts were washed out. It was reported that several of the schools have 
been forced to shut down because it is impossibl e to get the students to school. Requests were 
made for the Board of Supervisors to lend any support that was available.  Mr. Donald Chee  
from Forest Lakes addressed the board. He reported that there were three bus routes in his area 
to maintain - Pinon, Kayenta and Black Mesa.  He reported they had a road grader, but no 
money to maintain it.  Ms. Joan Greiser with the BIA in Gallup addressed the board indicating 
that the BIA has just learned that the Navajo Nation has declared a state of emergency.  She 
indicated the BIA has been keeping the paved ro ads open as much as possible.  She reported 
that she spoke with Ken Davis from the Arizona Emergency Department.  He indicated that if 
State funds were requested, there must be at  least $700,000 in damages and the roads must be 
major arteries.  She reported the roads could only be returned to their original state.  She added 
that the BIA was doing all they could to help with the situation.  Assessments were being done 
and funds applied for.   
  
The board adjourned into Executive Session to r eceive legal advice on this issue.  Upon their 
return the following action was taken:

Highway User Revenue Fund monies  for flood-related road repairs.   It was moved and 
seconded to approve funding from District Speci al Road Funds (HURF), in a total amount of
