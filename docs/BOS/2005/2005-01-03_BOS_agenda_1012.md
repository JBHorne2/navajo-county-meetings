2) Approval of proposed plan of action for pursuing improvements to the Winslow Levee
: Mr. Hieb 
advised the Board that there was a piping fa ilure in the Winslow Levee on December 31 st, which was 
possibly caused by: desiccation cracks, root channels , rodent burrows or structural flaws. Emergency 
repairs have been completed. Mr. Hieb presented a brief history of the levee, which is 7.2 miles long 
and owned by the Navajo County Flood Control District. While it is not known exactly when the levee 
construction was first begun, one early account of the levee activities is cite d in the July 30, 1954 
Winslow Mail newspaper. Navajo County first becam e involved in constructing levees and jetties 
along the river sometime in the early 1960’s. Damaging floods occurred each year from 1968 to 1972, 
and again in 1978 & 1979.The levee constructi on completed during the 1960’s and 1970’s does not 
appear to have followed any master plan or design. In fact, a U.S. Army Corp s of Engineers report in 
1976 stated, in part: “ …The levee is not yet complete, and it follows neither a set schedule nor 
standard design. For these reasons, the le vee is not entirely effective.”   The first efforts to build an 
engineered levee came following a flood in December , 1978. This flood caused extensive damage in 
Ames Acres and Bushman Acres subdivisions. In 1989, the Winslow levee is completed for an 
estimated cost of more than $5 million. In 1993, floodwaters overtopped the levee and flooded 
properties in Ames Acres, Bushman Acres and other areas. Temporary repairs were completed 
immediately on the levee. Affected property owners  filed a lawsuit agains t the county and others. 
Permanent repairs to a 3000-foot section of the levee were completed in December, 1994. In 1997 & 
1998, 2600 feet of the levee were improved by minor re shaping and the addition of filter fabric and 
rock rip-rap to both sides of the levee. In 2000, evidence from the studies done for the litigation 
indicated that the levee may not have the capacity to contain th e 100-year flood. Evidence from a 
variety of sources also indicate d that sediment might be building up in the river and reducing the 
capacity of the levee. Delph Engi neering is hired to determine the capacity of the levee and re-
delineate the flood hazard areas in the vicinity of the levee. Findings of the sediment study included: 
1) During the period from 1940 to 1980, approximately 3- 4 feet of sediment was deposited in the river 
along the reach from Holbrook to Winslow. This depos ition was along the entire  reach of the river, 
which indicated it was not related to any specific br idge, levee or structure. 2) Sometime after 1980, 
the river began to erode those sediments. Since 1980, the river has eroded 2-5 feet into the sediment, 
but the erosion is not expected to proceed much fu rther because the river has reached bedrock in some 
places. At this point, the channel is  stable (±1 foot within the next  50 years) along the most of the 
levee. However, 1-2 feet of aggradation is expect ed near the HWY 66 bridge and the railroad bridge. 
Reach-wide aggradation is not expected in the Winslow areas and therefore, sediment will not 
significantly impact the Winslow levee. No immediat e action needs to be take n to influence sediment 
transport in the Holbrook and Winslow areas. Howe ver, a sediment-monitoring plan is recommended. 
The Floodplain Study conducted in 2002 concluded th at the Winslow Levee does not meet FEMA 
requirements for 100-year flood protection. The levee does not have the capacity to contain the 100-
