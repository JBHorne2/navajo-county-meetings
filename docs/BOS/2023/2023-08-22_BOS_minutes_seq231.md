ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES :

a.

Presentation on Per- and Polyfluoroalkyl Substances (PFAS) and testing in Navajo County :
Paula Panzino, Chief Science Officer for the Arizona Department of Environmental Quality, talked about testing that they have been doing on small drinking water systems in Arizona.  She indicated that they have been presenting the information to the different counties.  A video was shown regarding what PFAS is and the impact it could have.  Ms. Panzino talked about the facts from Navajo County, indicating that there are 63 small drinking water systems, that are 3300 customers or smaller and 40 of them were eligible for testing.  She indicated that the EPA is also conducting testing under what they call the uncontaminated monitoring rule and that they are on their fifth batch of testing.  She stated that the large drinking water systems will be sampled as part of EPA system.  She stated that there are 32 systems participating in Navajo County, that only two have detected PFAS and that they were below the draft MCL levels.  She stated that Navajo County is doing well.  She talked about one location that they are testing due to wharf sites, which is the state superfund program and indicated that the site is under investigation for releases of chlorinated solvents, which are usually related to metals finishing processes or dry cleaning.  She talked about the location of the wells associated with the site. She indicated that there are also private wells in that area and that ADEQ has recommended that the owners sample their systems.  She stated that the fact sheet also talks about wastewater and biosolids sampling, but that no locations in the county were sampled, that it was a voluntary sampling and that no one volunteered.  She talked about the AFFF foam take back program.  She indicated that they did reach out to the fire departments, that the fact sheet lists the fire departments that volunteered to have their AFFF taken and replaced with non PFAS foam and that 950 gallons have been replaced in Navajo County.
Supervisor Seymore inquired how to notify the private well owners of the testing.
Ms. Panzino stated that they don't have a program for that, that under the wharf program they would go door to door and ask if the well owners wanted their well sampled but that since PSAF is not regulated they don't have authority to do that.  She indicated that they have instructions on the website on how to test private water systems.
Chairman Peshlakai inquired if all of the testing has been off reservation.
Ms. Panzino indicated that there is some testing that the EPA is doing with the Navajo and Hopi Tribes but that she doesn't know the details.  She indicated that she can provide contact information for getting additional details on that.

REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

Presentation of 2023 Community Health Assessment (CHA) report and Community Health Improvement Plan (CHIP) :
Janelle Linn indicated that the number one priority for Public Health has been the 2023 Community Health Assessment since January and that it is an assessment that is performed every six years to assess public health and safety needs.  She stated that the results from that is a community health improvement plan and that they will be presenting the findings today.  A presentation was shown.  She talked about the contributing partners that help with the assessment.  She stated that Navajo County was the top contributor and took responsibility for the project.  She talked about the four phases; data collection, data analysis, CHA Development and CHA Report.  She addressed the timeline that they have been working from.  She stated that the data appears to be negative, but that is what the survey is designed for to identify needs, the barriers, and the issues in the county so that it can be addressed and make the community healthier and safer.  She talked about the different parts of the data that they worked with.
Cathy Solomon talked about the difference in perceived data and real data.  She indicated that there were 2393 completed surveys, which is approximately two percent of population, that it was mostly females, that approximately sixty percent of the population that took it was white and the second being twenty percent that were American Indian and then a few other races.  She stated that the survey was available through a link, a QR code and paper.  She addressed the regions that the data was received from.  She talked about the five priorities that came from the project and the goals and strategies for the findings.
Mr. Linn talked about the percentages being low and needing to encourage people to change behavior and habits.  She talked about the plan moving forward, indicating that it's a six-year project and that mid-way through they will do a mini-assessment to update the data and information.  She indicated that over the next six weeks they will be presenting to the different boards and communities throughout the county and that the formal release to the public will be September 1, 2023.  She stated that Public Health will oversee and facilitate the project over the next six years and that they will have work groups for the different priorities, with each group having a chair/co-chair. She indicated that they will provide training and make sure that all groups are working in a similar direction.  She talked about the next steps for the groups. She recognized each of the team members, thanked and shared her appreciation for each of them.
Chairman Peshlakai inquired if next time the assessment is done, they could include the 638 and IHS as partners to help.
Ms. Linn indicated that they did reach out to both organizations, that they indicated they would do their own type of assessment, but that they do serve on many of the workgroups and are still involved in the process from other angels.
Supervisor Seymore inquired as to some of the statistics.  He thanked the team for all of the hard work done on this report, indicating that they are here to support in any way they can.
Vice-Chairman Whiting talked about how impressed he is with the Health Department and the organization that they have with everything that they do.  He thanked them for all that they do.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and possible adoption of  Resolution Number 30-2023 , approving/denying text amendments to Article 25, Section 2519 of the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance, regarding light pollution, and effective ways to prevent light trespass :
Tyler Richards stated that this is for a proposed amendment to the Navajo County Lighting Ordinance and that the purpose of the ordinance is to prevent light from projecting upward into the night sky and have detrimental effects of astronomical observations.  A presentation was shown.  He stated that the original ordinance was adopted in 1997, that it's been twenty-six years since the last revision and that lighting practices and trends have changed.  He stated that currently the ordinance does not have any provisions to assist code enforcement when dealing with complaints of light trespass.  He talked about the proposed amendments that they are looking at making. He stated that residential shielding would be required.  He addressed the reasons for the shielding and the definitions for shielding. He indicated that the Planning and Zoning Commission did recommend approval of the amendment, with the further recommendation that they strike the provision concerning fully shielded light fixtures.  He stated that staff is recommending full approval of the amendment.

Chairman Peshlakai opened the meeting for public comments.
Jamie Thorpe, 7313 Tall Pine Drive, Pinetop, inquired as to the grandfather clause, indicating that they have issue with their neighbor's lights shining inside their home and that another neighbor had to put up a shade and privacy fence due to it and that there is a pending lawsuit.  She stated that she hoped this amendment would help with the issue, but with the exemption, it appears that it won't change the issues.  She indicated that prohibition 7E indicates that light shouldn't affect other neighboring properties which applies to situation that they are having.  She talked about the contact that she has had with the Sheriff's Office regarding the situation and stated that it has been going on for over two and a half years.  She stated that she hopes the non-conforming clause can be reevaluated to help with problems right now.
Vice-Chairman Whiting requested that questions during public comments be addressed.
Cody Cooper addressed the concerns that were brought up, indicating that there is nothing that can be done to remove non-conforming, but that code enforcement can address the light trespassing issue.  He stated that it would be subject to code enforcement investigation.
Supervisor Seymore requested clarification regarding which proposal staff is requesting approval of the first proposal or the proposal with the recommendation by the Planning & Zoning Commission.
Mr. Richards indicated they are requesting approval of the original proposal.
Vice-Chairman Whiting addressed the examples that were shown, indicating that the ones that they are asking to be replaced are very common and popular fixtures and requested further explanation for why they would recommend the ones they are.
Mr. Richards talked about intention with fully shielded fixtures compared to unshielded light fixtures.
A discussion was held regarding how to accomplish what they are requesting with the amendment.
Mr. Cooper further addressed the differences between the fixtures.
Vice-Chairman Whiting addressed the issues with new builds in the county not being able to use the lights that are typical.
A further discussion was held.
There being no additional comments, the public hearing was closed.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to adopt  Resolution Number 30-2023 as recommended by staff, approving text amendments to Article 25, Section 2519 of the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance, regarding light pollution, and effective ways to prevent light trespass, motion seconded by  Supervisor Benally .  Motion passed 4-1 with Vice-Chairman Whiting opposing the motion.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and possible adoption of  Resolution Number 31-2023 , approving/denying a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction of a 105' monopine wireless communication tower and associated facilities in the Lakeside area on APN # 212-26-117A :
Sondra Starke indicated that this is a request for a Special Use Permit for a cell tower.  A presentation was shown.  She talked about the location of the proposed tower, the design, and the plans.  She stated that there is a school nearby, but that it is in a primary commercially related area which would be compatible for the use.  She addressed the site plan for the project. She talked about the staff analysis, indicating that it was properly noticed, and posted that it meets the comprehensive plan and has been determined to be compatible with current and future uses.  She stated that they have not received any comments in support of or opposed to the proposal.  She further stated that staff recommends approval subject to the conditions in the staff report and that the Planning & Zoning Commission unanimously recommended approval.
Linda Grise, on behalf of Young Design for Verizon Wireless, addressed the Board regarding the site, indicating that this would provide for a gap in coverage in the area and that it would improve for Verizon and E911 usage.  She stated that the monopine would be compatible to the area and that the fence would hide the equipment behind it.  She indicated that this would be in line if storage units were put in at a later time.

Chairman Peshlakai opened the meeting for public comments.  There being no comments, the public hearing was closed.

Supervisor Seymore inquired about the fence height.
Ms. Grise indicated that there will be a generator and the reason for the ten-foot fence is to disguise everything but that there is a possibility of changing it.
Supervisor Seymore indicated that it would be a little more conductive to the area if it wasn't as high.  He requested that they take it into consideration but indicated that he would not make that a requirement.
Chairman Peshlakai inquired as to the title not matching what is on the resolution.
Ms. Starke indicated that the applicant gets to select the name for the project and indicated that is the reason for the difference.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to adopt  Resolution Number 31-2023 , approving a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction of a 105' monopine wireless communication tower and associated facilities in the Lakeside area on APN # 212-26-117A, motion seconded by  Vice-Chairman Whiting .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
