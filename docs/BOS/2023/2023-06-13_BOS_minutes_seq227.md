REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

Presentation of the 2023 Wildfire Season Outlook :
Catrina Jenkins stated that this is the annual outlook.  A presentation was shown.  She indicated that 2023 has a good outlook, but that there is a way to go.  She addressed short-term drought, indicating that it has an important role, and that they look at it on a weekly basis to determine fire restrictions.  She addressed the drought differences from last year to this year and the winter storms and monsoons.  She stated that long-term drought is still a factor.  She talked about the outlook for significant wildfire potential, indicating that the potential is low this year but that it doesn't indicate that it could happen.  She stated that wildfire is always a threat, that it is part of living in the wildland urban interface, that we have to stay vigilant, and it is everyone's responsibility to prevent fires.  She talked about knowing before you go for restrictions and weather, having enough water for cooking, drinking, and putting out fires, not leaving fires unattended and always putting out your fire.  She further talked about the weekly meetings and regular communication with the regional partners, the organizations that make up the White Mountain Fire Coordination Group and the information provided by those organizations.   She talked about the Ready, Set, Go campaign and the Navajo County Alert System.
Chief Swanty stated that they have a communication circle within the regional partners so that when there is a situation requiring evacuation they are on the same page, and that everything moves forward orderly.  He indicated that you need to have a plan in place so that you can get your valuables and family out when the time comes.  He stated that you may only get one warning due to fire being unpredictable and that there may not be time for a second warning.  He addressed the procedures and challenges when someone does not wish to leave their property during an evacuation.  He encouraged citizens to use vetted sources for social media when relying on information.
Chief Chevalier talked about the monsoons helping with the drought but indicated that it also brings fine fuels, such as grass.  He indicated that Tonto National Forest recently went into some restrictions and indicated that although we don't currently have restrictions, we need to not to get complacent and to only have a fire in safe areas.  He addressed the block party that will be held July 15, 2023, from 9:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. for the communities to learn more about how to make your home and communities safer and be more resilient to wildfire.

Vice-Chairman Whiting stated that he appreciated the presentation and all of the partners working together and being united.  He inquired as to post put out regarding some fires left unattended.
Ms. Jenkins addressed the unattended fires.
Vice-Chairman Whiting talked about the increase of people in the forest since COVID.  He addressed the monthly meetings he has with the State Forester and indicated that he could share any information needed with him. He thanked them for their presentation.

b.

Consideration and adoption of the proposed Tentative Budget for Fiscal Year 2023-24 for Navajo County :
Jayson Vowell stated that since Navajo County is a political subdivision, the State mandates some of the services that the County provides, such as public safety, building and maintaining roads and transportation infrastructure, supporting health and welfare in the community, providing land use planning and building permit review, and provides essential services and support for other services.  He talked about the foundation of the budget, indicating that they want to develop and maintain a structurally balanced budget, to maintain a healthy reserve, and that they want to financially support the offices and departments to provide the services that they're in charge of for residents and the communities.  He stated that some of the highlights are that there will be no change to property tax rates, that property tax paid will have a slight increase due to rising home values, the focus on maintaining county services, and continuing the sustainable compensation plan for employees.  He talked about historical investment and regional projects, including the broadband middle miles network, the regional public safety communication network, the Winslow Levy, the Code Enforcement Building in Taylor, and improvements to the Holbrook County Complex.  Mr. Vowell reviewed the proposed revenues and talked about what property tax is based on and what it is used for.  He talked about the truth in taxation public hearing that will be held.  He reviewed the general fund expenditures.  He addressed the HURF fund that is used for road repair and maintenance, indicating that the reduced revenue could be from electric vehicles and higher efficiency vehicles and addressed the adjustments that were made to offset the loss in revenues.  He talked about the budget for HURF funds and the projects that are planned.  He addressed the public health revenues and expenditures, indicating that they have seen an increase in the demand for services.  He addressed expenditures for capital projects.
Supervisor Whitesinger indicated that they have been doing budget discussions for a while and indicated that being in a good position is positive for the organization and the citizens of Navajo County.  She stated that she appreciates the thought process for being able to maintain expenses but also enhance our services.  She thanked all of those who have invested time in getting to this point.
Mr. Vowell talked about the process that they use for the budget.
Supervisor Whitesinger stated that there are a lot of variables that impact the budget, that conservation is really important for being able to understand it and to be able to plan for the future to provide the quality services and the mandated service that are required.
Supervisor Seymore thanked everyone who has worked on the budget.  He indicated that it is the Board of Supervisors' number one responsibility to make sure that dollars are spent in a way that helps the communities.  He talked about how inflation has caused challenges and thanked staff for all of their efforts to keep making improvements.
Mr. Vowell talked about the inflation pressures that they are experiencing.
Vice-Chairman Whiting thanked the team for the work they have done on this.  He talked about being able to find solutions that are credible, being able to accommodate the services that are needed by the citizens but also working to have a budget that is expected.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to approve the proposed Tentative Budget for Fiscal Year 2023-24 for Navajo County, motion seconded by  Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

NAVAJO COUNTY FLOOD CONTROL DISTRICT: Board of Directors Session :

REGULAR AGENDA :

a.
Consideration of approving JE Fuller's contract "Silver Creek Flood ALERT" for the development of the Silver Creek Flood Alert System in the Silver Creek watershed :
Bill Bess stated that the Flood Control District has approximately twenty rain and flow sensors throughout the southern boundaries of the County to identify heavy rains and that they have used these for the past twenty years.  He indicated that this contract would allow J E Fuller to work with the National Weather Service in the Colorado Basin River Forecast Center to gather data and model the watershed so that they can predict or model a peak flow down the Silver Creek into the Snowflake/Taylor area.  He stated that they will be adding some new depth gauges.  He stated that once they get all of the data in, it will be available to the public on the websites so that they can see the existing and predictive flow and the camera that they will put on some of the major streams.
Vice-Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Bess and his team for the work done on this.  He talked about the scares that they have had in the past couple of years with flooding.  He stated that he appreciates them working with the different stakeholders on this solution.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to approve JE Fuller's contract "Silver Creek Flood ALERT" for the development of the Silver Creek Flood Alert System in the Silver Creek watershed, motion seconded by  Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to return to regular session, motion seconded by  Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
