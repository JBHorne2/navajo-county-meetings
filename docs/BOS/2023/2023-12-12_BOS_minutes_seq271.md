CALL TO THE PUBLIC :
Donna Gierst, White Mountain Lakes, provided the Board with a handout, and addressed the Board regarding Agenda Item 4d, the White Mountain Lakes DWID.  She talked about the steps that the community has taken to get try and get the water district back up and running to allow them to try and purchase the water company rather than a private company purchase it.  She indicated that she was told to get community input and form an advisory committee.  She talked about the board that was formed, indicating that it has been in effect for two months, and that an introductory letter was sent to the Board of Supervisors.  She further talked about the meetings that they have had with the Water Infrastructure Financial Authority.  She indicated that the community got together to address advantages and disadvantages for a DWID.  She indicated that there is an election in March.  She talked about the proposed resolution and the County Improvement District guidebook addressing dissolving a DWID.  She stated that the community is asking for an opportunity to look into being able to get funding.
Debi Bismarck, 8592 Fox Circle, White Mountain Lake, stated that she is on the Community Association Board and talked about meetings that were held regarding the DWID.  She stated that the community is not opposing the takeover, that they have a lot of hardship in the area, and that they don't need additional costs for another company to come in.
Eric Gierst, 1971 Daggs Circle, Show Low, stated that the DWID has been in place for over thirty years.  He talked about the work that the community members have done to have an opportunity to create and implement a DWID to manage their own water system.  He indicated that they want to be recognized as a potential buyer for the system.  He talked about the choices that are provided in the proposed resolution, indicating that both of those choices will destroy the communities chance to try and purchase.  He stated that they have a legal DWID in place now, and that they are requesting that their Board be recognized either as an advisory committee or a Board of Directors so they can take the steps necessary to move forward to try and buy the water system.  He indicated that if the community doesn't purchase it then another company will, and they will suffer for it through their bill.  He stated that by approving the resolution it takes away their opportunity.
Rob Hossler, 1949 Norton Place, stated that they formed an advisory committee as that he was selected as chairman.  He requested that they be given time to research their options and determine if this will work for them.  He stated that there are a lot of families in the community that are struggling and that if a private company comes in, they will raise their rates.  He indicated that there is a lot of work that needs to be done on the water district and that people will not be able to afford it.  He stated that they would have the option of grants with reduced funding which would save the community a lot of money to keep the water bills lower and more sustainable for families.     He requested the opportunity to do wants right for the community.

Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 49-2023 revoking the White Mountain Lakes Domestic Water Improvement District's Board of Directors authority and further dissolving the White Mountain Lakes Domestic Water Improvement District :
A presentation was shown.  Rochelle Lacapa talked about the water service provisions in Navajo County, indicating that they are provided in multiple ways, such as municipal water systems, private water companies via private wells, and through Domestic Water Improvement Districts, which in turn is a type of special taxing district and is governed by a separately elected board of directors.  She indicated that Navajo County does not provide water services and is not a utility provider anywhere in the county, that they rely on municipalities as well as other types of service providers to provide water.  She stated that in July 2023 community members from White Mountain Lake area started reaching out to county to reestablish DWID created in 1991 and that they received feedback from other community members that it not be reestablished, so it was unclear to staff if the community as a whole was in favor.  She indicated that staff started researching options.  She provided some background information regarding the 1991 DWID, indicating that it was established in 1991, that the board was appointed by the Board of Supervisors, that in 1993 the Board of Supervisors called for an elections which was held October 12, 1993 but that is was never canvassed, a bond was never passed and a tax was never collected, so the DWID existed on paper but never fully functioned as a fully elected board and that it's unclear when the board became inactive.  She stated that according to statute, when a special district becomes inactive or lacks a quorum for thirty days or more the Board of Supervisors can revoke that authority and dissolve that special district.  She indicated that they have worked with the Finance Department, Elections, the Recorders Office, and County Attorney's Office but were unable to find any documents since the 1993 Board of Supervisors minutes.  She talked about the current situation with the water company, which is owned by Voyager, indicating that in March 2023 the sole proprietor passed away, that the future of the company is unknown, and that the estate is going through probate.  She indicated that this caused some uncertainty for the community members and that some of the community members found the paperwork from the 1991 DWID and reached out to the county about reestablishing the DWID so they could take over operations, feeling that this would allow them more control over water rates within the community rather than a private entity coming in and possibly raising rates.  She stated that staff and would with the legal department to determine what options the Board of Supervisors has.  She talked about the options available to the Board, indicating that they can revoke the authority and dissolve the special district, or they can revoke the 1991 DWID and call for elections.  She talked about the concerns, advantages, and the disadvantages of both options, indicating that with either option it doesn't stop the private water company's ability to still be sold.  She stated that the community members are asking that the Board reestablish the water district.  She further stated that staff recommends option one, that the DWID be revoked and dissolved.
Vice-Chairman Whiting stated that he recognizes that the community is trying to find potential solutions but indicated that they have to look at what's legal.  He inquired if the Board that the community put together has any authority.
Jason Moore indicated that they don't have any authority.  He indicated that they don't know who the last governing board members were since it was so long ago, that they are operating on the assumption that all of the seats are vacant at this point and addressed the law regarding fill vacancies.  He stated that there is a third option, that the Board of Supervisors could revoke the authority and the Board could run the district, but that Navajo County hasn't been in the business of being a public utility provider in the past, so they are not recommending that option.
A discussion was held regarding the different options.
Vice-Chairman Whiting thanked the citizens for their statements.  He shared his appreciation to the community members who worked to try to put together something and looked at a way to move forward.

Vice-Chairman Whiting made a motion  to adopt  Resolution Number 49-2023 revoking the White Mountain Lakes Domestic Water Improvement District's Board of Directors authority and further dissolving the White Mountain Lakes Domestic Water Improvement District, motion seconded by  Supervisor Seymore .  Motion passed 4-0, with Supervisor Dawnafe Whitesinger being absent.

PUBLIC HEARING: Consideration and possible adoption of Resolution Number 53-202 3 approving/denying a request by Pinnacle Consulting, Inc. for a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction of a 100' monopole wireless communication tower and associated facilities in the Heber area :
A presentation was shown.  Sondra Starke stated that this item is for a call tower.  She addressed the location of the proposed project, indicating that the current use is commercial, and that zoning is industrial.  She stated that there will be a six-foot chain-link fence around the project area and that there is a dedicated access and utility easement.  She advised that the Planning and Zoning Commission did unanimously recommend approval with no additional conditions.  She reviewed the site plan and the minimum setbacks, indicating that it is in compliance.  She indicated that this has been properly noticed and advertised but that it was not posted due to inaccessibility.  She further indicated that there were note cards mailed out.  She stated that this proposal is in compliance with the zoning ordinance, that it meets several goals of the comprehensive plan and use has been determined as compatible with current and likely future uses.  She indicated that they haven't received any public comments.  She stated that staff recommends approval with the conditions outlined in the staff report.

John Katsiris, Pinnacle Consulting, (appearing via Zoom), talked about the location for the tower, indicating that it is an industrial zone and that there is a commercial company on the property.   He indicated that the setback meets the requirements.  He stated that the area is in need of upgraded service and that there has been no community opposition.

Vice-Chairman Whiting opened the meeting for public comments. There being no comments, the public hearing was closed.

Supervisor Seymore requested if the tower could be monopine instead of monopole due to the pine trees and the aesthetics of the area.
Mr. Katsiris addressed the reason that the monopine was not used.
A discussion was held regarding the type of pole that is being used.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to adopt  Resolution Number 53-2023  approving a request by Pinnacle Consulting, Inc. for a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction of a 100' monopole wireless communication tower and associated facilities in the Heber area, motion seconded by  Supervisor Benally .  Motion passed 4-0, with Supervisor Dawnafe Whitesinger being absent.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 54-2023 approving/denying a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction and operation of a 112.5 MWdc solar energy generation facility, 75 MW Battery Energy Storage System, and associated facilities on approximately 625 acres located in the Snowflake area :
A presentation was shown.  Cody Cooper stated that this is for a solar generation facility as well as battery storage.  He addressed the location of the project, indicating that it would be for one hundred twelve and a half megawatts of DC storage and for seventy-five megawatts of battery storage.  He talked about the access points to the location, construction management and the operations for the project.  He reviewed the site plan and indicated that the applicant has completed reviews for cultural resources, plant and wildlife populations, as well as wetland and stream features and that the site has been found to have little to no impact on those resources based on the review.  He stated that the applicant has also worked wit5h the State Fire Marshal's Office as well as the local fire jurisdiction to ensure that fire protection will be provided and the project and that is goes through the proper fire code.  He indicated that the Planning and Zoning Commission unanimously recommended approval of the project, that staff has not received any comments and that it is compliant with the relevant goals and policies of the comprehensive plan and will not have any effect on the public health, safety and welfare based on the conditions provided.  He stated that staff is recommending approval subject to the conditions listed in the staff report.
A presentation was shown.  Don Bugles, applicant, stated that the company is based out of Denver and that they develop renewable energy projects all over the country.  He talked about the proposed project, indicating that it will not alter the footprint or any aspect of the design to accommodate the project.  He addressed the location of the proposed project, stating that there are no residential neighbors in the area.  He talked about the economic impact for the project, indicating that it will generate tax revenue and create jobs during the construction as well as several permanent maintenance jobs.  He advised that the power will be generated and absorbed locally and that it will be owned by a group of private investors so there will be no rate increases from APS.

Vice-Chairman Whiting opened the meeting for public comments. There being no comments, the public hearing was closed.

Supervisor Seymore requested that staff, on decommissioning, follow the guidelines as much as possible and that the bond requirements be reevaluated every three to five years.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to adopt  Resolution Number 54-2023 approving a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction and operation of a 112.5 MWdc solar energy generation facility, 75 MW Battery Energy Storage System, and associated facilities on approximately 625 acres located in the Snowflake area, motion seconded by  Chairman Peshlakai .  Motion passed 4-0, with Supervisor Dawnafe Whitesinger being absent.
