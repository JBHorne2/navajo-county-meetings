b.

Consideration of adoption of Resolution Number 34-2023 , adopting the new voting precincts maps that were adjusted to meet the New Board of Supervisor Districts pursuant to A.R.S. 16-411 :
Jason Moore stated that they are not asking for final approval today, because they still need to make some final adjustments to the Justice of the Peace precincts and there can't be a finalized map until Justice Court precincts are lined out.  A presentation was shown.  He indicated that Title 22 allows the Justice Court precincts to be redrawn at any time.  He stated that they are also requesting that there not be a final approve due to concerns from some citizens with the precinct lines and that they would like to sit down with leadership of both political parties in Navajo County to get input on what they would like to see for the lines.  He provided some background regarding the redistricting process from 2010, the multiple precincts that were in place at that time and the legislation that was passed in 2011 to adopt voting centers.  He stated that when you use the precinct-based voting system the precincts were more important than they are today.  He talked about the frustration of voters going to a voting precinct to then be told they were in the wrong precinct and then having to go to a different precinct to be able vote, not being able to vote or if they insisted on voting at the wrong location being issued a provisional ballot and that ballot not counting due to it being cast in the wrong precinct.  He stated voting centers allow voters to cast their vote at any voting center within Navajo County.  He talked about some provisions in the law for combining precincts under certain circumstances when no other precinct is available, indicating that precincts are drawn for administrative purposed only and help the Elections Department and Recorder put on the election and not for the benefit of a person or political party.  He stated that the reason this is before the Board now is due to redistricting having taken place earlier this year and it being necessary to adjust the precinct boundaries accordingly and the statute saying is has to be done by October 1 of the year preceding a general election.  Mr. Moore talked about the guidance from the statute regarding drawing precincts.  He addressed precinct foundations for the different boundaries.  He talked about how precincts are used, indicating it is necessary for the ballot layout, rotating candidate names on the ballots, being able to accurately report election results and in the election for political party precinct committeeman.  He stated that Navajo County currently has fourteen precincts and that the proposed map that they are asking the Board to approve, in the interim, also has fourteen precincts with the boundaries having been modified to reflect the redistricting in Navajo County. He stated that we have continued to have thirty-nine voter centers throughout the county for getting people access to vote and polling place locations near them, which should be what is focused on to make sure voters have a vote center that is reasonably close.  He talked the concerns that were brought up at the presentation given to the Board by some citizens regarding compliance with the ARS 16-411(a)(1), indicating that due to the statute not being really clear, there could be some implementation differences between the various counties, and indicated that the precincts we have are already included within election district boundaries of the state and its political subdivisions. He stated that it's difficult to draw precinct boundaries in a way that isn't going to end up with different ballot styles within the same precinct and provided some examples of how that could happen.  He stated that tribal election boundaries aren't something that they take into account due to not overseeing those elections but that they are asked to take into account census designated places, which are units created by the federal government.  He addressed concerns from the Elections department regarding having more ballot styles when more precincts are created and the problems that could occur if a voter gets the wrong ballot.  He further addressed concerns with being able to fill precinct committeeman positions, indicating that they currently have a difficult time filling the positions.  He stated that there is not a statute in Arizona that puts a minimum or maximum number for precincts that you can have or the number of people that you can have within a precinct.
Rayleen Richards talked about the precinct boundary lines and what goes into creating ballots and ballot styles.  She further talked about when the super precincts were created reducing precincts from sixty-one to fourteen.  She stated that they did not reduce the polling locations but that it did cut down on the ballots and ballot styles, which was less confusing for poll workers and with voters getting the wrong ballot style when there were smaller precincts.  She stated with the voting center module, voters can go anywhere in Navajo County and vote the correct ballot style and provided some examples for how it works.  She stated that the Navajo Department of Justice has praised Navajo County on the vote center module and has asked for help getting Apache County to use it.  She indicated that her goal is to make sure voters of Navajo County can vote the correct ballot styles.  She stated that staff is recommending approval of the precinct lines and polling locations as presented.
Mr. Moore answered questions from the Board regarding the deadline of October 1, the request today being approval on an interim basis, meeting with both parties to get input and having the ability to adjust the lines prior to making a final decision.

1.

PUBLIC HEARING:  Consideration of the adoption of  Resolution 35-2023  approving/denying a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction of a 80' monopole wireless communication tower and associated facilities on a vacant, 13.15-acre parcel, in the White Mountain Lakes area :
A presentation was shown.  Cody Cooper talked about location of proposed tower in relation to the nearest runway at the White Mountain Lake Air Park, the site plan provided by the applicant and the property, indicating that it does qualify for the residential designation for a wireless communication facility.  He talked about the requirements for the tower height, setback and design and the fencing.  He advised that the applicant did hold a community meeting as required by the zoning ordinance.  He showed the determination by the FAA that there is no hazard to air navigation.  He stated that the Planning and Zoning Commission unanimously recommended denial of the project.  He stated that staff has gone through the ordinance as the item was received to review for full compliance and that based on the fact that this proposal meets all relevant goals of the Navajo County zoning ordinance, that it meets all relevant goals of the comprehensive plan and appears to have minimal adverse effects of health, safety and welfare staff recommends approval of the special use permit subject to conditions.
The applicant showed a presentation and talked about the concerns that were brought up in the Planning and Zoning Commission hearing, including the flight path, the altimeter errors, and the community opposition.  He stated that the FCC protects the construction of personal wireless service facilities given the provided documentation is in accordance with the Navajo County ordinance for wireless communication facilities, along with the FAA determination of no hazard as well as the airspace safety analysis and compliance study.  He further stated that finding the site to not be a hazard, they believe the further denial of the request would not follow a reasoned approach, which is required to block the proposal from proceeding.

Vice-Chairman Whiting opened the meeting for public comments.  He noted that he has a note that reflects that Buzz Essel is giving his three minutes to Tony Tangalos.

Tony Tangalos, 8319 Pinon Drive, stated that another individual, Rob, has given his three minutes to him.  He stated that he sold approximately sixty percent of the air park to Kathy and Scott Smith.  He further stated that there is a lot of evidence that 5G frequencies are more harmful than lower frequencies in adversely affecting health care.  He talked about the measurements of radiation that they measured with a industry standard radiation meter, indicating that the information being issued by pinnacle engineering is flawed.  He invited the Board to appoint someone to go out with them to see the measurements. He indicated that they are misrepresenting the power output, which is a problem.  He further indicated that they are misrepresenting the power, which he stated is unhealthy, and that they are trying to persuade everyone into thinking that it isn't very strong. He stated at the August 17 meeting there was not one person in the community that spoke on behalf of the tower, that everyone was against it and that there was a unanimous consensus in the Planning Commission to recommend denial.  He spoke about the information sent regarding the International Federation of Airline Pilots Association and the Airline Transportation Association issuing a safety bulletin saying 5G radiation is dangerous for pilots and that Airbus issued their own safety bulletin with concerns about 5G frequencies disabling radio altimeters, which allows pilots to know how high they are off the ground when landing.  He talked about the article where Verizon agreed to delay activation in light of the FAA's concern of 5G tower radiation harming radio altimeters.  He talked about being blasted with excessive amounts of electromagnetic radiation from a 5G box and the health issues that he is battling with.  He stated that he has requested a sixty-day delay to be able to get further information and be able to present it to the FAA.  He talked about the concerns the pilots have with flying here.
Katherine Smith, 1900 Bourdon Ranch Road, White Mountain Lake, talked about the investment that they made when taking over White Mountain Lake Airpark, indicating that they wanted a safe space for their family away from the pollution of the city, including 5G and electromagnetic frequencies.
