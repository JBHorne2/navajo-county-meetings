AGREEMENT made as of March 13, 2023, between Navajo County ("County") 
and Kimley-Hom and Associates, Inc. ("Consultant"). 
 
WHEREAS, County requires certain professional services for Buck Springs Road 
Equestrian Crossing ("Project"); and 
 
WHEREAS, Consultant represents that it has the necessary expertise to provide 
such services in accordance with the terms of this Agreement. 
 
NOW, THEREFORE, the parties agree as follows: 
 
1. Services. Consultant shall perform the work  described in the Scope of Work dated 
March 7, 2023, in a competent and professional manner to the satisfaction of 
County. The Scope of Work and the Consultant's schedule and fee proposal are 
attached hereto and by this reference incorporated herein. If any incorporated term

provide an adequate staff of experienced personnel, capable of and devoted to the

Agreement, key personnel shall not  be removed or replaced without County's prior

contractor. Neither party is or shall purport to be an agent, employee, partner, joint

(Revised 03/07/2023) 
 
Describing a specific agreement between Kimley-Horn and Associates, Inc. (the Consultant), and Navajo 
County (the Client) in accordance with the terms of the Master Agreement for Continuing Professional 
Services dated March 30, 2020, which is incorporated herein by reference. 
 
Identification of Project: 
 
Traffic Engineering Services consists of providing final design associated with the proposed installation of 
Rectangular-Rapid Flashing Beacon (RRFB) as recommended in the Equestrian Crossing on Buck 
Springs Road Memorandum, dated March 25, 2021. Based on our meeting held on December 20, 2022, 
we understand the following: 
 
• Consultant to provide plans, specs, and estimate (PS&E) to obtain construction bids for 
the installation of the Buck Springs Road RRFB. 
• Design will generally follow the proposed improvements outlined in the memorandum 
• Construction is estimated for Fiscal Year (FY) 2023 
• Cost estimate is necessary to assist with defining construction budget and programming 
funds 
• Submittals will consist of 30% estimate as well as 90% and 100% (Final) PS&E for 
advertisement 
 
Specific scope of basic Services includes: 
 
Task 1 Data Collection I Topographic Survey 
1. Topographic survey is not included with this project. Kimley-Horn will utilize NearMaps and as 
built plans obtained as part of Task 1.2. 
2. Kimley-Horn will obtain county as-built maps, such as roadway, water, sewer, right-of-way, and 
drainage to assist with the defining the existing project and base design map conditions 
3. Kimley-Horn will develop a base design file in AutoCADD, using the data collected. 
4. Field data will be provided by Navajo County Staff. Kimley-Horn will send request to assist Navajo 
County with list of data  and photos to collect. 
 
Task 2 Utility Coordination 
1. Kimley-Horn will be responsible for contacting Blue Stake and collect utility as-builts to determine 
the location of existing utility facilities within the project limits. The utility data collected will be 
utilized to develop an existing utility CAD file. 
2. Kimley-Horn will prepare utility clearance letters to send to the utility companies within the project 
limits. Letters will be submitted at 90% stage. 
3. Kimley-Horn will work with the utility companies to identify any potential utility conflicts and will 
communicate these conflicts with Navajo County. 
4. Pot-hole data is not included. Pothole data will be included as a construction activity in technical 
special provision to ensure clearance from utilities. 
 
Task 3 Buck Springs Road Equestrian RRFB Crossing Design & PS&E Production 
1. Kimley-Horn will prepare RRFB construction drawings for the crossing as follows: 
a. The design will include necessary RRFB poles, signing, marking, and solar equipment. 
b. Design will be done in accordance with Navajo County and ADOT Traffic Signal 
Standards and Specifications, ADA, PROWAG, and MUTCD requirements.

2. Kimley-Horn anticipates shoulder/ path adjustments to accommodate the placement of RRFB 
poles and landing areas for access to equestrian and pedestrian devices. 
3. Kimley-Horn's signing and pavement marking design will be done in accordance with ADOT 
Signing and Marking Standard Details and Specifications and expected to consist of high visibility 
crosswalk, pedestrian/equestrian crossing signing and advanced warning signs. All other existing 
striping/signing is expected to remain. 
4. Kimley-Horn anticipates improvements to existing drainage pipe culverts being necessary. 
Replacement and  grading to drain will be expected by the Contractor and covered in the 
Technical Specifications. No drainage studies or design are included in this scope of work. 
5. Kimley-Horn will prepare construction plans for submittal to County staff at 90% and 100% (Final) 
design stages. Construction plans will be developed using AutoCAD. RRFB plans will be 
prepared at 1" = 20' scale and will include the following sheets: 
a. Cover Sheet (1 Sheet) 
b. General Notes and Legend (1 Sheet) 
c. RRFB and Signing/Marking Layout (1 Sheet) (20 Scale) 
d. Pole Schedule and Details (1 Sheet) 
6. Construction delivery will be design-bid-build. Technical specification will be required and 
provided at 90% and Final stages. 
7. Kimley-Horn will prepare engineers Estimates of Probable Cost (EOPC) at 30%, 90% and 100% 
stages. 30% will be utilized to assist County Staff with programming and funding for construction. 
8. All deliverables will be provided electronically in PDF format. 
 
Task 4 Project Management and Meetings 
1. Kimley-Horn will establish a project management system consisting of coordination and 
monitoring subconsultants, documentation, and preparation and control of project schedule and 
budget. 
2. Kimley-Horn will attend up to two (2) project meetings, including  a kickoff meeting and one 
progress/comment  resolution meetings to resolve comments following 90% plan submittals. All 
meetings will be held virtually utilizing Microsoft Teams and will include the Kimley-Horn Project 
Manager. Kimley-Horn will provide a meeting agenda, schedule, handouts as necessary, and will 
develop brief meeting minutes.

3. Describe the proposed project, objectives, and your plan to implement. 
The Show Low area is home to numerous communities and a vast expanse of forests and grasslands.  This area 
is prone to wildfires, which pose a significant threat to the lives and properties of the local residents.  In recent 
years, the frequency and intensity of wildfires have increase, which has made it essential to equip the local fire 
departments with the necessary equipment to fight these wildfires effectively.  One of the most critical needs

4. Describe how the proposed project satisfies one or more of the priority funding areas identified by the Gila River 
Indian Community. 
This proposed project satisfies the priority funding area associated with Public Safety.  As indicated above, 
TMFMD serves the communities of Lakeside and Show Low which have been identified by Federal Forest 
personnel, AZ State Forestry personnel and both town governments in Pinetop Lakeside and Show Low as 
having a significant risk of a catastrophic wildland urban interface fire similar to those seen in California in 
recent years.  The risk is of such potential that currently there are multiple state and federal grants in place 
within the boundaries of the District that are focusing on wildland fuel mitigation in some of the highest risk 
areas.  A key component in combating this risk is the prevention of and early attack of any wildland fire started 
in the District.  A Type 6 wildland fire engine is a versatile and highly maneuverable firefighting vehicle that is 
designed to operate in rugged terrain and narrow roads. It is smaller than larger fire engines and can access 
areas that are inaccessible to larger engines. The Show Low area is characterized by narrow roads, steep slopes, 
and dense vegetation, which makes it challenging for larger firefighting vehicles to maneuver. A Type 6 engine 
is an essential piece of equipment that can provide fast and effective response to wildfires in the area and 
allows fire personnel to be able to catch fires while they are smaller before they become established and to 
large to stop.  In summary, without this equipment, and the ability it affords to aggressively and quickly attack 
fires, the public and the community face a significant risk of a wildland fire becoming established and 
unstoppable and running into the communities of the White Mountains.  TMFMD has been severely limited in 
it’s ability to make capital purchases and currently, the type 6’s in service in the District are aged and past the 
NFPA recommended useful life of 20 years.

The need to be addressed stems from the need for funding for the purchase of critical equipment.  Fire districts 
in Arizona are limited in several ways with respect to their funding sources. We are the only taxing entity in 
Arizona that has a hard rate cap. Our budgeted levy growth is limited to 8% annually and the Limited Property

 . Proclamation 
  Week of the Young Child  
April 1 – April 8, 2023 
 
WHEREAS, the First Things First White Mountain Apache, Navajo/Apache, and Navajo Nation 
Regions and other local organizations, in conjunction with the National Association for Education 
of Young Children, are celebrating the Week of the Young Child™, April  1 – April 8, 2023; and 
WHEREAS, these organizations are working to improve early learning opportunities, including 
early literacy programs, that can provide a foundation of learning for children in Navajo County; 
and 
WHEREAS, the first years of a child’s life are the period of the most rapid brain development 
and lay the foundation for all future learning; and  
WHEREAS, high–quality early childhood programs provide important benefits to children, 
families, and our state and national economies; and  
WHEREAS, high–quality early childhood education depends on high–quality early childhood 
educators who ensure that children, supported by families, have the early experiences they need 
for a strong foundation; and  
WHEREAS, teachers and others who make a difference in the lives of young children in Navajo 
County deserve thanks and recognition.  
NOW, THEREFORE, we, the Board of Supervisors of Navajo County, do hereby proclaim the 
week of April 1 – April 8, 2023, as the Week of the Young Child in Navajo County, Arizona, and 
encourage all citizens to work to make a good investment in early childhood in Navajo County.

Background:
The Teesto Chapter has been a great example of self-reliance these past few years in road
maintenance duties. Throughout the monsoon and winter seasons they have been working
alongside BIA, NDOT and Navajo County to maintain and clear public routes within their
community. Due to the lack of manpower for heavy equipment operators, it may take weeks in
response time to get roads opened or maintained but in the case of Teesto Chapter they have been
able to respond in days with their own heavy equipment in answering the call to the public.
Unfortunately simple maintenance repairs and parts for their heavy equipment hinders the
partnership they have in maintaining routes. So the Chapter has come to the County looking for
assistance in purchasing parts and repairs to continue keeping their equipment healthy and up to
date. The Chapter does have an IGA set up with BIA to maintain public routes within their region
which will allow us to utilize County funds in assisting.

BACKGROUND 
 
Wireless communication facilities in Navajo County currently require the Special Use Permit 
process in order to be  considered a permitted use.  Despite this, standards for development and 
permitting of these facilities are limited within the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance.  In order to 
create more clear and consistent standards for applicants for wireless communication facilities, Staff 
is proposing a Zoning Ordinance Text Amendment that outlines all guidelines and requirements for 
this type of use. 
 
STAFF ANALYSIS 
 
Navajo County does not currently have clear, distinct standards regarding the permitting and siting 
of wireless communication facilities within its unincorporated boundaries.  The only reference to 
this usage type is within Section 2001  – Special Uses, Paragraph 34, which states, “Television, 
wireless towers, and radio transmitter towers and stations, subject to approval by Federal Regulatory 
Agencies.”  The inclusion of this information is not clear in providing minimum standards for 
wireless communication facilities to become established within the County.   All that is conveyed 
with this information is that a Special Use Permit is required for wireless communication facilities. 
While Navajo County has permitted wireless communication facilities using similar conditions 
applied to all Special Use Permits, the addition of a new section regarding this usage type to the

Section 2521 – Wireless Communication Facilities 
Purpose 
The purpose of this Section is to establish rules and regulations for the siting of wireless communications 
facilities.  The goals of this Section are to provide for the development of wireless communication services 
County-wide by:  
1. Considering the public health, safety, and welfare. 
 
2. Considering impact on adjacent properties. 
 
3. Encouraging configuration which minimizes additional visual impact through proper siting, design, 
landscape, and camouflage techniques. 
 
4. Considering historical and environmentally sensitive areas. 
 
5. Encouraging the collocation of facilities. 
 
Definitions 
As used herein, capitalized terms shall have the following meanings: 
1. Alternative Tower Structure: Vertical components not generally designed for use as antenna support 
structures including, but not limited to, structures such as church steeples, ballpark light poles, and 
water towers. 
 
2. Antenna: Any exterior device for transmitting and receiving wireless communication mounted on a 
tower, alternative tower structure, building, or structure and used for transmitting and receiving

and surrounding environment.  Examples of concealed antennas include, but are not limited to, 
manmade trees, clock towers, flagpoles that do not exceed ten feet (10 ’) above the maximum 
building height, light structures, steeples, water tanks, and architectural façade and parapet features. 
 
5. Certification: A written statement of the fact to be certified, made under oath by the applicant or 
licensed professional working for the applicant and notarized. 
 
6. Collocation: Use by two (2) or more wireless communication providers located on the same tower 
or alternative tower structure.

Collocation 
 
The purpose of this Section is to encourage collocation. 
 
1. An applicant who certifies in writing that the tower constructed will be suitable for 
collocating multiple providers of v arying wireless technologies and, as a condition of 
zoning, executes a written agreement (collocation agreement) with Navajo County, 
consenting to application of the terms of this provision shall, unless waived by the 
applicant have  their request considere d more preferred as outlined in Performance 
Criteria. 
 
2. In addition to equipment proposed for the applicant’s use, proposed antenna facilities, 
including concealed antennas, shall be designed to accommodate collocation for at least 
one (1) additional wirele ss communications provider for every thirty foot (30’) segment 
of facility height, or portion thereof, over thirty feet (30’). The Board of Supervisors may 
reduce the required shared capacity, if a facility necessary to provide for such collocation 
adversely alters the area’s visual character. 
 
3. Collocation Agreement:  
 
The collocation agreement shall provide for at least the following: 
 
a. The applicant shall accept for collocation any FCC licensed wireless 
communication provider (additional user) using any co mpatible technology on 
commercially reasonable terms considering all of the factors a reasonable tower 
leasing company would deem relevant in entering into such an agreement. 
 
b. Any additional user seeking collocation s hall submit specifications for its 
equipment and use (request) to the applicant and applicant shall, within thirty (30) 
days thereafter, respond to such party in writing (response) furnishing all 
technical requirements that must be resolved before collocation. 
 
c. The applicant and the additional user shall, thereafter, attempt to resolve any 
technical or business terms. If, after thirty (30) days from the response, the 
additional user believes the applicant has not negotiated in good faith, additional 
user may submit in writing, a request for arbitration to applicant and the American 
Arbitration Association which shall designate a person knowledgeable in 
collocation of wireless communication carriers to act as arbitrator and decide all 
issues between the parties. Such arbitration shall be held within thirty (30) days of 
the request for arbitration. Upon the written agreement of both parties, a different

<!-- page 117 -->
procedure for binding dispute resolution may be used. The result of the arbitration 
or other resolution metho d agreed to by the parties shall be bin ding and non -
appealable. 
 
d. If the arbitrator certifies in writing to Navajo County that the applicant has failed 
to comply with the decision of the arbitrator within fifteen (15) days of its 
issuance by the arbitrator, the Special Use Permit for the wireles s communication 
facility in question shall be terminated and the wireless communications facility 
shall be removed within thirty (30) days of the date of the arbitrator's certification, 
failing which, Navajo County sh all have all of the remedies available to it for 
elimination of a use in violation of the zoning code. 
 
e. The additional party, upon submitting the request shall become a third -party 
beneficiary to the collocation agreement. 
 
f. Navajo County shall not be a par ty to any contract between the applican t and the 
additional party and shall not be a required party and shall not be made a party to 
any dispute or arbitration and applicant shall indemnify, defend, and hold Navajo 
County harmless from any cost, including reasonable attorney fees, associated 
with such matters. 
 
g. A lease or other agreement containing the business terms proposed by the 
applicant for collocation shall be attached as an exhibit to the collocation 
agreement. 
 
Removal 
 
1. Within thirty (30) days of Board of Supervisors approval, financial  assurances shall be 
posted by the applicant for the occurrence or possible need for removal of the tower and 
returning the natural state of the site, prior to submittal and approval of building 
permits/zoning clearances. 
 
2. Towers and antennae shall be remo ved, at the owner’s expense, within one hundred 
eighty (180) days if not used for a permanent use within that time unless this period is 
extended pursuant to this Section. If the tower or antennae is not timely remov ed Navajo 
County may give notice that it will contract for removal within thirty (30) days following 
written notice to the owner. Thereafter, Navajo County may cause removal at the cost of 
the owner. 
 
3. An owner wishing to extend the time for removal or reac tivation shall submit an 
application sta ting the reason for such extension. The Planning and Development 
Director or their designate may extend the time for removal or reactivation up to sixty 
(60) additional days upon a showing of good cause.

<!-- page 118 -->
4. Upon removal of the wireless telecommunications facility, the site shall be returned to its 
natural state and topography and vegetated consistent with the natural surroundings. 
 
Modification 
 
No existing wireless telecommunications facility may be changed or modified  except if it meets 
all of the following criteria: 
 
1. The change or modification is required by a change in user or technology or is required 
for the collocation of additional carriers on the existing structure. 
 
2. The change does not increase the height of th e tallest component above the height 
approved in the Special Use Permit, or in the case of a pre -existing facility, its then 
current height. 
 
3. At the conclusion of the change or modification, the facility complies with all 
requirements of the County Planning and Development Department. 
 
4. An explanation is submitted to the Planning and Development Director stating why the 
modification is necessary, and an updated Provider’s Communication Plan, including any 
proposed changes in the service areas, antennae, towers, and policy direction is provided. 
 
5. The modification submitted shall be approved by an Arizona Licensed professional 
engineer to certify that it meets all safety standards for structural stability and is in 
conformance with the current adopted code by Navajo County for structural towers.
