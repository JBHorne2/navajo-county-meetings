PUBLIC WORKS :

a.

Consideration of approving the Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA) between the Teesto Chapter and Navajo County for assistance on heavy equipment material and parts :
Madhav Mundle stated that the BIA has a long standing IGA that allows the District Chapters to maintain roads within their region, but that routine repairs that require spare parts issues to the operations and this IGA will allow the County to provide assistance purchase parts or miscellaneous materials so that they can keep their equipment is operation.  He stated that staff recommends approval.
Chairman Peshlakai thanked Mr. Mundle for his presentation and staff for the work done on this item.  He stated that this IGA is the first of its kind.  He talked about the number of Chapter communities on the Navajo Nation and addressed how some have the authority to work independently to enter into agreements with other agencies to enhance or supplement services.  He further talked about how the worked to help the communities in the past to maintain roads.  He stated that this IGA will be very beneficial to the communities and that he appreciates the work done on this.

Supervisor Benally made a motion  to approve the Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA) between the Teesto Chapter and Navajo County for assistance on heavy equipment material and parts, motion seconded by  Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

PLANNING & ZONING :

1.

PUBLIC HEARING:  Consideration of adoption of  Resolution Number 10 - 2023 , approving/denying text amendment to Article 25 of the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance for the addition of Section 2521, regarding zoning requirements for wireless communication facilities :
Cody Cooper clarified that they are proposing a text amendment to the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance for wireless communication facilities.  He stated that they are limited for zoning ordinances on the provisions they have for wireless communications, that currently they just have a special use category for wireless towers with no provisions or guidelines that outline what would be required, that staff goes out and reviews towers on a case by case basis but that this would provide a uniform set for standards.  A presentation was shown.  He talked about what they are proposing.

9:55 a.m. Supervisor Benally left the meeting.

Mr. Cooper addressed the differences between residential and non-residential, the general provisions for setbacks and heights and the provision for towers above 199 feet in height.  He talked about the performance criteria, application review, collocation, removal, and modification.  He addressed phone calls and the comments that they have received regarding the item.  He stated that the Planning and Zoning Commission unanimously recommended approval of the text amendment at their March 17, 2023 meeting.  He further stated that staff recommends approval.
Chairman Peshlakai opened the public hearing.
Reg Destree, 22831 N. 21st Street, Phoenix, addressed the Board regarding the item, indicating that it is helpful to have standards.  He indicated that he has provided feedback through a letter but that he wanted to address two of the main points.  He talked about the height limit in the residential zones, indicating that he believes going from 80 foot to 99 foot would be appropriate since it is in line with the order of preferences schedule and promotes collocation.  He talked about the issues of being able to place a site with the requested medication of a 2 to 1 setback in residential zones.  He indicated that it would be appropriate to allow for an engineer's letter if they keep the set back to allow an exception indicating that the structure will remain on the property and that it is engineered to have a fall zone of less than two to one.
There being no additional comments, Chairman Peshlakai closed the public hearing.
Mr. Cooper answered questions of the Board regarding the Engineer's letter, the tower height in residential areas, the towers being camouflaged in residential areas and the setback in residential areas.
Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to adopt  Resolution Number 10 - 2023 , approving text amendment to Article 25 of the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance for the addition of Section 2521, regarding zoning requirements for wireless communication facilities, with the amendment that a registered engineers letter would need to be brought before the Board of Supervisors, on a case to case basis, for consideration of a reduction of the setback of two to one foot on residential classifications, certifying that the setbacks can be reduced safely; motion seconded by  Vice-Chairman Whiting .  Motion passed 4-0 with Supervisor Benally being absent for the vote.
