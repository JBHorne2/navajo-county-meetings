CALL TO THE PUBLIC :

Mr. Spike addressed the Board regarding Bikers Against Child Abuse indicating that their mission is empowering abused kids, that there are multiple chapters across the State of Arizona, that the White Mountain Chapter covers from Payson to New Mexico and Payson to Utah, that they cover all states with the exception of Hawaii and that they cover twenty-two other countries.  He stated that they spend a lot of time in Navajo County working with the County Attorney's Office and Victim Services supporting children going to court.  He talked about the history of their organization and how they help children who are living in fear.  He indicated that members of the organization go through an FBI background check, that they recently signed an agreement with DCS to work with kids that meet their criteria across the state and that they have completed the DCS registry check.  He advised that services provided are empowerment services to children living in fear, assistance in paying for counseling, attending court with the children and being available for whatever they need at anytime.  He indicated that they have no interest in the perpetrators, that they only focus on the child and helping them become stronger.
Chairman Peshlakai and Supervisor Seymore thanked Mr. Spike for the presentation.
Brad Carlyon thanked the organization for all they do in Navajo County and indicated that they are a great partner with the Family Advocacy Center and Victim Services.  He indicated that the help they provide to the children makes a huge difference on their journey to recovery.

Consideration of a voluntary pledge of general fund equivalent of 1% of Navajo County's total Local Assistance and Tribal Consistency Fund (LATCF) allocation, payable over the next two years, to the National Center for Public Lands Counties (The Center) :
Rochelle Lacapa addressed the Board regarding the item, stating that as a state with substantial federal public land holdings and a significant stake in improving information and awareness associated with public lands in our communities, Arizona counties have a unique opportunity to invest in and benefit from The Center.  She indicated that NACo and WIR set a fundraising goal of $15 million over the next two years to capitalize on the project and that the activities of The Center will be funded by interest earned on the fund, leveraging a one-time investment to empower the public lands conversation for generations to come and that once capitalized, NACo would be able to hire the necessary talent to conduct important public lands-related research and create associated content but that the resources will not be used for partisan political purposes. She advised that the funds will be managed under a 501(c)(3) within the NACo Research Foundation to support WIR's collaboration with The Center. She stated that NACo and WIR are requesting state associations report the state's contribution by January31, 2023.  She further stated that staff recommends approval in the project.
Vice-Chairman Whiting thanked Ms. Lacapa for the presentation.  He indicated that due to the Eastern states not having a lot of public land, they don't understand the issues faced with public land.  He stated that the hope is that by creating the database and the information they will have the ability to share the information so they can see the issues that are being faced.  He indicated that this would be worthwhile and help us, in a county that has a lot of federal land.

Vice-Chairman Whiting made a motion  to voluntary pledge of general fund equivalent of 1% of Navajo County's total Local Assistance and Tribal Consistency Fund (LATCF) allocation, payable over the next two years, to the National Center for Public Lands Counties (The Center), motion seconded by  Supervisor Seymore .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
Supervisor Seymore stated that it is his understanding that all fifteen counties in the state has agreed to be a part of this.

PUBLIC HEARING:  Consideration of selecting up to three projects for FY2023 CDBG funds ($500,000 in total) plus two additional contingent projects and adoption of Resolution Number 01-2023 , authorizing the submission of an application for FY23 State Community Development Block Grant Funds, Certifying that said application meets the community's previously identified housing and community development needs and the requirements of the State CDBG Program, and authorizing all actions necessary to implement and complete the activities outlined in said application :
Ken Maruyama addressed the Board regarding the item, stating that the CDBG program provides funding for projects benefiting low to moderate income families and special populations such as seniors, people with disabilities and the homeless and that it is expected that the county will receive approximately $500,000 for the FY2023 CDBG funds.  He indicated that there are eleven potential projects that have been identified and that the county may choose up to 3 projects.  He advised that the CDBG Management Team met and evaluated the potential projects and prepared recommendations for the Boards consideration.  He indicated that upon the Boards approval staff will continue to work with the applicants on the full application.  He requested that the Board select up to three projects for funding plus two contingency projects, approve Resolution 01-2023 and authorize the submission of the full applications.
Chairman Peshlakai opened the meeting for public comments.  There being no public comments, the public meeting was closed.
Supervisor Seymore thanked staff for the time that was spent on this and the applicants for the submitted projects.  He requested that City of Show Low and the Rim Country Senior Center be considered for the project grants.  He talked about the Rim County Senior Center project that has been submitted for consideration and the needs for that facility/community.  He further talked about the top three projects that were ranked and how two of those projects could possibly be paid for with other funds.
Vice-Chairman Whiting inquired if the incorporated communities have an opportunity to receive funds in the cycle.
Mr. Maruyama indicated that there are local municipalities that do get funds when it is their turn.
Vice-Chairman Whiting talked about how the funds should be used to help with projects that the County is responsible and would not have to come out of general funds.
Supervisor Seymore recommended that the fourth project be the Rim Country Senior Center and the fifth project be the City of Show Low.
Chairman Peshlakai thanked Mr. Maruyama for the presentation and the team for the work done on the recommendations.  He indicated that he would recommend that the Holbrook Senior Center be considered for the fifth project.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to approve the top three proposed projects, with contingency projects being forth, the Rim Country Senior Center and fifth, the City of Holbrook and to adopt Resolution Number 01-2023, authorizing the submission of an application for FY23 State Community Development Block Grant Funds, Certifying that said application meets the community's previously identified housing and community development needs and the requirements of the State CDBG Program, and authorizing all actions necessary to implement and complete the activities outlined in said application; motion seconded by  Vice-Chairman Whiting .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

PUBLIC HEARING: Consideration and possible adoption of Resolution Number 02-2023 , Extinguishing A Public Roadway Easement over the South 25' of the Southwest ¼ of the South East ¼ of Section 21, Township 12 North, Range 17 East of the Gila And Salt River Base and Meridian Recorded On Fee# 1962-04550 :
Madhav Mundle addressed the item, stating that Public Works received an extinguishment application from Mr. Simmons, signed by fifty-one individuals representing thirty-three difference parcels and that seventeen of those are located within the three hundred feet of the proposed distinguishment.  He indicated that staff reviewed the proposal, that they found no negative impact and that notices were sent out of the proposed extinguishment to parcels within three hundred feet for a total of forty-six parcels.  He stated that information has been provided to the Board regarding three who are opposed as well as those who did not respond.  He stated that staff recommends approval.

Chairman Peshlakai  opened the meeting for public comment.
Jon Burgher addressed the Board regarding the easement and requested that it be extinguished so that it doesn't split his property.
Aimee Webster stated that she supports the extinguishment of the roadway, that there is not a need for it.
Roland Webster stated that he supports the extinguishment.
Michael Simmons addressed the Board, stating that he submitted the application, that the easement runs along the backside of the HOA and that it couldn't be used as a public road and be maintained properly due to the size.  He stated that it also switches along the roadway from public to private property.
There being no additional comments, Chairman Peshlakai closed the public hearing.

Supervisor Seymore stated that he doesn't believe that there is anything proposed for a roadway to be developed.  He talked about concerns due to the easement being there since 1962 and that it hasn't changed.  He talked about how the easement gives some of the property owners access to the back of their property.  He stated that if the easement is granted, a property owner could put up a fence that could block the road and not allow access to the other properties.  He inquired if the property would have to be re-deeded to the property owners or what would happen with the easement.
Bryan Cook stated that the underlying properties belong to the property owners, that it would not have to be re-deeded and that it would just take away the public access.  He addressed how the property would be accessed without the easement based on the recorded surveys.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to adopt  Resolution Number 02-2023 , noting that there were three letters of opposition, Extinguishing A Public Roadway Easement over the South 25' of the Southwest ¼ of the South East ¼ of Section 21, Township 12 North, Range 17 East of the Gila And Salt River Base and Meridian Recorded On Fee# 1962-04550, motion seconded by  Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
