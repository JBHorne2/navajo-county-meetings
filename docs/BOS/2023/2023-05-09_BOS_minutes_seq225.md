COUNTY ATTORNEY :

a.

Consideration of a Proclamation declaring May 2023 as "Navajo County Mental Health Awareness Month" :
Ciera Hensley, Mental Health Program Manager for Public Health, stated one in five adults and teens struggle with mental illness.  She talked about how many people know someone suffering yet the stigma keeps people from having the important conversations around it.  She stated that less that forty percent of people with mental illness receive treatment and approximately half of the youth are receiving treatment.  She indicated that a majority of suicides each year involve someone with mental health or substance abuse disorders and that the suicide rate in Navajo County is double that of Arizona.  She stated that we all play a part in ending the tragedies by ending the stigma and reminding people that there is hope for them.  She talked about the free education that is offered by the Health Department, indicating that many of the programs are offered virtually and to the tribal communities.  She stated that they are working to get the programs into the schools for youth but that they face challenges due to the stigma.  She stated that by proclaiming May Mental Health Awareness Month it shows our hurting communities that we support them and that they are not alone.
Brad Carlyon stated Public Health and the County Attorneys are making this presentation together as a sign of partnership which allows them to be more effective than they could be individually.  He stated that mental health issues are widespread but still a stigma around it which attaches them to the criminal justice system.  He indicated that there is a high percentage of adults and juveniles in the system due to mental health issues.  He stated that they are starting to address mental health better, indicating that it was ignored for many years.  He stated they with doing this proclamation they are hoping to get the word out about the different programs that are available through Navajo County Health Department, such as calling 988 for someone having a crisis will provide behavioral health responding rather than 911 which is law enforcement.  He indicated that the impacts are not only the in criminal justice system but also in our schools and businesses.  He talked about how adopting the proclamation will help get the word out through the media and social media to help promote the concept to destigmatize mental health issues, which can help people to help themselves and regain control of their life.
Supervisor Seymore talked about the joint effort of the County Attorney's Office and Health Department.  He stated that this is something that can't be ignored and talked about the stresses and circumstances that can lead to mental health issues.
Supervisor Benally thanked them for the presentation.  She talked about the 988 numbers indicating that she has encouraged those in her community to use it when needed.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to approve a Proclamation declaring May 2023 as "Navajo County Mental Health Awareness Month", motion seconded by  Supervisor Benally .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Consideration of a Proclamation designating May 9, 2023, as "National Fentanyl Awareness Day" in Navajo County :
Linda Teague, Program Manager for Navajo County Community Connection for Overdose Prevention, indicated that she is in long term recovery, that she hasn't had a problematic relationship with drugs or alcohol since 2014.  A presentation was shown.  She talked about the stigma and indicated that people need to understand that treatment works, and that recovery is possible.  She stated that the reason they call it community connection is because all of the staff have experienced substance abuse disorder, that they understand that addiction is about isolation and that the opposite of that is connection.  She stated that they want to recognize today as Fentanyl Awareness Day to help the communities understand the dangers of the drug and how it affects our communities.  She talked about the fentanyl crisis, stating that it is a synthetic opioid and that that it is involved in fifty percent of the causes of death for people under fifty.  She further talked about the training they are providing in the schools and with the police departments on tribal land.  She advised that all of the Navajo County Health Departments have Narcan that they will give to you with no questions asked or you can call the hotline. She stated that the way to bring people into recovery is connecting with them.   She further stated that they are asking that May 92023 be proclaimed National Fentanyl Awareness Day.
Supervisor Benally thanked her for the presentation and for providing training and information on tribal lands.
Supervisor Seymore congratulated Ms. Teague on her recovery.  He stated that he appreciates those work for tougher laws against those who manufacture and distribute fentanyl.  He thanked them for they work they do to get the word out, stating what they do is important.

Supervisor Benally made a motion  to approve a Proclamation designating May 9, 2023, as "National Fentanyl Awareness Day" in Navajo County, motion seconded by  Supervisor Seymore .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

NAVAJO COUNTY FLOOD CONTROL DISTRICT: Board of Directors Session :

REGULAR AGENDA :

a.
Consideration of an Intergovernmental Agreement with the Town of Pinetop-Lakeside for their request for financial assistance for the  Top of the Woods subdivision drainage  project in the amount of $140,000 :
Bill Bess stated that the Town of Pinetop-Lakeside is requesting financial assistance for their Top of the Woods subdivision storm drainage improvement project, estimated at 1.9 million dollars worth of work.  He indicated that the IGA will allow a partnership, that the county and town share a common interest to improve the drainage system and to help reduce chance of future flooding.  He stated that the County would provide financial assistance to purchase materials and supplies, not to exceed $140,000, for the project and that the Town will be solely responsible for the design, procurement, permitting and construction.
Mr. Patterson, Pinetop-Lakeside Public Works Director, stated that they have been working on this project since the flooding in 2019, indicating that it affects homeowners not only in the town but also in the county.
Supervisor Whitesinger thanked Public Works and all those who have worked on this item.  She stated that she appreciates the partnership with the Town of Pinetop-Lakeside.

Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion  to approve an Intergovernmental Agreement with the Town of Pinetop-Lakeside for their request for financial assistance for the Top of the Woods subdivision drainage project in the amount of $140,000, motion seconded by  Supervisor Benally .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
