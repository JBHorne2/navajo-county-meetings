Memorandum of Understanding  
Between Program Partners 
 
 
The following Memorandum of Understanding outlines the working relationship between Alice's Place, Inc., 
and the following partners: 
  
City of Winslow Police Department (WPD) – Winslow AZ   
Navajo County Attorney's Office/Victim Services - Holbrook AZ 
Winslow Municipal Court – Winslow AZ  
 White Mountain Legal Aid – Pinetop AZ 
Little Colorado Medical Center (LCMC) - Winslow AZ 
 Winslow Indian Health Care Center (WIHCC) – Winslow AZ 
Community Counseling Center (CCC) - Winslow AZ 
Parenting Arizona - Winslow AZ 
 
All partners understand that the goal of this program is to continuously develop and implement a coordinated, 
multidisciplinary partnership between victim advocates, law enforcement, legal, medical, behavioral, and social 
service providers for the purpose of enhancing victim services while supporting victims throughout these 
processes with Trauma Informed Care and  all due confidentiality.  All actions of and between the partners are 
aimed at reducing crimes of domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking, and dating violence.  These 
collaborations are also aimed to improve the consistency of response to victims of violence in Arizona by holding 
the offenders accountable through a coordinated response on the part of all partners involved. All partners will 
assist victims after commitment of crimes of domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking, and dating violence by 
uniting the efforts of law enforcement, prosecution, courts, medical, behavioral, social, and victim services.   
Subsequently, all partner’s collaborations are aimed at prevention through education with individuals, community 
organizations and through our schools. 
 
Individual MOUs follow herewith for each of our collaborative partners. 
 
 
Alice’s Place *core services will be signified with an asterisk in the text of each MOU and are listed as follows: 
 
1.  24 Hour Crisis Intervention

4.  Case Management 
5.  Victim and Lay Legal Advocacy 
6.  Transportation 
7.  Life Skills Development:  Financial Management, Workplace Skills, Career    
       Development, GED Preparedness and Basic Education Tutoring 
8.  Support Group with Domestic Violence Education 
9.  Prevention and Awareness Education

Memorandum of Understanding 
Navajo County Attorney's Office/Victim Services 
 
 
Navajo County Attorney's Office/Victim Services (NCAO/VS) agrees to: 
1. Receive referrals from AP for incidents of domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking, and dating 
violence for assistance with Victim Compensation Claims and any other relevant victim services. 
2. Refer victims of domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking, and dating violence to AP for *core 
services. 
3. Share and exchange training opportunities with AP staff in areas of domestic violence, sexual assault, 
stalking, and dating violence. 
 
Alice's Place, Inc. (AP) agrees to: 
1. Be available 24 hours every day providing confidential, professional, knowledgeable, and timely support 
to victims of domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking, and dating violence with our *core services to 
assist the justice system in increasing safety throughout Navajo County. 
2. Receive referrals from NCAO/VS for victims of domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking, and dating 
violence.  
3. Make referrals to NCAO/VS as needed for victims of domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking, and 
dating violence.  
4. Provide training opportunities relevant to domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking, and dating 
violence.  Make AP staff available to receive training from NCAO/VS. 
5.     Provide professional, confidential, timely, and knowledgeable lay legal support with legal matters 
relative to the victims of domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking, and dating violence maintaining 
confidentiality necessary for the safety of the victim.  
6. Provide transportation and accompaniment to court hearings and other relevant appointments.

Intergovernmental Agreement, Contract Number CTR063750, Arizona's Prescription Drug
Overdose Prevention Program to allow us to continue to offer valuable preventative services to
Navajo County residents in effort of reducing drug overdose deaths
Background:
Navajo County Public Health Services District (NCPHSD) has received this grant award since
2016, allowing us to implement strategies to reduce overdose deaths.  We have been progressive
in our approach, starting with educating the providers on safer prescribing practices and the
importance of utilizing the prescription monitoring program.  NCPHSD is now the HUB for
Narcan educating and dispensing to those most likely to experience or witness an overdose.  We
are working with entities to review their policies and protocols regarding connecting individuals
with resources.  Our staff are trained Peer Support specialists with life experience who can help
those in need to navigate the system and overcome barriers to care.  This funding will allow us to
continue to expand these services to help reduce overdose deaths.

2. BACKGROUND 
 
2.1. ADHS OIVP administers funds provided by the CDC for operation of the Overdose Data to Action (OD2A) 
Cooperative Agreement , and AHCCCS’ State Opioid Response (SOR) funding from the Substance Abuse 
and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA); 
 
2.2. The overarching goal of the State Opioid Response (SOR) grant is to increase access to OUD treatment, 
coordinated and integrated care, recovery support services and prevention activities to reduce the 
prevalence of OUDs, stimulant use disorders, and opioid- related overdose deaths. The project approach 
includes developing and supporting state, regional, and local level col laborations and service

<!-- page 47 -->

enhancements to develop and implement best practices to comprehensively address the full continuum of 
care related to stimulant and opioid misuse, abuse and dependency;  
 
2.3. Abuse and addiction to opioids is a serious and challenging national public health problem. Deaths from 
drug overdose have risen steadily over the past two (2) decades and have become the leading cause of 
injury death in the United States. The latest numbers from the CDC show a reported 92,452 overdose 
deaths for the year 2020, up thirty percent (30%) from the 71,130 deaths in 2019. Of those 2020 deaths, 
opioids were involved in 69,031, which accounts for seventy -five percent (75%) of all drug overdose 
deaths;  
 
2.4. Previously, this opioid epidemic had been driven by prescription drug use. According to data from Arizona’s 
CSPMP, there were 4.1 million Class II-IV prescriptions written and 240,511,812 pills dispensed in Arizona 
in 2019. This equates to thirty -four (34) Schedule II -IV controlled substance pills for every person, adults 
and children, living in Arizona. According to experts, recent prescribing practices in Arizona rank our state 
as twenty-eighth (28
th) for opioid prescribing with forty-four point one ( 44.1) prescriptions per 100 people; 
but this is no longer the root cause of overdose deaths; 
 
2.5. Now, the main driver of the opioid crisis is fentanyl. In 2019, synthetic opioids were involved in more than 
36,000 deaths in the U. S., which is about seventy -three percent (73%) of all opioid- involved deaths that 
year. Most of these fentanyl deaths were due to illicitly-made fentanyl, which is found in counterfeit pills and 
being mixed into other drugs such as heroin. Other street drugs (such as methamphetamines) may be laced 
with fentanyl without the user’s knowledge, adding to risk of overdose. In Arizona, presence of fentanyl in 
overdoses significantly increased from nine percent (9%) in 2017 to fifty percent (50%) in 2021; 
 
2.6. In addition to the human cost, the financial burden of opioid misuse is enormous. In 2019, there were 56,623 
hospital visits related to opioids in Arizona, at an average cost of $11,942 per visit. This equals about $676 
million dollars in health care costs due to opioids; and
 
 
2.7. Prescription and illicit opioids, like fentanyl, are addictive and respo nsible for an increasing number of 
deaths in Arizona. This rise reflects a growing problem across the nation and overdose deaths are the 
leading cause of preventable injury death. 
 
3. OBJECTIVE 
 
With resources awarded through the CDC and SAMHSA, Arizona will be well equipped to continue expanding 
prevention services and strategies to halt, reverse, and diminish the opioid crisis in our state. Key strategies and 
initiatives that will guide ADHS’ work include:
 
 
3.1. Enhancing the capacity of county health departments  to address the opioid epidemic through
