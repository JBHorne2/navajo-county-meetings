CALL TO THE PUBLIC :
Renee Rowles, 400 Freedom Lane, Taylor, provided documents and talked about the benefits to the county and voters when precincts are drawn in accordance with ARS16-411, indicating that it ensures that voters have sufficient and convenient voting locations.  She talked about the upcoming school elections, stating that there was an issue with having sufficient voting sites for voters.  She further talked about the distance that voters will have to go to vote at the voting locations, using the Blue Ridge School District election as an example.  She addressed the increase in voters over the past four to six years and stated that she has not heard any discussion about how the upcoming elections might account for this by adding additional polling locations in the areas of growth.  She talked about the meeting that was held regarding drawing precinct maps and urged that the follow-up meeting be scheduled as soon as possible.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration of Renewal of franchise agreement with the Arizona Water Company :
Jason Moore stated that franchise agreements are agreements with public utility companies that allow them to construct, maintain and repair their systems that are within right-of-way off the sides of county roads.  He indicated that the term of the franchise would be for a term of twenty-five years.  He stated that staff recommends approval.  He indicated that Lee Hetrick, Local Division Manager for Arizona Water Company, was present to answer any questions.

Consideration and approval of a Proclamation declaring October 2023 as Domestic Violence Awareness month :
Roxanne Pergeson, Victim Services Director, read the proposed proclamation.  She stated that the numbers in the proclamation highlight the need for adoption of the resolution.  She indicated that Domestic Violence makes up over thirty-three percent of their overall caseload and that it has been consistent for at least the last five years.  She stated that there is a question as to whether this is a criminal issue or social issue.  She indicated that this is something that they have to continue to talk about and raise awareness about.  She talked about the event they will be hosting for Domestic Violence Awareness Event on October 1, 2023, at the Navajo County Fairgrounds and provided the Board with flyer regarding the event.

Vice-Chairman Whiting thanked Ms. Pergeson and the County Attorney's Office for bring awareness to this.  He inquired about details for the event.
Ms. Perguson talked about what the event will entail and what the funds will go to support.  She stated that they do the fundraiser because they no longer receive grant funding for it so use the funds to help with services.

ELECTIONS :

a.

Consideration of adoption of Resolution Number 39-2023  approving Poll Workers and designating Vote Centers for the Special Election :
Rayleen Richards talked about the reason that there are only being two voting locations in Blue Ridge and three locations in Snowflake, indicating that a majority of the voters get their ballots by mail.  She reviewed the statistics for voter turnout at polling places for elections in 2019 and 220.  She stated that the majority of voters in Navajo County vote by mail and that's why they determined where and how many polling locations are set.  She talked about the cost that would be incurred to add an additional polling location for equipment and poll workers.  She stated that all of the polling locations would be overstaffed if there was a hundred percent turnout.  She stated that staff requests approval of Resolution 39-2023.

Supervisor Seymore inquired if she had the numbers for the Holbrook area.
Ms. Richards indicated that she didn't have those numbers because they have locations on the reservation.  She indicated that they have made sure that there are enough polling locations on the reservation.

Supervisor Benally made a motion  to adopt  Resolution Number 39-2023  approving Poll Workers and designating Vote Centers for the Special Election, motion seconded by  Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE: Report from County Manager, County Attorney and Board members :
Brad Carlyon talked about Bread of Life Annual Banquet that was held, indicating that he and Sheriff Clouse did a pumpkin carving competition, indicating that his raised $750.00 and that Sheriff Clouse raised $800.00.
Supervisor Seymore invited everyone to Scarecrow Lane in Show Low, indicating that it's a fun event for families.  He stated that he had the opportunity to participate in the 50th Anniversary of the Heber-Overgaard Fire Department.  He talked about the history of the fire department, indicating that they have done a fantastic job moving it forward.  He reported on visiting a transitional housing facility to see how they run it, indicating that what they are doing is phenomenal and that we need to learn more about it.   He stated that he is looking forward to the CSA summit this week.
Supervisor Whitesinger reported on attending a partnership meeting in the White Mountain Tribal Community with Cincinnati Children's Hospital representatives to talk about cardiovascular health.  She indicated that one of the alignments was the Navajo County assessment that was done in determining health needs within our communities.  She stated that the underlining need is to have collective discussion around it.  She stated that yesterday she was able to attend the reading of the Indigenous Peoples Proclamation by Governor Hobbs, indicating that there are twenty-two federally recognized tribes within our state that have had significant contributions and impact on our communities.  She reported on being interviewed by Rebecca Montoya, a Counselor at Blue Ridge Middle School, who received a grant to be able to do counseling sessions regarding connecting with people in the communities who have overcome a challenge, stating that her show is Not all Wounds are Visible.  She indicated that this also aligns with the work Navajo County has done to assess the needs of the communities and being able to support our community members and feel supported when going through adverse times.  She talked about the numbers that are available, such as, 988 for individuals going through a mental health crisis or contemplating suicide or 911 for a medical overdose.  She indicated that we, as community members, need to participate in these conversations and help educate one another about the best place to reach out in a time of need and support those who are in crisis.
Supervisor Benally reported on attending the groundbreaking for the Winslow Library and thanked those who attended.  She stated that she attended the Veterans Memorial in Birdsprings.  She reported on attending an event at the Navajo Nation and indicated that she had a discussion with some of the kids who talked about the issues with fentanyl.  She addressed the meeting she attended in Kayenta regarding issues they have had with improvements to the playground, indicating that a major issue is with drug distributions at the playground/ recreational area.  She indicated that one of her projects now is to pass a resolution through the chapters within the Kayenta and Black Mesa region to have a rural bus route study done.  She indicated that a study has been done in Window Rock and Tuba City but that the Kayenta area needs to have one done.  She stated that NACOG has provided a resolution template and that they are looking into what can be done to get federal and state funding for it.  She talked about Indigenous Peoples Day, indicating that it was a joy to know that it was changed to Indigenous Day and that it's important to the Native people to be recognized.  She thanked Evelena Claw for coming out to the District 1 area to assess the roads and help with scheduling a meeting with NDOT.
Vice-Chairman Whiting stated that he is looking forward to the CSA Summit and being able to work on legislative priorities with fellow Board Members and other County Supervisors from around the state.   He talked about the coming holiday season, indicating that there are a lot of charities and organizations that do thoughtful and good things and that hopefully we have a chance to support some of those and ways to help out.  He talked about the Giving Machine, which is a vending machine, that will be located in Snowflake, where you can purchase items that will go to the charities, indicating that some of the organizations are local, to help them continue the good work they are doing in our region.  He stated that he has had the opportunity to meet with some of the organizations and that he is amazed at the good they're doing year-round and how they support our communities.  He thanked Melissa Buckley and those that were able to attend the Bread of Life Banquet, indicating that he appreciates their good work.
