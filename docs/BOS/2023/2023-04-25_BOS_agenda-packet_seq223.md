CONTRACT FOR PROFESSIONAL SERVICES 
Agreement made as of April 25, 2023 between Navajo County ("County") and Glenn Kephart 
("Consultant") with an effective date to begin August 1, 2023.  
Whereas, County desires to continue Broadband Infrastructure Program to improve internet 
access, capacity, connectivity, availability, reliability, and redundancy throughout Navajo County 
and to assure that Navajo County’s broadband program is coordinated with and complements 
regional and statewide broadband initiatives and effectively capitalizes on available grant funding 
opportunities and federal and state programs and;  
Whereas, Consultant currently has led the County’s efforts to move forward the development of 
the broadband infrastructure program as the County Manager and has provided the County notice 
of termination of his employment contract effective July 18, 2023, and; 
Whereas, the County recognizes that the consultant is uniquely qualified to seamlessly continue 
the progress on the County’s broadband efforts and consultant represents that he has the ability, 
knowledge, and expertise to continue efforts to assure that the County’s Broadband program 
effectively maximizes resources including; grant funding; tribal and regional partnerships; industry 
experts; and statewide partners; resulting in effective completion of the County’s middle mile 
project and ultimate delivery of high speed/capacity internet to County residents and businesses 
through federally funded BEAD and other programs and contributes to regional and statewide 
connectivity and redundancy.  
Now, Therefore, the parties agree as follows: 
1.  Services.  Consultant shall provide services in a competent and professional manner as 
 outlined in attached document titled Attachment A, to the satisfaction of the County. 
2. Consultant's Expertise.  Consultant warrants that it has the ability, authority, capacity and 
 professional expertise to perform these services.  Consultant shall self-perform this 
 agreement and be devoted to the successful accomplishment of the work to be performed 
 hereunder. 
3. Independent Contractor. Consultant acknowledges that it is an independent contractor.  
 Neither party is or shall be an agent, employee, partner, joint venture or associate of the 
 other. An employee or agent of one party shall not be deemed an employer or agent of the 
 other party for any purpose.  
4. Subcontracts.  Consultant shall not enter into any subcontract with respect to any of the 
 work to be performed hereunder without the County's prior written approval.  All 
 subcontracts shall comply with applicable federal and state laws and regulations and shall 
 impose on the subcontractor substantially the same obligations as are imposed on

Consultant by this Agreement with respect to those matters covered herein.  Consultant is 
 responsible for full performance of this Agreement regardless of whether subcontractors are 
 used or not. 
5. Term of Contract.  Consultant's services shall be performed on an on-going basis. It is 
anticipated that the need for services will continue for several years and the initial term of 
the contract shall be for 24 months and may be renewed upon mutual agreement by both 
parties.  
6. Payment.  County shall pay Consultant a monthly rate of three thousand and eight hundred 
dollars ($3,800).  The County shall reimburse the Consultant in state travel expenses in 
accordance with the  Navajo County travel policy reimbursement rates. Out of state travel 
arrangements shall be mutually agreed upon between both parties prior to travel. Payments 
shall be due and payable upon receipt of Consultant's itemized invoice. Each invoice shall 
detail the work performed during the billing period. 
7.  Defects in Work.  County may reject any work product that fails to meet customary 
 professional standards or Project specifications.  Consultant agrees to promptly remedy all

 him alone or in conjunction with others in connection with the performance of services

 to analysis, reports, graphics, memoranda, slogans, and taglines.

 "indemnitees") from and against all claims, damages, losses and expenses (including but 
 not limited to attorney’s fees, court costs, and the costs of appellate proceedings) relating

 in the performance of this Agreement.   Consultant's duty to defend, hold harmless and 
 indemnify the indemnitees shall arise in connection with any claim, damage, loss or

10.  Records.  Consultant shall retain and shall require each subcontractor (if applicable) to

ATTACHMENT A 
Consultant shall provide professional services to Navajo County towards assuring that the County’s 
Broadband program effectively maximizes resources including; grant funding; tribal and regional partnerships; 
industry experts; and statewide partners; resulting in effective completion of the County’s middle mile project 
and ultimate delivery of high speed/capacity internet to County residents and businesses through federally 
funded BEAD and other programs and contributes to regional and statewide connectivity and redundancy 
through the following efforts and activities: 
• Assist and provide leadership support to Navajo County’s Capital Projects Manager to assure effective 
and timely delivery of the County’s twenty-million-dollar broadband network design and construction 
and future projects. This will include participation in design and construction meetings; field reviews as 
needed; assistance in developing communication to the Board of Supervisors and public; assistance to 
project manager in resolving project challenges and advisement to project management team as 
needed.   
• Attend and participate in the Navajo Nation Broadband Initiatives meetings and provide briefings, 
talking points, and advisement as may be required to the appropriate District County’s Supervisors.   
• Coordinate and provide advisement to EX2 to ensure effect commercialization of the County’s middle 
mile network.  
• Participation in meetings with Internet Service Providers interested in expanding service in Navajo 
County.  
• Coordinate with interested vendors and assist County in developing RFP to select a partner to 
participate in securing federal BEAD grant funding to be administered through the ACA. 
• Coordination with Arizona Commerce Authority (ACA) to ensure that the County’s efforts are in 
alignment with State initiatives and eligible for State and Federal grant funding.  
• Provide information and guidance to Navajo County’s Grants manager to assure the County is effectively 
securing eligible grants related to broadband.  
• Research legislative matters pertaining to broadband and provide information and advisement to Navajo 
County Government Relations director concerning proposed actions and assist in communication with 
legislative delegation as requested.  
• Provide Broadband updates to REAL AZ Economic Development Corporation. 
• Research Latest developments and emerging technologies and report opportunities to the County 
• Coordinate with regional partners including White Mountain Apache Tribe, Hopi Tribe, Navajo Nation, 
Apache County, Gila County, Coconino County, and NACOG as may be required to assure that our 
broadband efforts align with and contribute to enhancing broadband services for Northern Arizona.  
• Other activities as may be required to assure effective planning, delivery, and implementation of an 
effective broadband program for Navajo County.

TASK 1. PROJECT MANAGEMENT AND COORDINATION  
Task 1.1. Project Management Plan 
Following Notice to Proceed (NTP), we will develop a draft Project Management Plan (PMP) 
document. The PMP will include: 
⚫ Contact information and communication protocols for key team members
⚫ A brief project narrative that includes the goals and objectives for the Design Concept Report
(DCR)
⚫ A refined, detailed scope of work
⚫ A public engagement plan containing the expected timeframes and methodologies for
engagement activities, advertising options, and comment documentation processes
⚫ A detailed milestone schedule
Task 1.2. Project Kickoff Meeting 
Following submittal of the draft PMP, Kimley-Horn will hold a virtual kickoff meeting with County staff 
to review the contents of the draft PMP, identify members of the Technical Advisory Committee 
(TAC), and discuss known issues along the State Route 260 (SR 260) corridor. Following the kickoff 
meeting, we will revise the PMP, as necessary. 
Task 1.3. Technical Advisory Committee 
The TAC will be comprised of key staff from project stakeholders, potentially including Navajo County, 
the City of Show Low, the Town of Pinetop-Lakeside, the Arizona Department of Transportation 
(ADOT) Northeast District, Blue Ridge School District, Fort Apache Tribe, and others as considered 
necessary by the County. We will conduct three TAC meetings throughout the DCR process: 
1. Project Introduction and Corridor Tour. Kimley-Horn will conduct an in-person meeting to
review the contents of the PMP and receive feedback from TAC members. Following the
PMP review, we will drive the corridor with interested TAC members to discuss known issues
and potential solutions. The PMP will be finalized after receiving TAC comments.
2. Existing Conditions Review and Alternatives Visioning. Kimley-Horn will conduct an in-
person meeting to discuss the findings from the Existing Conditions Working Paper and
receive feedback from TAC members. We will then conduct an interactive visioning session
to develop alternatives to be analyzed and prioritized as part of Task 3.
3. Alternatives Analysis Review. Kimley-Horn will host an in-person meeting to discuss and
allow the TAC to provide comment on the results of the alternatives prioritization process, the
second round of public engagement, and initial preferred design alternative
recommendations.

TASK 2. DATA COLLECTION AND EXISTING CONDITIONS  
Task 2.1. Previous Plan Review 
Kimley-Horn will collect and review recommendations from recent previous planning efforts. 
Recommendations will be organized into an evaluation matrix organized by topic area to be 
referenced in later steps of the planning process. 
Task 2.2. Public Engagement – Round 1 
Kimley-Horn will conduct a hybrid virtual and in-person approach to engagement. We will leverage 
Kimley-Horn’s proprietary map-based virtual engagement platform, Public Coordinate, to allow the 
public to map areas of concern for pedestrians, bicyclists, and transit users along the SR 260 
corridor. This virtual engagement will be supplemented with an in-person event, ideally piggy-backing 
on an existing public event, such as Show Low Days, which mimics the topics covered in the virtual 
engagement. The project schedule has preliminarily been developed to have Round 1 of engagement 
occur during Show Low Days (June 9-11, 2023). 
Kimley-Horn will work with Navajo County staff and the TAC to decide effective advertisement 
methods. Options could include a project flyer to be posted in local activity centers, posts on 
stakeholder agency websites, press releases for local media outlets, email blasts to stakeholders’ 
listservs, and social media post content for use by stakeholder agencies. 
Task 2.3. Data Collection 
Kimley-Horn will collect pertinent data to evaluate and prioritize improvement alternatives: 
⚫ Peak-hour turning-movement traffic and pedestrian counts at up to 20 locations to evaluate 
signal or crosswalk warrants (informed by stakeholder input) 
⚫ 24-hour vehicle classification counts at up to 10 locations to support the alternatives analysis

⚫ ADOT and local jurisdiction right-of-way (ROW) data 
⚫ Land-use and parcel data along the corridor to identify activity centers 
⚫ SR 260 roadway characteristics, such as lane widths, sidewalk widths and accessibility 
compliance, bicycle infrastructure, turning lanes, and intersection and driveway spacing 
⚫ Topographic and utility infrastructure constraints 
⚫ The latest five years of crash data available with an emphasis on bicycle and pedestrian 
crashes 
Due to the significant seasonal fluctuations in pedestrian and traffic volumes on the SR 260 corridor, 
the project schedule has been crafted to conduct traffic counts during July of 2023. 
Task 2.4. Environmental Overview 
Kimley-Horn will develop a high-level Environmental Overview (EO) for the length of the SR 260 
corridor between US 60 and SR 73. The first step in the EO process will be to have an expectations-
setting meeting between Kimley-Horn’s Environmental Lead and County staff. This meeting will cover 
the information, analyses, and sources anticipated to be included in the EO.  
The EO will be performed to identify environmental opportunities and constraints that will be 
considered in developing and evaluating potential multimodal improvements. The EO will include a 
desktop review of the physical natural environment based on various sources to identify potential fatal 
flaws with potential design alternatives. These reviews can be supplemented with targeted 
“windshield surveys” if more information or field verification of information is needed. 
Task 2.5. Existing Conditions Working Paper 
Tasks 2.1-2.4 will be documented in Working Paper 1: Existing Conditions, which will include 
summary reviews of all collected and reviewed data, public engagement results, key takeaways, and 
technical appendices with detailed collected datasets. The draft Working Paper will be provided to 
County staff and the TAC for review and comment.

TASK 3. DESIGN ALTERNATIVES ANALYSIS  
Task 3.1. Alternatives Development 
Based on previous planning recommendations for the corridor, discussions with County staff, TAC 
Meeting #2, and the public engagement results, Kimley-Horn will develop a set of potential design 
alternatives for pedestrian, bicycle, and transit enhancements. Signal and intelligent transportation 
system (ITS) enhancements will also be developed to improve active transportation safety and 
corridor efficiency. We will then work to refine those alternatives to define logical project termini, 
perform sketch-level design, and develop planning-level cost estimates.

Task 3.2. Traffic Analysis 
For any multimodal alternatives that may affect traffic operations, such as signalizing intersections or 
installing mid-block crosswalks, Kimley-Horn will perform a traffic analysis to quantify expected 
impacts using the traffic data collected as part of Task 2. Impacts to traffic operations will be one of 
the factors used for alternative prioritization. A traffic analysis section will be included as part of 
Working Paper 2. 
Task 3.3. Alternative Prioritization 
Each design alternative will be evaluated against a series of criteria, which could include: 
⚫ Impacts to traffic operations 
⚫ Constructability 
⚫ Planning-level cost 
⚫ Proximity to activity centers 
⚫ ROW availability 
⚫ Anticipated level of use by pedestrians, bicyclists, and transit users 
⚫ Public support (established in Task 3.4) 
Each design alternative will receive a prioritization score based on these criteria to compare and rank 
potential recommendations. 
Task 3.4. Public Engagement – Round 2 
After creating an initial alternative prioritization based on the criteria in Task 3.3, Kimley-Horn will 
present the draft prioritized alternative list to the public for review and comment. Kimley-Horn will 
again implement a hybrid virtual and in-person engagement approach. We will develop an online 
interactive map allowing the public to review and comment on alternatives. A companion public open 
house or event will be held at a location along the corridor allowing the public to comment on the 
prioritized alternative list in-person. 
Task 3.5. Design Alternatives Working Paper 
The results from the public engagement will be added to the alternative prioritization to create a final 
prioritized alternative list. Tasks 3.1-3.4 will be documented in Working Paper 2: Design Alternatives 
Analysis, which will be provided to County staff and the TAC for review and comment. 
Task 3 Deliverables 
⚫ Public engagement materials and advertisements 
⚫ Draft and final Working Paper 2: Design Alternatives Analysis 
TASK 4. DESIGN CONCEPT REPORT  
Task 4.1. Conceptual Design 
Based on the prioritized design alternatives list developed in Task 3, Kimley-Horn will select preferred 
design alternatives for key locations along the SR 260 corridor in coordination with County staff and 
the TAC. Some multimodal projects may require interim and ultimate projects based on feasibility, 
cost, constraints, and public support. We will then perform conceptual-level (15%) design for the

which will be provided to County staff for review and comment. 
Task 4.2. AASHTO Controlling Design Criteria Review 
After an initial review by County staff, Kimley-Horn will update the designs accordingly and perform 
an analysis against American Association of State Highway Transportation Officials (AASHTO) 
Controlling Design Criteria to identify any locations that may require design exceptions. The review 
will be included as an appendix to the final DCR document along with justification and request for any 
design variances, as needed. 
Task 4.3. ADA Compliance and Feasibility Review 
Kimley-Horn will perform an analysis of the conceptual designs against Americans with Disabilities 
Act (ADA) requirements to identify any locations where the feasibility of ADA compliance may be a 
challenge. This review will be included as an appendix to the final DCR document and descriptions of 
any potential ADA challenges will be documented. 
Task 4.4. Cost Estimation 
Detailed estimates of probable construction costs will be developed for each of the preferred design 
alternatives to improve upon the planning-level costs developed as part of Task 3. The estimates will 
be based on unit costs from recent ADOT construction projects in rural Arizona and will be provided 
to County staff for review and comment. These detailed estimates will be included as an appendix to 
the final DCR document. 
Task 4.5. Design Concept Report Document 
Kimley-Horn will develop a DCR document that will include: 
⚫ Streamlined versions of Working Papers 1 and 2, which encompass the planning process 
⚫ Descriptions of the preferred design alternatives and conceptual-level design 
⚫ A write-up on the AASHTO Controlling Design Criteria Review results 
⚫ A write-up on the ADA Compliance and Feasibility Review results 
⚫ Summaries of the estimates of probable costs 
⚫ An implementation plan with a proposed order in which the preferred design alternatives 
should be pursued along with potential local, State, and Federal funding sources 
⚫ Technical appendices, including the AASHTO Controlling Design Criteria, ADA Compliance 
and Feasibility, and Estimates of Probable Cost 
The draft DCR document will be provided to County staff and TAC for review and comment. Kimley-
Horn will finalize the DCR document based on received comments and discussions with County staff.

⚫ Draft and final technical appendices (AASHTO Controlling Design Criteria Review, ADA 
Compliance and Feasibility Review, Estimates of Probable Costs) 
⚫ Draft and final DCR document
