Presentation of the WIC Baby Shower special event that was held on September 29, 2023 :
Kelsey Nies talked about her position in the WIC office.  She talked about the community baby shower that was held, indicating that they received a special project grant from the Arizona WIC office to be able to hold the event, which was the first of its kind in our county.  She reviewed the WIC values, including the benefits of breastfeeding and the importance of education and support.  She talked about the project goals of the event, which included to support, inform, and empower pregnant and new mothers, their support person, and their breastfeeding journey; establish peer connections; and gain new WIC clients.  She talked about the community baby shower that was held on September 29 in Show Low, indicating that they were awarded $3,125 to put the event on, had some informational WIC booths and community partner booths and provided breastfeeding goodie bags to all who attended and had raffle prizes.  She stated that they served thirty-one families, that they had nine new clients that registered, that they had approximately eighty total attendees, and they were able to make referrals for car seat/ pack n' play training and dental.  She talked about strengths from the event, including peer connections, referrals, attendance, and then lessons learned were that rushes were difficult to manage and the weather.  She indicated that moving forward they will collaborate with tribal and community partners and expand the event to other locations in Navajo County.
Supervisor Seymore thanked Ms. Nies for the report and for her excitement and enthusiasm.
Vice-Chairman Whiting thanked Ms. Nies and those that were involved for looking at out of the box ways to serve communities in ways that matter.

Consideration of an Intergovernmental Agreement, Contract Number CTR067944, with the Arizona Department of Health Services for WIC and BFPC services in Navajo County :
Ivy McCullough, Division Manager for WIC, stated that the WIC program has been funded as part of the Navajo County Public Health District since 1974 and requested consideration of the IGA with the Arizona Department of Health Services that would continue the services from October 2023 to September 2028.  She talked about the WIC program, indicating that the program serves low-income women, infants, and children and promotes nutrition interventions leading to healthier eating patterns.  She indicated that over one million dollars for WIC benefits were redeemed last year.  She talked about the aspects of the breastfeeding program. She addressed the different areas throughout Navajo County where they provide services, including in person, over the phone and virtual.  She stated that the services are available to all citizens of Navajo County who wish to apply and qualify based on income eligibility, category, and nutritional risk.  She indicated that USDA recently estimated that ten percent of all households are food insecure, and that WIC is a lifeline for new parents, serving approximately forty-three percent of all infants in the United State and twenty percent of children one to four.  She talked about their caseload for Navajo county, stating that the numbers have increased over the last year, and that they anticipate they will serve over two thousand participants monthly over the next year.  She stated that WIC helps parenting with rising food costs, provides accurate nutrition education and provided referrals to other community resources.  She requested approval of the IGA.

Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion  to approve an Intergovernmental Agreement, Contract Number CTR067944, with the Arizona Department of Health Services for WIC and BFPC services in Navajo County, motion seconded by  Vice-Chairman Whiting .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Consideration of adoption of Resolution Number 34-2023 , adopting the new voting precincts maps and polling places that were adjusted to meet the new Board of Supervisor Districts pursuant to A.R.S. §16-411 :
A presentation was shown. Jason Moore talked about the requirements for drawing precinct lines pursuant to ARS 16-411(A)(1).  He addressed the areas of agreement, stating that the precinct lines that are drawn can't include more than one Congressional district, one Legislative district, one Board of Supervisors district and one Justice of the Peace district and that the special taxing districts are not considered when drawing the lines.  He talked about the areas of disagreements, including if a precinct can include a municipality and unincorporated area, if it can include more than one municipality and if it can include more than one school district.   He addressed the requirements for boundaries pursuant to the law, regarding precincts being drawn within election districts of election officers for the state, political subdivisions, and community college districts. He addressed those who have election districts.  He indicated that school districts and cities and towns are political subdivisions that are not divided into election districts, that there is an exception by statute that allows a school district to divide the district itself into a single member district, but that those don't exist in Navajo County.  He talked about how Maricopa County does their precincts.  He addressed issues when applying ARS 16-311(K)(1), which is found in Chapter 3, Article 2 of Title 16, indicating that it governs the nomination, filing out nomination papers and circulation of the candidate nomination petitions.  He talked about school districts and the city and town elections.  He stated after the last Board meeting, he looked at all of the northern tier counties in Arizona and he provided information on how Gila, Coconino, and Yavapai Counties create their boundaries.  He stated that there is no limit on the number of people that can be included in a precinct under the statute, but that it is at the discretion of the Board regarding the number of precincts there are.

Chairman Peshlakai opened the meeting for public comments.
Ranae Rowles, 400 Freedom Lane, Taylor, talked about the precinct lines and the reasons that she feels that the lines should be drawn different from what it being recommended.  She talked about the packet that was provided for the Board to review that includes the statues that are relevant in her research and the steps for creating compliant precincts.  She talked about the draft maps that they have looked at and concerns with small parts of communities being isolated from the areas that they are in.  She requested that this not be approved until after the precincts are drawn.  She talked about the areas that should have additional polling places due to the number of voters. She recommended that what is being presented today not be approved due to insufficient information.
The public hearing was closed.

A presentation was shown. Ryan Taylor talked about the process for creating the vote precincts that are being proposed.
A presentation was shown.  Rayleen Richards stated that two thirds of Navajo County votes by mail.  She indicated that when looking at polling locations they go by voter registration minus those that get a ballot by mail, which is the reason they have thirty-nine locations.  She talked about the key points for going from fourteen to twenty-one precincts, that being keeping the Justice Courts and the Board of Supervisors as a whole.  She indicated that the thirty-nine polling locations will remain the same.  She stated that she appreciates the feedback from community members.  She requested approval of the item.
Supervisor Whitesinger inquired about numbers for the Woodland precinct regarding those going to the polls and early voting.  She indicated that when they previously met information was provided that clarified why there are thirty-nine voting locations to meet the needs of the entire population.  She noted that one third of the population is going to the polls on election day and as seen with presidential elections, there was a sufficient amount of time and voting locations.
Supervisor Seymore stated that he appreciates that time that staff has worked on this and the feedback from the citizens.  He talked about the increase in precincts from the feedback that was received and indicated that they can, in the future, have more collaboration and look at additional changes.
Vice-Chairman Whiting thanked those who have put in time and effort on this and stated that all those involved have a desire to get it right.  He indicated that there should always be an opportunity to look at everything and consider the different ways to do things.  He stated that he appreciates that people care enough about this to speak up and to work on it.
Supervisor Whitesinger expressed her gratitude to all those involved in the process and being able to bring the different perspectives into consideration.  She indicated that she appreciates the information provided on how it impacts the communities and that they are doing what is required to allow the votes to happen.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to approve adoption of  Resolution Number 34-2023 , adopting the new voting precincts maps and polling places that were adjusted to meet the new Board of Supervisor Districts pursuant to A.R.S. §16-411, motion seconded by  Vice-Chairman Whiting .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration of adoption of  Resolution Number 41-2023 , approving/denying a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction of a 80' rusticated monopole wireless communication tower and associated facilities on a vacant, 2.50-acre parcel, in the White Mountain Lakes area :
A presentation was shown.  Cody Cooper talked about the proposed area, indicating that there is a Post Office/Community building in the area.  He addressed the proposed area for the tower and the required setbacks.  He talked about the lease area where the equipment would be located and the proposed project design for the tower and the fenced equipment.  He showed different simulations for the tower.  He addressed the community meeting that was held, provided a list of the attendees, and talked about the questions that were asked at the meeting.  He talked about the review done by staff, indicating that it does not meet the minimum setbacks due to being in a residential area but that there is a provision within the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance that the Board of Supervisors may waive the setback requirement.  He stated that this proposal does not meet the relevant goals or the comprehensive plan for Navajo County and that staff recommends denial due to it requiring a setback waiver, which is out of the authority of staff.  He stated that that the Planning and Zoning Commission unanimously recommended denial at their September 21, 2023, meeting.  He indicated that if the Board wishes to approve the Special Use Permit that staff recommendations that the conditions outlined in the staff report be included.
Reg Destree, Representative of Vertical Bridge, addressed the Board, indicating that after approval of the tower near the airport the Engineering Team has advised that they would be able to offer the option of reducing the tower height for this request to 62 feet, shift the pole within the existing compound, which would allow the site to avoid the requirement and meet all code standards.  A presentation was shown.   He talked about the pole, the ground enclosure and how the proposed site is meant to work in conjunction with the surrounding existing and proposed sites for Verizon.  He addressed Verizon's plan to provide better service in the area.  He talked about citizen involvement.  He addressed the reason for the location and the site description and the layout.  He stated that the modified design meets all the standards, that they are not asking for relief and are requesting approval of a Special Use Permit.
Vice-Chairman Whiting inquired if the recommendation of staff has changed due to the new proposed tower height.
Mr. Cooper stated that if the new proposed height can meet the 2 to1 setback requirement staff would change the recommendation since it would meet all of the requirements.
Supervisor Seymore inquired if this item would need to be tabled or pulled since the item was submitted for the 80-foot pole and then resubmitted with the information for the shorter pole.
Brad Carlyon addressed the different options, indicating that they can either go forward or table the item.
Vice-Chairman Whiting stated that that want to get the publics input so that they have the information but indicated that the Boards role is to make sure if meets the requirements of the ordinance.  If indicated that it would give the public a chance to hear it and be aware of what the applicants are looking at.
Mr. Cooper indicated that if the Board would like the item to reenter the hearing process, the applicant could choose to withdraw and reenter through the Commission and then come back in front of the Board because that it would that it would be bringing the same item back with the adjusted height.
A discussion was held regarding the options.

Chairman Peshlakai opened the Public Hearing.

Tony Tangalos (appearing via Zoom) 8319 Pinion Drive, White Mountain Lakes, requested that the Board only vote on what was submitted on the agenda and not on the requested modification for a 60-foot tower.  He requested that they deny the 80-foot tower due to not meeting to ordinance and that the request for a 60-foot tower be resubmitted before being considered.  He stated that the community doesn't want the tower.
