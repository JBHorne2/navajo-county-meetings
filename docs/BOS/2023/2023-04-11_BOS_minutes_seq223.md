SHERIFF'S OFFICE :

a.

Consideration of a proclamation declaring the week beginning April 9th through April 15th, 2023 as "National Telecommunicators Week" in Navajo County, Arizona:
Sheriff Clouse requested that April 9-15, 2023 be recognized as National Telecommunicators Week. He introduced Caitlin Montoya, the Dispatch Supervisor, indicating that she has been with the Sheriff's Office for ten years, the Supervisor for approximately six years and that she does a fantastic job.  He talked about the workload and the exposure that the dispatch center handles daily and how they assist not only the emergency personnel but the public with care and compassion.  He read the proposed proclamation.  He talked about the statistics for the first quarter of 2023 indicating that they have taken 15,003 administrative calls, 5703 911 calls which averages out to 22,800 a year, 60,000 non-emergency calls and approximately 20,000 calls that are made out of the center.  He indicated that when the officers get dispatched to a call they never feel like they are on their own due to the Dispatchers always having contact and information on where they are at and doing welfare checks.  He stated that they never lose track of any of the emergency personnel and that they are monitoring all at the same time and always taking care of the personnel.  He thanked Ms. Montoya and her team for all that they do.  He requested approval of the proclamation.

2.

Consideration of an Intergovernmental Agreement between Navajo County and County Municipalities for the use of Opioid Settlement proceeds pursuant to the One Arizona Plan and Opioid Settlement Agreement Funding Project Vetting Process :
Janelle Linn talked about the One Arizona Agreement that the entered and indicated that they were delegated to oversee the finances to ensure the appropriate rollout of public health strategies.  She indicated that some of the settlements are still being finalized and that they will keep the Board updated on settlements and spending.  She stated that the municipalities, counties and tribes received their own money, that the municipalities portions were expected to be very small and that they have been working with towns and cities to have the best impact for the money and pull them together to have a more targeted response.  She indicated that the agreement requires that the money be utilized on public health approaches and indicated that this will provide an opportunity to work with the towns and cities through the criminal justice system, the County Attorney's Office, and Sheriff's Office to partner on strategies that they can implement in the communities.  She advised that the tribal entities chose to create their own class action lawsuit separate from the One Arizona Agreement and that many of them are still unsettled but that they will be receiving their own money, which gives the tribes flexibility to use funds for the challenges that are unique to their communities.  She indicated that it also provides the opportunity to partner with them on some public program options that can be provided on or off tribal lands.  She stated that they are going to be very actively engaged with those who provide services to those suffering from the damage of the opioid epidemic.  She talked about the steering committee that is outlined in the MOU and those who will be serving on the committee.  She talked about the guiding principles laid out by Johns Hopkins and the vetting process for the projects, indicated that it will be up to the Board to determine what projects to use the money on.
Vice-Chairman Whiting thanked Ms. Linn for all of the work on this and for the collaborations.  He stated that they need to maximize the spending and make sure that they look at appropriate ways to vet the projects.
Supervisor Whitesinger indicated that this is a challenge and she thanked Ms. Linn for her leadership and for helping the communities come together to be able to partner.  She stated that the work is important, and the partnerships are valuable.  She talked about the vetting process for the projects. She thanked all of those who were involved in this, indicating that there has been a lot of work invested in this.

Vice-Chairman Whiting made a motion  to approve an Intergovernmental Agreement between Navajo County and County Municipalities for the use of Opioid Settlement proceeds pursuant to the One Arizona Plan and Opioid Settlement Agreement Funding Project Vetting Process, motion seconded by  Supervisor Whitesinger .

Supervisor Benally commented on the tribal land benefiting in the projects and training.
Ms. Linn talked about the opioid program that they currently have and indicated that they do offer some services on the reservation.  She indicated that more than three hundred school staff members have been trained on how to use Narcan.  She stated that they are looking at ways to expand the programs and partner with them but that the many of the tribal settlements are still pending.
Brad Carlyon talked about how one of the factors for the programs would be to serve citizens on Navajo County which may be on tribal lands.  He indicated that if there are specific provisions in the motion that Supervisor Benally wished to amend that she would need to make that motion and there would need to be a majority vote.

Chairman Peshlakai thanked Ms. Linn for the presentation.  He talked about the services for on and off tribal land and the status of the settlement for the Navajo Nation.  He indicated that the tribals will be receiving a separate allocation and will be able to determine how to spend that settlement for services but that amounts have not been determined for the different tribes within Navajo County at this time.

PLANNING & ZONING :

1.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration of the adoption of Resolution Number 11-2023 approving/denyinga Special Use Permit to operate a recovery residential facility for ten (10) residents with 13 staff per week with overlapping shifts:
Supervisor Seymore disclosed that he has been contacted by applicant several times as well as citizens regarding the site for this item.  He advised that he has not discussed it with Planning and Development or the other Board members.
Cody Cooper stated that the applicants have applied for a special use permit to operate a licensed behavioral health residential facility.  A presentation was shown.  He talked about the location of the facility.  He indicated that Sunrise Health is licensed by the Arizona Department of Health Services, that they are proposing a maximum of 10 occupants, and that it would be a one to thirty- sixty- or ninety-day program.  He advised that there are 13 staff members on site on a weekly rotating basis.  He further advised that the facility does have two locked doors and all exits are supposed to have alarms and are locked twenty-four hours daily.  He stated that the residents would not park at the facility, that there are two ADA accessible spaces as well as twelve standard parking spaces, which does not meet the requirements for a facility of this type with the Navajo County Zoning ordinance.  He talked about the public meeting that was held on September 1, 2022, at the proposed location and was advertised in the White Mountain Independent.  He advised that Timber Mesa has not indicated any issues with the proposal but that they did need verification that there was emergency power to the electric fire pump, which the applicants provide to the fire department.  He stated that at the Planning and Zoning Commission meeting they did receive three calls against this project as well as eleven in support and since that hearing further letters, emails and petitions have been received which are all included in the Board packet.  He indicated that the applicants have advised that they have had further outreach since the Commission meeting.  He stated that the Planning and Zoning Commission unanimously recommended denial of the proposed.  He further stated that if the Board should wish to approve the project staff would recommend that it be with the conditions as outlined in the staff report.
Dr. Onkar Bhowra, applicant, stated that the facility has gone through the process of getting approval for the inpatient unit.  He advised that he has practiced internal medicine physician/primary care physician who has been practicing the last thirty years, that he has worked as a consultant in psychiatric facilities and has been thousands of patients with substance abuse, addiction and related issues.  He indicated that there are not enough treatments centers for all the individuals to be able to council them and help them return to their communities as good citizens.  He stated that they didn't have the opportunity to respond to some of the concerns at the Planning and Zoning Commission meeting which he believes led to their denial.  He talked about the concerns that have been brought forth regarding the facility being located close to a school.
Eugene "Ed" Gonzales stated that he has thirty-one years of clinical experience.   He talked about the layout of the facility, the program and the benefits for those who will go through the program.  He stated that they have security inside and out with cameras and that they are in the process of making sure that a barrier fence will be installed in the back. He stated that they have been working on this project for a long time.  He reviewed the program description and showed an example of a weekly program schedule.  He talked about concerns that were brought up in the Planning and Zoning Commission hearing.  He stated that they are working diligently to help save lives and provide a solution to the epidemic.
Dr. Bhowra stated that they tried to contact the Superintendent and principal at the school to hear their concerns and try to find some solutions, but that they were unable to meet with them.  He indicated that they did speak with some of the residents in the area and that they made a effort to meet with those in the area to try and address their concerns.

Chairman Peshlakai  opened the meeting for public comments.

Amanda Stabenow, the K1 teacher at Sequoia Village School, read a letter from the Dean of Students, who was unable to be present, which talked about the concerns with the facility being located across the street from the school.  She stated that they want to be able to provide a safe environment for the students and indicated that if this request is granted it will take away the option of their school from the families.
Derrick Luptak, teacher at Sequoia Village School and father of two students, addressed concerns with the location of the facility.  He talked about the history of the school indicating that this is a small public charter school that has been there for over twenty years and that it that offers small classes for students have hard time getting their needs met in the larger schools.  He further talked about how having a rehab facility located so close to the school could affect the mental health and sense of safety of the children.  He stated that the county needs substance abuse programs but that they also need to support the small schools to help the children and that they need to be mindful of where the rehabs are located.
Jamie Beth Kulish, 460 W. Reidhead, Show Low, stated that she is the Principal at Sequoia Village School.  She talked about concerns with the location of this facility and the reasons that this item should not be approved.  She indicated that most of the letters they received are regarding the students' safety and stated that they feel that students will be withdrawn from the school if this is granted, which would make it hard for the school to keep the doors open.  She requested that this be denied so that the families have peace of mind sending their kids to school and that they be able to provide a safe and supportive environment for the youth in the community.
Jessica Jarvis, 5470 Ripley Lane, Pinedale, talked about concerns with the location of the facility.  She further talked about her family history with drug abuse and the location of the facility that the family members visited.  She stated that she doesn't feel that the proposed location for this facility is appropriate.  She talked about the high school students that attend the school, the temptations that they are subjected to and indicated that it doesn't make for a good learning environment with the facility being so close to the school.  She advised that she did research on rehab facilities in larger areas and she talked about the statistics for those facilities.
Sylvia Allen, Snowflake, talked about her involvement with the school, her position as the Senate Education Chairman and the challenges that schools are experiencing.  She further talked about the concerns with the facility location being across from a school, indicating that it would increase the liability for the school, that they would have to determine and implement increased security needs and that it would be an increased cost for them.
Jerry Lewis, 1303 W. 7 th Street, Mesa, AZ, stated that he is one of the Assistant Superintendents.  He stated that he supports substance abuse and addiction recovery programs, but that they should not be located across from a school.  He talked about the concerns with the location, the pressures that are already in place for students and teachers, the stability of those who will be in the treatment facility and the need for the students to feel safe.  He further talked about the timeline for the public being notified of the facility.  He indicated that the school has been in the county since 1998 and that the proposed facility poses a threat to school enrollment if the request is granted.
Laurance Misner, Linden, AZ, stated that he has kids that currently attend the school and that will take them out of the school if this is approved.  He talked about his concerns with the location and requested that this be denied.
Melissa Mayural, Vista Avenue, Phoenix, stated that she is Human Resources for Sunrise Health and Recovery.  She indicated that they want to help the community and provide wholesome care to those who are struggling.  She talked about the care and support that will be offered to the individuals that are at the facility.  She stated that residence will be a in locked, alarmed facility with video monitoring inside and out.  She indicated that at the time of the application they had thirteen staff hired and that they planned to hire additional staff.  She stated that they plan to put up a security fence in the back.  She talked about the reasons that the request should be granted.
Eugene "Ed" Gonzales, talked about his history in the communities.  He further talked about the facility and the procedures that will be followed.  He indicated that they were unaware that they needed a special permit until October.  He addressed the concerns with the location of the facility being next to a school.  He talked about his work history with substance abuse programs and indicated that their goal is for long sustained sobriety.
Cheyanne Miser, 1076 Mogollon Circle, appearing via Zoom, talked about her concerns with the individuals in the facility coming and going and the response time for law enforcement in the event of an issue.

Chairman Peshlakai  closed the public meeting.

Dr. Bhowra addressed some of the concerns that were brought from the public comments.

Chairman Peshlakai stepped out of the meeting at 11:26 a.m.

Supervisor Seymore thanked staff for the work done on this item.  He thanked the citizens who shared comments and the applicants who are trying to find a solution.  He talked about some of the concerns that were brought forth during the public comments and the steps that the applicants have taken.  He stated that he is in support of the Planning and Zoning recommendation of denial, indicating that he doesn't feel that it would be in the best interest of the citizens in the area.
Vice-Chairman Whiting addressed the comments and the concerns, from those who live in the area or have kids in the school, regarding the location.  He indicated that there is a need for services in the area and that the citizens are not saying they don't want to have the services in the area but just not in the proposed location.  He stated that he appreciates the efforts that the individuals are making by taking the time to change what's going on in their lives.  He further stated that with the location being where it is and the concerns of the citizens, that he agrees with the recommendation of denial.

Supervisor Benally indicated that she doesn't believe that the proposed location is appropriate.
