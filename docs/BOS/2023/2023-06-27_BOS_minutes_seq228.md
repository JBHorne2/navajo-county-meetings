Presentation/Summary of Navajo County's Fiscal Year 2022 Annual Financial Report, Report on Internal Control and on Compliance, and Federal Single Audit Report :
Jayson Vowell introduced Donald Bohart, Nicholas Rabayda and Emily Diaz with the Auditor General's Office.
Mr. Bohart provided background as to the Auditor General's Office, indicating that it is a legislative agency made up on nonpartisan staff, that they report to the Joint Legislative Audit Committee and that they are an independent source of impartial information concerning state and local governments and programs. He indicated that the presentation today is in accordance with ARS 11-661d, which requires auditors to present results and findings in a regular meeting without the use of a consent agenda within ninety days of the audit completion. He stated that they will discuss the work on three required annual reports, the county's annual comprehensive financial report, the report on internal control and compliance and the federal single audit report.  He stated as to the annual comprehensive report for FY22 they reported an unmodified opinion or a clean opinion meaning the county's financial statements are reliable.  He indicated that on the report on internal control and compliance, which is included in the single audit report, is where they report and findings and recommendations resulting from the audit of the county's financial statement, resulted in four findings.  He stated the the federal single audit report includes assessments of the county's compliance with federal program requirements, where they reported four findings.  He reviewed the highlights on the internal control and compliance report, which included the county's largest primary revenue sources and how the monies were under over the last ten years.  He stated that the four main sources of revenue consist of federal and state grants and programs shared state sales taxes, county sales taxes and property taxes.  He indicated that the sales and property taxes have been relatively consistent over the last five years, that the federal and state grants increased over the last couple of years due to federal grant revenues directly towards COVID 19 and that the county sales taxes have increased starting the second half of FY20 due to the voter approved jail tax.  He indicated that the primary expenses consist of general government, public safety, highway and streets, and health and welfare stating that most expenses remained consistent with moderate increases, but that in FY19 the general government and public safety decreased by $14.8 million and $4.7 million from the prior fiscal year due to net decreases in pension expenses. He addressed the increase in the net position based on revenues exceeding expenses for the past few years.  He reviewed the four financial audit findings and the recommendations that were reported in the internal control and compliance report as follows. First, a deficiency in the county's financial statement preparation process, due to no written policies and procedures for preparing the financial statements and supporting schedules to ensure they were accurate and prepared in accordance with general accepted accounting principles before providing them for audit, with a recommendation that the department develop and implement written policies and procedures, and the County indicating that this will be corrected by June 2023.  The second was deficiencies in the county's purchasing card fuel purchase process, with a recommendation that the county ensure all employees approved to use county purchasing cards follow county policy and by improving the procedures over procurement card fuel purchases, indicating that the county plans to implement the recommendations by June 2024.  The third finding identified deficiencies in the county's process for managing and documenting its informational technology risks, with a recommendation that the county make it a priority to implement written policies and procedures to manage risks and determine where to implement critical controls and stating that the county continues to work towards implement the recommendations and anticipates to fully correct this deficiency by June 2024.  The fourth finding identifies deficiencies in the county's controls over its IT systems and date, with several recommendations to develop comprehensive written policies and procedures over restricting access its IT systems and manage system configurations and changes, indicating that the county has made progress on implementing several recommendations and anticipates to fully correct the deficiencies by June 2024.
Nicholas Rabayda stated that the single audit report is where the federal compliance audit findings are found.  He stated that the county reported four compliance findings related to the Forest Service, schools and road cluster, the Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act and the coronavirus state and local fiscal recovery funds program.  He further stated that the county did correct a previously reported finding over the Public Health Emergency Response program.  He reviewed the four findings which were, first, the County's Public Health Services Department failed to provide the required 45-day comment period, to the Advisory Committee and the public, of it's proposal to spend title three monies prior to spending them, second, that the county's WIOA youth activities department spent funds on unreliable purchases, third, that the WIOA youth activities department failed to ensure that it spend the required twenty percent of program dollars that were to be used to provide in school and out of school youth with paid and unpaid work experiences during the period of April 2020 through June 2022 and fourth, that the county's finance department did not review and approve all four federal progress reports including one interim and three project and expenditure reports before submitted them to the federal agency.  He reviewed the recommendations for reach finding, indicating that the county has responded that they concur with the findings and plans to implement the recommendations by June 20, 2023.  He stated that they will follow up with the findings as part of the FY23 audit and that they will focus on whether the county implemented the recommendations and complied with all applicable laws and regulations.
Supervisor Seymore inquired if any of the errors presented are a concern of embezzlement or anything like that.
Mr. Bohart stated that they did not find any indication of that.
Vice-Chairman Whiting thanked them for their presentation, stating that it's important to have the checks and balances.  He thanked them for their time and for providing the information to help them understand the areas that can be improved on.
Mr. Bohart thanked staff and indicated that they look forward to working with the team on the FY23 audit.
Mr. Vowell stated that they appreciate the partnership with auditors to ensure that they are being consistent and compliant with the laws and regulations and to show that the funds are being used appropriately.  He stated that they have three goals, one, to receive a clean opinion, second, to meet deadlines and third, to submit the audit the GFOA, which is like a secondary review.  He indicated that they are proud that the financial statements are accurate and transparent.  He advised that they were third out of fifteen counties to issues the financial report/financial statements and that with the single audit or federal compliance they were one of five counties to meet the March 31 deadline.  He stated that they have submitted financial report to the GFOA and expect to receive the award for the twelfth consecutive years.  He indicated that the audits are about improvement and not judgment, that it reveals helpful information that they can use to protect the organization from risks.  He talked about what finance has done to correct the previous errors, indicating that they are looking for solutions that are efficient.  He stated that no miss use of funds was found and that they expect a significant improvement going into the next years audit.
Supervisor Seymore thanked Mr. Vowell for his comments.

PUBLIC HEARING: TRUTH IN TAXATION pursuant to A.R.S. §42-17104 (primary) and §48-254 (secondary): Consideration and possible approval of property tax levy with no change to property tax rates from the prior year :
Jayson Vowell stated that the truth in taxation hearing is required by state statute anything there is an increase to taxes, even if the rate is reduced.  He stated that they are proposing the state property tax rate for FY23 to FY24, indicating that it is an increase for $7.4 to $7.8 million in property tax collection.  He addressed the reasons for the increase, indicating that part of it is due to new construction.  He stated that as they're seeing the cost increase with good and services or contracts, they are seeing the need to keep a flat tax rate to offset the inflationary pressures to the budget process.  He addressed the history for truth in taxation, indicating that even with keeping the rates the same they are still providing relief to taxpayers due to still being under the allowable levy.  Mr. Vowell advised that historically Navajo County has been the fourth lowest property tax rate in Arizona and that they don't expect that to change going forward.  He talked about how primary property tax and secondary tax is collected and what services that are used to provide.  He stated that they are also recommending the secondary tax rate to remain same, indicating that those are specific to the different special districts and that they are dependent on property tax due to not collecting sales tax and their other sources of revenue being minimal.  He talked about how the property tax will slightly increase even though the rate will remain flat.
Mr. Vowell answered questions of the Board regarding the minimal dollar amount impact without the tax rate being increased.

Chairman Peshlakai  opened the meeting for public comments.  There being no comments, the Public Hearing was closed.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion to approve a property tax levy with no change to property tax rates from the prior year, motion seconded by Vice-Chairman Whiting .  Melissa Buckley, Clerk of the Board, polled the Board with the following results: Supervisor Benally voted in favor, Chairman Peshlakai voted in favor, Vice-Chairman Whiting voted in favor, Supervisor Whitesinger voted in favor and Supervisor Seymore voted in favor of the motion.

PUBLIC HEARING:  Convene a Special Meeting of the Navajo County Board of Supervisors: FINAL 2023-24 BUDGET PURSUANT TO A.R.S. §42-17104 : Consideration and possible adoption of  Resolution Number 21-2023 , approving the proposed final budget for fiscal year 2023-24 for Navajo County :
Vice-Chairman Whiting made a motion to enter into a Special Meeting, motion seconded by Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Jayson Vowell stated that the Preliminary Budget for FY24 was presented at the last meeting.  He talked about the budget foundation, stating that one goal is to develop and maintain a structurally balanced budget, meaning the recurring revenues meet or exceed the recurring expenses each year by first not using one time resources to fund ongoing expenses, second to maintain financial reserves of at least ten but no more than twenty-five percent and third is operations, to financially support the offices and departments to provide services to the residents and citizens of the county. He addressed the highlights of there being no change to the property tax rates, maintaining county services, investing in building infrastructure, continuing sustainable compensation for employees and the historical investments in regional projects.  He stated that they are recommending a general fund budget of $65,000,000, with fifty-one percent of that being for public safety, eighteen percent in mandated services, seventeen percent county wide, and approximately six percent in administration.  He further stated that for the special revenue funds they are proposing a budget of $151,700,000, with twenty-seven percent of that being funded by grants and county services, seventeen percent for HURF funding and approximately fifteen percent in the health insurance trust reserve and special districts.  He stated that they have also budgeted for the continuation of the ARPA projects, which include the broadband project and the code enforcement building.  He indicated that they are proposing $32.4 million in capital projects, including the Winslow Levy project and a portion of the broadband project through the ACA grant and then some general funds offset for the code enforcement and the sheriff's regional communication.  He stated that the total budget for FY24 is $251.6 million.  He addressed the increase from FY23, indicating that the majority of the increase is due to either state or federal funding for specific investments in the region for broadband and the Winslow Levy.
Supervisor Seymore thanked staff in the different departments for the work they did on the budget.  He indicated that the Sheriff met with the Supervisor at different times through the process regarding an opportunity they had to get a helicopter from a different county that would help with search and rescue, law enforcement, public safety missions, Navajo County support missions and public relations.  He indicated that it wasn't approved in the budget and requested, after talking with Mr. Vowell and the Sheriff more, that the budget be amended to include the $70,000 in the budget.  He stated that the overall dollar amount wouldn't be changed but that it could be handled through the contingencies.  He further stated that there would be costs associated with the insurance and putting it in a hanger which would be approximately $20,000 and the rest of the costs would be when it was being flown.  He indicated that there are pilots in the Sheriff's Office as well as volunteers.  He indicated that it is long overdue to have the service available for search and rescue.
Supervisor Whitesinger indicated that she would like to know more about where the funds would come from and what the impacts would be.
Mr. Vowell stated as part of the budget that build in contingency funds, that it wouldn't increase the overall budget this year since funds would come from the contingency side and then they would build it into the budget for next year.
Vice-Chairman Whiting talked about concerns with this coming out of a one-time money and the ongoing expenses.  He indicated that he would want to have follow-up conversations but there were other asks from the Sheriff with greater priority than this.
Supervisor Whitesinger talked about the discussion for the priorities of the sheriff during the meetings, indicating that she is unsure where the sheriff currently sits with this request.
Supervisor Seymore stated that it could be reevaluated and looked at but that it's an opportunity so that they wouldn't have to spend millions to purchase a helicopter since they would be able to get it from another county and the cost would just be for the upkeep.  He indicated that it was something they would have liked to get in the budget but that he made the request now so they wouldn't have to go all the way back through the process since the tentative budget had already been approved.  He stated that it could still be done in the final budget, with the amount staying the same since it would come from the contingency and that they could make a condition that the Sheriff come and present at a meeting prior to Board approval.
Mr. Vowell stated that they can approve budget and make an amendment at another time once further discussions are held to determine where the funds would be from.
Chairman Peshlakai addressed the comment made regarding the Board members being approached individually regarding request, indicating that he heard it from staff, but that he was not involved in any meetings. He requested that a meeting be held with all board members to be able to get the same information during budget discussions.
Vice-Chairman Whiting addressed the having the Sheriff present at a later, indicating that he doesn't like the idea of including it in this budget and then next year not being able to accommodate it.
Supervisor Whitesinger expressed her gratitude to the Board and team members that worked to develop the budget.  She addressed sitting with a healthy budget and addressed the different areas that the money will impact to help our communities.

Chairman Peshlakai  opened the meeting for public comments.  There being no comments, the Public Hearing was closed.

Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion to adopt  Resolution Number 21-2023 , approving the proposed final budget for fiscal year 2023-24 for Navajo County, motion seconded by Vice-Chairman Whiting .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Supervisor Seymore wished everyone a fantastic 4 th of July and talked about the blessing of living in the United States.  He indicated that we need to protect those rights and help the youth learn the traditions.  He stated that he hopes that the communities in Navajo County will have wonderful memorial of the 4 th of July and enjoy their freedoms.
Supervisor Whitesinger congratulated Bryan Layton on his appointment and indicated that she looks forward to working with him.  She talked about Balloon Festival and stated that the Apache Sitgreaves extended their appreciation for the help of staff on fire preparedness, indicating that they were grateful to Catrina Jenkins and her team for their support.  She reported on the State Transportation Board approving funds for the repairs to SR260, stating that there were numerous people involved in the conversations and that she appreciates the support from Navajo County.  She advised that they will start of construction in 2026, that it will be on the 5-year plan and that it will be maintained for many years to come.
Supervisor Benally reported on attending the groundbreaking of the multi-purpose building in Chilchinbeto and indicated that they will be adding a museum for a rug display.  She stated that she has been attending the Chapter meetings in her District.
Vice-Chairman Whiting reported on the road trips he has been doing with CSA to visit the other counties and update them with ongoing efforts and legislative session.   He stated that it has been nice to meet with the other counties and see how they are doing things, indicating that he appreciates how the counties work collaboratively.  He advised that on Friday, the congressional staff will be here from Washington, D.C. and that they will be doing a 3-day tour to they can see how the counties work and how they partner with the federal agencies, indicating that on the East coast counties don't work the same due to not having public land, that it is private.  He wished everyone a good 4 th of July, indicating that we need to have more unity and less division, and that we should be uniting around what 4 th of July is meant to be.  He congratulated Bryan Layton on his appointment, stating that he is grateful for all that he has done and that he look forward to working with him in the future.
Supervisor Benally provided an additional report on attending the NDOT meeting, indicating that she learned more about the roads and stated that she appreciates Madhav Mundle for the assistance he has provided.  She congratulated Bryan Layton on his appointment.
Chairman Peshlakai reported on attending chapter meetings in his district, indicating that a majority of them are back to in person, but that they are still able to do them virtual.  He stated that he has been attending the Winslow Indian Health Care 5K series.  He indicated that they have also kicked off the District Spring into Summer 5k walk/run, that they have already had a few events, that they will be doing more over the next month and provided information on the different locations.  He addressed the funding for the Winslow Levy that was approved in the State budget.  He congratulated Bryan Layton on his appointment.

ADJOURN :   11:52 a.m. meeting adjourned.
