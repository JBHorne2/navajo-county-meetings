Presentation regarding SCR 1037 :
Senator Borrelli talked about SCR 1037, indicating that they found, through their studies and other investigations in other states, that the voting systems not as secure as everyone thinks.  He talked about the study done by Alex Halderman on electronic voting systems, indicating that many security vulnerabilities were found.  He stated that the Elections Assistance Commission and the Cybersecurity Infrastructure Agency, Sissa, did a study and examination of the machines in Tennessee where they found the Dominion system and ESNS system a code that they couldn't figure out how it got there, that the manufacturer said that they fixed it but that they weren't able to look and find out if it was fixed.  He talked about Cochise County election for 2022 where the Board voted to do a hand count, that two lawsuits were filed and that there was an injunction to prevent it.  He indicated that this is not about which candidate wins or loses but that it is free and fair elections.  He stated that this is about national security and that anything less than a hand count ballot is a disservice to all those from before us.  He further stated that the machines can be preprogramed, that they had a three-day hearing in the Senate where Clinton Curtis, who wrote the program testified and showed the US Senator how easy the machines can be manipulated to be hacked.  He indicated that the real issue is taxpayer oversight, and that you are not able to have that when there is a third-party vendor running the election.  He talked about different agencies that have had systems hacked even though they have high security.  He stated that he believes we need to go back to paper ballots and hand counts.  He talked about doing a test run where they do a hand count then run it through the tabulator to ensure the system is running correctly. He further talked about cybersecurity and the problems with electronics being hacked.  He stated that the elections belong to the people and that the people have indicated that they are willing to work to do a paper ballot hand count, and talked about a process that could be used to make it logistically possible.
Senator Rogers stated that the Senate Concurrent Resolution is a unique instruction that was voted on by the House and Senate, which is a five-page resolution that outlines all criteria that needs to be met to be able to continue to use machines for election infrastructure.  She talked about the what the SCR entails and indicated that to be sure of what the vote was that are advocating to go back to paper ballots with them being counted in the precinct and then sent to the voting center.  She stated that they have found through testimony and research that the mischief happens at voting center.  She indicated that we have delegated, in some ways, our freedom and sanctity of our vote to machines and a third-party vendor.  She talked about other counties they have visited and the reason that they are presenting to the rural counties, indicating that if we did a hand count of one election we would be leading and setting an example.  She talked about some of the other states that are going back to hand counting.

Supervisor Seymore stated that he has read and seen some of the presentations and documentation.  He indicated that he appreciates them coming and sharing the information.
Senator Rogers indicated that Gila County has is going to study the do ability of this and that they are in discussions with Pinal County for them to look into it.  She requested that they keep an open mind and look into doing this.
Senator Borrelli talked about giving voters peace of mind and indicated that he hopes that they consider doing a hand count paper ballot.

Rich King, 20 County Road 2018, Nutrioso, talked about election integrity.  He talked about doing signature verifications and concerns that he has.
Art Cucuel, 781 S. 7 th Ave., Show Low, talked about signature verifications and issues that he has seen.
Karen Slaton, 4422 Larkspur Rd., Show Low, talked about doing signature verifications.  She addressed the integrity of elections and about standing up for those who have stood up for us.

Consideration and approval of the following items: Receipt of the Annual Report of Collections and Delinquencies in accordance with A.R.S. §42-18002; Settlement of Accounts; and Request for Authorization to Invest Funds Pursuant to A.R.S. §35-327(G) :
Kari Lopez thanked her staff and indicated that without them none of this would be possible and that everything they do helps the county move forward. She reviewed the real property tax roll corrections and collections and the total amount of unpaid taxes as of June 30, 2023, indicating that taxes charged were approximately eighty-seven million and that approximately eighty-five million have been collected with approximately two million remaining in uncollected 2022 taxes.  She talked about the breakdown of the amounts by entities.  Ms. Lopez addressed the county investments and requested authorization to invest and reinvest county school and special district funds as necessary for the 2023/2024 fiscal year.  She stated that as of June 30, 2023, the County has over three hundred and sixty million invested in short term investments and government securities, that interest received was approximately 7.7 million combining saving and government securities for the 2023 fiscal year and that the interest received increased more than fifty percent for the 2023 fiscal year. She addressed the Treasurers Processing Fund, indicating that the anticipated fund revenue for fiscal year 23/24 is estimated to be ten thousand dollars based on the estimated sell of tax lien certificates of one million dollars. She requested that the Board approve receipt of the annual report of collections and delinquencies and settlement of accounts with approval to invest and reinvest funds.
Supervisor Seymore shared his appreciation for a job well done, indicating that this is one of the best reports he's seen in his nineteen years of service. He requested further explanation of the $10,000 for the tax lien certificates.
Ms. Lopez addressed the process of the tax lien sales for the remaining 2022 amounts that have not been paid.  She stated that when they do that sale, they are able to charge a fee and that the $10,000 is what they anticipate they will collect in fees.

SUPERIOR COURT :

a.

Consideration of Conference Technologies, Inc. (CTI) Contract, Proposal Number J23170032, to upgrade Division III Technology :
Ms. Garbini stated that they are requesting approval to proceed with Conference Technologies, Inc. to upgrade the Division Three technology.  She stated that the upgrade will allow the courts to effectively capture the official court record for remote appearances and facilitate the use of digital evidence.  She indicated that the funds have been approved thought the budget.
Supervisor Whitesinger inquired as to how the equipment could improve services.
Ms. Garbini addressed the difficulties with remote appearances during the pandemic and how this equipment would help will remote appearances, attorneys from other counties being able to assist with cases during attorney shortages and being able to do digital evidence during proceedings.  She stated that it will also allow the court record to be recorded due to court reporter shortages, indicating that good equipment is need to be able to capture and keep the record long term.

Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion  to approve Conference Technologies, Inc. (CTI) Contract, Proposal Number J23170032, to upgrade Division III Technology, motion seconded by  Supervisor Seymore .  Motion passed 4-0 with Supervisor Whiting being absent.
