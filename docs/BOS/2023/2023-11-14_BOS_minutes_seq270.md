SHERIFF'S OFFICE :

a.

Consideration and approval of new funds awarded to the Navajo County Sheriff's Office by the Arizona Department of Emergency and Military Affairs :
A presentation was shown. Sheriff David Clouse stated that they are going to be asking for approval of the grant opportunity that the Sheriff's Office has qualified for, indicating that it will supplement the rising costs due to border issues and border crimes that are coming into our communities as well as costs raised in detention, prosecution, and enforcement.  He shared his gratitude to his staff, Kimberly Willis, Finance Director; Bruce Tucker, Chief of Enforcement; Lt. Allen Whipple, Deputy Chief Ernie Garcia; and Chief Deputy Bryan Swanty, for all of the work that they have put into this grant.  He talked about the rise in stats from 2018 to 2022 for Fentanyl and Methamphetamine.  He addressed the increase in calls for service over the last decade, indicating that it's starting to level off, but that they are still analyzing it.  He talked about the different types of crimes, such as crimes against a person, property crimes and violent crimes.  He indicated that they have been watching violent crimes closely, noting that over the last three to four years they have risen and that they all stem to the drug world.  On the detention side Sheriff Clouse talked about the increase in detox cases, indicating that it's a direct correlation to fentanyl and stated that it also increases the medical transports and need for life saving events.  He talked about in rise in costs due to these situations.  He addressed changes that they have made to try and keep drugs from being smuggled into the jail.  He indicated that with all of the increases it makes it financially difficult to work on other projects such as hiring additional staff and buying needed equipment.  He stated that in the 2022 legislative session two bills were introduced, HB2317, which is a border security fund/border technology fund for public safety and HB2862, which is a border security fund for prosecution and imprisonment, and that they were passed and signed into law June 2022.  He talked about the process that they went through to apply for the grant and the amount that would be available.  He stated that for HB2317, the Border Security Fund, they submitted a grant for approximately eight million and that they received an award letter for $4.1 million which can be used for equipment, radios, radio tower installations, MCAT operational overtime and operating expenses.  He talked about how this will not only help the Sheriff's Office but the entire county.  He stated that for HB2862, for the detention side, that will be able to fund one half of the positions that are related to issues outlined, that they will buy equipment needed for overdose situations and that it will assist with inmate housing and inmate medical expenses.  He indicated that between the two grants they were awarded a total of $7.1 million, that it will be for the next five years and that it is a reimbursable grant, that there is no match needed from the county.  He requested approval of the item.
Vice-Chairman Whiting thanked Sheriff Clouse and the Sheriff's Office team for the work that they have done to find ways to do their job and to creatively find funding to support that work.  He indicated that he appreciates that they look for ways to work through difficult situations.
Supervisor Seymore thanked Sheriff Clouse and his team for the information that was provided.  He indicated that it is good to know the stats for what is coming into our communities, how vital it is and how the fallout spills out into our society.  He stated that he appreciates the work, that it has paid off and that this is huge for our county.
Supervisor Whitesinger thanked Sheriff Clouse and his team.  She stated that the service to our citizens through public safety really stands out.  She talked about the impact on the communities with all the changes.  She thanked them for being invested in communities.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to approve new funds awarded to the Navajo County Sheriff's Office by the Arizona Department of Emergency and Military Affairs, motion seconded by  Supervisor Whitesinger .  Motion passed 4-0, with Supervisor Fern Benally being absent.

1.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration of adoption of Resolution Number 41-2023 , approving/denying a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction of a 62' rusticated monopole wireless communication tower and associated facilities on a vacant, 2.50-acre parcel, in the White Mountain Lakes area :
A presentation was shown.  Cody Cooper stated that this Special Use Permit is for a wireless communications facility.  He talked about the area for the proposed tower, the site plan, and the tower height, indicating that they have revised it from the original eighty foot proposed down to sixty-two feet.  He addressed the setbacks, stating that it is compliant with the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance for residentially designated properties.  He stated that the applicants did hold a community meeting on the original proposal on July 27, 2023, and that notice was sent July 8, 2023.  He indicated that with the tower being reduced to sixty-two feet and having a minimum setback of one hundred twenty-four feet the proposal now meets all relevant goals and policies of the Navajo County Comprehensive Plan and meets all provisions of the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance.  He stated that with that staff recommends approval, with the recommended conditions included in the staff report.  He noted that the Planning and Zoning Commission did recommend denial of the original eight-foot proposed tower.
Reg Destree, Representative for Vertical Bridge, addressed the Board regarding the proposed tower.  He talked about Verizon wanting to improve the coverage in this area, indicating that with the sites already built or approved and the proposed sites it would provide excellent coverage for 4G and 5G.  He requested approval of the tower with revised height.
A presentation was shown.  Steven Kennedy, Radio Frequency Engineer Consultant, addressed the Board regarding the reason for carriers to build a site, coverage and capacity, and the coverage objective.  He reviewed the information for the proposed tower, reason for the proposed area, and the distance between the towers in the area.  He talked about the lack of service in the proposed area and the poor quality of service.  He addressed the current coverage for the area.  He talked about ionizing and non-ionizing energy, the differences between the two, the amount of energy transmitted/absorbed from cell phones compared to the amount from the towers, and the two types of limits, general public and occupational.  He addressed about the amount of power at the base of the tower.  He reviewed coverage for the other carriers in the area.

Chairman Peshlakai opened the meeting for public comments.

Kathy Henderson-Essel, 1770 Edwin Place, stated that the White Mountain Lakes citizens will be challenging the validity of the lease.  She addressed concerns about the lease and talked about notices not being properly provided to those within three hundred feet of the tower.
Buzz Essel, 1770 Edwin Place, Show Low, addressed the Board regarding concerns with the lease agreement.  He talked about items that have been ignored or discounted.
Tony Tangalos, 8319 Pinon Dr., Show Low, requested that the Board postpone voting on this item due to the community opposing the tower for health reasons, the studies that they have found and researched and issues with the lease agreement.  He addressed concerns with the lease and indicated that they have hired an attorney.
10:55 a.m. Chairman Peshlakai stepped out of the meeting.
10:58 a.m . Chairman Peshlakai returned to the meeting.

There being no further comments, the public hearing was closed.

Vice-Chairman Whiting inquired of the County Attorney if there are any concerns or if the Board has any authority regarding the lease.
Brad Carlyon stated that we are not a party to the contract/lease and that Navajo County does not need to be concerned with it.  He indicated that they have indicated that they are addressing it with the courts.
Vice-Chairman Whiting inquired who has authority over the health concerns that the citizens have addressed.
Mr. Cooper stated that the Telecommunications Act of 1996 has indicated that the FCC is responsible for concerns with health and looking at the environmental effects, that they are the ones that do the studies and do the approvals as far as what is or isn't allowable.  He indicated that it is outside of scope of staff or local/state jurisdictions.
Vice-Chairman Whiting requested that Melissa Buckley address what proper notification is.
Melissa Buckley stated that the Department is required to post it in the newspaper for two weeks prior to the first hearing with the Planning and Zoning Commission and then again prior to the Board of Supervisors meeting, that they post signs on the property and that they notify those living within three hundred feet of the area.
Vice-Chairman Whiting inquired about the options that are available for citizens to attend the meetings if they are not able to appear in person.
Ms. Buckley stated that currently community members are able to appear via Zoom either by video or by phone, that they don't have to have prior approval and that the information is posted on the agenda on Thursday or Friday prior to the Board of Supervisors Meeting.

Mr. Cooper stated that due to the Board having tabled this item for today's meeting there was no requirement to readvertise legally.  He stated that they did send out notifications regardless so that the public could be informed.  He indicated that the details were correct but that there was an error regarding there being limited seating.  He further indicated that when it was brought to their attention, they have corrected the error.  He stated that they follow through with all procedures that they have according to the zoning ordinance and state law.
Supervisor Seymore inquired as to the concerns with this affecting property values.
Mr. Cooper stated that property values are outside of the scope for Planning and Development staff.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration of the adoption of  Resolution Number 45-2023 , approving/denying the usage of the existing single-family residence as an institutional residence with a maximum residency of seven clients and two staff members in the Lakeside area :
A presentation was shown. Sondra Starke stated that this is a Special Use Permit for the Step-In Intensive Transitional Living Home.  She indicated that it is a single-family residence with an addition that would be used as a transitional living home.  She addressed the lot size, the current zoning, the location, and the character of the neighborhood for the project.  She provided some background regarding the LLC being formed, the purpose of the project and indicated that the staff will be trained professionals and will maintain all state licensing requirements and regulations.  She advised that the Planning and Zoning Commission recommended approval at their October 19, 2023, meeting.  Ms. Starke talked about the site plan, the policies and house rules that will be implemented, the planned fencing around the property and the proposed landscaping.  She stated that the item has been properly noticed and posted, that the proposal has been found to be in compliance with the zoning ordinance and meets several goals with the Comprehensive Plan.  She indicated that as of November 7, 2023, staff received five public comments in support of the project and one letter is opposition, as well as some calls for information about the project.  She stated that staff recommends approval subject to conditions included in the staff report.
