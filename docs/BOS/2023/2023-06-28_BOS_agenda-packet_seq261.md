Board of Supervisors Special 1. a. 1.        
Meeting Date: 06/28/2023  
Title: Declaration of Emergency due to extreme fire danger to enact Stage 1 Fire
Restrictions
Submitted By: Catrina Jenkins, EMP Director
Department: NC Public Health Services District Division: Emergency Management
Presented By: Catrina Jenkins
Motion before the Board:
Consideration of a Declaration of Emergency due to extreme fire danger and to enact Section 6
of the Navajo County Ordinance 01-19 Emergency Fire Restrictions, to enter into Stage 1 fire
restrictions.
Background:
The White Mountain Fire Coordinating Group met Monday, June 26th to discuss the current
conditions and the possible need to enter into fire restrictions. The WMFCG consists of Apache
and Navajo County Emergency Management, Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests, Arizona
State Department of Forestry and Fire Management, Bureau of Land Management, Bureau of
Indian Affairs - Ft. Apache Agency, Northeast Arizona Fire Chiefs Association and Northeast
Arizona Police Association.  The primary goal of the White Mountain Fire Coordinating Group
is to create a unified approach to fire restrictions as well as information and education. The
entire region coordinates going into and moving out of restrictions in a unified manner based on
scientific data and updates provided by subject-matter experts. 
In addition to the very dry conditions we will be seeing moderate to high Haines weather. The
Haines index measures the potential for fires to become larger and plume dominated. After
heavy discussion of unfavorable weather forecasts, firefighting resource availability, and
current forest conditions (including the high energy release components values and fire
behavior), it was voted unanimously to enter Stage 1 fire restrictions.  There are currently ample
resources available, however as more fires are started these resources will be called upon to help
in other regions. The current state of our land is extremely dry, and little to no relative humidity
recovery values at night. There have already been multiple human caused fires, including over
20 abandoned campfires during a Red Flag Warning. NCEM reminds all of our citizens and
visitors that it is NEVER permissible to leave a fire unattended or abandoned without first
completely extinguishing the fire until it is cold to touch. If sparks or embers were to ignite a
fire, the potential wildfire could be devastating to our communities. 
 
Recommendation: 
Staff recommends approval.

Declaration of Emergency 
 
 
WHEREAS, the Navajo County Board of Supervisors is authorized by ARS § 26 -311 and to 
declare an emergency exists due to wildfire which endangers life or property within the city,  or 
the unincorporated areas of the county; and 
 
WHEREAS , the Navajo County Emergency Manager is recommending enacting Emergency Fire 
Restrictions as set forth under Navajo County Ordinance 01 -19 Section 6 which outlines 
Emergency Fire Restrictions for Navajo County: and 
 
WHEREAS, Navajo County has a legal and ethical obligation to protect the health, safety and 
welfare of the citizens of Navajo County; and 
 
WHEREAS, the people, lands, infrastructure and economic activities within the boundaries of 
Navajo County are under imminent threat from wildfire due to strong winds, relatively high 
temperatures and extremely dry conditions, and; 
 
WHEREAS, the threat of such fires serves as a detriment of the County’s residents’ health, safety, 
welfare and economic well-being; 
 
NOW, THEREFORE the Navajo County Board of Supervisors issues this Declaration of 
Emergency in Navajo County and the Chairman is hereby authorized to take all further 
actions as may be necessary or appropriate to implement this declaration pursuant to ARS 
§ 26-311. 
 
PASSED, ADOPTED AND APPROVED at Holbrook, Arizona, on June 28, 2023.
