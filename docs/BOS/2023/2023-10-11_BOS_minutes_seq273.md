Board of Supervisor's Roles & Responsibilities :
Bryan Layton talked about the organizational chart that was created and will be put on the website.  He acknowledged everyone who has helped through the transition of him taking over as County Manager.
A presentation was shown.  Rochelle Lacapa talked about roles and responsibilities going into an election year and campaign season, including public officials vs. private citizens.  She indicated that those running, that hold a public office, need to be more mindful of what they are doing while campaigning.  She stated that public resources can't be used for campaigning, including facilities, equipment, personnel, or supplies.  She talked about how defining when they are on campaign time versus county time, helps maintain the trust of the public and transparency.  She stated that when going through the statute, campaign finance law, and the policies and procedures, there's a gap in information around the topic. She has reached out to CSA and other counties to see if there was anything that clearly defines ethics or the law when campaigning if you're an elected officer.  She indicated that our policies manual covers political activities for employees but that there is a disclaimer that elected officials are excluded.  She talked about potentially changing that if they wanted it to include the Elected Officials.  She indicated that a helpful tool for staff would be to clarify on their calendar when they are taking personal time for campaigning.  She indicated that the House Ethics Committee uses a guidebook for what elected officials can and can't do and talked about how they decipher their time on their calendars.
She talked about how some efficiencies can be built into the policies and procedures to help with the gap of information, including the Chair's agenda for how meetings are run and the Board policies, that were adopted in 2009 which outline what the Boards roles are.  She further talked about whether there is a need to revise the Board's policies, a new Code of Ethics, revise the personal policies manual or if there are other options the Board would like to establish.  She indicated that this could establish a guide for future supervisors coming in, to help them with how the county operates and what their role is within the county.  She requested feedback on if there is a need for these things to be updated and who should work on the updates.
Supervisor Whiting stated that he likes the idea of staff working on recommendations.  He indicated that it would create an expectation for the Board around actions and communication when engaging with the public and each other.  He further indicated that it would be a good way of onboarding newly elected officials and/or a yearly refresher.
Supervisor Seymore stated that this is something we should have in place, that it doesn't need to be recreated, but that we should explore what works well in other counties when restructuring it.
A discussion was held regarding what would be included in the board policies, including the vetting process for requests from the public for placement of items on the agenda; items being on the consent agenda versus the regular agenda; formalities for the meeting including time allotted for call to the public or public comments on an item, where in the agenda comments should be made and if time should be yielded and not allowing jump drives from outside of the county to be used on county computers.
Ms. Lacapa thanked them for the conversation, indicating that it is helpful for staff to know what to incorporate into the board policies when updating and provides clarity for staff and the public.
Supervisor Seymore talked about the Board currently being elected on the same cycle and possibly looking at staggard terms in the future.
Supervisor Benally requested a copy of guidelines for campaigning for elected officials.
A discussion was held regarding being ethical while campaigning as an Elected Official.

c.

Public Works Update :
A presentation was shown. Madhav Mundle highlighted some of the major accomplishments from the past year, including purchase of equipment, paving and gravel projects, adoption of the new building permit fee schedule and being able to reduce building permit approval times.  He stated that staff is working with a vendor to create a portal so that applications can be submitted online.
Mr. Mundle answered questions regarding the reduced time for permitting.
Supervisor Seymore addressed the time reduction and indicated that the department has done a great job.
Mr. Mundle continued to address major accomplishments, including, getting approval for a hearing officer for code compliance and approval of a new ordinance for wireless cell towers.  He talked about the accomplishment of receiving funding through HURF Exchange, AZ SMART, the Highway Safety Improvement Program, Off System Bridge and CDBG funding.
Supervisor Benally inquired which of the funds could be utilized on tribal land.
Mr. Mundle stated that AZ SMART and Off System Bridge are not available on tribal lands unless they apply for it on their own and that HURF Exchange is exclusively for Navajo County facilities.
Supervisor Benally inquired what the end of county-maintenance means, indicating that it's her understanding that the county doesn't own any roads.
A discussion was held regarding county roads versus roads on tribal land, the funding for roadways and what roads can be maintained.
Supervisor Whitesinger talked about the partnerships that are needed for requests to help with road maintenance.
Supervisor Benally talked about how she has requested assistance on tribal roads and has not received the assistance she requested.
Supervisor Whiting indicated that this sounds like a district specific issue that needs to be worked through with Public Works.  He talked about the need to have a meeting with tribal leaders regarding partnerships.
Mr. Mundle continued to address the accomplishments in Public Works, such as the 5-year capital improvement projects and the pavement management program.  He talked about road health checks, indicating that the pavement management program is only for paved roads, that it is needed, that it's a pilot program to see if it will work and he inquired if the program should continue if it is cost effective and makes economic sense and which roads that it should be used on.  He addressed the cost for maintaining the roads.
Supervisor Whitesinger inquired about cost effectiveness.
Mr. Mundle indicated that they are working on that, that the first program had a lot of inefficiencies, that the more work done the better it will be and that they plan to do it on low volume and low speed roads.
A discussion was held on the roads and the costs for maintenance.
Supervisor Seymore stated that it's great to know the conditions of the roads and indicated that it's an asset to the county.
Mr. Mundle talked about challenges and how to proceed.
Mr. Layton talked about how the experience and connections Mr. Mundle has with ADOT has brought in new things for Navajo County and thanked Mr. Mundle.
Mr. Mundle stated that Navajo County is on the ADOT donors list for assistance. He talked about some topics for future discussions on a new comprehensive plan, a roadmap for future growth, a new engineering design and construction standards that provides consistency, direction, and guidance for developers, contractors, and residents.

Update on ARPA Projects :
A presentation was shown.  Ken Maruyama talked about ARPA funding that was provided and that Navajo County received, the board approval of the county projects and the grant agreement process.  He thanked everyone who has worked to help make this process/project successful.
Bryan Layton talked about looking at ways to help communicate the value that the county brings to the communities.  A video was shown.  He talked about working with partners to highlight the region by using a professional company to get the information out.
Vice-Chairman Whiting talked about the communities not understanding all the services that the county provides and how this approach could help rather than the traditional presentations.
Supervisor Seymore stated that this is very promising on how to get information out to the communities.
Mr. Layton talked about the professionalism of the video company, indicating that this company is very workable with how things are done and that it's a local company in snowflake.
Mr. Maruyama talked about some of the different projects that have been completed, the Kayenta Fire Department radios, the City of Show Low splash pad, the Rim Country Senior Center, the White Mountain Coalition Against Homelessness, Walking Down Ranch Veterans Center, and the Winslow Public Library.  He reviewed the status of the different projects that were approved, indicating some of the projects are still in progress.  He indicated that he has been staying in contact with the different entities to make sure the projects get completed in the required time and to help in any way needed.
Mr. Evans provided an update on the county projects.  He advised that the design is complete for the broadband project, that they are in the permitting phase, and he talked about the work being done on the permitting phase.  He stated that today an RFQ goes out for BEAD program and that Navajo County put out an RFQ to solicit for a consultant or professional to help with the challenge process, which will happen in early 2024.
Supervisor Seymore talked about what the process is for BEAD program.
Mr. Evans advised that all the materials have been ordered and approximately 70% are on site for the broadband project.  He talked about the Taylor Code Enforcement building progress, and the County complex upgrade indicating that the design is complete and that they will most likely get the project out to bid this month but that nothing has changed on the project as far as scope.  He indicated that the Treasurer's Office remote pay project had some issues and that they may have to look at other options due to the challenges.
Mr. Maruyama reviewed the status of the remaining Navajo County projects.
Mr. Layton thanked Mr. Maruyama for the work he has done with the APRA projects.  He talked about a proposal idea being able to have community partnerships on capital fund projects in unincorporated areas, indicating that it would not be as a sole source funder, but that the County could match or partner, and that they would have a vetting process similar to the ARPA process.
Mr. Maruyama talked about the AG SMART fund being similar to this proposal.
Supervisor Seymore stated that he has some interest, not only for his district but for the county as a whole.  He talked about some of the areas that would benefit from this opportunity, indicating that it would help the communities that aren't able to incorporate at this point.
Vice-Chairman Whiting indicated that he likes the concept but would like to see funding source information.
Supervisor Benally indicated that the tribal communities should be considered for some of these opportunities as well to help with their needs.
Mr. Layton stated that it could be something to explore, that they want to make it meaningful but also make sure that funding is available for it.

e.

Financial Sustainability and Outlook :
Jayson Vowell stated the county is in a good solid financial position considering where it was at 5 years ago.  He reviewed the FY23 budgeted revenues vs. actual revenues and budgeted expenditures vs. actual expenditures.  He talked about the normal cost of doing business, including market fluctuations, professional service increases, changes to revenue streams/cost share, capital outlay and labor.  He talked about the differences that have affected the budget from 2019 to current for the expenditures and revenues, indicating that going forward they would like to see it start to flatten out and be more consistent.  He talked about property taxes and keeping the rate flat.  He spoke about where the revenues come from, including sales tax, county sales tax and property tax plus some federal state money from intergovernmental agreements.  He talked about the future focus for organizational maintenance, including a pay plan, COLA and performance pay; new hires; increase to capital outlay, including investments in technology, building repairs and modernization, building replacement/expansion and vehicles and equipment; and subscriptions vs. a one-time purchase.
Supervisor Seymore talked about being able to retain the employees that we have by keeping up with performance pay and salaries.  He addressed the need to do upgrades due to the buildings being outdated and for having subscriptions versus one-time purchases for equipment.  He addressed budgeting versus using the reserves for different needs.
Supervisor Whitesinger talked about investments that have been made over the past years and the position that we are sitting in now compared to in the past, indicating that we need to be prepared for the continued cost increase for everything.
Mr. Vowell talked about the steps taken to make salaries competitive, indicating that they don't want to have to do another market upgrade after the work put in with the adjustments they have made.  He indicated, from a budget perspective, each department needs to know that they will be paying more in the future for the different tools that they use.
Supervisor Whitesinger indicated that they don't want to be put in a position of having to look at layoffs of cutting back, but that they want to be able to continue to provide the services that are needed.
Mr. Layton talked about the work that has been done to make the pay competitive, investing in the employees, and about the cultural environment being made to help employees want to stay.  He addressed the things that they want to do to help employees show that they are valued and recognized.
Mr. Vowell indicated that they will be looking at FY25 soon and indicated that when going into the next budget cycle they want to get feedback on the process and determine if they should keep as is or make changes.
A discussion was held about some possible changes that could be made such as getting more input from the departments on what they are asking.
Mr. Vowell thanked the Board, indicating that he appreciates their guidance and the tone that they set for the organization.

f.

Public Health Service Delivery & County Facility Planning :
Mr. Layton stated that when looking at renovations and facility upgrades, they were looking to increase, promote and improve access to the county as well as make it safe and secure for both employees and customers in an affordable way.  He reviewed the current county projects, such as customer access, the restrooms, a breakroom, and a training room, the repair to the sewer pipes, the modernization of the superior courtrooms for technology and furniture, the upgrade to the county internet, the organization of records retention, the upgrade to the fire alarm, offering CPR/First Aid classes to all employees, emergency signage, adding shade sails to the outdoor areas for breaks, repurposing the basketball court and put up shade pavilions, outdoor picnic seating and paint the markings for pickleball courts.  He talked about some proposed projects that they are looking at such as solar protection for vehicles/equipment, he talked about the benefits for the solar covered parking; the proposal for the branding/color options for the seal and the proposed monument signage.
A discussion was held regarding the proposed monuments and the different options.
Mr. Layton talked about turning the area into a place where people can stop and take pictures.  He talked about the next steps for the proposed projects.  He addressed long term needs for the county, including necessary expansions at the Sheriff's Office and Public Health; the necessity for creating another courtroom; the need for professional office space, indicating that it is currently limited; additional public access in the board chamber, due to it being limited; and the difficult and confusing customer experiences.  He stated that looking down the road we are not set up to be able to meet the needs and services needed for customers.
Supervisor Benally inquired if there is space to be able to expand at the complex to build additional offices.
Mr. Layton talked about the area that could possibly be used for expansion.
Janelle Linn stated that the number one responsibility of public health is to make sure that they have an appropriate infrastructure which allows them to keep the communities safe, healthy, protected from environmental hazards and against emergencies.  She stated that their primary jurisdiction is on non-tribal lands but that they do support and provide aid to the tribal health departments when asked.  She talked about different opportunities that they have had for partnering.  She addressed some of the impacts to the service model over the past few years, such as North Country opening in Winslow, the economic recession, AZ SB1070 immigration laws, the Affordable Care Act and Vaccine for Children funding cuts.  She talked about the current services offered in public health and the trend for patients coming into the offices.  She further talked about opportunities with ARPA, partnerships, fluid service model and an expansion for major grant opportunities.
Mr. Layton talked about the operations in South County and reviewed the need and demand for the expansion in Public Health.
A discussion was held on different ways to expand the South County complex if needed.
