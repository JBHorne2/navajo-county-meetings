DEVELOPMENT SERVICES : Dave Ashton, Director: Waiver of building fees for the construction of the 
Girls & Boys Club APN #209-03-005: Mr. Ashton explained that the Boys & Girls Clubs of Metropolitan 
Phoenix (an Arizona non-profit corporation) had their retreat burn down last year in the Rodeo-Chediski 
Fire.  They are now developing plans to re-build the retreat on its present site. The organization has many 
volunteers involved in this project, and they are requesting a waiver of the building permit fees ($2863) for 
this proposed 6100 sq.ft. building and 2000 sq.ft. covered patio. Mr. Ashton said his staff will be required 
to do various inspections during the construction of this building, and with the budget constraints the 
county is under, he cannot justify the waiver of the fees. This could set a precedent wherein other such 
charities, churches, non-profit organizations, etc., could also submit requests to waive fees. For these 
reasons, staff recommends that the fees not be waived. Mr. Shumway made a motion to deny request for 
waiver of building fees for Girls & Boys Club; motion seconded by Mr. B rownlow; vote unanimously 
carried.

BOARD OF SUPERVISORS : 1) Meeting with Major Tax Payers - Public at Large: Present for the 
meeting were representatives of ATRA (Arizona Tax Research Association); El Paso Gas; APS; and 
CSA (County Supervisors Association).  Interim County Manager Dennis Miller presented the tentative 
budget. He advised that all departments took a required 15% reduction in their budgets. This resulted in a 
reduction in the work force, approximately 15 people. This was made up by a combination of positions 
being left vacant and actual lay-offs. (Assessor Frank Turley noted that his department lost 2.5 positions; 
County Attorney Mel Bowers lost 3 positions). To offset some of these “harsh” things that happened, the 
Board did award a 3% COLA raise to, in part, handle some of the increases, ie., retirement fund 
contributions and health care premiums. The Board also authorized the Count y to pay 50% of the 
increase in the dependent medical premium. The Board allowed a 90 reprieve for those who are to be 
laid off to allow them to find other positions. The entire package will cost the county slightly less than 
$500,000. Mr. Miller said we fared fairly well with the Legislature due to the efforts of our representatives 
and senators. The issue of the tax lawsuit was decided, and the $865,578 we owe will be paid off this 
year. This will result in the tax rate being reduced from about $0.69 to $0.47. Mr. Miller noted that although 
we do have contingency funds budgeted this year, we need to be very careful in spending those funds. 
The departments have been cut to the bare bones already, and there are items sitting out there we have 
to consider. We have runaway health costs, and are grossly underfunded in our health plan. We are also 
looking at a possible $1 million for the election changes.  
 
Mr. Miller advised that we have a total General Fund budget of $24,479,435, which is up over a million 
dollars from last year. The total of all budgets, including grants, is $61,942,867. This will put us within our

PUBLIC WORKS : Bill Cox, Director:  1) Amend Contract Bid #B03-04291, Hot Asphaltic Concrete 
installation of pavement fabric: Mr. Cox explained that they have used the pavement fabric on several 
other projects in the past and have been quite pleased with the results. He said he has done a cost 
benefit analysis on  using the fabric versus pulverizing and liming the base. The cost savings of using the 
fabric has saved more than $200,000 on past projects. The cost of the amendment to the contract will be 
$1.00 per square yard (per Combs Construction and Hatch Construction, the primary contractors for this 
contract). Mr. Thompson made a motion to approve amendment to Contract #B03-04291, Hot Asphaltic 
Concrete installation of pavement fabric as presented; motion seconded by Mr. Brownlow; vote 
unanimously carried.  
 
2) Approval of Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA) with Tow n of Pinetop -Lakeside and the White 
Mountain Apache Tribe concerning Arizona TEA-21 Transportation Enhancement Program to establish a 
multi-purpose trail along Highway 260 from Pinelake Road to Hon-Dah Casino: Tom Thomas, Pinetop-
Lakeside Street Superintendent, explained that this request was originally brought to the Board two years 
ago. It is to allow Pinetop-Lakeside to submit, on behalf of the Board and the White Mountain Apache 
Tribe, an application for the TEA-21 Enhancement Program. The application is for a trail which would 
lead from the town of Pinetop -Lakeside through the county and on to the White Mountain Apache 
Reservation. This is a project that will help unify the two communities. Mr. Thomas noted that, in light of 
recent events, the project will be renamed the “Rick Lupe Trail of Heros”. The Tribe is in full agreement 
with the project, and the BIA will be doing the maintenance on the trail. Mr. Brownlow  made a motion to 
approve (IGA) with Town of Pinetop-Lakeside and the White Mountain Apache Tribe re: Arizona TEA-21 
Transportation Enhancement Program for a multi-purpose trail along Highway 260 from Pinelake Road to 
Hon-Dah Casino, as presented; motion seconded by Mr.  Deal; vote unanimously carried.  
 
NAVAJO NATION/JEDDITO CHAPTER: Funding assistance from District II Special Project Funds to 
address water shortage emergency: Mr. Thompson advised that Jeddito is experiencing a severe water 
shortage. Water is now being hauled by the Navajo Tribal Utility Authority from Ganado to Jeddito. They 
are asking for assistance from Navajo Nation President’s office, Department of Emergency 
Management, the counties and the state. Mr. Thompson advised that the requested funding, at this time, 
would likely be used for a storage tank and fuel for the trucks hauling the water. The funding will be more 
specifically addressed when the contributions from the other governmental entities have been received. 
Mr. Thompson  made a motion to approve $500 from District II Special Project Funds to address water 
shortage emergency, as presented; motion seconded by Mr.  Deal; vote unanimously carried.

BOARD BUSINESS: Mr. Brownlow, along with Sheriff Butler, Emergency Services Director Paul Jaster 
and Cooperative Extension Agent Steve Campbell provided an update on the Kinishba fire. As of this 
morning, 22,550 acres have burned and the fire is 65% contained. The fire was caused by lightning and 
the estimated cost to fight the fire so far is $4.9 million. Coconino and Apache Counties, as well as the 
National Guard, helped with the evacuation of the Whiteriver area. They were also ready in case it 
became necessary to evacuate HonDah, McNary and Pinetop -Lakeside. Should this have been 
necessary, evacuation shelters were established in Snowflake, with Holbrook ready if needed. The 
resource management and procurement procedures in the EOC (Emergency Operations Center) went 
much better this year. It was noted that many departments, including the Sheriff’s office, Public Works, 
Finance, Emergency Services, Health District and the amateur HAM radio operators (RACES) helped 
with the emergency. The fire will be turned over to the BIA tomorrow and it is estimated that the fire will be 
fully contained within a few days. Mr. Campbell presented information regarding treated areas in the 
forest. He noted that there are grants out there to help us get the treatments done which will mitigate the 
damage a fire can cause. Mr. Brownlow added that he and Mr. Shumway met with Senator Jon Kyl on 
Saturday. There was discussion regarding forest treatments which have been done and it was noted that 
these treatments must be done on landscaped areas. The communities north of the reservation need to 
do more; they are at risk of catastrophic fire. We need to move forward with thinning and other projects; 
the grants are out there. Sheriff Butler reported that the employees of Navajo County really pulled together 
during the fire, and he was very proud of everyone. The public needs to know and understand that we 
were prepared for this emergency. Thanks also go to all of the citizen volunteers who did whatever was 
asked of them. Mr. Brownlow presented a Certificate of Appreciation from the White Mountain Apache 
Tribe to the Board in recognition of its support to the family of Rick Lupe. He added that the very area 
where Firefighter Lupe was working with the fuel treatment was what stopped the fire on the southwest 
side.
