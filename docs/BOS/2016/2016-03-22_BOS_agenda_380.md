BOARD OF SUPERVISORS : Presentation of the Volunteer Lawyers Program – Family Law Clinic : 
Supervisor Williams introduced Emery La Barge and spoke about the great job she has done for the Volunteer 
Lawyers Program.  Emery La Barge spoke of her experience  as an attorney and professional associations.  She 
indicated that at least 30% of the Navajo County population is at or below poverty level and 60% of the people 
age 16 and above are unemployed, which means that most of those people do not have access to the Court system.  
She stated that up until recently for Navajo County, the self -service that was offered to the public was to hand 
them a packet and tell them to fill it out.  She indicated that approximately 15 to 16 months ago, she, in conjunction 
with Southern Arizona Legal Aid, instituted a Volunteer Lawyer’s Program, which is open to everybody in the 
County at no cost to help people go through forms, fill them out, and take them to the Clerk’s Office to get papers 
filed.  She indicated that Southern Arizona Legal Aid funded the pilot program, which ended last July.  She 
indicated that the Superior Court judges are in support of the Volunteer Lawyers Program and that she has had 
no difficulties getting attorneys in the area to volunteer.  She indicated that she has submitted a funding request 
and that Navajo County would be the first county in the state to help fund a legal aid program.  Mike Sample , 
Docket Clerk Supervisor, Clerk’s Office,  spoke about the packets which are handed to pro se litigants  and that 
the Clerk’s Office cannot give legal advice and  can only hand them the packet and explain the time constraints. 
He stated that the Clerk’s office fully supports the Volunteer Lawyers Program.  Ms. La Barge provided packets 
describing how many people have been helped through the program, statistics regarding participant’s satisfaction, 
and a copy of an article about the program.  She stated that the clinic is an advice-only clinic, but there are three 
volunteers who follow up with some of the clients.  Kristopher Carter, Southern Arizona Legal Aid, appearing 
via Skype, spoke about the summary sheet which sets forth the benefits to the community.  He stated that 80% of 
those who have participated in the program indicate that they have received a substantial benefit in their case, and 
100% indicate that they received some benefit from the services.  He stated that about 80% of domestic relations 
cases involve at least one party who is self -represented and that the Volunteer Lawyers Program is a g reat 
opportunity, a great service that is worthy of consideration for funding.  Supervisor Williams inquired whether 
the program ties into other counties.  Ms. La Barge indicated that they have seen several clients from Apache 
County and one client from Maricopa County  and that Apache County has pledged some funds and will set up 
teleconferences so people from Apache County can appear at the clinics via Skype.  She invited the Supervisors 
to speak to the Clerk’s Office about the program and to come to one of the clinics.  Supervisor Williams spoke 
about how overwhelming being handed a packet would be to a pro se litigant and expressed support of the program 
and tipped his hat to Ms. La Barge and to other attorneys who have volunteered.  Supervisor Williams stated 
that he will contribute what he can and will encourage his fellow supervisors to contribute if they feel they can.  
Supervisor Thompson thanked Ms. La Barge for her service and spoke about how difficult it is for those who 
cannot afford an attorney to get something thr ough the legal system, some of whom cannot speak English .  
Supervisor Whiting thanked Ms. La Barge for the work the Program does.
