BOARD OF SUPERVISORS:  Presentation regarding  USDOJ initiative for In dian Country : Supervisor 
Thompson introduced Hope MacDonald Lone Tree, the Tribal Relations Advisory at the U.S. Attorney’s Office, 
who gave a brief presentation regarding United States Department of Justice initiative for Indian Country.  Ms. 
Lone Tree indicated that on this day, during World War II, the flag was raised on Iwo Jima and the Navajo 
communications that were flawlessly sent during that time. She spoke about a number of initiatives , including 
sexual assault resp onse, violence against women  and Reentry. She thanked Shanda Breed , Chief Probation 
Officer, for joining the United States Department of Justice on the Reentry initiative, specifically, on the Navajo 
Nation. She spoke about the number one reason for the Reentry initiative is public safety. She addressed the 
information in the packets that were handed out to each Board member . She indicated that the Reentry within 
Indian Country is proceeding, and she indicated that she is really excited about the work that is being done. Shanda 
Breed, Chief Probation Officer, Navajo County Probation Department, spoke about the partnership with the 
USDOJ and Community Corrections  and the work being done in Navajo County. She spoke about a gap in 
Reentry when it comes to those offenders who are not on probation or parole who are going back into the 
community with no supervision or services. She further spoke about the Navajo Count y Criminal Justice 
Collaborative Committee.  Ms. McDonald Lone Tree spoke about initiatives going on right now trying to help 
individuals reintegrate and be successful  and about the work with three federal judges trying to push the effort 
with all of the federal agencies . She asked that she be invited back for a possible work session to discuss this 
information further. Vice Chair man Thompson made comments  regarding Code Talkers and  the needs of 
veterans. Supervisor Williams spoke about a story about a prison warden and the efforts he made to rehabilitate 
his prisoners. He thanked Ms. McDonald Lone Tree for her efforts.  Supervisor Whitesinger had some questions 
about recidivism, which Ms. McDonald Lone Tree addressed. She indicated that the halfway houses are all in 
Phoenix and Tucson in Arizona, yet, the majority of the individuals returning home are in Northern Arizona. She 
spoke about a pilot project in the Concho area  which began late last year . She talked about the work with the 
tribal governments to transition the individuals back to tribal land. Vice Chairman Thompson made comments 
about an activity coming up on April 1 for Navajo Nation Day. Ms. McDonald Lone Tree thanked the Board for 
inviting her here today.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE:  Report from Board members, County Attorney and County Mana ger: 
Supervisor Whitesinger reported on the National Board for Youth Conservation Legacy regarding providing 
core programs.  She spoke about the strategic plan that they are currently working on  with regard to measuring 
how students leave the program, including their stewardship of land and community and how to get young people 
to participate in these conversations. She reported on the County Supervisors’ Association Board meeting.  She 
reported on her meeting with the White Mountain Apache Tribal Council introducing the new voting equipment 
and the work Sunrise is doing.  She also reported on the Sunrise Board of Directors meeting anticipating to have 
the zip line open for Memorial Day. There wil l be a contest on what to name the zip line open to the entire 
community. Check with Supervisor Whitesinger for more information. She reported on the First Things First 
meeting and the opening of a facility in Cibecue  for early childcare services. Supervisor Williams reported on 
the Pinedale Clay Springs Library in Heber-Overgaard area and expressed his appreciation for the volunteers who 
make the libraries run.  He reported on his meeting with the leaders in the Heber-Overgaard community and with 
the Chambers of Commerce at Tall Timbers Park and thanked Mr. Bess for being there. He attended the Heber-
Overgaard Chamber meeting and the Small Counties Dinner at CSA , along with some legislators. He spoke of 
the bill for JTED funding which the Governor signed. He spoke of the good relationship Navajo County has with 
fellow supervisors and legislators to make that happen.  He spent a day touring Forest Energy and spent time with 
leaders from the City of Show Low addressing economic development and also healthy for est and natural 
resources.  He reported on a meeting with Regional Forester with Chairman Whiting and Paul Watson regarding 
ways to open up the forest more to industry. He reported that his son, Beau, who ran for Student Body President, 
did get elected.  He reported that Kevin Brackney, former Superintendent of Show Low School District,  passed 
away.  He asked that the Board send a sympathy letter  to Mr.Brackney’s wife . Vice Chairman Thompson 
reported on the passing of former Navajo County Supervisor , Pete Shumway.  He spoke of Mr. Shumway’s life 
and his work within Navajo County. He further reported on school bus routes and the bill going through the state 
legislature for funding for that program to allow children to get to school . He further spoke of the fatalities and 
the need to fix those roads.
