d. Update on Cedar Fire: Adam Wolfe gave an update on the Cedar Fire, which is 25% still active and has burned 
45,977 acres. He played a video taken by some of the Hot Shots on the scene.  He stated that as of this time, 
the Cedar Fire is the largest fire in the United States for 2016, with nearly 1,000 personnel and 51 engines 
battling the fire at its peak . He indicated that the Cedar Fire is 1/10th of the Rodeo-Chediski Fire. He stated 
that the Southwest Area Type One Incident Management Team was brought in and did a phenomenal job.  He 
presented a Prezi which illustrated how the fire progressed.  He stated that when the monsoon rains come, 
there will be a threat of flooding  in the area .  He expressed how Navajo County h ad a great presence in 
response to the Cedar Fire, and Governor Ducey came to the White Mountains for a briefing. He spoke about 
how Navajo County took charge of the communications during the fire.  Mr. Wolfe thanked all those 
employees and agencies that played a part during the Cedar Fire. He talked about the shelters and evacuations 
places which were set up in Round Valley, Cibecue, Show Low, Snowflake and Holbrook. He spoke about 
the reach of social media during the fire and that the total amount of people reached were 500,000 by Facebook 
and over one million by Twitter and that retweets went international. He spoke about the tremendous 
partnerships that Navajo County has and thank ed all of those communities for their support. Supervisor 
Thompson commended Mr. Wolfe on his work with the communications during the Cedar Fire emergency 
situation.  He expressed appreciation to Mary Springer and Catrina Jenkins, the Sheriff’s Department, and all 
those from the County.   He also thanked the public for offering s upport and prayers. Supervisor Williams 
thanked Mr. Wolfe, the team at Navajo County, the Sheriff’s Office, and the communities for their continued 
efforts and support during this fire.  He spoke about how neighbor reached out to neighbor during the pre -
evacuation and was reminded of why he and his family feel blessed to live in this area.  He spoke about how 
the work of the White Mountain Apache Tribe in forest restoration played a role in the fire not spreading more 
than it did.  Supervisor Whitesinger  expressed her appreciation and indicated that this was a great 
opportunity to grow in our partnership with the White Mountain Apache Tribe.  She stated that we have 
learned that we are not  two separate communities, but one community.  She reite rated the words of the 
Chairman: “We live here together, so we should fight here together,” which resonated with many people 
across the community.  She spoke of the stewardship of the White Mountain Apache Tribe over their land in 
mitigating potential fires.  She expressed gratitude for Mr. Wolfe in being the face  of Navajo County, the 
Sheriff’s Department, the Emergency Management team, and all those who participated.  Supervisor 
Whitesinger spoke about the leadership and community efforts which came together which helped to remind 
us of what a great community and a great county  in which we live. Supervisor Williams thanked the local 
media. County Manager Jayne expressed his appreciation to Mr. Wolfe, the Emergency Management Team, 
and the Sheriff’s Office for their support, which speaks of the partnership throughout the County organization. 
He spoke about how there will be some recognitions at the next Board meeting to say thank you to people 
within the communities and the organization for coming together.  Supervisor Thompson thanked everyone 
for coming together. Mr. Jayne also thanked Paul Watson for playing an active role and all of the Supervisors.
