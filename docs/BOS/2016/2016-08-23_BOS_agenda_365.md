NAVAJO COUNTY FAIR:  
a. Navajo County Fair Update : Michael Sample indicated that the fair opens on September 14 and ends on 
September 18. He stated that every year, the fair gets bigger and better and that the Fair Board consists of 21 
volunteer members, many of whom are County employees. He thanked Navajo County for the partnership it 
has with the fair . He indicated that the fair will open on September 14, at 12:00 p.m., with Opening 
Ceremonies beginning at 6:00 p.m., which will allow those participating in 4H to bring their livestock on 
Tuesday evening.  He spoke about some of the events which will be held at the fair, including Demolition

<!-- page 2 -->
Derby on Thursday night and the Rodeo on Friday and Saturday nights. He stated that the fair has contracted 
with the Country Band, Emerson Drive, that will hold a concert. He further stated that instead of the Ranch 
Rodeo on Sunday , they will have the Southwest Junior Rodeo Association. He spoke about other events 
throughout the year, to include the local Gymkhanas once a month, Tulani Lake Roping in March, Southwest 
Indian Rodeo Finals the weekend after the fair, and the Southwest Junior  Rodeo finals, as well. He spoke 
about the grandstand roofing being completed with the partnership of Supervisor Whiting. He stated that 
they were audited by the Governor’s Office for the Governor’s Fund, who were amazed at how they manage 
their finances with the partnership of the Navajo County Finance Team. He thanked the department directors 
and elected officials who allow their employees to volunteer during the fair, along with Sheriff Clark and his 
crew. Supervisor Whiting thanked Mr. Sample and the Fair Board and all of the volunteer hours that are put 
in and everything that goes into putting on the fair and all of the other events throughout the year . He spoke 
about the Fair Board also helping tourism and expressed appreciation for Mr. Sample’s leadership and vision 
and partnership. Vice-Chairman Thompson thanked Mr. Sample for reaching out to other agencies.

d. Budget Update: Bryan Layton gave an update on the FY17 budget and indicated 50 percent of the County’s 
revenues comes from sales tax, which was down $38,000.00 in July for State Shared Tax and $86,000.00 in 
July and August for County Sales Tax, which, if this trend continues, could lead to a $1,000,000.00 shortfall 
for FY17. He stated that the decreases can be attributed to the energy sector. He spoke about how the County 
can respond by beginning to prepare specific strategies and monitor  and implement reduction strategies as  
needed. He also spoke about looking to structural and programmatic  strategies, with a County -wide 
perspective. He indicated that this will require intense partnerships and participation with Departments. He 
indicated that the Legislature is a big part of this, and, therefore, we will need to continue the conversations 
with the Legislature, other County managers throughout the State, CSA, and AACo to develop a common 
message and set of priorities. He stated that he anticipates having a recommended strategy by the next Board 
meeting.  Chairman Whiting inquired as to how much data we need to look at to anticipate the trend . Mr. 
Layton indicated that, ideally, we would like to have 3 months , but because of the dramatic shortfall in the 
first two months, we need  to develop strategies.  He stated that we are coordinating with the Department of 
Revenue to obtain the current numbers. Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Layton for his work with CSA and 
spoke about the continued cuts the County has done and the employers which have been lost and jobs which 
have been threatened . Supervisor Whitesinger  made comments about the impact the budget has on 
departments, the organization, and services . She spoke about having the budgetary responsibility to protect 
the dollars. She thanked Mr. Layton for being proactive.   She spoke about supporting one another.  Vice-
Chairman Thompson commended Mr. Layton for providing a head’s up on the budget, and further spoke 
about the quality service that Navajo County provides to our citizens  and expressed hope that the shortfall 
will be short-term. He spoke about continuing to reach out to our legislators.
