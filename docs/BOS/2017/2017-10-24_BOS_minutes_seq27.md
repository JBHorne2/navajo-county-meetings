BOARD OF SUPERVISORS :

a.

Recognition to retiring Chairman Ronnie Lupe for his years of service to the White Mountain Apache Tribe:   Supervisor Whitesinger recognized Chairman Ronnie Lupe from the White Mountain Apache Tribe. She stated that Chairman Lupe was elected in 1966 as the Chairman of the White Mountain Apache Tribe and has recently made the decision to retire. Sheriff KC Clark presented Chairman Lupe with a small token of his appreciation.
Supervisor Whitesinger spoke about Chairman Lupe having his own Wikipedia page from which she read. She spoke about the regional impact that Chairman Lupe leaves. She also spoke about how Sunrise Park Resort was established under his leadership and was actually his brain child in the beginning to develop the ski resort and what that would look like. She stated that Chairman Lupe is known as a son of Cibecue. She indicated that the White Mountain Apache Tribe has been a sound contributor to the region under Chairman Lupe's leadership. Supervisor Whitesinger presented Chairman Lupe with a certificate of recognition and a small token of Navajo County's appreciation. Chairman Lupe stated that he came from Cibecue on the White Mountain Apache reservation. He spoke about how it took many years for him to understand that there was another world outside of the reservation.  He stated that he has represented the White Mountain Apache Tribe for over 50 years. He stated that there are good people in the area of Arizona considering the fact that we have so many people coming to see the State of Arizona and stopping by to see their area. He spoke about the growth of Arizona, the people, and the political atmosphere that they bring about and doing something for the people, and he was involved in some of that. He stated it was a very cohesive process in development to justify the use itself for advancement of the people who live here in Navajo county. He indicated that he has met so many outstanding individuals in this county and has seen growth within the region and the people. He stated that there were issues that existed for us to tackle and resolve, and we have done it.  He thanked Navajo County, Navajo County Board of Supervisors, and the White Mountain Apache Tribe.  He stated that the people of Navajo County are so wonderful and although we have challenges, we are not alone. He indicated that he was able to meet some of the top members in the nation due to his role as Chairman, and he is grateful for all that this county has given him. He spoke about the progress in Navajo County. He also spoke of the leadership from the Chairman and the Board of Supervisors, as well as the people of Navajo County.  He spoke about the progress on his reservation with the assistance of Navajo County, including the dedication of many lakes in his area. He thanked Navajo County for the enjoyment he has been given and thanked the Board for the recognition. Supervisor Whitesinger again thanked Chairman Lupe for the legacy he has left us and that we will continue to work on behalf of the White Mountain Apache Tribe within the region. Chairman Thompson spoke about his niece and nephew who are enrolled with the  White Mountain Apache Tribe.  He stated that Chairman Lupe has told us to always have faith. Chairman Thompson thanked Chairman Lupe. Supervisor Whiting thanked Chairman Lupe for his words today that were very uniting and expressed appreciation for his leadership.

At 9:35 a.m., Lee Jack, Sr., Supervisor District I, is present. Supervisor Jack recognized Chairman Lupe and thanked him for being present and for his leadership. Chairman Thompson thanked Chairman Lupe for his legacy.

b.

Ceremonial Crowning of the Navajo County Rodeo Queens; Kaiyla Pentard , Rodeo Queen, Gracie Jo Perkins , Teen Queen, and Jacey Perkins , Little Buckaroo:   Michael Sample, Fair Board President, thanked the Board and the County Manager for participating in the opening ceremonies.  He further thanked the volunteers from each department, including Sheriff Clark and his office and Paige Peterson and her team.  He indicated that it was a successful fair and attendance was up.  He indicated that they are already working on next year's fair.  The Navajo County Fair Queen Coordinator, Amy Jo Perkins, spoke about the pageant.  Navajo County Fair and Rodeo Queen, Kaiyla Pentard, introduced herself stating she was from Prescott Valley; Navajo County Fair and Rodeo Teen Queen, Gracie Jo Perkins, stated she attends Show Low Junior High School and is excited to represent Navajo County; and Navajo County Fair and Rodeo Little Buckaroo, Jacey Perkins, thanked the Board of Supervisors for their work and stated that she attends Show Low Junior High Shool.  Mr. Sample invited all of the volunteers to the Volunteer Appreciation Dinner on November 18, 2017 at 6:00 p.m. at the Navajo County Fairgrounds at Martinez Hall. Supervisor Whiting congratulated these three young ladies and their parents.  He further thanked Mr. Sample, Jme Baloo, the Navajo County Fair Board and all of the volunteers.

c.

Consideration of Approval of U.S. Department of Commerce-Economic Development Administration Financial Assistance Award in the amount of $104,265.00 :  Paul Watson spoke about the Assistance to Coal Communities Grant.  He stated that Stephanie Ray stepped up and put together the grant application and got it submitted in a short period of time.  He stated that the purpose of this grant is to give us an economic update of what the adverse impacts of the coal industry will be and then help us identify industries compatible to our region and work with the existing coal plants to identify resources they have available.  He stated the next part of the project will be to identify infrastructure needs which would allow us to make further applications for assistance.  Stephanie Ray spoke about the next phase after we get the results from the consultants which is to create a second report to determine the action steps based on the report and the timeline moving forward.  She spoke about working with the local workforce development board to make sure we have the workforce ready for when the industry comes.  Mr. Watson recognized the partners in this endeavor, including APS, Real AZ, as well as in kind services from Navajo County.  He stated there will be a kick-off meeting on November 9 during the REAL Az meeting. He stated that we will target the coal-related industries and other economic partners, including the tribes and private landowners.  Chairman Thompson thanked Mr. Watson and Ms. Ray for obtaining this grant and spoke about lands within the City of Winslow.  Supervisor Whiting thanked both Mr. Watson and Ms. Ray for the work they have done stating that this is a big deal and a lot of hard work pointing in the right direction. He offered help however he can in this effort. Supervisor Whitesinger thanked Mr. Watson and Ms. Ray for the information and the work that they do.  Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion to approve the U.S. Department of Commerce-Economic Development Administration Financial Assistance Award in the amount of $104,265.00; motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.  Glenn Kephart expressed his appreciation to Mr. Watson and Ms. Ray for their efforts in obtaining this grant.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE: Report from Board members, County Attorney and County Manager:   Supervisor Jack thanked everyone for the work they do. He spoke about the Red Dog project and losing the Bureau of Indian Affairs (BIA); however, Navajo Department of Transportation (NDOT) is picking up where BIA they left off. He spoke about the cost of the Red Dog project.  He thanked Leonard Gorman for being present today.  He stated that Navajo Nation is disappointed with the dismissal of the case involving the shooting of Loreal Tsingine.  Supervisor Whiting expressed appreciation to this Board for their presence at the CSA Legislative Summit this week. He spoke about the State talking about a $100 million shortfall. Supervisor Whitesinger spoke about attending the Fall Tourism Forum in Pinetop-Lakeside.  She also spoke about attending the Rural Transportation Summit last week, the lack of dollars for transportation and the work of trying to generate conversations about tax initiatives.  She stated that she attended the First Things First White Mountain Apache Regional Partnership Meeting, and they continue to look at services for children zero to five.  She expressed appreciation to Bill Bess and his team regarding a frontage road in the Pinetop-Lakeside area in front of High Country Awards and read a thank you letter from Brian Parrack of High Country Awards. Glenn Kephart invited the supervisors to participate on Halloween where children in the community will be coming through the complex to trick or treat.  Chairman Thompson stated that all of the Board members will be attending the CSA Legislative Session this week.
