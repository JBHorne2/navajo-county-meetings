Update on Navajo Generating Station-Peabody Coal Mine :   Chairman Thompson introduced former Supervisor Percy Deal.  Mr. Deal gave an update on the Navajo Generating Station and Peabody Coal Mine.  He spoke of the negotiations which have been ongoing between Navajo Nation and Salt River Project whereby they would continue the operation up to December 2019 with the decommissioning process to begin after 2019, which takes 18 months to 2 years.  He stated that he does not know who will be responsible for the decommissioning.  He spoke about the revenue impact of the Navajo Generating Station and Peabody Coal Mine, including revenue and jobs.   He stated that he feels that the sooner the better as far as the expiration of the lease due to three issues:  Water, health impacts and the environment.  He stated that the families in the area do not have water due to the water being dedicated to Navajo Generating Station and Peabody Coal Mine and how lack of water affects wildlife, plants and birds.  Adele Begay spoke about the health impacts of extractive energy, especially coal mining.  Vice-Chairman Williams and Supervisor Whitesinger thanked Mr. Deal for the information given today.  Supervisor Jack spoke about the Navajo Nation Council considering more options regarding the Navajo Generating Station.  Chairman Thompson thanked Mr. Deal for the information.

ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES :

a.

FY2016/2017 Budget Update and FY2017/2018 Budget Overview :  Bryan Layton gave an FY2016/2017 budget update and FY2017/2018 budget overview.  He spoke about industry challenges and cost challenges.   He spoke about the possible delay in the closure of NGS and the opportunity that the delay gives us.  He stated that as to property tax, we are projecting a flat property tax which is appropriate and prudent.  With regard to sales tax, he indicated it is better than expected at .46%, and we are forecasting a modest rate increase of .4% in State shared sales tax. As to County sales tax, he stated that we've seen a little recovery, but we're still  -.69% lower than expected.  He stated that we are forecasting a 1% decrease for next year.  He spoke about the cost of doing business.  He stated that we have had a very difficult year in health care costs.  He further spoke about immediate capital needs.  He spoke about the positive developments, which includes the housing of Federal Inmates, full funding of payment in lieu of taxes, continuing the one-time reduction to ADJC payments and the restoration of HURF funding similar to FY 2017. He spoke about the FY2018 proposed reductions and advised the Board that all of the departments have cut a lot over the last ten years. He gave a brief outlook summary indicating the closure of NGS is expected to take place December 2019 and a corresponding closure of Cholla is forecasted, which will result in lower sales tax and property tax and up to 12% increase in annual healthcare. He spoke of the opportunities with the delay in the NGS Closure and the PILT funding, we can provide for the cost increases for the next year and also meet some of those capital needs.  He also spoke about a modest reserve contingency fund.  He spoke about Strategic Partnerships including collaborations within departments, cross funding of positions, partnership with cities, towns, NPC, Summit, fire districts and other entities. He spoke about our staff is leading the innovation and providing leadership, which includes a supervisor training series and also drones. He further spoke about our health plan and the plan to move forward with better value contracts, more aggressive claim review, resources, support and incentive for healthier lifestyles.
Chairman Thompson indicated that over the years we knew that this was going to happen and now we are posed with the questions on how we continue to provide the services that we have been giving to our citizens with the money that we have. He stated that we feel the pain that these departments have had to feel. He stated that he knows that everyone is looking for ways on how to deal with the shortfalls that we are faced with. Vice-Chairman Williams stated that in District IV, there are more new building permits this year over last year and the housing market is improving.  He asked even with that, due to the coal industry, is that why we are projecting the -1%? Mr. Layton indicated that he has also heard that homes are selling quicker in the southern part of the county, but realtors in the central part of the County are worried about home prices and the ability to sell homes.

BUDGET HEARINGS FOR DISCUSSION ONLY :

Board of Supervisors/Administration :  Mr. Layton indicated that we have decreased the travel budget; however, we would like to redirect a portion of those funds county wide for continuing education for staff.  He spoke about reducing the financial contribution to the County Fair to $40,000.00 matching the City of Holbrook's contribution.  He also spoke about the elimination of the General Fund Special Project funding and discontinuing the County's membership with the Alliance for Innovation.  He spoke about maintaining funding for the community clean-ups and the senior center contributions.  He spoke about a reduction in HSA funding of 50%.  He indicated that we are not recommending any reductions to the Public Defender and Legal Defender.

Assessor :  Cammy Darris spoke about the Assessor's database.  She spoke about how other departments use the Assessor's database to produce revenue.  She spoke about her 2017 budget.   She spoke about her operating budget line item cuts of $142,024.00 and the loss of an Appraiser II position for one-half of a year for 2018 for a savings of $26,500.00.  She spoke about postponing the Pictometry contract, which will save about $23,000.00.  She spoke about how her full-time employees since 2013 has not increased.  She stated that 78% of her current budget goes to staffing.  She spoke about her staff and that her office is not top heavy.  She spoke about the software which is utilized by the Assessor's Office, including maintenance of maps, improvement sketches, database maintenance, statistical analyzing and regression techniques.  She spoke about training and sending only a few employees to training and then use the train the trainer technique.  She stated that they do not attend any out-of-state conferences.  She spoke about not having any other budgets to move people around to.  Vice-Chairman Williams thanked Ms. Darris for doing all she can to be a team player and to make this work for all departments.

Judicial :  Superior Court/Clerk of the Court/JP Courts/Adult and Juvenile Probation/Juvenile Detention:  Judge Robert Higgins thanked the Board for working with the departments.  He spoke briefly about the Judicial budget and indicated that the 10% reduction was $719,257.00, and in fiscal year 2018, Judicial will have a reduction of $1,048,029.00 and in the next year $1.1 million.  He stated that he would like to see a Superior Court placed in South County so people who are involved in litigation can come to a court in Show Low.  Vice-Chairman Williams expressed appreciation to Judge Higgins for his role as presiding Judge and indicated that he feels having Superior Court in South County is a great vision.  Judge Higgins thanked Judge Marez for being present and further thanked Marla Randall for putting the budget together.

Recorder/Voter Registration/Elections :  Doris Clark thanked Mr. Layton, the HR Department, the Finance Department, Rayleen Richards, Lilene Sanchez and her whole department for helping her department continue to be successful.  She indicated that they were able to cut 20% in the Elections Department, 10% in the Recorder's Department and 21% in the Voter Registration Department.  She stated that these cuts were possible because there are no major elections this year.  She spoke about a week-long training that staff will need to take for Elections in Phoenix and other staff will need to attend a two-day training for recertification.  Chairman Thompson expressed appreciation to Ms. Clark for covering the whole County.  Supervisor Jack thanked Ms. Clark for her efforts in educating the public about elections.

Sheriff/Jail :  Sheriff KC Clark indicated that he was able to make some reductions.  He spoke about starting to charge inmates $10.00 for a medical visit, which resulted in the medical visits going down dramatically.  He also spoke about selling e-cigarettes to inmates which helped to reduce contraband in the jail.  He stated that he reduced the uniform allowance to $1,000.00 per year and $1,000.00 to start.  He spoke about the Sheriff's Office buying the belts and holsters which will be returned when deputies leave the department.  He spoke about looking for other ways to get money to use rather than the general fund.  He spoke about a five-year plan to include recidivism, a drug treatment program in the jail and an education program in the jail that we can possibly do through grants.  He spoke about mentoring future NCSO leaders to get them ready to fill vacancies.  He spoke about looking at expanding public/private partnerships for training while maintaining and expanding our current partnerships with NPC and partners across the State.  He spoke about jail contracts that bring in about $1.2 million a year; however, he stated that we need to look for a more stable funding source.  He spoke about maintaining growing technology for law enforcement.  He spoke about body cameras and that ACIP helped with that project.  He spoke about regional dispatch which will be kicked off in July.  Chairman Thompson thanked Sheriff Clark for all that he has done to continue to be a partner with the County budget.  Mr. Jayne spoke about the Sheriff finding the contracts.  Mr. Jayne thanked the Sheriff for his partnership.

Superintendent of Schools :  Jalyn Gerlich thanked the Board of Supervisors for their support over the past year.  She stated that her office is in transition.  She stated that she would like to work with the Sheriff on his plan for education in the jail.  Vice-Chairman Williams thanked Ms. Gerlich for her continued efforts.

Treasurer :  Debra Kester spoke about her full-time staff of 5 employees.  She indicated that there are 4 temporary employees that will start the second week in October for four weeks, with the hours being cut this year.  She spoke about the high ratio of parcels per employee.  She gave a brief overview of what the Treasurer's Office does.  She indicated that they mail over 90,000 tax notices, 38% of which require an individual posting.  She indicated that they received over 1500 phone calls in the month of February, which represents calls to the main number.  She spoke of the current treasury system which was developed prior to the launch of Windows 95.  She spoke about the new treasury system and stated that it is not complete.  She spoke about other options for a new system.  She spoke about the annual tax lien auction and the deeding of tax delinquent properties to the State.  She spoke about conducting internal and investment banking for Navajo County and reconciling the general fund banking account and savings account and investment portfolio.  She spoke about providing banking services for the various districts, producing monthly financial reports for 22 special districts and producing monthly financial report to the Arizona State Treasurer in regards to the judiciary collections.  She spoke about tracking the cash flow to maximize the availability of funds for all entities and invest the County monies while maintaining liquidity to conduct County business.  Supervisor Whiting thanked Ms. Kester for her work.

Facilities Management :  Bret Starns spoke about some of the things that the Facilities Management Department does, including snow removal, water systems, locksmith, HVAC systems, welding and fabrication, fire systems, landscaping, fairgrounds, document destruction, remodel and construction and toiletries and cleaning supplies.  Mr. Starns spoke about how we can provide the same level of services with less.  He spoke about technology, training, part-time employees, partnering with other agencies on buildings and a compensation system that recognizes top performers.

Information Technology :  Ken Dewitt spoke about cyber security and how County employees are working to help protect the network.  He spoke about purchasing a training package called KnowBe4.  He spoke about Information Technology staff and indicated that they do a great job.  He spoke about the future plans of IT and that it is an ever changing landscape.  Mr. Jayne thanked Mr. Dewitt for all of his work and leadership and finding solutions.  He spoke about Mr. Dewitt being appointed as the County Representative to the Administrative Office of the Courts Technology Team.  Mr. Dewitt indicated that we are going to see some exciting things happening in the Courts area.

Legal Defender :  Mariclare Hannah spoke about staffing and the cut of one attorney last year.  She indicated that cutting from three attorneys to two attorneys was hard and to cut any more would not be cost effective, because that would result in a need for more contract attorneys.  She stated that they are still doing Early Resolution Court.   She spoke about the challenge of driving to ten different locations five days a week.  She indicated that they put on a CLE, Continuing Legal Education, for defense attorneys.  She stated that they continue to provide an informal GED for inmates who qualify.  Vice-Chairman Williams inquired as to how often the attorney will be going to South County for juveniles and other justice courts.  Ms. Hannah stated that right now the attorney goes to South County once a week, but sometimes hearings are set on other days.  She stated that sometimes, sending an attorney to Justice Court is a waste of time, because the hearings are discontinued, and the attorneys are not notified until they get there.  She stated that each attorney is handling about 90 felony cases.

Public Health Services District :  Jeff Lee discussed the Navajo County Public Health Services District's budget.  He thanked Mr. Layton, Mr. Jayne, many department directors and all staff members who have continued to help him.  He spoke about what public health is, what some of the challenges are, and in what areas we could do better including moving forward in technology.  He stated that he is most excited about his staff.  He spoke about the different programs within Navajo County Public Health Services District.  He spoke about some of the strategic goals.

Public Works/Planning & Zoning/Flood Control :  Glenn Kephart stated that Public Work consists of managing five separate funds:  the HURF fund, Flood Control, the Little Colorado River, Planning and Zoning and Waste Tire Program.  He thanked Jeanine Carruthers for all of her hard work.  He indicated that she makes this budget process easy for the rest of us.  He spoke about FY2017 accomplishments, including road partnership projects.  He spoke about the Flood Control District budget, which most of that budget goes towards the Winslow Levee; however, he indicated that the money does get distributed county wide.  He spoke about HURF revenue and thanked the Board for their efforts in helping to restore that to the counties.  He stated that the Vehicle License Tax is steadily increasing.  He spoke about Public Works' five-year CIP and the partnerships with other entities.  He spoke about the vehicle and heavy equipment replacement plan.  He spoke about Planning and Zoning and indicated that there is a nice, steady increase in new building permits.  He thanked Sandy Phillips for the work that she continues to do with Planning and Zoning.  He spoke about county wide drainage projects.  He reviewed the Winslow Levee timeline.  He spoke about the Waste Tire budget, indicating that the program is funded through the State.  Chairman Thompson stated that we are very fortunate to utilize the HURF funds to maintain the roads on the reservation.  Vice-Chairman Williams thanked Mr. Kephart for being here and reviewing the budget with them.  James Jayne thanked Mr. Kephart for everything that he does.
