Presentation of the activities of the County Supervisors Association, including a discussion of the recent legislative session :  Craig Sullivan, County Supervisors Association (CSA), discussed the activities of the County Supervisors Association during this last legislative session and what some of the results were.  He thanked Chairman Thompson and JR Despain for bringing him on the team at CSA.  He spoke about the primary function of CSA.  He thanked the Board of Supervisors for their involvement and expressing Navajo County's perspective.   He stated that CSA is regularly working on issues related to county budget, structure and opportunities to save costs.  He spoke about working with all 15 counties to get the message out to lawmakers.  He thanked Chairman Thompson and Supervisor Whiting for coming to the Capitol to testify.  He stated that there is so much competition for resources at the Capitol, and it's important to build momentum and awareness and to build a group of knowledgeable lawmakers to take the message to leadership.  He indicated that we have achieved or made progress towards all of the objectives which were set related to financial relief.  He spoke about maintaining lottery funds and a HURF restoration to the counties.  He indicated that we need to watch the State's financial situation.  He spoke about some bills relevant to counties which have passed and bills which failed.  Mr. Sullivan discussed the post session work plan.  He spoke about the CSA Legislative Policy Summit hosted by Maricopa County on October 24-26 at Talking Stick Resort and Conference Center. Chairman Thompson thanked Mr. Sullivan and his team and indicated that the Board of Supervisors tries to make every effort to attend. He further thanked Supervisor Whiting for his work with our Legislative Policy Committee. Supervisor Whiting indicated that he is very appreciative of the work that the County Supervisors Association team does and for providing good, credible information to help the Board make good decisions. Supervisor Whitesinger extended her appreciation for the CSA team. She spoke about the budget process and the partnerships that CSA has built and for helping us educate our communities. She spoke about structural sustainability and what we can maintain in the coming years. She spoke about our budget capacity. She thanked Mr. Sullivan for his work and his team's work.

Mr. Sample stated that the fair runs from September 13 through September 17.  He spoke about different events held at the fairgrounds throughout the year.  He invited everyone to attend the opening ceremonies on Wednesday, September 13, at 6:00 p.m.  He spoke about the Buyers' Luncheon on Friday at 11:30 a.m.; the Rodeo on Saturday night during which they will honor one of the last remaining Navajo Code Talkers; and a new event on Sunday, the Rank Lil Buckers.  He stated that Redgie Justman is challenging the Board to partake in the salsa contest which will be held Saturday.  Mr. Sample thanked the Board of Supervisors for partnering with the fair. He thanked the Elected Officials for allowing their employees to partake and volunteer their time at the Fair. He thanked the Finance team for participating in the fair. He thanked the SAV's for participating in the fair. Former Supervisor JR DeSpain thanked the Board of Supervisors for serving in their capacity. He spoke about serving and the challenges that come their way. He personally invited the Board of Supervisors to attend Opening Ceremonies at 6 pm on Wednesday, September 13, and stated that he would open the microphone to any politicians during Opening Ceremonies. He stated that Navajo County's fair is the biggest little fair in Northern Arizona. He gave each Board of Supervisors member and Mr. Kephart an envelope with a pass that will get them into any gate or any event and a fair schedule. He spoke about the Lil Buckaroo Rodeo. He also invited Mr. Kephart to the opening ceremonies. He thanked Mike Sample for serving as President of the Fair Board and all of the volunteers. Chairman Thompson indicated that he recognizes the work that the Fair Board does that touches all communities and all citizens. He thanked Mr. Sample and Mr.Despain for the invitation and the partnership.  Supervisor Whiting read the proclamation and made a motion to approve a proclamation declaring September 13 through September 17, 2017 as "Navajo County Fair Days"; motion seconded by Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.  Mr. Despain spoke about how his grandfather and an ancestor of Supervisor Whiting's , Arthur Whiting, took the first load of lumber and built the first grandstand at the fairgrounds.

b.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 26-17 approving/denying a Zone Change on 1533 acres currently zoned A-General and RU-20 to Light Industrial (Ind-1) and Heavy Industrial (Ind-2) on Township 13 North, Range 19 East, Section 25 and a portion of Section 27; and Township 13 North, Range 20 East, Section 30; west of Snowflake, north of Highway 277 :  Sandy Phillips discussed the item indicating that the Planning and Zoning Commission recommended approval. She indicated that the applicant has made some concessions that substantially addressed the concerns raised by residents at the first Planning and Zoning meeting. She stated that the intent is to have the property rezoned in order to attract potential employers to the area and that any development would go through the permit process.  She spoke about the conditions and stipulations that are in the proposed resolution.  She spoke about notifications that have been made and that there are some letters of objection and support. Chairman Thompson opened the public hearing. Steve Brophy, the applicant, spoke about the rezoning application. He indicated that when the paper mill closed, he worked with a private company to save the railroad.  He stated that they further sought to acquire the land that had belonged to the paper mill and  use a portion of it to recreate the jobs and industry that was lost.  He indicated that this area has better infrastructure than anywhere along I-40.  He spoke of the land and the economic infrastructure that exists, including a water pipeline, electrical power, a natural gas line and approximately 2 ¼ miles of frontage adjacent to the Apache Railway and the Apache Railway headquarters.  He stated that he has received and responded to numerous inquiries from industrial development companies.  Lacy Frasier spoke in opposition of the item. He spoke about the pollution that goes on in the city and that is why they moved to this area. He indicated that his wife is on oxygen. He spoke about the trees being pulled up from all that property and that his quality of life is compromised. He spoke about the railroad being so close to his home. Mark Niezipinski spoke in opposition of the item. He stated that Sections 25 and 30 are designated as light industrial, but District 27 is not and is not part of the development.  He spoke about how the residents of Forest Town will be surrounded by this industrial zone.  He spoke about what is included under Industrial One, all of which creates dust and fumes which would affect three of the community members who have asthma  and chemical allergies. He stated that he would like the Board to approve the plan in Sections 25 and 30 and to not approve the plan in Section 27 or reject it as a whole until it can be amended to remove Section 27.  Fred Gentile spoke in opposition of this item and expressed concern that it will contaminate the air and water.  He handed the Clerk of the Board a letter. Sandra Phillips indicated that the Board of Supervisors can modify the resolution as it stands. She clarified the uses for light industrial.
Supervisor Whiting asked about the fence and outdoor storage. Ms. Phillips reviewed the stipulations within the proposed resolution. She indicated that the comprehensive plan goes all of the way to Sections 28 and 33, which is currently forest, and the entire area is identified as community village. Laura Singleton from the White Mountain Independent newspaper indicated that she is currently working on a story regarding the proposed rezoning and that her job is to show both sides as fairly and equitably as she can.  She asked that the Board of Supervisors put themselves in the residents' shoes.  She stated there is no question of the economic need.  Mr. Niezipinski indicated that he read the plan, and Section 27 is not in the Aztec Area plan.  Mr. Gentile stated that there was no opposition to Sections 25 and 30, but Section 27 is in closer proximity to his home.  Steve Brophy indicated that when the Aztec master plan was approved, they only owned Section 25, and when the County did its planning ordinance, it designated those sections as community village, which permits industrial development.  He stated that Forest Town is within 1000 feet of the nearest track of the railroad, and the water pipeline goes on the south right-of-way edge of the west half of Section 27.  He stated that the 300-foot setback that he agreed to during the Planning and Zoning Commission Meeting constitutes 110 acres throughout the entire project in open space, and in addition to that, the truck traffic on Highway 277 today is approximately 250 semis a day, so there is industrial use today.  He indicated that the closest residential area to that industrial use is Forest Town, within half a mile of the old paper mill, the active NOVO power plant and two sawmills. He requested that the Board of Supervisors approve the project as a whole for a broader ray of choices for those looking to come here.
Chairman Thompson asked if the concerns expressed here were considered at the Planning and Zoning meetings. Ms. Phillips indicated that the concerns were similar as those voiced to the Planning and Zoning Commission, and the Commission recommended approval unanimously.  Dan Freeman spoke about the smell from the paper mill, and he stated he was glad when they closed it, but was sad for the loss of jobs. He stated that he doesn't have a problem with the item if there is not something that is going to come in that smells and be something clean that we can live with. Jack Carlisle, J&M Land, spoke in favor of the item; however, he stated that he wants to know how we control what goes on after the fact. He stated that he is sick of the trash that comes from those places. He indicated that he is for the growth but not the trash. He spoke about the need for jobs. Ms. Phillips indicated that there are some uses in industrial two that would have to go through the public process. She spoke about the trash issue, and she indicated that we are encouraging people to be a good neighbor. She stated that there is a new code enforcement officer, and he is good at working with people and getting things corrected.  She spoke about codes which require people to be good neighbors, and those are being enforced.
Chairman Thompson closed the public hearing. Supervisor Whiting asked what the zoning is now.  Ms. Phillips stated that it is zoned A-general and RU-20.  Supervisor Whiting inquired about some of the different things that can go on those properties as it is zoned right now.  Ms. Phillips stated A-general is a blanket zoning, and it allows a chicken or egg farm. Supervisor Whiting spoke about the concerns which were expressed and that he sees that concessions have been made. He stated that this property sits on infrastructure that already exists. He expressed concern about the lack of jobs in the area   He expressed appreciation to Mr. Brophy with regard to the concessions which he has made.  He inquired regarding whether the Board of Supervisors is held to Aztec's master plan. Mr Carlyon indicated that the master plan is guidance. Brandt Clark indicated that several years ago, Aztec Land and Cattle developed a master plan for the land that they owned at that time, and it was a subset of the comprehensive plan that Navajo County adopted which does encompass Unit 27, 28 and 30.  He stated that portion was not owned by Aztec Land and Cattle at that time, so that was not included in the subsidiary plan that they had developed; but the comprehensive plan that Navajo County has did include those sections of land.

Supervisor Whiting made a motion to adopt Resolution Number 26-17 approving a Zone Change on 1533 acres currently zoned A-General and RU-20 to Light Industrial (Ind-1) and Heavy Industrial (Ind-2) on Township 13 North, Range 19 East, Section 25 and a portion of Section 27; and Township 13 North, Range 20 East, Section 30; west of Snowflake, north of Highway 277; motion seconded by Vice-Chairman Williams .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
