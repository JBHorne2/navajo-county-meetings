BOARD OF SUPERVISORS :

a.

Presentation to the Snowflake Junior High School STEAM participants for winning the Samsung Solve for Tomorrow Contest on April 26, 2017, in New York:   A short video was shared describing the project.  Supervisor Whiting introduced Michael Eilertson, the teacher who led the students with this project.  Mr. Eilertson explained the Samsung Solve for Tomorrow competition.  He indicated that this is the seventh year of the competition and that they start with having the students identify a problem in their community and ask how they can fix it.  He spoke about how this competition empowers youth and helps them realize that they can make a difference.  He stated that they had to first write a three-paragraph write-up describing the project and how they would fix it, they then had to develop a lesson plan on how to execute the project and then they had to prove it.  He indicated that these students volunteered their after school time beginning in January to participate.  He stated that they submitted their video and was selected as one of ten schools to go to Washington DC and present in front of a panel of judges.  He indicated that they won the community choice award in addition to being one of the national winners which resulted in $170,000.00 in technology for Snowflake Junior High, plus Samsung is donating to a charity of their choice, which they have chosen the Herpetological Society.  Each student gave their name and described with which portion of the project they helped.  The students gave a live demonstration of what the project is and what it is designed to do.
Supervisor Whitesinger spoke to the team indicating that this project is very impressive.  She spoke about the teamwork, logic, thought process and analysis that went into this project.  She congratulated the teachers, students, school and parents.  Vice-Chairman Williams stated that this is a great demonstration of greatness found in youth.  Supervisor Whiting introduced Principal Hoopes and Superintendent Merrill.

b.

Recognition to White Mountain Apache Head Start for receiving the Four-Star Quality Plus rating through Arizona's First Things First Quality First program:   Superviser Whitesinger spoke about what Quality First is and what it means for White Mountain Apache Head Start to have received the four-star quality plus rating.  She introduced the program facilitators.  Jessica Beach, Regional Director for the First Things First White Mountain Apache Tribe Regional Partnership Council, spoke about the level of quality of the center.  She stated that the White Mountain Apache Head Start is the highest rated center in southern Navajo and Apache County in quality first.  Kimberly Henry, lead teacher, spoke about the program and indicated that it has made her become a better teacher.  Nina Edison, teacher, spoke about being stronger and a better teacher.  Bridgette Benally, a parent and volunteer, spoke about her son's learning skills improving through this program.  She indicated that she is very grateful for this program.  RoAnna Daley, a parent, spoke about the development of the children from the beginning of the school year to the end of the school year.  She stated that she is grateful for the First Things First program.  Mona Lupe, supervisor of the teaching staff, indicated that she is very proud of the staff and feels that it is an honor and a privilege to be a part of this program and indicated that it is wonderful to have this program in our rural area.  She stated that to receive this rating, it takes the work of all of the teachers, parents, community and the little children.   Leola Larzelere, director, spoke of the school which consists of 14 teachers and classrooms.  She expressed pride for the teachers.  She said that for this to happen, it takes people who want to do their job, the help of Quality First for coaching and more technology and hard work.  She spoke about building a foundation of Native Americans doing their very best.
S upervisor Whitesinger spoke about the success rate across the country of Native American students going on to college and completing it and that Head Start is aware of their part in this and that they are significant in laying that foundation.  She indicated that she was grateful and honored to recognize all of the teachers and parents.

PROBATION :

a.

Presentation for partnership with NCIS :  Jalyn Gerlich, Superintendent of Schools, spoke about a collaboration between Probation, Superintendent of Schools,  Superior Court and AOC to find an alternative for non-violent juvenile offenders by reallocating $150,000.00 to youth care workers utilizing the Navajo County Accommodation District building.  She stated that the juveniles would attend school and learn life skills and counseling and go back home with electronic monitoring.  She stated that this would be a redirection of funds at no cost to the County.  She spoke about probation being housed in that facility where they could visit the juveniles by walking down the hall.  Vice-Chairman Williams inquired as to with which Superior Court this proposal has been discussed.  Ms. Gerlich stated that they have spoken with Judge Higgins, and he indicated that he would speak with Judge Ruechel.  Arno Hall spoke about the partnership with Probation and the need for this program to exist with the loss of the juvenile detention center.  He stated that with the collaboration between the two departments, they would be able to provide services and address some of the concern that the school has had with the juveniles, and probation would be better able to supervise them onsite.  He stated that this would be part of the cost savings that the County is already realizing.
Vice-Chairman Williams stated that where juveniles are housed would be up to the juvenile judge and not the Board of Supervisors.  Mr. Hall stated that in his conversations with Judge Higgins and Judge Ruechel, they expressed a preference to keep Navajo County kids in Navajo County.  Lynda Wilson indicated that the baseline number of juveniles is 15 in detention on a daily basis.  She stated that a probation officer can require a juvenile to attend school and maintain passing grades.   She spoke about the proposed program would be 8:00 a.m. until 6:00 p.m. and that most  offenses by juveniles happen between 3:00 p.m. and 6:00 p.m.  She stated that it will cost $175.00 per day for these juvenile to go elsewhere.  Ms. Gerlich indicated that, yes, the judge would make that decision as far as what the sentence is and that this will give the judges another option.  Vice-Chairman Williams indicated that detention of juveniles is very driven by the judge's philosophy, and the Board of Supervisors does not have anything to do with how many juveniles are detained.  He expressed appreciation for the collaborative efforts.  He stated that adult courts are generally set up for retribution, for punishment, but juvenile court is for rehabilitation.  Ms. Gerlich stated that the judges have indicated that they like this option because it provides an alternative.  Jason Moore stated that he spoke to the juvenile prosecutor in his office about the loss of the juvenile detention center, and it is the intention of the County Attorney's Office to ask for detention less frequently due to the cost to send them out of county and that his office anticipates that the juvenile judge is going to be a lot less likely to send the juveniles out of county, and the proposed program makes this more feasible.  Vice-Chairman Williams stated that this would be the judge's call, but the proposal makes sense to him.  James Jayne stated that Bryan Layton visited the school yesterday.   He stated that he and Mr. Layton visited with the team this morning.  He thanked the team for thinking big and coming to the table with proposed solutions and new ideas.  He spoke about the preliminary discussion regarding this partnership and that there will be a further meeting today.  He further spoke about the breakdown of the $150,000.00.  Supervisor Jack expressed appreciation for those involved finding another solution.
