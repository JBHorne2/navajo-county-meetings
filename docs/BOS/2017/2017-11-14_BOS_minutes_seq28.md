BOARD OF SUPERVISORS :

a.

Consideration and adoption of a Proclamation declaring the month of November as "Navajo County Caregiver Month ":  Brandon Baxter from NACOG discussed the item and thanked the Board of Supervisors for this proclamation.  He spoke about seeing the largest aging demographic our world has ever seen, so family caregiving will continue to be important.  He stated that there is a family caregiver meeting tomorrow that all are invited to.  He indicated that he is here to bring awareness to the caregivers in our community that it is okay to ask for support and that we can help them to stay healthy so their loved ones can stay in the home and be healthy.  Vice-Chairman Williams read the proclamation and made a motion to adopt a proclamation declaring the month of November as "Navajo County Caregiver Month; motion seconded by Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

b.

Native American Heritage Month:   Adam Wolfe gave a short presentation regarding the history behind Native American Heritage month.  He spoke about there being 20 Native tribes in Arizona, 3 of which are in Navajo County.  Chairman Thompson thanked Mr. Wolfe for the presentation and stated that it is important for us to know these stories. Supervisor Whitesinger thanked Mr. Wolfe for the recognition and spoke about the relationship. She spoke of the relationship between communities, which is very important.  She spoke about the importance of education in the Tribal community and encouraged everyone to learn about the Tribes in our community.

At 9:29 a.m., Jason Whiting, Supervisor District III , was present telephonically.

c.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 34-17 , approving/denying the creation and establishment of Pinedale Estates Domestic Water Improvement District:   Melissa Buckley spoke about the item.  She stated we received a petition for the creation and establishment of Pinedale Estates Domestic Water Improvement District, and today is the day set for the hearing.  She indicated that notice of hearing was published and mailed out to property owners, 231 lots, out of which 9 have been returned undeliverable.  She stated that the notice of hearing and petition was posted in 3 places in the district, and we have not received any letters.  She introduced Douglas Brown, attorney for the proposed district.  Mr. Brown gave a background of the water issues in the area which led to the proposal to form this water improvement district.  He stated that he has not received any objections to the formation of the district.  He stated this is the only way that the property owners can make improvements.  He expressed appreciation to Melissa Buckley and Jason Moore for being responsive and easy to work with. Chairman Thompson opened the public hearing.  Ray Hindsley, resident of Pinedale Estates, addressed the board.  He stated that this came about because of quality issues and unreliable service.  Charles Mead, resident of Pinedale Estates, addressed the board.  He spoke about always having water issues in Pinedale Estates and that he believes this is the best option.  Chairman Thompson c losed the public hearing.  Vice-Chairman Williams indicated that almost 2/3 of the landowners approved the formation of the district.  He commended the property owners for their efforts to find solutions.  Vice-Chairman Williams made a motion to adopt Resolution Number 34-17 approving the creation and establishment of Pinedale Estates Domestic Water Improvement District; motion seconded by Supervisor Whitesinger .  Supervisor Whitesinger complimented the community for coming together.Vote unanimous approving the motion.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 35-17 approving/denying a Special Use Permit to allow a hunting and processing ranch with lodging accommodations on APN 110-09-001S & 001T, a portion of Township 16 North, Range 22 East, Section 29 in the Woodruff area:   At 9:53 a.m., Sandra Phillips discussed the item. She spoke about the easements and a road the applicant has put in to maintain the fence, and the applicant is responsible for maintaining the animals on the property. She stated that there is a sighting range which is not a public firing range, but is only for those guests to use during the day to sight their firearms. She reviewed the stipulations and spoke about posting.  She stated that this item was approved unanimously by the Planning and Zoning Commission and have received no objections.  Chairman Thompson opened the public hearing; there being no comments, closed the public hearing.  Supervisor Whiting indicated that he went out to the site and believes that the owners have done everything that they have been asked to do. Supervisor Whiting made a motion to adopt Resolution Number 35-17 approving a Special Use Permit to allow a hunting and processing ranch with lodging accommodations on APN 110-09-001S & 001T, a portion of Township 16 North, Range 22 East, Section 29 in the Woodruff area; motion seconded by Vice-Chairman Williams .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 37-17 approving/denying a revision to the Navajo County Addenda & Additions to the IBC, page 4, the Design Load Table to reflect wind speeds for signs, cell towers, cell tower modifications and similar construction to a wind load in alignment with the current state code:   Sandra Phillips discussed the item. She spoke about cell towers and the requirements for those cell towers. She spoke about updating the International Building Code to 2015 and the changes to be made for safety.  She stated that the Planning and Zoning Commission approved this item.  Chairman Thompson opened the public hearing, there being no comments closed the public hearing. Vice-Chairman Williams asked about the existing cell towers, and Ms. Phillips indicated that they would not need to do anything to the existing tower, only if they were going to add to or modify the tower.  Supervisor Whitesinger thanked Ms. Phillips and her staff for looking out for public safety and further made a motion to adopt Resolution Number 37-17 approving a revision to the Navajo County Addenda & Additions to the IBC, page 4, the Design Load Table to reflect wind speeds for signs, cell towers, cell tower modifications and similar construction to a wind load in alignment with the current state code; motion seconded by Vice-Chairman Williams .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE: Report from Board members, County Attorney and County Manager:   At 10:22 a.m., the Board and audience sang Happy Birthday to Melissa Buckley.  Supervisor Whitesinger spoke about attending the Pinetop-Lakeside Town Council meeting, introducing Glenn Kephart. She further spoke about losing several Tribal community members within the last two or three weeks and that she has been thinking about that and not taking life for granted and share that we care about one another. She stated that she is blessed to serve in the capacity that she does. She spoke about learning about Marines and the crucible, which is 54 hours that is dedicated out in the field in a training routine, 48 miles, 45 pounds of gear, 36 warrior stations, 29 team building exercises, 6 hours of sleep and 2 MRE's. She stated that during this time, family and friends leave a candle or their porch lights on to guide them home. She indicated that on Wednesday, November 8, Chad Amos successfully completed the crucible and became a United States Marine. She thanked our service members and their family members at home. Vice- Chairman Williams spoke about the County Supervisors Association Annual Legislative Summit and thanked staff for being present, as well. He further spoke about the Patterson family who lost their 18-day old son and asked for prayers on their behalf and spoke about the memorial service on Saturday. He thanked all those who put together the County Thanksgiving lunch. He stated that he participated in the November issue of  the County Connection Show where they interviewed Bryan Layton and Jason Owens about technology in the County.  He spoke about the Show Low Community Fast and showed a short video about the fast. He stated that the community celebration closing the fast will be on Sunday, November 19, at the Show Low Auditorium. Chairman Thompson spoke about a veteran who passed on right before Veteran's day. He spoke about several events that he attended during Veteran's day. He spoke about a meeting in Winslow talking about establishing commercial services on lands within the city limit that belong to Navajo Nation in partnership with APS, U of A, and USDA, and this group will be presenting at NACOG at the next meeting. He stated that he appreciated getting together at the CSA Summit.  He spoke about not attending Small Counties Forum because he will be attending the Arizona State Transportation Board meeting in Wickenburg. Brad Carlyon spoke about the AACo Annual Conference and encouraged the Board to attend the AACo Board meeting this Thursday morning and asked for support for his proposal to have the State legislature set up a study committee together to study county financing.  He stated that while the legislature acknowledges that Rural Arizona has problems, they have not been willing to sit down and discuss solutions, and he is hopeful that this proposal will help with that.  Glenn Kephart thanked Vice-Chairman Williams for his participation in the Community Fast program.  He thanked all those who had items on the agenda today for their preparation and professionalism. He thanked Mr. Wolfe for working over the weekend keeping our social media healthy. He acknowledged Brent Turley and Pam Phillips who graduated from the CPM program. He indicated that he attended the Pinetop-Lakeside Town Council meeting to introduce himself and express that Navajo County will continue the strong partnerships with our cities and towns. He stated that we have received 23 applications for the public works director, which have been shortlisted down to 10 qualified candidates, and they will shortlist further at which time we would have the Supervisors and Elected Officials participate.  Melissa Buckley indicated that she will be attending the AACo conference.  She spoke about the Clerk's Association meeting and invited Board members to attend.
