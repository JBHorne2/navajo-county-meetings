PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 31-17 approving/denying a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction of a self-mini storage unit facility that will accommodate a commercial buffer, individual storage units, and covered storage spaces for boats and RVs on a parcel which is zoned C-R on the front half and A-General for the back half, APN 409-20-015P, a portion of Township 10 North, Range 21 East, Section 4, in the Show Low area.   Nick Coussoulis discussed the item.  He spoke about the location and zoning of the subject property.  He spoke about the concerns regarding Chamberlain Parkway.  He spoke of the recorded easements and the notification made to surrounding neighbors and advertised in the White Mountain Independent.

At 9:16 a.m., Lee Jack Sr., Supervisor District I , was present.

Mr. Coussoulis spoke of a stipulation requiring that the applicants prove that the ingress/egress was suitable to the applicant's intended use for the parcel and as of today, they have not received sufficient proof that Chamberlain Way is for the public.  Bill Bess indicated that we received a new site layout from the applicant which still shows the access roads coming off Chamberlain.  He recommended that this item be tabled until the applicant works out the legal and roadway access.  Vice-Chairman Williams inquired about the concerns of the neighbors regarding Chamberlain Way.  Mr. Coussalis indicated that the issue of access has not been resolved to our satisfaction. Mr. Bess indicated that staff is recommending we table the item so that we can work out the access of the property. Chairman Thompson opened the public hearing.  Kent Lauderbach, Sr., 1030 Chamberlin Way, spoke about the access going through his property. He stated that the back half of this property does not lend itself well to do what Mr. Fernau wants to do with it. He stated that this will devalue his property and not enhance their lifestyle. He stated that he is in opposition of this item and would like this left residential.  Vice-Chairman Williams asked Mr. Lauderbach regarding the access to the front half of the property versus the back half of the property. Mr. Lauderbach indicated that he is not opposed to having access to the front half of the property. Mr. Lauderbach indicated that Chamberlain is a private road that he maintains.  He spoke about notification, indicating that there is no posting on the property regarding meetings, and that should have been done.

At 9:25 a.m., Brad Carlyon, County Attorney, is present.

Kristina Peterson provided a handout to members of the Board.  She questioned how the approval of this project has progressed so far when there remains so many unanswered questions.  She spoke about the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance and indicated that there have been no postings done for any of the hearings on various dates.  She stated that many of the residents in the area have not received notice.  She questioned whether the Commission used mailing addresses versus property addresses. She questioned why Mr. Fernau was allowed to join these two parcels without notice to the community.  She spoke about the Assessor Parcel map  showing the zoning as commercial residential. She indicated that the residents do not feel that their concerns have been heard. She provided some handouts on the ordinance and her letter of opposition. She spoke about commercial versus residential. She spoke of development standards and neighboring land uses.  She stated that residents' letters of complaint have of complaint have not been acknowledged.  She reviewed Navajo County's Land Use Policy from 4.0 Land Use Elements.  She stated that expectations have not been heard, mitigated or met.  She presented a petition of 14 people in opposition to the project, which meets the 20-percent criteria for opposition.  She spoke about the elevation of the subject property and expressed concern about possible damage to downhill properties.  She indicated that the City of Show Low has a moratorium on storage facilities in the western portion of the city.  She stated that Mr. Fernau began grading this morning again.  She spoke about zoning rules to limit adult businesses to 1500 feet from schools.  She spoke about density and usage.  She spoke about nearby storage units in the area.  She provided proof that Chamberlain Way is a private street with deeded access to this easement included for all Chamberlain Way property owners, and this access is not within the County's purview to act.  She questioned why zoning changes were granted without community notice or community involvement and indicated that Mr. Fernau's brother was past mayor of Show Low which might help to explain why many of these County oversights occurred.  Steve Heckathorne spoke in opposition of the item. He indicated that there was no posting at the property.  He stated that he developed a mini storage in Linden which required notice in the newspaper, mailing notice to property owners within 300 feet and posting on the property.  He read to the Board Navajo County's ordinance.  He handed to the Board of Supervisors his positing notices that he had to do. He asked why that was not done on this project when he was forced to do those things. He stated that the County needs to follow their own regulations. He indicated that he feels like this item has gotten special preference.  Mr. Curtis Fernau, the applicant, spoke in regards to those things discussed by those in opposition regarding a meth lab and moving dirt on the property today, and indicated that if someone is moving dirt, it's not him, and he will not move any dirt until he gets the okay to do so. He spoke about having a revised drawing of the property that has not been given to the Board which shows an entrace from Chamberlain Way at less than 300 feet from the highway and a letter from Arizona Department of Transporation that he read.  He stated that he does not know anybody who would twist their decision because his brother happened to be the former Mayor of Show Low.  He stated that he intends on building a nice facility with diffusing fencing and make it as nice as possible.  He spoke about the need for covered RV parking in the area.  He spoke about the drainage going down Chamberlain Way.  He stated that he had a detention area on one side.  He indicated that his phone number is on the sign in front of the property, and he would have been happy to address any issues someone might have had.  He spoke about the Resolution before the Board today.  He indicated that he does not know about the problems that have been voiced. He stated that there is less traffic with self-storage.

Vice-Chairman Williams asked Mr. Fernau if he would be willing to look at a solution of accessing the property in a way other than Chamberlin Way.  Mr. Fernau stated that he really does not see the need for it because the access is already there, it's a commercial turnout, and it has a reduction in speed lane.  He indicated that he doesn't know if Mr. Heckathorne has a reduction in speed turnout for his facility.  He stated that he does not see any need to put one 100 feet or 200 feet down the road.  Steve Heckathorne spoke again about the public notice issue. He stated that he wants to know before he leaves today the answer as to why this particular application was not posted. Bill Bess indicated that it is his belief that posting is only required when there is a zone change, and there is no zone change for this property. As to the notification of people, he stated that the only thing they have to go on is the owner's address that's listed in the Assessor's information.  He reviewed some of the stipulations that were listed by the Planning and Zoning Commission, including Item Number 3, drainage and grading plans for all new site facilites shall be completed and appoved by Navajo County Public Works prior to the issuance of a building permit for any of the new structures. He spoke of Item 6 on the Resolution that reads that the property must be screened with a wall, a maximum combined height of 6' measured from the top of the footing to the top of the wall to reduce visual impacts to surrounding properties.  The wall shall be earth tones.  If multiple walls are needed to screen the facility, a 6' horizontal distance between walls, with this area landscaped.  He also spoke about Item Number 11, the Special Use Permit shall run with the land, once the development meets all requirements.

Vice-Chairman Williams inquired as to whether it is accurate that because there is no zoning change, posting was not necessary.  Brad Carlyon spoke about Article 20 of the zoning ordinance which refers to Article 29 where the notice is.  He stated that it's not clear when you read all of Article 20 that it does.  He stated that this Board and Planning and Zoning Commission needs to rewright the zoning ordinance.  He stated he can make legal arguments both ways.  He indicated that he believes a judge would rule against us and stated that there needs to be notice because they will find a need for the community to be informed. Mr. Carylon recommended that the item be tabled, post it and then come back.  Vice-Chairman Williams thanked those who came to speak on this issue.  He thanked Mr. Fernau for coming and commended him for what he is wanting to do for the property.  He indicated that the Board does hear the property owners' concerns.  He advised Ms. Peterson that he took great issue with one thing she suggested having to do with Mr Fernau's last name and special treatment.  He stated that he understands that she is upset with the process, but his experience in dealing with our County and the two-and-a-half years he has been on the Board and dealing with our County Attorney's Office and the other Board of Supervisors, we want to do what's right by the people of Navajo County, and if someone has a certain last name, it has nothing to do with anything.  We want to do what's right.  We want to follow procedure.  We want to follow the rule of law.  He stated that when he hears the County Attorney say that if this gets challenged in law, we probably didn't follow the proper procedures and postings, he therefore believes we need to table this and do the right thing procedurally.  He indicated that the other issue he would raise is that there are conflicting views on access on Chamberlain Way.  He stated that he drove that this morning, and he didn't see any dirt disturbed on the property.   He indicated that there are other businesses right there and homes farther down Chamberlain Way.  He stated that what is being proposed does not seem an unreasonable use of the property provided some of the concerns are addressed, but he has a concern regarding the access on Chamberlain Way and also procedurally on the posting.  He stated that we need to be sure we do what is right procedurally.  Vice-Chairman Williams made a motion to table the matter so we can do the posting properly and also the Chamberlain Way issue.  Motion seconded by Supervisor Whitesinger . Chairman Thompson closed the public hearing.  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 32-17 approving/denying Final Plat Extensions on two (2) residential projects that were initiated in 2006, on a total of 46.14 acres currently zoned Special Development and R1-43, north of Interstate 40 and west of Holbrook, on Township 18 North, Range 20 East, Section 33:   Nick Coussoulis discussed the item. He spoke about a letter of good intent from the applicant.  He stated that the original application was approved in 2003.  He reviewed staff recommendations as follows:  Approval is conditional upon all subdivision improvements being substantially completed within 24 months after the date of the P&Z Commission time extension approval.  As this is the third (3rd) extension, if ground is not broken, grading of site and construction of improvements begun, it is recommended that the Final Plat expire.  If the applicant desires to appeal the expiration, they can petition to go before the Board of Supervisors, by submitting request to the Clerk of the Board; Upon approval of the Final Plat, the subdivision roads will not be accepted into the Navajo County road maintenance system until they are brought up to meet the Navajo County road maintenance standard by approval of the Superintendent of Streets, at which time the roads will automatically be accepted for maintenance by Navajo County with no further action required; and The Final Plat document and CC&R's will not be recorded until all subdivision improvements have been completed to the satisfaction of the Navajo County Engineer and receipt of a financial assurances agreement for work within the Navajo County Right-of-Way is approved by the Navajo County Attorney's office and signed by the Chairman of the Board of Supervisors.  Bill Bess indicated that they have received improvement plans, and they meet the current standards, and the applicant has demonstrated good faith in proceeding.  He stated that staff has recommended a time extension. Chairman Thompson opened the public hearing, there being no comments, closed the public hearing. Supervisor Whiting thanked staff for consideration and recommendations on this item and agrees with the sunset clause in the resolution.  Supervisor Whiting made a motion to adopt Resolution Number 31-17 approving Final Plat Extensions on two (2) residential projects that were initiated in 2006, on a total of 46.14 acres currently zoned Special Development and R1-43, north of Interstate 40 and west of Holbrook, on Township 18 North, Range 20 East, Section 33; motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE: Report from Board members, County Attorney and Interim County Manager:   Supervisor Jack thanked the County and staff for the good work they do for our citizens in Navajo County and also the Sheriff's Office for what they do on the reservation and trying to help the Navajo Police, especially during this time of year during the Navajo Nation fair. He spoke of a meeting in Gallup regarding the Red Dog projects with Navajo Department of Transportation (NDOT) and Bureau of Indian Affairs (BIA). He indicated that BIA had to pull out of the project, therefore, the projects are slowing down.  He stated that the Navajo Nation is going through their budget session, and there will be a lot of cuts.  Supervisor Whiting spoke about the Payments in Lieu of Taxes (PILT) Fly-In back in Washington which he attended last week. He stated that the days were packed with meetings and that PILT is not a permanent thing, and we cannot count on it as part of our budget. He indicated that the representatives realize this now, and he believes that they now understand the relationship with secure rural schools and PILT.  He stated that representatives on the east coast see PILT as a west coast problem and as a subsidy.  He indicated that most of the people that he met with were representatives from the western states, and they all were in favor of it.  He stated that he inquired of those representatives what we can do to help in those conversations with representatives on the east coast, so he expressed to these representatives that we are actually subsidizing the Federal Government as opposed to the Federal Government subsidizing us.  He stated that while they remain optimistic, it still has not been passed for this next year.  He stated that he thought it was productive and the representatives know who Navajo County is. Chairman Thompson thanked Supervisor Whiting for representing us and CSA. Supervisor Whitesinger thanked Supervisor Whiting for those conversations he had in Washington.  She reported on an Oral health partnership conversation she attended and learned more about how we provide those services and the hundreds of children who are in need of services.  She congratulated Sunrise on the First Annual First Responders Patriot Climb which turned out to be very successful.  She spoke about the Veterans Summit going on tomorrow at Hon-Dah and invited all those interested to attend. She stated it is a two-day Summit. She further extended her appreciation to  Vice-Chairman Williams for his leadership and example. She spoke about visiting Blue Ridge High School last week on Tuesday with Vice-Chairman Williams . Vice-Chairman Williams thanked Supervisor Whitesinger for attending, as well. He further spoke about September 11 and the events that went on yesterday. He stated that we seem to come together and focus on what we have in common and build upon that and that it is difficult to remember; however, it is important that we remember it. He spoke about the visit to the high school and the memories that came with that.  He indicated that we have narrowed the applicants to two for the county manager position.  Chairman Thompson reported on the continuation of working with the Army Corps of Engineers with regard to the Winslow Levee, and we need to stay on schedule. He spoke about Chip Davis and the meeting that was held regarding the Winslow Levee. He spoke about the 9-11 remembrance in Winslow and thanked Adam Wolfe. He spoke about his meeting with Arizona State Transportation Board meeting on Friday.  Vice-Chairman Williams thanked Mr. Carlyon for giving this Board guidance.
