d.

Economic Development Update: Paul Watson gave an update on the activities of economic development in Navajo and Apache Counties. He stated that on a national level, unemployment continues to decrease and is now less than five percent and non-farm-related jobs have increased, as well as construction-related jobs. On a local level, he gave an update on Navajo Generating Station, which is looking to close in 2019, and that the greater struggle with coal is economic as opposed to environmental. He spoke about the cutbacks at Cholla Power Plant and that we continue to look at that with concern. He stated that he serves as executive director of REAL Az Development Council, and there were presentations from Novo Power and Tri Star Logging and Novo Star Sawmill at the last meeting. He stated that the supply for those industries is currently meeting the needs. He indicated that the sawmill is looking for additional improvements to better refine the wood product that they ship out, which will mean adding more jobs. He stated that Novo Star Sawmill is working towards extending the Apache Railway rail tracks into their operation. He stated that these three operations employ a significant amount of people. He spoke about the Shooting, Hunting, Outdoor Trade Show that he attended and stated that he had a booth set up at the supplier's showcase to demonstrate that we have the resources for manufacturing and other industries and had some good contacts with whom they need to follow up. He spoke of the issue of marketing and that the REAL Az website will be revamped. He indicated that the next REAL Az meeting is May 11th at noon in the upstairs room of Celebrations in Snowflake. Mr. Watson discussed broadband in the region. He indicated that the State of Arizona has come to recognize the issue of connectivity in rural Arizona is a major issue, so the Arizona Corporation Commission is meeting and proposing to increase the Arizona Universal Service Fund fee to generate $8 million to have matching funds for E-rate which is designated to meet the needs of schools and libraries in rural Arizona. He spoke about prospective projects. He stated that he met with Nextera Energy a couple of weeks ago which is currently looking at Apache County for a wind turbine project for Apache County, which will result in significant jobs in the area. He discussed his work with WIA as the interim administrator and spoke about how many of the clients find jobs in the health care industry or trucking. He stated that the regional dispatch continues to move forward at a slow pace. Chairman Thompson inquired about what businesses Mr. Watson and his organization sees in the future. Mr. Watson indicated that there is significant infrastructure with the power plants and other possible uses for that infrastructure and stated that research is being done on a national level for that use. Vice-Chairman Williams thanked Mr. Watson for the update and the work that he is doing and indicated that NGS has a tremendous impact in the Navajo Nation, Coconino County and Navajo County.

e.

Budget Update: Bryan Layton gave a budget update.  He reported on the latest county sales tax and state shared tax, which is down.  He spoke about the communications which have occurred with Elected Officials, Department Directors, staff and residents and indicated that information has gone out in the County Manager note, press releases and social medial.   He indicated that the loss of the coal industry will result in a $1.2 million decrease in property tax, which affects county sales tax, which triggers a decrease in state shared tax.  He stated that those losses, combined with a 129% increase to EORP, will result in a critial loss to Navajo County's budget.  He stated that the loss of revenue that we are facing cannot be overcome by eliminating discretionary spending and that there needs to be a significant and important reduction in the general fund operating budget.  He spoke about the next steps moving forward and that we have to change the way we provide services.  He stated that he will have an initial list of reductions at the March 28th Board of Supervisors meeting.  He spoke of changes to how we approach Juvenile Detention, Child Support, Courts, overall reductions in staffing levels and changes to the County's overall footprint.  Chairman Thompson inquired regarding any large construction projects that we are committed to this year or next fiscal year.  Mr. Layton indicated that we do not have any at this time.    Vice-Chairman Williams inquired about whether everybody feels that they have a voice at the table.  Mr. Layton indicated that he has tried to be very open, has made himself available for questions, and the departments have started to send in their spending plans which include not only reductions, but the impact on the community and that this will be a continuing dialog for some time.  Supervisor Whitesinger spoke about our financial situation and indicated that she sees this as a team effort.  She inquired about ways Board members can inform their constituents better, including possible town halls and other meetings.  Vice-Chairman Williams thanked Mr. Layton for his continued efforts and indicated that we need to consider the human element, as well as make decisions based on the data.  Supervisor Jack spoke about the challenges ahead and about the President talking about allocating money for infrastructure and inquired whether the County had any plans for infrastructure they would like to submit.  He stated that the Navajo Nation is working really hard to try to keep the power plants open.  Chairman Thompson spoke about establishing partnerships to see how the facilities can be used in the future.

BOARD OF SUPERVISORS:

a.

Presentation by the Arizona Game and Fish Department regarding approaches to wildlife recreation opportunities in Navajo County and the White Mountains:  Chris Bagnoli, Arizona Game and Fish Department, addressed the Board regarding the Natural Resource Working Group.  He spoke about the Arizona Game and Fish Department's mission.  He spoke about the Pinetop Regional Resource Deployment and the land ownership in the region.   He stated that the Pinetop region is unique due to the diverse topography; the key relationships with the Apache Sitgreaves National Forest, rural counties, the four unique Native American tribes and the Morenci copper mine; commission-owned properties; and cold water fisheries resources.  He spoke about from where their budget comes and the population estimates.  He spoke about the changing customer base and their response to that change.  He gave information about fishing events at Patterson Ponds at St. Johns; fishing clinics across the region; annual Fools Hollow Lake fishing derby; Pinetop Regional office wetlands, ponds and trailhead; High Country Hummingbird Festival; Youth-Family Hunt Camps; Wildlife Workships; and Tiger Trout which have been placed in White Mountain Lake and Rim Lake; Navajo Nation Youth Hunt; Rocky Mountain Horn Sheep relocation; Second Knoll Target Range and Show Low Creek.  He spoke about the economic benefits of wildlife.   Vice-Chairman Williams thanked Mr. Bagnoli for the presentation.  Chairman Thompson asked about programs involving the schools, and Mr. Bagnolia indicated that there are programs which are offered across the state for youth to include a fishing clinic at the Navajo Nation.

PROBATION:

a.

Presentation of Share Hope Initiative:  Arno Hall spoke about best practices and how those have evolved over the years into evidence based practices.  He further spoke about the current laws which guarantee juveniles and their families rights under the Juvenile Court system.  He indicated that there have been significant strides in producing positive outcomes.  Lynda Wilson gave a presentation on the Share Hope Initiative and answered questions of the Board.  Chairman Thompson thanked Ms. Wilson for the presentation and her work with the youth.  Vice-Chairman Williams inquired about the likelihood of a juvenile re-offending if they are sent to ADJC in Phoenix.  He also inquired about video visitation in ADJC.  Ms. Wilson indicated that juveniles being detained away from home is detrimental to them due to not having their support system and being housed with other juveniles with antisocial behavior.  She stated that she does not believe ADJC is equipped with video conference at this time.  A discussion continued about recidivism of these juveniles.  Ms. Wilson spoke about the 15 core juveniles who are constantly in the facility, because there is nowhere else for them to go.  Supervisor Whitesinger inquired about the data and how the data is translated to Navajo County and how many of the juveniles are second and third time offenders.  Ms. Wilson indicated the data is specific to Navajo County and that last year, there were 166 repeat offenders.  Supervisor Whitesinger spoke of the correlation between education and incareration.  Vice-Chairman Williams inquired about the optimum miles away from home where juveniles are detained, and Ms. Wilson indicated a 30-mile radius.  Supervisor Jack thanked Mr. Hall and Ms. Wilson for the presentation.
