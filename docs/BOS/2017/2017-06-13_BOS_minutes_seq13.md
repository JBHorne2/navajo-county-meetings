Presentation to Northland Pioneer College employees and Navajo County employees who helped provide assistance during the United Way Volunteer Income Tax Assistance (VITA) program :  Eric Scott spoke about the United Way Volunteer Income Tax Assistance (VITA) program.  He recognized Leann Baker, Becky Healy and Cherie Camp for volunteering during the United Way Volunteer Income Tax Assistance Program. Steve Peru with United Way recognized Navajo County and Northland Pioneer College for their work with the VITA program.  He spoke about the history of United Way.  He further spoke about looking at each community individually and coordinating services with other United Ways.  He spoke about the different programs provided by United Way, including Kindercamp and Volunteer Organizations Active in Disasters (VOAD).   He spoke about different organizations United Way provides resources for through fundraising.  He stated that the funds raised locally remains in that community to assist those residents.  Ilene Sherlock, United Way, discussed the VITA program which provided free tax return services and indicated that 200 tax returns were prepared, which brought in $180,000.00 in refunds to individuals at no charge. c.

Presentation of Emergency Management update and fire season outlook and possible action entering into Stage One Fire Restrictions if conditions warrant it :  Mary Springer, Emergency Management Director, gave a fire season update and showed a short presentation on the fire season.  Mary Springer indicated that at first, we thought that there was a lot of moisture; however, we are now in full fire season, and we have some wildfires burning.  She spoke about 15 separate fires which were started along the side of the road caused by a commercial truck dragging a chain.  She indicated that from last year, 2016, to this year, the amount of human-caused fires is pretty even; however, the acreage is very different: 119,968 acres for last year and 132,670 acres so far this year. She spoke about the partnerships which began four years ago and the meetings which occur on a weekly basis to discuss how the season is looking in all areas with all agencies. She spoke of the criteria to enact fire restrictions before coming to the Board of Supervisors.  She spoke of the current conditions and recommendations.  Because of our current conditions, she recommended that the Board approve going into Stage One Fire Restrictions beginning Friday, June 16, at 8:00 a.m. until significant monsoon moisture minimizes the threat.  Ms. Springer spoke about Commercial Fireworks which fall under a different Navajo County Fireworks Ordinance. She stated that during Stage One fire restrictions, people can still use charcoal and fire as long as it is in a barbecue pit or at their home in a contained unit. She also gave an update on the mitigation efforts which include submitting three grants with the Wildland Hazardous Fuels Grants for Pinetop-Lakeside, Pinedale-Clay Springs and Heber-Overgaard, Prospective Firewise Communities, and a Firewise event in Pinetop which will be held on July 22.  Ms. Springer requested that the Board of Supervisors approve Stage One Fire Restrictions for Navajo County for all three zones. Chairman Thompson stated the need for talking about fire restrictions and awareness. Supervisor Whitesinger thanked Mary for building those partnerships and having a regional effort. She indicated that we are fortunate to live and recreate in this beautiful area; however, this often comes with a give and take and being conscientious about our behaviors around fire. Supervisor Jack also thanked Ms. Springer for her work and efforts for this fire season. He spoke about our individual responsibility in this matter. Motion by Supervisor Whitesinger to approve Stage One Fire Restrictions for Navajo County in all three zones beginning Friday, June 16, 2017, at 8:00 a.m., until significant monsoon moisture minimizes the threat; seconded by Supervisor Jack . Vote unanimous approving the motion.  Mr. Jayne thanked Ms. Springer and her team for their efforts and the partnerships.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE: Report from Board members, County Attorney and County Manager :  Supervisor Jack thanked his colleagues and staff for their good work. He reported on the status of Navajo Generating Station (NGS) replacement lease, indicating that the Navajo Nation Council has been deliberating on this issue for the last few weeks. He stated that this last week, it came before the full council, and there's a lot at stake. He spoke about how it will impact the counties, the tribe and the state and is a very touchy issue. He stated that they are trying to do their best to address it. He spoke about how the council looked at it, debated it for a while, and then they tabled it because there are some amendments being proposed in the document and some of the council members still want to discuss it and understand it. He indicated that the document is 8- to 900 pages long. He stated that there will be a work session on June 14 and then there will be a special meeting to try and pass it.  He stated that the consensus is to go ahead and do the two-year extension to 2019. He indicated that this is just a first step, and they have a deadline of July 1 set by the five owners. He stated that the document is still in the NAVA Committee, and, hopefully, the council will get it before July 1. He stated that he has been visiting the Chapters that he represents to explain the situation with them. He stated that he has not heard anything from Washington. He indicated that he should have more information at the next meeting.  Supervisor Whitesinger stated that she attended the ribbon cutting for the White Mountain Apache Tribe Fort Apache Connection Transit.  She stated that she attended a firewise in Pinetop on Saturday where they were talking about the same issues which were spoken of earlier.  She indicated that on Friday, she helped facilitate a town hall on Education in Show Low where she heard from people that they were invested in education and were okay if taxes were increased specifically for education. James Jayne tendered his letter of resignation effective Friday, August 4th, as the Navajo County Manager.  He stated that on Monday, August 7th, he will be starting as special initiatives director in the office of the Coconino County Manager, Cynthia Seelhammer.  He expressed gratitude for spending the last nearly 14 years at Navajo County.  He expressed appreciation for the elected officials, department directors and staff.  He requested that the Board of Supervisors appoint Bryan Layton as interim County Manager at an upcoming meeting. He spoke about Paige Peterson and Eric Scott and the great job that they do. He stated that he is leaving for no other reason than to take advantage of an incredible opportunity and that he is leaving the County in a good place.  He indicated that his last Board of Supervisors meeting will be July 25th. Chairman Thompson spoke about going for opportunities when they come. Supervisor Whitesinger  stated that the Navajo County team and the region will miss Mr. Jayne. Supervisor Jack expressed appreciation to Mr. Jayne. Chairman Thompson stated that we have a really good team who understand accountability and responsibility and that that team will be relied upon to continue to help Navajo County grow.   Chairman Thompson reported on how to generate more business here in Navajo County. He spoke about a meeting regarding the impacts of the closure of Navajo Generating Station, Kayenta Mine and Cholla Power Plant. He spoke about a meeting at Indian Wells during the elderly activities. He spoke about the Arizona Transportation Board meetings and the projects coming for Navajo, Apache and Coconino counties.  He stated that one project that has been recently approved is the La Posada project. He spoke about a number of other projects before the Arizona Transportation Board.
