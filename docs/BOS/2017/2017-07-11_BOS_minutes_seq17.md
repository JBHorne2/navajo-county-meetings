BOARD OF SUPERVISORS :

a.

Recognition of the White Mountain Apache Tribe, Chairman Ronnie Lupe and Transportation Director, Tara Chief, for their efforts in implementing the new Fort Apache Connection Transit service :  Supervisor Whitesinger thanked the Board for the opportunity to recognize the White Mountain Apache Tribe for the implementation of the new Fort Apache Connection Transit Service. She spoke about the transit system and how it helps the White Mountain Apache tribal communities. She spoke about the need for transportation in our everyday life. She spoke about her grandparents who lived in Cibecue and how they took a wagon to Globe to get married. She further spoke about the evolution in transportation. Supervisor Whitesinger recognized Tara Chief and her team and presented them with a certificate of appreciation. Tara Chief thanked Supervisor Whitesinger for the recognition and her continued support.  She further spoke about the services provided through the transit system. Supervisor Whitesinger thanked the Tribal Council and the Chairman's office for their support. Alvena Bush, District 4, thanked the Board of Supervisors for recognizing the White Mountain Apache Tribe and Tara Chief. She advised the Board that they are getting their road fixed from the Hospital to Fort Apache Canyon Day.  Councilwoman Colleen Faden, District 2, indicated that her district and Cibecue really needs the transportation system because they are farther away from Whiteriver. She thanked the Board for their support. Chairman Thompson thanked the Council for allowing Navajo County to meet with them. Chairman Thompson indicated that being able to provide this transit system helps our elderly, as well as our youth. He congratulated them all.

At 9:13 a.m., Brad Carlyon, County Attorney is present.

At 9:19 a.m., Lee Jack, Sr., Supervisor District I, is present.

b.

Recognition to Ronald LaMar, Marty LaMar, Don Murrow, Eric Kramer, Amy Hanridge and Carol Sletten for their contribution and work in our community to support Native Women Scholars, Inc.,which provides college assistance to local Native women :  Supervisor Whitesinger thanked the Board for allowing her the opportunity to recognize the Native Women Scholars, Inc. She spoke about Ron and Marty LaMar who initiated this investment into young people in the community. She spoke about how this group wanted to help their community and developed a scholarship organization specifically for women. She stated that they wanted their investment to be impactful, and it certainly has created a lasting impact and this last year they were able to impact the lives of 12 students who are attending universities throughout Arizona. She spoke about how Mr. and Mrs. Lamar have become the grandparents of these scholars and are available to them day or night to support them by sponsorship and through moral support.  Ron Lamar introduced Jerry Gloshay, Jr., Chairman Lupe's office; Don Murrow, Treasurer; Carol Sletten and Eric Kramer, council members. Mr. Lamar spoke about Ms. Sletten's and Mr. Kramer's book called "The Story of the American West. "  He stated that Ms. Sletten put on a play called "Three Strong Western Women," and she wanted to help a young Native American Girl, and five years ago, the organization began, and it has raised over $64,000 to give scholarships to Native American women. He recognized his wife, Marty Lamar. Mrs. Lamar spoke about the first four graduates:  Leann Clarkson, Northern Arizona University; Christine Jones, University of Arizona; Davette Susan, Fort Lewis College; and Pamela Morris, Fort Lewis College.  She indicated that they are so proud of these girls. Mr. Lamar stated that the mean GPA for all of the young women that the organization is helping is 3.79. Mr. Lamar spoke about the applicants and the interviews that are conducted prior to giving out the Scholarships. Supervisor Whitesinger spoke about the selflessness and the commitment of this organization.   Supervisor Williams stated that this group is inspiring and thanked them for their service in the community.   Supervisor Whiting thanked the group for their leadership and example in their community.  Supervisor Jack thanked the group for making a difference in these young women's lives.  Supervisor Whitesinger thanked the group for being present and giving her the opportunity to recognize them.  She thanked Brad Carlyon for his continued contribution to Native Women Scholars, Inc.

c.

Recognition of Melissa Buckley, CMC, for earning the designation of Certified Municipal Clerk, awarded by the International Institute of Municipal Clerks (IIMC), Inc .:  Adam Wolfe indicated that Melissa Buckley, Clerk of the Board, has received the designation of Certified Municipal Clerk (CMC), which required demanding education and demonstrations of significant contributions to local and state government.  He stated that this is a declaration that she is proficient in her profession and has demonstrated a mastery of administrative skills critical to Navajo County operations and good government practice.  He read a statement from Jonathan Nez, Vice-President of Navajo Nation and former Navajo County Supervisor.  Chairman Thompson indicated that a lot of the responsibility to support the citizens comes from Ms. Buckley and her team and thanked her for being who she is.  Vice-Chairman Williams congratulated Ms. Buckley.  He stated that she does not need those three letters behind her name, the Board of Supervisors already know what a superstar she is.  Supervisor Whitesinger stated that Ms. Buckley has been an example to her of quality leadership and that she understands the challenges Ms. Buckley faces being in a leadership role and being a mother and how Ms. Buckley has always demonstrated that she is a sound leader and sound person with a genuine heart.  Supervisor Whiting stated that Ms. Buckley is outstanding.  He stated that the CMC designation is letting others know what the Board already knows, that she is the best of the best.  He stated that he would describe Ms. Buckley with these five attributes:  determined, responsive, reliable, trustworthy and kind.  He indicated that he hears the amount of appreciation that people in the community have for Ms. Buckley.  Supervisor Jack congratulated Ms. Buckley for her achievement and believes it is well deserved.  He thanked her for always being there for the Board and that she is very important, and the County is very proud of her.  Ms. Buckley thanked the Board of Supervisors.  She stated that she is proud to serve Navajo County.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE: Report from Board members, County Attorney and Assistant County Manager :  Supervisor Jack thanked the County for being there for our constituents and thanked his colleagues. He spoke about the Red Dog project, indicating that it came to halt for a few weeks. He spoke about coming together with all of the partners in this important project, and Navajo Department of Transportation will step in, and the project will get started again. He thanked Glenn Kephart and Public Works for their help with this project. He spoke about the Navajo Generating Station closure being extended for another two years, with the impending closure being an impact to our communities and the State of Arizona.  Supervisor Whiting spoke about the activities that continue in and around his district. He spoke about the 4th of July celebrations, Wild West days this weekend in Holbrook and the Pioneer Day celebration in Snowflake and Taylor the following weekend.  Supervisor Whitesinger made comments about the community of Cibecue with regard to the water shortage and a suspected shooter being seen in the area.  She thanked the Sheriff and the Sheriff's Office for their partnership with the White Mountain Apache Tribe in trying to help to find the shooter.  She stated the community was grateful for their response to this community. She spoke about the difficult budget conversations and that being able to decide what is the best capacity in moving forward and being able to serve our community members and being able to think of our community members and each of us being strapped in our own ways and asking them to support our endeavors is a hard thing to do.  She stated that when we look at the budget capacities of our townships and our school systems, each of us is challenged in some way, all of us have our constraints and looking for better ways to do government.  She indicated that looking at a levy is not always the best way to do government, but when we look at the budget and taking each of the departments and being able to evaluate them and what are those ways we can cut back or eliminate services and how we do not want to be in that position.  She spoke about being forced to eliminate $2.5 million from our budget capacity is a great ask. She stated that these asks continue to come on the backs of our State lawmakers and asking us to be able to find those dollars within our capacity when we do not have those dollars, which forces us to be put in a position that we do not desire to be put in. She indicated that we are all hopeful and she wants to communicate the levity of this decision in being able to support our community and continue to provide services that with our partnership with the State and being an arm of the State, are required to provide, including judicial services, public safety services, and transportation services and not being able to provide those services becomes a constraint and difficult to do business. She expressed gratitude to the departments which helped us look at ways to better understand our budget and the organization and departments and ways we can be more effective in utilizing our dollars. She indicated that we certainly look to brighter days. Vice-Chairman Williams indicated that good things can come out of difficult circumstances. He spoke about one of the good things that came out of our revenue challenges and anticipated revenue challenges was the necessity to take a top-to-bottom look at how we provide the services that we do and is there a better way to do them. He stated that he believes that we have had a healthy conversation on better ways to do things which are more cost effective.  He stated that we are much smarter collectively than we are individually. He expressed appreciation to those who participated in those difficult conversations.  Paul Watson spoke about the process of applying for a grant of an economic assessment of Navajo and Apache Counties. He stated that it is our intent to do an assessment of our local economy and the dependence on coal and what are the opportunities for us to take infrastructure and other assets related to coal and put those to use in other industries.  Brad Carlyon spoke about the partnership that Navajo County has with the White Mountain Apache Tribe and indicated that we had to have permission to go onto tribal land to search for the shooting suspect. He expressed appreciation for the partnership and cooperation that we have with the Tribe. Chairman Thompson spoke about his meetings with the State Transportation Board. He spoke about a meeting coming up regarding Copper Mine and thanked Glenn Kephart for his help. He spoke about making a presentation to the Fort Defiance Agency Council. He spoke about a meeting with Sharon Pinto, the regional director with BIA, regarding road projects and their possible agreement for a road grader operator using our grader. He stated that the money will come from Navajo County, turned over to BIA and they will hire a grader operator to get on BIA roads and go on roads on Navajo land and Hopi land. He indicated that he is also meeting with the Hopi Tribe regarding the Winslow Levee.
