CALL TO THE PUBLIC :  Delayed until after Item 12.  At 10:38 a.m. Judge Michala Ruechel addressed the Board regarding the proposals to close down the Juvenile Detention Center and Child Support Enforcement.  She expressed support to the concerns previously expressed by Brad Carlyon.  She stated that she has not seen a copy of the specific proposal regarding the Juvenile Detention Facility, but asked that the Board look at the proposal not only through the eyes of the guardians of public funds, but also through the eyes of the law, the juveniles, their parents, law enforcement and the citizens of this county.  She requested that the Board consider the following things:  1) Is closing the detention facility the right and just thing to do? 2) How much will the proposal really save in tax payer funds? 3) How will closing the detention facility affect public safety? and 4) Are there additional risks to the juveniles?  She addressed how closing the detention facility will affect their ability to continue the current efforts to reduce recidivism.  She stated that closing the facility may create public safety risks as officers are required to leave the county to transport juveniles. She addressed the issue of whether there would be a true savings to Navajo County and requested that the Board consider the following:  Does that include the loss of revenue from BIA and Apache County contracts? Do the figures include medical expenses related to juveniles?  Do the figures include mental health services for the juveniles?  Do the figures include transportation?  What will happen if ADJC is closed and numbers go up?  What will happen if the new facility raises rates due to higher numbers or they have to bring in more staff?  If the juveniles are released, who pays to get them back to Navajo County?  If their parents are unable to pick them up, what is that detention facility supposed to do?  She spoke about the juveniles' Sixth Amendment right to meaningfully confer with counsel.  She spoke about additional risks to the juveniles, including being transported over difficult roads, being in need of emergency medication, families being unable to afford travel to see their juveniles, and our juveniles will be considered outsiders.  She stated that many juveniles are incarcerated pre-adjudication and are presumed innocent and have constitutional rights.  She indicated that closing this facility will take jobs away from individuals who, as a group, have devoted over 100 years of service to Navajo County.  She asked the question of where members of the Board would want their child to go.  Arno Hall spoke in support of Judge Ruechel's comments.  He spoke about the presentation he made at the last meeting and stated that over the past 4 years, there has been significant traffic through the Juvenile Detention Center, about 332 bookings each year, most of which are pre-adjudication juveniles.  He requested that the Board take into consideration Judge Ruechel's comments and the data which has been shared and vote against the closure of the facility.  Chief Dan Brown, Northeastern Arizona Police Association President, addressed the Board regarding the closure of the Navajo County Juvenile Detention Center.  He spoke about the IGA between Apache County and Navajo County. He stated that this is the last juvenile detention center in northeastern Arizona.  He requested that the Board delay the vote and bring the issue to the  law enforcement agency heads to be able to discuss what is going to happen and research the issue further.  Sheriff K C Clark indicated that the issue of closing the Juvenile Detention Center has been discussed for some time with city managers and so forth.  He stated that the Navajo County Sheriff's Office booked and arrested 35 juveniles last year of which 7 were booked in.  He indicated that he is committed to looking at ways of utilizing our already existing transportation system to transport the juveniles to a different facility.  He spoke about the dire financial trouble Navajo County is in and that this closure would be an anticipated savings of over $600,000.00.  He expressed commitment to make this work if the Board votes in favor of closing the Juvenile Detention Center.  He further expressed support to the Board either way they vote.  Lynda Wilson addressed the Board regarding the closing of the Juvenile Detention Center.  She stated that she believes that the subject matter experts were not brought to the table and included in the discussion.  She further stated that she believes that the $600,000.00 savings is speculative, but there are many collateral costs which have not been considered.  She indicated that other associated costs which will begin as early as next year will offset the projected savings.  She spoke about the reduction in recidivism among juveniles who are incarcerated closer to home and that incarcerating them 200 miles away will lead to increased costs.   She spoke about the undue burden on juveniles and their parents who are not able to make that trip for visitation and do not have the technology for video conferencing should that become available at the facility.  She requested that the Board reject the proposal to close the Juvenile Detention Center.

ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES :

a.

Budget Update :  Bryan Layton gave a brief budget update.  He stated that the issue we're facing in Navajo County is the loss of an entire industry, which will result in a $1.2 million loss in property tax collections.  He indicated that sales tax information is confidential and cannot be disclosed publicly until permission is given by business owners.  He stated that the total loss in sales tax will be greater than the loss in property tax.  He spoke about the Elected Officials Retirement Plan increasing 129%.  He spoke about the increased cost of doing business.  He stated that our situation is not unique, and other areas are struggling.  He spoke about the different ways that the message has been shared with employees and the community.  He spoke about the substantial changes in our County over the past decade:  A 12% reduction in staffing, a 254% increase in PSPRS costs, a 40% increase in healthcare costs, and no regular wage increases for Staff except a 2% base wage increase in 2014.  He also spoke about how there has been no regular fleet vehicle, heavy equipment or IT infrastructure replacement.   He indicated that there has been $8.8 million in State cost shifts.  He spoke about the $1.3 million for housing federal inmates which is not a secure source of funding.  He further spoke about State Supreme Court mandated Court security. He spoke about the next steps and proposals for Board consideration and action and that staff will continue to work with elected officials and department directors to develop reduction proposals.  He spoke about working to help impacted employees.
Chairman Thompson indicated that we have met with our legislators and have explained to them what has been taken that we need our revenues back, and we are moving forward trying to retrieve as much funding as we can.  Supervisor Whiting stated that there has been and continues to be a consistent effort to get our message to the legislators and although they are hearing us, they are not taking any action.  Supervisor Williams thanked staff for the work that they continue to do and stated that budget discussions have been going on for many days.  Mr. Layton indicated that the announcement of NGS has been a huge surprise.  James Jayne indicated that the Salt River Project, who is one of the 4 owners and the operator of NGS, are in discussions with Navajo Nation regarding a two-year extension of the 2019 agreement; but if there is no agreement by July 1st of this year, NGS will close December of 2019.  Supervisor Jack spoke about the closure of NGS and the concerns to the Navajo Nation and the tribes.  He indicated that the tribes are working very hard to help in the solution to keep the plant open further than 2019.  He stated that the closure of NGS is really impacting all of Navajo County and northern Arizona.  He stated that there have been meetings with the approximately 400 miners who face losing their jobs.  Mr. Layton stated that if NGS extends for two years, then we would continue to receive the sales tax money and property taxes; however, that will delay the tough conversations for two years.  Supervisor Whiting inquired whether the trend will continue downwards if we prolong for two years, and Mr. Layton indicated that, yes, the whole industry seems to be ramping down.  Chairman Thompson inquired regarding in what ways we can hope to generate revenue.  Mr. Layton indicated that communication of our message to legislators by pulling all of our voices together with the rest of the counties and bringing in new businesses can help.

b.

Consideration to discontinue Child Support Services effective June 30, 2017 : Bryan Layton discussed the item.  He proposed to discontinue our Child Support Services administered by the County Attorney's Office.  He stated that Child Support Services is ultimately the responsibility of the State Attorney General's Office.  He further stated that closing Child Support Services will result in a savings to the general fund of $200,000.00 annually.  Brad Carlyon stated that this is the 8th budget cycle and the 8th time that he has fought to keep the Child Support Services in Navajo County operating due to the fact that we can provide those services better than the Attorney General's Office can.  He stated that the Attorney General's Office does not have the responsibility to run Child Support Services but they have the co-existing ability to do so.  He stated that for some, this is a savings of $200,000.00; but, for him, it is the loss of friends.  Chairman Thompson indicated that we have seen over time how much effort has been put into the budget, and he realizes the impacts that this will have.  Vice-Chairman Williams thanked Mr. Carlyon for his comments and agreed that local control is always better.  He also stated that he knows that the issues which are brought before the Board are financial, but there is also the human element.  He spoke about how the Attorney General's Office provides this service for 13 of the 15 counties presently, and our citizens will continue to receive this service.  He thanked Mr. Carlyon for the service that his office has provided to the citizens of Navajo County.  Supervisor Whiting thanked Mr. Carlyon for his efforts.  He stated that Child Support Services is not a mandated service by the County, and these services will continue to be provided to our citizens through the State.  He stated that the Board understands that this decision will affect our people.  Supervisor Whitesinger thanked Mr. Layton for tackling this challenging decision and helping us to be better informed.  She spoke of the relationship with the State lawmakers and the partnerships we have with the State.  She stated that when we are faced with the tough decisions such as this, the dedication from our County employees and the effects that it will have on our community members and the human factor is the first factor that we are looking at.  She spoke about the partnerships with the State agencies. She expressed appreciation for the leadership of Mr. Carlyon.   Motion by Supervisor Whiting to approve discontinuing Child Support Services effective June 30, 2017, motion seconded by Vice-Chairman Williams.  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

c.

Consideration of closing the Juvenile Detention Facility effective June 30, 2017 : Bryan Layton discussed the item.  He spoke about the annual cost of the juvenile detention center being $1.2 million which does not include the meals and medical provided from the Sheriff.  He stated that we house an average of 7.1 juvenile offenders daily in the Juvenile Detention Center, 78.9% from Navajo County, 18% from Apache County and 3% from BIA/DPS/Other.  He stated there are 16 full-time positions and 7 part-time positions which currently staff the detention center.  He indicated that there are 4 other counties within the State of Arizona which have contract agreements for juvenile detention services.  He spoke about the different options which were explored, including increasing the population and partnering with Coconino County. He stated that the 2 most realistic partners would be Graham County and Pinal County, with Graham County holding more potential and would be an annual rate.  He spoke about the potential for providing treatment services through a regional partnership.   He stated that we will continue to partner with Apache County to provide transport, and the Sheriff has offered his support.   He spoke about the opportunity to explore technology to allow for a face-to-face connection between juveniles, legal counsel and families.  Chairman Thompson stated that he understands the concerns which have been expressed.  Supervisor Whitesinger inquired whether closing the detention center is truly cost effective and whether the projected savings is annual.  Mr. Layton stated that the $600,000.00 savings is an annual projected savings.  Supervisor Whiting inquired regarding the legality of closing the detention center.  Mr. Carlyon stated that Title 8, dealing with detention centers, indicates that the presiding juvenile judge shall be over the detention center and gives the County the right to partner with public or private entities to run a detention center but does not explicitly state that that detention center has to be in the County.  Supervisor Whiting asked Sheriff Clark regarding the risk of having detention officers transporting juveniles to Graham or Pinal County.  Sheriff Clark indicated that the Sheriff's Office travels across the State, and there have been two incidents in 30 years.  He stated that every precaution is taken for safety.  Supervisor Whiting inquired regarding how the average daily count of juveniles in the detention center was derived.  Mr. Layton stated that he has received daily counts which have been added up and averaged.  Supervisor Whiting asked that Mr. Layton state why he feels confident in the projected savings.  Mr. Layton stated that he feels confident that we will receive the $600,000.00 savings and that it is a very solid estimate. Vice-Chairman Williams indicated that one of the concerns raised in call to the public was that stakeholders have not been talked to about the proposal.  Mr. Layton indicated that reductions have been discussed at the judge level, not the staff level.  He spoke about discussions with other counties and has been meeting with the judges.  He stated that we will need input from staff moving forward.  He indicated that he met with Arno Hall within the last two weeks.  Vice-Chairman Williams indicated that he believes that conversations on this have been going on for a few months now.  He stated that the initial conversation was reaching out to neighboring counties to see if they would partner with us to bring their juveniles here.  Mr. Jayne indicated that eastern Arizona counties have been in discussion over a significant amount of time on ways to enhance the capacity in our existing juvenile detention facility in Navajo County.  He stated that he has been visiting with the County Manager in Graham County regarding what a regional detention facility would look like.  He indicated that there were discussions with Apache County, and they were not willing to pay additional money to us to house their juveniles.  Vice-Chairman Williams indicated that he was surprised when stakeholders indicated that they were not part of the conversation, because he knows that staff has met with at least three of the four Superior Court judges in multiple meetings, staff has talked with other county managers, all of the town managers, Sheriff's Office, County Attorney's Office and there was a presentation by Juvenile Probation at the last Board meeting.  He stated that this proposal is difficult on a lot of levels.  He indicated that there is a significant cost savings to the County.  He spoke about representing juveniles as an attorney and that a lot of the juveniles do not have family support.  He stated that partnering with other counties may result in being able to provide a higher level of counseling, treatment and services due to resources.  He stated that he does not disagree with Judge Ruechel, and everyone wants what's best for the juveniles and what's best for the County as a whole.  He thanked all those who presented their viewpoints.  Supervisor Whiting inquired whether the proposal was for partnering with either Graham or Pinal County.  Mr. Jayne stated that we have two counties that are both viable options for us, and we understand what the pricing structure will be, and he is confident that the $600,000.00 is a solid estimate.  He stated that this is not unprecedented and that there are four counties which are currently contracting for juvenile detention services.  He stated that we will figure this out moving forward to finalize all of the details.  Supervisor Whitesinger inquired regarding whether we looked at other tribal communities as another resource.  Mr. Jayne indicated that over the years, options have been explored for federal contracts, and we get an occasional youth from the BIA, but none of those options have come to fruition.  Supervisor Whitesinger spoke about being accountable to the entire community.  She stated that she believes that we have vetted this and that this will send a clear message to our legislators that we are in a financial crisis.   Motion by Supervisor Whitesinger to approve closing the Juvenile Detention Facility effective June 30, 2017, motion seconded by Vice-Chairman Williams.   Supervisor Jack opposed the motion.   Chairman Thompson , Vice-Chairman Williams , Supervisor Whiting and Supervisor Whitesinger approved the motion.  Motion passed 4-1.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE: Report from Board members, County Attorney and County Manager : Supervisor Jack spoke about his vote in opposition of closing the Juvenile Detention Center and indicated that he feels the issue could have been further explored.  Supervisor Whiting indicated that this was not an easy day.  He spoke of recognizing our youth at the beginning of the meeting.  He stated that there are still more hard decisions to make going forward.  He spoke about the mandated services that we are to provide and how we need to do business differently.  Supervisor Whitesinger indicated that today is a rough day, and difficult decisions were made which impact several lives.  She stated that this is always about the people that we serve.  She spoke of the human aspect of these tough decisions.  Vice-Chairman Williams thanked staff for collecting all of the data and expressed his appreciation for the efforts of staff.  He stated that today we made some tough decisions and that going forward, we need to make sure that those were the right decisions.  Chairman Thompson spoke about the hard decisions made regarding our employees and the youth in our communities.  He spoke about his first meeting with the Transportation Committee and that he looks forward to upcoming meetings.  Brad Carlyon indicated that the County is dying a death of mandated cuts.  He stated that he wished the legislature would wake up.
