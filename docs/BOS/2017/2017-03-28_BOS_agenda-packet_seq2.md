The Navajo County Board of Supervisors will hold a meeting open to the public on Tuesday, March 28, 2017, in the
Supervisors’ Chamber in the Navajo County Governmental Complex, Holbrook, Arizona. The Board may vote to
meet in a closed executive session to discuss certain matters as indicated on the following agenda. The Board
may meet in executive session pursuant to A.R.S. § 38-431.03(A) (3) for legal advice on any item.
Persons with disabilities who need accommodation to attend or participate in the meeting may contact the Clerk of the
Board’s office at (928) 524-4053; at least 48 hours prior to the meeting so an accommodation can be arranged.
Items on the agenda may be considered out of order at the Chairman’s discretion. A copy of the agenda background
material provided to the Board, with the exception of items to be considered in executive session, is available for public
inspection at Navajo County website, www.navajocountyaz.gov

AGREEMENT FOR PROFESSIONAL SERVICES 
 
THIS FEE AGREEMENT  (“Agreement”) is made and entered into this 28th day of 
March, 2017, by and between Navajo County hereinafter referred to as “NAVAJO COUNTY”), 
and M & O Agencies, Inc., an Arizona corporation doing business as The Mahoney Group, 
(hereinafter referred to as “TMG”). This agreement will commence on April 1, 2017. 
 
WHEREAS, NAVAJO COUNTY, desires to engage with TMG to render certain 
professional services related to NAVAJO COUNTY Employee Benefit programs. 
 
WHEREAS, NAVAJO COUNTY, understands the fee set forth in this Agreement is in 
addition to commissions TMG receives for the Voluntary Individual and/or Group Policies (i.e. 
accident, critical illness, gap, etc.) of NAVAJO COUNTY. 
 
NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the premises and mutual obligations herein, the 
parties hereto do mutually agree as follows: 
 
 1. Scope of Services. TMG shall perform the following services related to 
NAVAJO COUNTY’s Employee Benefit programs (hereinafter referred to as the 
“Services”) in a satisfactory and proper manner, as reasonably determined by 
NAVAJO COUNTY: 
 
A.  Consulting 
 
1. Provide on-going information, analysis and assistance regarding 
health care and life insurance guidelines, legislation and trends. 
2. Assist in the negotiation of rate adjustments and/or benefit changes 
with the existing carriers on contract renewal dates, including 
complete evaluation of all plan reserves, retention costs and plan 
design. 
3. Provide analysis for alternative funding methods and 
recommendation for change, if advantageous. 
4. Assist in determination of the need to bid existing group insurance 
contracts, including third party administrators. 
5. Assist and recommend risk management strategies to sustain 
employee benefits and reduce total healthcare costs. 
6. Employee Wellness programs 
 
B.  Technical Services 
 
1. Assist in the review and negotiation of benefits contract provisions 
with carrier to assure employee benefits and rights are protected.

<!-- page 33 -->
2. Assist in developing strategies for the next renewal/contract period 
in a manner, which assures Navajo County’s interests are properly 
addressed. 
3. Prepare reports that may be required to maintain historical 
information on expenditures, rates and participation levels. 
4. Assist on projecting the annual cost of benefits programs to 
determine budgetary rates and amounts. 
 
C.  Serviceable Lines of Coverage 
 
1. Assist in the review and negotiation of benefits contract provisions 
with carrier to assure employee benefits and rights are protected. 
2. Assist in developing strategies for the next renewal/contract period 
in a manner, which assures Navajo County’s interests are properly 
addressed. 
3. Prepare reports that may be required to maintain historical 
information on expenditures, rates and participation levels. 
4. Assist on projecting the annual cost of benefits programs to 
determine budgetary rates and amounts. 
 
D.  Open Enrollment Services 
 
1. Assist with all open enrollment activities including employee 
meetings, benefit counselor coordination, etc. 
2. Assist in compiling and coordinating all communication materials 
on behalf of Human Resources and other entities covered by the 
employee benefit plans. 
 
E.   Comply with the terms and conditions of the HIPAA Business Associate 
Agreement attached hereto and incorporated herein as Exhibit A. 
 
2. NAVAJO COUNTY’S Obligations.  NAVAJO COUNTY agrees to the following: 
 
A.  Assist in obtaining data from the carriers. 
 
B.  Define direction and appropriate action to TMG. 
 
C.  Comply with the terms and conditions of the HIPAA Business Associate 
Agreement attached hereto and incorporated herein as Exhibit A.  
 
3. Time of Performance.  Services of TMG shall commence on April 1, 2017, and shall 
be undertaken and completed in such sequence as to assure their expeditious 
completion in light of the purposes of this Agreement. 
 
4. Compensation and Method of Payment. 
 
A.   This Agreement reflects a 36 month rate guarantee.

<!-- page 34 -->
B. Compensation.  For performing the Services specified in Section 1 
hereof, Navajo County agrees to have the selected Third Party 
Administrator (TPA) pay TMG $14.00 Per Employee Per Month. 
 
C. Method of Payment.  Such amounts shall be paid to TMG on the 1st day 
of each month during the term of this Agreement.  
 
D. Broker of Record. TMG will receive the standard commissions, fees and 
revenue on the ancillary lines that are currently in place. TMG will 
manage (Serviceable Lines of Coverage) and services on behalf of Navajo 
County. 
 
5. Indemnity.   TMG agrees to defend, indemnify and hold harmless Navajo County 
and its employees from and against any and all claims, actions, suits, or 
proceedings of any kind brought against said parties for or on account of any 
matter arising solely from the willful misconduct or negligent conduct, whether 
active or passive, on the part of TMG or TMG’s employees, subconsultants or 
agents from the services performed by TMG under this Agreement. 
 
Navajo County agrees to defend, indemnify and hold harmless TMG and its 
employees from and against any and all claims, actions, suits, or proceedings of 
any kind arising solely from the willful misconduct or negligent conduct, whether 
active or passive, on the part of Navajo County or Navajo County employees, 
subconsultants or agents for or on account of any actions taken by  Navajo County 
under this Agreement. 
6. Insurance.  TMG shall procure and maintain at its expense, until final payment 
by Navajo County for Services covered by this Agreement, Workers’ 
Compensation Insurance for TMG’s employees in accordance with the provisions 
of the Workers’ Compensation Act of the State of Arizona as now enacted or 
hereafter amended.  
 
7. Reports and Information.  At such times and in such forms as Navajo County 
may reasonably require, there will be furnished to Navajo County such 
statements, records, reports, data and information, as Navajo County may request 
pertaining to matters covered by this Agreement.  Unless authorized by Navajo 
County, or under order of a court or administrative body, TMG will not release 
any information concerning the work product including, but not limited to, any 
reports or other documents prepared pursuant to this Agreement until the final 
product is submitted to Navajo County. 
 
8. Establishment and Maintenance of Electronic Records.  Electronic Records 
shall be maintained by TMG in accordance with applicable law and requirements 
prescribed by Navajo County in writing with respect to all matters covered by this 
Agreement.  Except as otherwise authorized by Navajo County, such electronic

10. Compliance With Laws.  In performing the Services required hereunder, TMG 
shall comply with all applicable laws, ordinances, and codes of the Federal, State

Agreement attached hereto and incorporated herein as Exhibit A, represents the

Agreement at any time and for any reason by giving at least sixty (60) days’ 
notice in writing to the other party.   If termination occurs prematurely, both

of 1996, Public Law 104-191, known as “the Administrative Simplification provisions,” direct the 
Department of Health and Human Services to develop standards to protect the security, confidentiality 
and integrity of health information; and  
 
WHEREAS, pursuant to the Administrative Simplification provisions, the Secretary of Health and 
Human Services has issued regulations modifying 45 CFR Parts 160 and 164 (the “HIPAA Privacy Rule”

but not limited to, the Security Breach Notification; and

Security Rule, and other good and valuable consideration, the receipt and sufficiency of which is hereby 
acknowledged, the Parties agree to the provisions of this Agreement in order to address the requirements 
of the HIPAA Privacy Rule and Security Rule and to protect the interests of both Parties

Section shall have the definitions set forth in the HIPAA Privacy Rule and the HIPAA 
Security Rule. In the event of an inconsistency between the provisions of this Agreement

the HIPAA Privacy Rule and Security Rule shall control.

as the term “business associate” at 45 CFR 160.103, and in reference to the party 
to this agreement, shall mean The Mahoney Group.

Notification, and Enforcement Rules at 45 CFR Part 160 and Part 164.

Proclamation 
  National Crime Victims’ Rights Week 
WHEREAS, Americans are the victims of more than 20 million crimes each year, affecting 
individuals, and communities; and 
WHEREAS, providing victims with knowledge of their rights and available services further 
strengthens their ability to recover by restoring a sense of self-empowerment; and  
WHEREAS, Unaddressed trauma from victimization weakens the resilience of victims and their 
communities, impeding their ability to withstand future trauma; and 
WHEREAS, Victims who feel understood and supported are more likely to disclose their 
victimization, seek services, and participate in the justice process; and 
WHEREAS, A multidisciplinary response, involving collaboration among victim service 
professionals, criminal justice officials, legal professionals, medical and mental health providers, 
and community leaders is essential to reach and serve all victims-especially those who are 
marginalized, have disabilities, or live in remove locations; and  
WHEREAS, Navajo County is hereby dedicated to strengthening victims and survivors in the 
aftermath of crime, building resilience in our communities and our victim responders and 
working for justice for all victims and survivors. 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT PROCLAIMED, by the Board of Supervisors of Navajo County and the 
Navajo County Attorney do hereby proclaim the week of April 2-8, 2017, as “National Crime 
Victims’ Rights Week” in Navajo County, Arizona, in honor of victims of crime that need relevant 
assistance, support, justice and peace.

1. Briefly describe the organization’s history, mission and goals. 
Navajo County and the Cities and Towns in the region are committed to providing the best quality of life for its 
citizens.  This idyllic area contains a full range of municipal services, a modern hospital network, natural gas 
and power utility, and a positive outlook for residential, recreational and business growth. The region’s 
residents and visitors, rely on the services of the first responders. Reliable, effective emergency 
communications is essential for sustaining and protecting the County and surrounding neighborhoods. While 
the need for public safety continues to grow, the economy in Navajo County, however, continues to diminish. 
Over one third of the population lives below the poverty line, yet we see significant surge in population during 
summer months when absentee home owners settle in for another season with desirable weather and 
recreational options. In Navajo County, “doing more with less” is more than a current challenge, it’s a way of 
life.  
Navajo County, in the northeast region of Arizona, is a large (@ 10,000 square miles), rural county that is 
bisected by Interstate 40 and the Burlington Northern Santa Fe Railroad, which run parallel to each other. 
Uniquely located such that a cross-country trip to the Midwest or eastern part of the United States, other than 
traveling east on Interstate 10, would run through Navajo County. These two major transportation 
thoroughfares (including truck routes) are critical pieces of the region’s infrastructure. Disruption or wholesale 
closure would cause substantial economic damage to the region. Additionally, a major disruption to these 
transportation networks could create significant response delays to other areas of the state. Both systems are 
means of transporting a wide variety of resources including hazardous materials and illicit items in a multitude 
of forms and modes. It is for this reason that the Office of National Drug Control Policy (ONDCP) designated 
Navajo County as a High Intensity Drug Trafficking Area (HIDTA) in 2010.  
In addition to the transportation of illegal drugs as well as their proceeds, law enforcement teams in the 
region routinely handle transnational cases regarding identity theft, human smuggling / trafficking, and other 
organized criminal enterprises. The Federal Bureau of Investigation, Drug Enforcement Administration, and 
Homeland Security Investigations, have partnered with Navajo County Law Enforcement creating task forces in 
the region to execute their federal mission combatting drug production and trafficking, and reduce drug-
related crime, violence, and disease.  
The Public Safety Community in this region recognizes the need to be able to respond quickly and investigate 
emergency situations regardless of their source or cause, particularly areas with known criminal and drug 
activity and those at high risk for threats of terrorism. We seek assistance in expanding the access to what will 
become a regionally-shared database for law enforcement agencies to fill the accessibility gaps in a way that

3. Describe the proposed project and planned goals and activities. 
The proposed CAD Networking Project (CNP), proposed herein, will test the viability of sharing real-time 
incident data for situational awareness, in a multi-jurisdictional public safety setting, using National 
Information Exchange Model (NIEM) and Global Reference Architecture (GRA) standards. These standards, 
renowned in the justice community for supporting information sharing activities that span jurisdictional 
boundaries, meeting the needs of government at all levels, fulfill the need for improved collaboration across 
law enforcement and fire / medical service communities like ours.  
CAD incident information will be shared using open-source geospatial tools. The project will demonstrate the 
capability to transmit a selected set of safeguarded “incident” data set from an existing CAD system to a 
common network environment, where the incident data will be hosted for sharing by any interested and 
authorized consumers from the public safety, homeland security, and other related public service disciplines.  
The efforts of the CAD networking project team will leverage recognized and emerging promising practices 
and Global-supported information sharing policies, practices, technology solutions, and existing reference 
exchanges. Through these efforts, the project team will: 
 Have a substantial positive impact across traditionally disparate public safety organizations’ networks; 
 Enhance the day-to-day operations for emergency communications centers, law enforcement, fire 
service, EMS, emergency management and others; 
 Enhance operational functions and coordination of incident response; 
 Have a direct and positive impact to overall public safety in the region. 
The scope of this project is to extend access of the NCSO Spillman CAD System to the local public safety 
agencies in the County including their 9-1-1 PSAP, Dispatch Center, Management and Patrol Staff, which will 
include the enhanced sharing of public safety-related data between North County public safety agencies , and 
Navajo County Sheriff’s respective offices.  Further, by expanding access to this data out to the field, 
investigators and patrol officers have real-time access to the data, improving their overall efficiency. 
 
A successful showing in this project will be used as a springboard to providing access to  local public safety 
agencies throughout Navajo County.

March 20, 2017 
  
From Judge David Widmaier, Justice of the Peace / Magistrate, Pinetop-Lakeside. 
  
An open letter to the Board of Supervisors of Navajo County and Elected Officials of the 
Judiciary and Criminal Justice System. 
  
Good afternoon: 
I am writing you to discuss issues that have been brought to our attention regarding possible 
significant changes to our court system that may dramatically impact our ability to do justice in 
our courts. 
  
None of these rumored changes have been vetted with me or, other Justices of the Peace or 
Constables. I have not seen the County manager in more than a year, nor has Judge Little, Judge 
Peterson or Judge Nelson. The manager had one brief meeting with Judge Marez to tell her they 
were closing her building and moving her court, the Winslow Court and Kayenta courts into the 
1,350 square foot basement in the county complex and she should expect the move in 
September. 
  
The manager has met with Judge Price to look at moving to the White Mountain Facility in the 
fall. And that’s it. We have been given no specifics about anything. And when we ask, we get 
no specific responses of any kind. These rumors are causing a stunning amount of stress on our 
staff all due to the lack of transparency and a county manager with his finger firmly on the 
panic button. 
  
We have heard they want to consider eliminating the Pinetop and Snowflake Justice Court 
precincts. So where will this new “mega” court go. It won’t fit in the White Mountain facility 
building in Show Low. The space for the new JP court is 4,100 square feet. Currently Pinetop 
and Show Low JP courts combined take up more than 10,000 square feet with a total of 15 
employees. Pinetop, Show Low and Snowflake combined would require 22 plus employees and 
more than 12,000 square feet. This would be the largest court in all of rural Arizona. It would 
be bigger than the Flagstaff Justice Court which has 2 full time judges, a full time Constable, a 
deputy constable, 22 employees and supervisors. Eliminating the Pinetop precinct will reduce 
the staff by 1 judge and 1 constable, however the new judge and constable will both be 
receiving significant pay raises in such a precinct. So where is the savings? And at what cost? 
  
None of these issues have been vetted in any significant way with the JPs or Constables.   
  
The manager has met with city managers and outlined the doom and gloom scenario, but done 
little more than throw out wild ideas for creating mega courts in the north and south counties. 
Nothing specific, no real numbers for savings of any kind. And despite the manager’s apparent 
assertions, the city managers are not all on board. They understand the challenges we all face, 
but not the solutions proposed. When I spoke with my town manager his response was “why are 
you telling us and not talking with the judges?”

<!-- page 123 -->
Pinetop-Lakeside city court will not be moving to Show Low. I have those assurances from our 
town council. And that will cost Navajo County $73,000 in lost revenues from fines, fees and 
prosecution reimbursement. Again, where’s the savings. 
  
Regarding these unknown proposals: 
Have we utilized a fact based analytical decision making process to make informed decisions?  
Have we invited key stake holders to participate? 
Have we gathered all the relevant information? 
Have we explored all options? 
Have we considered all the consequences? 
Has a comprehensive impact statement been prepared? 
Has any form of implementation process and procedures document been completed? 
Has a public communications campaign process and procedure document been completed? 
Has management worked with respective City and Town Councils? 
Has management vetted any of these ideas with the Tribal authorities? 
What documentation exists to demonstrate any financial savings? 
  
I cannot answer any of these questions as the Judiciary has mostly been uninvited to the table. 
Except for a couple of lunch meetings the manager has had with Superior Court, there has been 
only doom and gloom discussions about possible budget cuts. But no concrete discussions of 
any plans. Secret meetings without public vetting and full transparency is not the way we do 
business in our courts. 
  
My request is simple: The Judiciary wants to be on the March 28 agenda in a significant way – 
we want to be heard, not put on the chopping block. 
  
We look forward to working with you through these challenging times. 
  
  
Sincerely, 
  
David L. Widmaier
