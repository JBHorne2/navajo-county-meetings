9:01 a.m . Call To Order: Pledge of Allegiance and Invocation was led by Vice-Chairman Williams .

CALL TO THE PUBLIC :  JoLynn Fox and Leann Baker from the Navajo County Historical Society invited the Board to lunch at the Historical Society sometime. She indicated that they have had approximately 753,000 people go through the Historical Courthouse since the visitor center was opened. She indicated that the building is 119 years old and is having some issues. She stated that she believes that this is a direct reflection on Navajo County because it is the Navajo County Historical Society. She asked the Board of Supervisors for help with this building indicating that a new roof is needed. She indicated that in 1998 there was some work done which created more damage. She thanked the Board for their help over the years, including the air conditioning last year. She stated that they have caulked some windows and changed some lightening fixtures to LED, and that saves on electric costs for the annex. She again invited the Board to come visit for lunch. Chairman Thompson indicated that there is a lot of history in that building and the jail was used up until 1976. Vice-Chairman Williams directed staff to set up lunch with the Historical Society. Chairman Thompson also directed staff to set up a meeting with Mr. Kephart and our Building and Grounds Department.

Consideration of award of Contract #B17-08-010 Supply of Aggregate for Cover Materials to Brimhall Sand, Rock & Building Materials, Inc., Perkins Cinders, Inc., and Hatch Construction & Paving, Inc .:   Jeanine Carruthers discussed the item.  She indicated that this contract is for supply of aggregate for cover material.  She indicated that Type A 3/8 inch came in at the same price as last year, the 1/2 inch came in about ten cents under per ton; and the Type B, which is not commonly utilized, 3/8 inch came in at $1.00 over, and the 1/2 inch came in at $5.00 over from last year.   She recommended award of the contract for Brimhall Sand and Rock for Items 1, 2 and 4; Perkins Cinders for Items 1 and 2; and Hatch Construction & Paving for Items 3 and 4.   Vice-Chairman Williams made a motion to award Contract #B17-08-010 Supply of Aggregate for Cover Materials to Brimhall Sand, Rock & Building Materials, Inc., Perkins Cinders, Inc., and Hatch Construction & Paving, Inc.; motion seconded by Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Consideration of award of Contract #B17-07-028 Woodruff Concrete Bridge Repairs to J. Banicki Construction, Inc .:  Bill Bess discussed the item. He stated that this contract is for the construction of the Woodruff Concrete Bridge Repairs, removal of existing asphalt and repair the concrete deck and also some miscellaneous work.  He stated that only one contractor submit a bid, their estimate is $118,000.000, and the engineer's estimate was $177,000.00.  He recommend approval of this contract.  He indicated that the concrete bridge on the west side of Woodruff will be shut down for two to three weeks, and we will make sure we have some maintenance on the Old Country Road during the construction until the bridge is open again.  Supervisor Whitesinger   made a motion to award Contract #B17-07-028 Woodruff Concrete Bridge Repairs to J. RECONVENE : Discussion and possible action on employment contract for County Manager position:
Vice-Chairman Williams indicated that this has been a wonderful experience and expressed appreciation to Brad Carlyon, Bryan Layton, Eric Scott, Melissa Buckley and fellow board members for their efforts through this process.  He stated that we had 20 applicants, very impressive candidates, and he was very impressed with the final candidates.  He spoke about Glenn Kephart serving as interim county manager, and he has been very impressive.  He spoke about working with Mr. Kephart in a number of occasions, and stated that he is impressed with his leadership in the Public Works Department and his integrity.   He stated that Mr. Kephart espouses a work ethic and values that we try to have here in Navajo County, is visionary,  has leadership, is easy to work with, understands the need for partnerships, and has done a wonderful job as interim.  Vice-Chairman Williams made a motion to appoint Glenn Kephart as Navajo County Manager and adopt the contract as discussed in Executive Session.  Supervisor Whitesinger reiterated the same comments made by Vice-Chairman Williams .  She thanked all those who helped in the vetting process of finding a County Manager.  She stated that in our dialogues and conversations, it became more and more apparent why the position is so important because the leadership capabilities and that person's ability to partner and to be an advocate for our community and for our county is certainly of value and importance.  She indicated that we put a lot of ownership and responsibility on that person to help the Board do their job in being able to provide the services or to listen to our constituents or to understand the concerns of the community across the county. She thanked all those who participated and to Mr. Carlyon's office.  She stated that we are excited to have Mr. Kephart join the family as he has demonstrated great skill and ability and certainly all of those skills we are looking for.  Supervisor Whitesinger  seconded the motion. Supervisor Jack thanked everyone and congratulated Mr. Kephart for being our new county manager. He stated that Mr. Kephart has always had the qualities of a great county manager.  He thanked his fellow board members for their work in picking our new county manager.  Chairman Thompson indicated that ever since becoming a part of the Navajo County family, Mr. Kepart has been a part of facing many challenges and struggles in furthering the interests of Navajo County representing the constituents at large.  He stated that finances has been a really hard subject to deal with, and Mr. Kephart has been a part of it.  He indicated that we would like to keep Navajo County moving forward. Vote unanimous approving the motion. Chairman Thompson indicated that the County Attorney and Ms. Buckley have given the Board the kind of guidance that they need in going through the interviews.

Glenn Kephart thanked the Board. He stated that this appointment means a lot to him. He stated that we have a great team at Navajo County, and he believes we will live up to the Board's expectations and follow their leadership.  He expressed his sincere appreciation for the trust the Board has placed in him with this appointment, and he is both humbled and honored to serve as Navajo County Manager.  He expressed gratitude to each member of the Board and all of our elected officials for their unending dedication and commitment to serving our county.  He stated that each day, the Board sets the example for us to follow, and we're excited to do so.  He indicated that Navajo County is known for our healthy, productive partnerships with each other, with every POA, HOA, town and city within our county, the Navajo Nation, Hopi Tribe, and the White Mountain Apache Tribe.  He stated that we are recognized regionally as a leader at both the state and other counties in the state and nationally on county issues and legislation that may affect the quality of life of people who live in Navajo County.  He stated that these strong and healthy partnerships have been critical to our past success.  He indicated that through these partnerships, we are able to accomplish more for our communities and the greater good of the county than we ever could by working alone.  He stated that our future success depends on maintaining, fostering, and continuing to grow these partnerships, and this we will do.  He stated that, additionally, we will leverage our partnerships to ensure that our environment is conducive to retaining the good existing business that we have in the county and is also attractive to new businesses and industry so that we can provide high quality employment for our children and our grandchildren.  He recognized and acknowledged the extremely talented, dedicated and unique team of people who strive every day to follow the leadership of the Board of Supervisors in serving our public.  He stated that the culture of support and family atmosphere and genuinely caring for each other is at a uniquely high and healthy level, and he's proud to be part of it.  He further stated that the people who make up the Navajo County team understand their purpose and role in serving our county and to an individual, they are proud of what they do and committed to doing their best to achieve this purpose and are engaged with our community.  He indicated that Navajo County is very fortunate to have such a team, and he is proud to be a member of that team.  He stated that moving forward, we will continue to honor, value and foster this healthy work environment.  He thanked the Board again for this opportunity to serve Navajo County.  He stated that through the Board's leadership, continuing our healthy and productive partnerships and our dedicated team of highly engaged people, we will continue to provide high quality services to the County and, working together, we will succeed.  He gave a shout out to everybody in this room who either has been a part of him getting engaged with the County and the Public Works team, including Jeanine, Bill, Ricky and Glen Hoskins, and stated that they have done a great job.   He spoke about since he has been interim county manager, Melissa, Debra, Donna, Eric, Paige, Bryan, Bret and Jeff have been so helpful and made this transition a smooth and comfortable transition where he has confidence that we will succeed.  He spoke about being asked whether this excites him or scares him, and he stated that if he had to do it alone, it would scare him, but he is not in this alone.
Chairman Thompson thanked Mr. Kephart for his comments and congratulated him again.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE: Report from Board members, County Attorney and County Manager:   Supervisor Jack stated he is looking forward to working with Mr. Kephart.  He spoke about the Navajo Nation Fair over the weekend and thanked the sheriff for being there.  He further spoke about a partnership meeting on the roads and thanked Bill Bess for being there.  He stated that the Red Dog project is moving forward again.  He spoke about the Navajo Nation budget and indicated that there is a $10 million shortfall of money, and services will be taking cuts. He thanked the County and Melissa Buckley for informing him of all the meetings, and he is trying to make them. Supervisor Whitesinger spoke about the veterans training that the White Mountain Apache Tribe hosted.  She further spoke about the knowledge that she was able to gain and the resources that are available to our local veterans and our veterans across the state.  She stated that this gave her a great deal of pride in our local community and those who have served our country.  She spoke about the conversations on the county manager position.  She stated that that was a good teaching experience for her in valuing what we do here and what it is that we hope to be able to do in the future and finding someone who can meet and align with our hopes.  She spoke about how the budget is a great concern and how do we continue to have positive partnership conversations and moving forward not only locally but with state and federal lawmakers. She spoke about the County Fair and how it was also heartwarming to hear the Holbrook students and experience the small county fair.  She indicated that there have been lots of teachable moments within the last few weeks and being part of the community and being reminded of the great people who live here and how we serve one another and support one another.  Vice-Chairman Williams indicated that the County Fair was a success and expressed appreciation to the Fair Board. He spoke about Michael and Linda Sandoval who take a week's vacation every year and donate time at the County Fair.  He indicated that he attended the Small Counties Forum.  He stated that of the 15 counties, 7 of the counties are part of the small county caucus.   He stated the next day he participated in a meeting with all of the county supervisors state wide and had discussions with representatives of the Governor's Office.  He spoke about the CSA Legislative Summit next month and how leading up to that, the supervisors across the State talk about the legislative issues that the counties can band together on that impact the counties and present a united front to the legislature.  He told Mr. Kephart that he is excited to have him as county manager.  He suggested that Mr. Kephart consider his phrase to be Giddy Up! Let's get going and make changes that need to be made.  Chairman Thompson suggested Biijha! Let's do it.  Let's make it happen.  He spoke about the Arizona Transportation Board meeting at Oak Creek where they spoke about having traffic and parking space concerns.  He indicated that he attended part of the Dilkon Fair.  He stated that he attended the Arizona Transportation Board meeting at Moenkopi.   He stated that one thing he heard at that meeting was that we need more preservation dollars coming our way.  He stated that after he leaves today, he will be heading down to the Annual National Tribal Transportation Conference in Tucson and will then have a meeting in Flagstaff for the Flagstaff Metropolitan Planning Organization where he has been appointed as their vice chair.  He thanked his fellow supervisors for attending CSA.  He stated that he did participate telephonically.  He spoke about attending the Dilkon and Bird Springs chapter meetings.  Mr. Kephart stated, on behalf of Supervisor Whiting, that the Eastern Arizona Counties Organization (ECO) are sponsoring a legislative field trip to begin on Thursday, October 5 to October 7th.  He stated that there are 22 to 24 legislators who will attend.   He indicated that Melissa will work with the Board to see if they want to attend. He spoke about the tour and how we will communicate to our legislators regarding forest restoration and the opportunity and challenges that go with that.  He further spoke about the environmental issues and the value of lumber, and indicated that we are doing a good job in the White Mountains clearing out our forest.  He stated that there is a significant challenge called biomass, the magnitude of the biomass and disposing of that.  Vice-Chairman Williams stated that those legislators will be up in our county.
