Consideration to update the precinct boundary between Justice of the Peace Precinct 5 & Precinct 6 :  Bryan Layton discussed the item. He indicated that the statute gives the Board authority to redraw these boundaries and with approval will go into effect late next year with the elections. He indicated that he has meet with Supervisor Whitesinger, Supervisor Williams, Judge Price, Judge Widmaier, City manager of Show Low and the Town manager of Pinetop/Lakeside.  He stated that he has also made this same presentation to the City Council of Show Low and Town Council of Pinetop/Lakeside.   He showed a map of the area. He spoke about the impacts on public safety, residents and the Justice Courts and Constables.  He indicated that the County proposes to update the precinct boundary between Precinct 5 and Precinct 6 to more closely reflect the existing city limits of Show Low to include the commercial/retail development on White Mountain Road north of Scott Ranch Road.  He stated that this change would allow Show Low Police Department officers to appear on cases in the Show Low Justice Court which would decrease travel time for law enforcement when they need to appear in court.  He indicated that most of the residential homes in unincorporated land within the vicinity are proposed to remain unchanged in Precinct 6.  He stated that an analysis of the area using the most recent census data shows that 399 residents currently in Precinct 6 would move to Precinct 5 and, in total, the Precinct 5 population is projected to increase from 17,463 to 17,862, or 2.3%; conversely, the Precinct 6 population is projected to decrease from 24,283 to 23,884, or -1.6% and as a percent of total population within the precincts, the estimated impact to the Native populations would be less than a 1% change in both precincts. He indicated that although the specific number of court cases attributed to the retail/commercial area on White Mountain Road north of Scott Ranch Road is not available, both judges from Precinct 5 and Precinct 6 estimate that the total impact will not be sufficient to change the tier size of either court based on Judicial Productivity Credits (JPCs), and it is expected that both courts will remain in their existing tiers with compensation for judges remaining unchanged.  He stated that it is also expected that the impact on Precinct 5 and Precinct 6 Constables will be similar and compensation for constables will remain unchanged.  Vice-Chairman Williams indicated that this proposal makes sense so that law enforcement does not have to leave their precinct to go to court and the Justices of the Peace support this. He thanked Mr. Layton for his work on this as well as the County Attorney's Office for their part.  He spoke about there being no opposition, and this makes sense for everyone.  Vice-Chairman Williams made a motion to update the precinct boundary between Justice of the Peace Precinct 5 & Precinct 6.  Supervisor Whitesinger thanked Mr. Layton for his efforts in working with all of the different entities involved.  Supervisor Whitesinger seconded the motion. Vote unanimous approving the motion.

ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES :

a.

Presentation from Steve Best, USFS Forest Supervisor on prescribed burning activities on the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest, Gene Beaudoin, Regional Forest Manager for State Forestry and Fire on forest thinning projects :  Mary Springer introduced Steve Best, Forest Manager for Apache-Sitreaves. He spoke about the history of the forest and the thickening of the forest. He spoke about past catastrophic fires in the area. He further spoke about reducing the vegetation and how they are doing that at this point by the timber sales, logging, mechanical operations, along with prescribed fires. He spoke about the rigorous prescriptions which must be in place prior to a prescribed burn trying to mimic natural systems. He spoke about some of the benefits of prescribed burning, including a reduction in fuel, low intensity burns and maintaining the vegetation and nutrient recycling.  He stated that we have windows in the fall and in the spring where we can do prescribed burns.  He indicated that we are burning 10- to 30,000 acres a year on the Apache-Sitgreaves, with 8500 acres so far this year. Chairman Thompson asked about moisture coming, and Mr. Best indicated that the 10- day weather forecast indicated no moisture. Mr. Best stated that they do news releases before and on the day of a prescribed burn. Supervisor Whiting asked about whether most of what is burning is biomass as opposed to timber, and Mr. Best stated it depends on the timber sales with biomass, but they have other contractors who do not want the biomass, they will pile it, and then later we will go back and burn it. A discussion was held regarding the timber industry and the leftover biomass.  Mr. Best indicated there are a lot of areas you can't get into, and it is not economical to have a timber sale. He stated that it is the natural part of the eco system and how the forests have developed and been maintained for centuries. Mr. Best spoke about the risk of not having prescribed burns, including the economics, because if we do not do the prescribed burns we would have to do a mechanical treatment which is more expensive.  Also, if we don't do the prescribed burns maintenance, it will allow the fuels to build up, and we run the risk of catastrophic wildfires. Supervisor Whiting spoke about the biomass facility in the area. Mr. Best stated that that would be preferred to have it hauled off and dealt with at the biomass facility, but it depends on the contractor that purchases the material and whether they want it or not.  He stated that it is expensive for them to haul it, and the timber sales are far from the facilities. He indicated that if we cannot get people to purchase the biomass or use it, then we have to burn it or pay for it to be hauled off. Supervisor Whitesinger commented about the community that we live in, high density area, and when we make the decision to live here, there is give and take, including smoke in the air. She stated the reservations have acres and acres of healthy forests due to the prescribed burns, and she believes in the importance of what Mr. Best and the Forest Service is doing, but the question is how do we manage the smoke. Mary Springer spoke about notification of communities through local radios and local newspapers, along with 311-Info and the County Facebook page. Gene Beaudoin, State Forestry, spoke about thinning projects in Navajo County and the Fire Wise program. He spoke about thinning projects that we are getting to work in the area of Safeway in Pinetop and the Wagon Wheel area.  We spoke about their grant process which can be obtained year after year. Chairman Thompson spoke about the dry weather. Supervisor Whiting thanked Mr. Beaudoin, Mary Springer and Mr. Best for being present and for the presentation today.
