REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

Consideration of award of contract B21-04-001 Provision of Health Care Services to Advanced Correctional Healthcare :  Eric Scott addressed the item.  A presentation was shown.  He indicated that the jail is mandated by statute to provide medical services, pharmacy, medical evaluation and medical care, to the inmates.  He stated that the goal is to find the most effective and efficient way to provide the services while protecting county.  He indicated that currently we have a Medical Director, a Physician's Assistant and a team of nurses.  He advised that NACo recently did a survey, and that fifty percent of inmates have a serious condition, sixty-four percent have a major condition, fifty-three percent have a drug dependency and forty-nine percent have co-existing mental health and substances abuse conditions.  He stated that they are required to provide nursing staff, medical doctor, physician's assistant and liability insurance.  He advised that the liability/medical malpractice insurance is what protects the county.  He addressed the current challenges and concerns for staffing and the extended hours recommended by ACIP, who provides the insurance.  He stated that the liability insurance is no longer covering for jail medical, that the current insurance doesn't met the adequate insurance limits and that the costs have continued to increase.  He addressed the timeline for the current policy/coverage and this request.  He advised that Navajo County and Apache County did a joint RFP in April 2021 and that Apache County approved a contract in June.  He further advised that Navajo County received the RFP results in June and that they had a team internally that reviewed the submitted bids.  He addressed the current and projected costs and projected amount it would cost to outsource, with a consist five-year rate structure, which would be a two-year term with option to renew for three one-year terms.
Ernie Garcia talked about issues with the rising cost of the insurance, the limited possibilities of securing insurance in the future, the need to increase staffing to commit to 24-hour care and the rising cost of medical treatment.  He addressed the proposal with outsourcing and indicated that they would have extended capabilities of staffing, a consistent rate structure and stated that the company is an experienced provider that specializes in jails.  He indicated that through their bid they demonstrate medical cost savings, competitive wages, that they will look at keeping the employees who would like to stay on and that they have good promotional packages.  He advised that are accredited through the American Correctional Association and the NCCHC and that they recently contracted with the Santa Cruz Jail and Apache County.

Supervisor Whiting inquired if they feel there will be a better service with comparable pricing.
Mr. Garcia stated that they bring in a lot of external services that the jail doesn't currently have.  He indicated that they can reach out to specialist and set up virtual healthcare to do assessments and treatment plans.
Supervisor Whiting inquired if this would eliminate our exposure so that Navajo County no longer would need to purchase a policy.
A discussion was held regarding the options for Navajo County's policy and the different coverages.
Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger inquired how they came up with the cost estimate, if it was on the based on the average need over time, the population and/or staff.
Mr. Garcia stated that during the bid process they requested average population, the amount spent in a year and the level for medical staffing level, that they take all of that into consideration go off what the requirements are in the RFP's.  He indicated that there is an additional pool which pays for medical services and supplies and at the end of year the money that is left comes back to county at a rate of 90% of the remaining amount.
Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger inquired if this is an annual contract that would allow them to determine if it is a good fit for the County.
Mr. Garcia stated that it is a two-year contract with the option to renew annual for three additional years.

Supervisor Whiting made a motion  to approve the award of contract B21-04-001 Provision of Health Care Services to Advanced Correctional Healthcare, motion seconded by  Chairman Seymore .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Supervisor Whiting made a motion  to return to regular session, motion seconded by  Supervisor Peshlakai .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Consideration of the ARPA project vetting process and allowable expenses from March 19 through June 30, 2021 :  Rochelle Lacapa addressed the Board.  A presentation was shown.  She addressed the timelines and provided an update of the ARPA process.  She indicated the Management Team is at a point where it would be helpful to have a formally approved process that they could use for any department or individual who would like to request ARPA funds or a partnership with the County.  She addressed the process that they have put together and indicated that it consists of the guiding principles that the Board had previously addressed.  She spoke about the process that they have put together and the questions that would be asked for each proposed project.  She addressed the different categories that the projects could be placed in to determine if they are appropriate.    She stated that after determining which projects would be appropriate, they would come back before the Board to determine which projects they want to allow through the ARPA funds. She stated that if the process is approved, the team would start the process of vetting the projects and come back before the Board in a couple of months to get the decision on which projects should be funded through ARPA.  She indicated that they will also provide the list of the determined reserve projects as well as the projects that didn't qualify.

Supervisor Whiting stated that he appreciates the vision at looking at productive ways to funnel the requests and that the team is listening to what people are wanting to share and then taking it through a productive process.
Chairman Seymore thanked the team for efforts they have put forth.  He stated that he feels it will be a fair vetting process as well as a process that will be able to maximize the money.
Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger indicated that she agrees with the previous comments.  She indicated there is an importance in being able to communicate with our organization and also for the public to be aware of the process and the accountability to the money being spent.  She indicated that all of that has been considered in creating this tool used in communicating the process.

Ms. Lacapa stated that they are also requesting expenditures from March 19 – June 30, 2021 for expenses from the Magellan contract, the Public Health staff salaries and ERE's and the ARPA Management team's salaries and ERE's from the ARPA funds.  She stated that they are requesting approval of the ARPA vetting process and also for the expenses under first reporting period.
Jason Moore addressed the motions being made under two separate motions.

c.

Presentation on PSRPS unfunded pension liability and debt financing option :  Jayson Vowell addressed the item.  A presentation was shown.  He provided a background of the pension system and indicated that when you have an unfunded liability the sooner it's paid the cheaper it will be. He advised that the current debt as of June 30, 2020 for the Sheriff's Department is thirteen and a half billion dollars, for the Core Detention Plan is approximately one and a half million dollars and the plan for the Sheriff's Investigators is approximately five hundred thousand.  He addressed the increases for the funds over the past year, the reasons that the debts are where they are at and indicated that it will take seventeen years to pay down the debts.  He addressed the unfunded liability debt, indicated that the amount being paid will almost double by 2034 and that it is very costly to be able to pay it in the allotted amount of time.  He addressed the normal costs for payroll in the Sheriff's Department, if there wasn't the unfunded liability for the pensions, and talked about the percentages that are paid for contributions.  He talked about financing options that would allow unfunded liability amount to be less.  He further talked about the differences with the financing options and saving.  He requested guidance from the Board on which option they would like staff to go towards and indicated that his recommendation would be to finance the bonds for the Sheriff and CORP Detention plans and to use cash reserves to pay for the Investigator fund.
Supervisor Whiting addressed the different options and the projected savings.
Mr. Vowell addressed the options.
Chairman Seymore indicated that he appreciates the work done on this.  He stated that it is not a problem that goes away easily, that it is not an issue we created and that it was done at a higher level.  He indicated that we need to be proactive in reducing our costs.  He stated that it needs to be addressed, that it will help us in the future and will stabilize what we are doing as a County.  He recommended allowing them to move forward and staff can bring it back as a proposal.
Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger stated that we don't know what the future will look like and that there are resources at this time.  She stated that she supports the comments of investigating what our options are.
Mr. Vowell advised that they will move forward with a financial advisor and see what the options look like.  He indicated that he believes there is budget capacity and addressed a possible budget amendment if it moves forward.
