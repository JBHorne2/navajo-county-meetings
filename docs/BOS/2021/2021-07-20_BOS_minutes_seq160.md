REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

COVID-19 Update :  Janelle Linn addressed the Board regarding the item.  She indicated that they are seeing a resurgence of COVID in our communities.  She advised that to date in Navajo County there have been 16,978 cases and that 7,423 of the cases have been worked by the Public Health Nurses.  The stated that there have been 22 new cases in past 24 hours and that they have been consistently seeing 20-25 new cases a day.  She advised that a new trend they are seeing is an increase in hospitalization compared to the previous surges.  She stated that they feel we are on the start of a new surge of cases and that the trend matches the situation from last summer prior to going into the first surge.  She talked about the high percentage of hospitalizations and addressed the number of people who are in the hospital and the current number of home cases.  She advised that they have an in-home program which means that they are sick enough to be in the hospital but that they are trying to stop the spread and keep beds open, so they Doctor and nurses do home visit rotations to assess those patients and then only admit them if they feel they can no longer handle those cases at home.  She indicated that the percent positivity rates have increased back up to between 27-35 percent which moves the school dashboard back into moderate risk.  She stated that they are getting a lot of questions and concerns regarding variants of the virus.  She further stated that they don't want people to get to hung up on the variants, that there are multiple variants that have been identified in our communities, including the Delta variant, but that they want to stress how you take care of the patients and do contact tracing doesn't change.  She indicated that you need to protect yourself though the same measures that we have been practicing, wearing a mask, especially if you are not vaccinated, social distancing and washing your hands. She spoke about how there has been frustration with them not being able to share the results from the testing with patients, providers or the public but  indicated that the test has not been vetted to be used as a diagnostic test so it is for surveillance purposes only at this time.  She spoke about the low vaccination rates as a whole in Navajo County.  She indicated that those who are vaccinated are faring better than those who are unvaccinated.  She stated that the off-reservation vaccine coverage rate is about 30.5%, that for our community we need to be at sixty to sixty-five percent and that if enough of the community is vaccinated it stops the spread.  She addressed the vaccination rate for the elderly and indicated that due to their age they don't have as good of an immune response and therefore can still have breakthrough disease even though the vaccine is still covering them with a sixty-four percent efficacy rate and ninety-four percent if they complete the series.  She indicated that the vaccine helps reduces the risk for hospitalization and death.  She addressed the importance of getting the vaccine and the reduction of symptoms for those who had been vaccinated.  She stated that if you have concerns with the vaccine to contact your health care provider or public health.  She advised that there is no longer a shortage for vaccines and addressed the different locations in Navajo County that provide the vaccine.

Chairman Seymore inquired as to the new cases if they know the percentage for those who were and were not vaccinated.
Ms. Linn stated that for those vaccinated they are only seeing about a two percent breakthrough and that only twenty-five percent of those are symptomatic.
Supervisor Whitesinger inquired as to the percentage for those vaccinated in Navajo County.
Ms. Linn stated that 30.5 percent are vaccinated off tribal land, that the White Mountain Apache Tribe is reporting close to 80-85 percent coverage rate and the Navajo Nation is reporting 50-60 percent.

Quarterly Report and Update :  Mark Honberger addressed the Board.  A presentation was shown.  He gave an update from the holiday weekends for Memorials Day and 4 th of July.  He indicated that they had concerns with the parking lots filling up, that there wasn't a need for street parking this year and that the facilities handled the visitors well.  He advised that he has been meeting with public on regular basis and that the next Community Meeting will be August 19, 2021, at the Silver Creek Golf Club.  He addressed how the meetings are being advertised.  He reported on the Bass Club Tournament was held on July 11, 2021, that it was a catch and release tournament and that there were sixteen teams.  He indicated that the Bass Club is regional group that does a tournament at a different lake each month.  He further indicated that they provide a service of the fish habitats, that they built them, put them in the lake, that every year they replenish them, check them and repair them, if needed, and they also provide information about the fish that are in the lake.  He stated that they will be back in September for their Championship weekend.   Mr. Honberger stated that they have been working to get a fitness class started, a Beach Boot Camp which would be Monday, Wednesday and Friday at 6:00 a.m. and indicated that they will be marketing the class.  He addressed the water levels and how they are working with Silver Creek Irrigation District on the levels.  He indicated that the parking lot lines needed to be replaced, that they remeasured the lines and were able to get additional spaces.  He talked about the wet moor or remote anchor and indicated that they are testing out the idea with one person.  He stated that they are looking at bringing in concessionaires and that they will continue to pursue bringing in food/drink vendors.  He talked about kayak and paddle board rentals, he indicated that there is a demand and that they are working on getting everything approved.  He addressed the dock repairs, indicated that they have several floats that are failing, that they are in the process of purchasing some and then will get them installed.  He stated that they now have staff t-shirts for more of a professional look and easy for visitors to recognize.  He indicated that their first newsletter is complete, that it has been made available digitally, is on Facebook and has been posted on local community bulletin boards.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE: Report from County Manager, County Attorney and Board members:
Glenn Kephart thanked Judge Ralph Hatch and congratulated him on his retirement.  He stated that people will remember how others make you feel more than what they say to you and indicated that he knew he came to good place because when he first came here Judge Hatch reached out and made him feel welcome.
Supervisor Whitesinger spoke about attending the Community Health Needs Assessment Meeting, which was specific to our county.  She stated that the assessment was completed over a year ago and that the information received was a community effort in being able to understand what the greatest needs as to health-related issues specific to all of our regions.  She stated that they prioritized the needs and that the top priorities were substance use disorder, mental health, poverty, chronic disease and sexually transmitted disease.  She spoke of the importance of having conversations with within our communities where we are active in creating healthy community spaces.  She indicated that for chronic disease the average in Navajo County is greater than the national average and that our communities are not very healthy.  She stated that it is important to have the opportunity to advocate for health communities.  She further stated that sexually transmitted disease continues to be on the up rise.  She indicated that we need to make the efforts to be healthy and to advocate for healthier communities.  She indicated that she was able to attend the meeting with Tribal Leaders regarding transportation and infrastructure needs.  She talked about the challenges with things such as roads, water, electric and transportation on the reservations.  She stated that the longer we prolong addressing specific needs, those needs we keep becoming greater in the future.  She addressed the challenges with online schooling during the pandemic for those who didn't have the infrastructure available to them.  She stated that it is important to keep up on those issues and continue to work on ways to address them.
Chairman Seymore talked about Workforce Development Conference that he attended.  He indicated that they talked about how important it is going to be to start working not only on broadband but on digital literacy.  He indicated that it does no good to have broadband if people don't know how to use it and discover how they can improve themselves.  He indicated that we need to focus on how we can make our work force better and how to get people back to work.  He talked about working together to keep thing local and make our communities better.
