2.

COVID-19 Update :  Janelle Linn stated that there were 304 new cases reported in July off tribal lands, 760 cases in August and 817 for September.  She stated that the last few weeks have been an indicator that cases are not leveling off.  She advised that last week there were 140 new cases reported to and worked by the Public Health Nurses and five new hospitalizations.  She further advised that of the 140 new cases most of them are from unvaccinated patients.  She stated that the vaccine is effective against preventing disease and that it's highly effective against preventing severe disease.  She indicated that they are seeing higher mortality rates with this new surge and that there have been twelve new deaths reported over the past two weeks off tribal land.  She advised that there have been twenty-four cases of the one-hundred forty cases that were linked to a school district.  She stated that fifty-three percent of the cases reported are underage of forty.  She advised that the vaccine coverage rate off tribal land remains at forty percent.  She further advised that on tribal lands the Hopi's have reported a seventy-six percent vaccinated rate, the White Mountain Apache is reporting above a ninety percent vaccinated rate and the Navajo Nation has reported a sixty-five to seventy percent vaccinated rate.  She indicated that they are still encouraging vaccination and that they will start updating the dashboard on Mondays with the information for the week prior.  She spoke about the booster dose recommendations.  She stated that the Pfizer product is now FDA approved for first 2 doses.
Supervisor Benally inquired if you have to previously had the Pfizer vaccine to get the booster.
Ms. Linn stated that you should stay with the brand you started your vaccine with.
Supervisor Benally inquired when Moderna will be coming out with a booster.
Ms. Linn indicated that she does not have information on that.

PUBLIC WORKS :

a.

Consideration of Task Agreement No. 24 to the Intergovernmental Agreement between the Bureau of Indian Affairs- Navajo Region and Navajo County that authorizes funding assistance for the Chinle and Western Agencies to provide culvert pipes :  John Osgood addressed the item.  He talked about the importance of the drainage on dirt and gravel roads after it has rained and snowed.  He stated that this task agreement is a creative and proactive approach to expediting the repair of failing culvert pipes on certain BIA maintained roads.  He addressed the locations in District One for the repairs and indicated the County will fund the culvert pipes and that the BIA will perform the repair and installation work.  He indicated that the fiscal impact is $30,000 and that the source of funding is the Highway User Revenue Tax.  He stated that staff recommends approval.
Supervisor Benally indicated that it is vital to get the roads repaired and necessary to get the culverts replaced.

Supervisor Benally made a motion  to approve Task Agreement No. 24 to the Intergovernmental Agreement between the Bureau of Indian Affairs- Navajo Region and Navajo County that authorizes funding assistance for the Chinle and Western Agencies to provide culvert pipes, motion seconded by  Supervisor Whiting .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
