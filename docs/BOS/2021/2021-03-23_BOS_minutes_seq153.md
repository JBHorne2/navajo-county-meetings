PUBLIC WORKS:

a.

PUBLIC HEARING: Consideration and possible adoption of a Resolution Number 08-2021 approving/denying a Special Use Permit to construct and operate a 60 meter (approx. 197 foot) meteorological tower on APN 201-05-001B, a portion of Township 13 North, Range 19 East, Section 09 of the Gila and Salt River Meridian :  Cody Cooper addressed the item.  A presentation was shown.  He advised that the location of this permit is north of SR277.  He indicated that the letter of intent indicates that they will be using different instruments at varying heights to collect data and understand the wind characteristic in the area which will be used to determine if a wind project could be viable for the area.  He advised that the applicant will be accessing the area through private roadways.  He stated that staff and the applicant have worked with the Arizona Game and Fish Department, that the department has seen benefit from and staff recommends as conditions that they install bird flight diverters on the meteorological tower, aircraft warning markers, paint the tops of the towers orange and white, and provide AGFD and well as the Planning and Zoning Department with GPS coordinates when the towers are finished and locating the towers away from wildlife attracting features to the fullest extent possible.  He advised that part of the condition including these recommendations will be that the applicant works with AGFD, so they can review and make sure that the project is compliant. He stated that the permit was unanimously recommended for approval at the Planning & Zoning Commission Hearing in January with the condition outlined regarding the expiration of the permits.  He advised that one of the concerns of the Commissioners was the visual impact.  He further advised that all the property's surrounding received mailings, that staffers have not received any comments for or against this project, that they received a call prior to the Commission Hearing where the property owner wanted to know the proposed location in relation to their property, that they were provided with information and did not have concerns.   Mr. Cooper advised that based on staff review, the letter from the Arizona Game and Fish Department as well as the Planning & Zoning Commission, they recommend approval of the Special Use Permit subject to the conditions outlined.
Chairman Seymore inquired as to the maximum time frame they would use this for their study.
Mr. Cooper addressed the typical time frame.
Stephanie Ellis, with Blue Earth, addressed the time frame for the study.
Clare Brophy Bellendir, with Aztec, addressed the Board regarding the item.  She requested that the Permit be approved without an expiration date and addressed the reason for the request.
Supervisor Whiting indicated that he agrees with comments that have been made regarding the expiration date due to the remote location.
Chairman Seymore opened the item for Public comment, there being no comment, the Public Hearing was closed.

Supervisor Whiting made a motion to approve as presented Resolution Number 08-2021 approving a Special Use Permit to construct and operate a 60 meter (approx. 197 foot) meteorological tower on APN 201-05-001B, a portion of Township 13 North, Range 19 East, Section 09 of the Gila and Salt River Meridian, with the stipulation of removing Condition 14 that creates a timeline expiration, motion seconded by Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

e.

Consideration of Task Agreement No. 11 to Memorandum of Agreement between the Hopi Division of Transportation and Navajo County that authorizes funding assistance for roadway aggregate material :  John Osgood addressed the item.  He indicated that the Hopi Transportation Director has requested assistance for continuing the improvement of a public roadway leading into the Hopi Waste facility.  He indicated that the natural road material is clay which makes traveling on the road difficult with the heavy truck traffic accessing the facility during bad weather and for the community members using the facility.  He advised that in 2018 Navajo County provided gravel aggregate to approximately a mile and a half of that road and that it held up well and has been effective during the weather.  He further advised that the Hopi Division of Transportation is requesting that Navajo County provide an additional mile worth of gravel, which will be placed by Hopi DOT and will complete the route into the Transfer Station.  He indicated that the fiscal impact is $50,000.00 and that the source of the funds is the Highway User Revenue Fund allocated specifically for District II.  He stated that staff recommends approval.

Supervisor Peshlakai made a motion to approve Task Agreement No. 11 to Memorandum of Agreement between the Hopi Division of Transportation and Navajo County that authorizes funding assistance for roadway aggregate material, motion seconded by Supervisor Benally .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Update on Navajo County's Health Insurance Plan :  Eric Scott addressed the item and stated that this is an annual update.  A presentation was shown.  He addressed the data provided to help with the decisions for health insurance.  He stated that there has not been an increase in premiums for over five years.  He further stated that they have been able to keep the Health Savings Account contributions high, that some employers don't contribute to the employee's HSA and addressed the contributions made through participation in incentives such as an annual physical and the wellness challenges.  He addressed the stop-loss insurance and how it has been reduced.  He further addressed the air ambulance insurance.  He spoke about the internal Health Guide and the help they provide to the employees.  He spoke about the Employee Health and Wellness Program, the benefits of the program and some of the challenges they did over the past year.  He addressed the Healthcare Trust Spend, the projected amounts, the actual amounts spent and the savings for the County.  Mr. Scott talked about the new programs for 2021 such as Virta Health for diabetes management, GI Thrive for gut health, Everyone Health and Edison Health.  Mr. Scott stated that they are proud of everything they have accomplished.
Chairman Seymore thanked Mr. Scott and his team for all that they have done.  He stated that health care is very important and addressed the savings aspect.
Supervisor Whiting thanked Mr. Scott for all that they are doing and indicated that it has resulted in a cost savings for the County.  He addressed the programs that have been brought in.
Supervisor Whitesinger addressed the gratitude in being able to save and the services provided in the way of health care and other programs. She addressed the importance of being mindful and resourceful.

c.

Communications Update - Year End Outreach Report :  Alicia Deets addressed the item.  A presentation was shown.  She spoke about communications and outreach in 2020 and having to transition to virtual.  She advised that Facebook is the most successful channel.  She further advised that they launched the Quarterly Newsletter to residents in 2020 and that there are currently 68 subscribers.  She indicated that all social media showed spikes in 2020 due to COVID.  She addressed the numbers for posts, followers, the social media engagement, which is when a follower reacts by following or sharing, and impressions.  She spoke of how they have started developing the brand for Navajo County, which allows people to recognize posts from Navajo County.  She advised that Facebook had a 20% increase in 2020 and addressed the top posts.  She addressed the Instagram page, indicated that there was over a 100% increase and addressed the top posts on there for 2020.  She stated that there are over 4000 followers on Twitter, that there was a 30% increase and addressed the top posts.  She talked about the YouTube and the views for that page.  She advised that they have add Next Door and addressed how that platform is used.  She spoke about the contract with Critical Mention, which is a media monitoring software, and spoke about how she has used it to track mentions of Navajo County.  She addressed the press releases and media coverage for Navajo County. She spoke of the Quarterly Newsletter, provided an update on it and indicated that it is growing.  She provided an update on the website users and advised that the top pages were the Home Page and Treasurers Map.  She indicated that we have established a communications network stronger than she has been it before and that it continues to grow.
Chairman Seymore thanked Ms. Deets for her presentation and indicated that she has done an excellent job.
Supervisor Whitesinger indicated that she feels it is important to share our message with the communities, to share what different departments are doing and what the Supervisors are doing.  She indicated that when she started there was a misconception of Counties and that she thinks social media has done a good job with communication, so people better understand.  She stated that she appreciates the time that Ms. Deets takes to get the messages out.

BOARD OF SUPERVISORS :

a.

Consideration and possible adoption of Resolution Number 10-2021 , approving the Establishment of a Redistricting Advisory Committee and Guidelines for the Redistricting Advisory Committee :  Jason Moore addressed the item.  He addressed the redistricting specialist, Bruce Adelson, that was hired for the redistricting process for the upcoming year.  He addressed the presentation that was given to the Board a few weeks ago by Mr. Adelson regarding the requirements for the redistricting process.  He indicated that one of the first steps is to establish a redistricting committee.  He stated it is not required by law, but that it's traditionally what has been done in Navajo County. He advised that the proposed Resolution is to establish a committee and sets forth some guidelines for the committee.  He stated that it would allow each Supervisor to select a member from their District to sit on the committee.  He reviewed a few of the guidelines for being eligible to sit on the committee.  He indicated that the duties and responsibilities for the committee are included in the Resolution.  He further indicated that they would like for the committee to be able to recommend to the Board some different plans for consideration.  He advised that something different they have included in this Resolution was to establish priorities for the redistricting plan.  He stated that the first two requirements are legal requirements that must be met regardless of anything else.  He talked about some of the other requirements.  He addressed the differences with 2011 and this year.
Chairman Seymore inquired if they could ask interested citizens to put in an application for selection.  He addressed having more generic meetings that could be held regarding the redistricting.
Mr. Moore indicated that he can address those options with the County Manager.
Supervisor Whiting inquired if each District can determine how they wish to select the individual for the committee.
Mr. Moore stated each District would be able to determine how to do the selection.
Supervisor Whiting addressed the stipulations on the requirements that must be meet in order to be considered and indicated that it narrows the scope of who could and could not be selected.
Supervisor Peshlakai indicated that he agrees with Supervisor Whiting on having each District determine how to do the selection.  He also thanked Mr. Moore for drafting the Resolution.  He inquired if this Resolution is similar to the previous Resolutions.
Mr. Moore addressed the differences between this Resolution and the previous one.
Supervisor Peshlakai requested that a courtesy notice be provided to the Navajo Human Rights Commission.
Mr. Moore advised that a copy of the Resolution can be sent.  He stated that they played an important role during the previous process of redistricting.
Supervisor Benally stated her concerns with redistricting.

Supervisor Whiting made a motion to adopt Resolution Number 10-2021 , approving the Establishment of a Redistricting Advisory Committee and Guidelines for the Redistricting Advisory Committee, motion seconded by Supervisor Whitesinger .  Supervisor Benally indicated that she is not opposing but that she is abstaining.  Motion approved, with Supervisor Benally abstaining.

REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

COVID-19 Update : Janelle Linn reported that things are going well with COVID.  She stated that to date there have been 15,604 total cases for Navajo County and that 6,683 have been worked by the Public Health Nurses.  She further stated that consistently over the past couple of days they have had two cases per every twenty-four hours reported.  She indicated that only six cases were reported from the Navajo Nation and four from the White Mountain Apache Tribe.  She advised that there have been 595 non-tribal cases related or linked to a school.  She further advised that there have been 1,612 hospitalizations and 518 deaths for Navajo County.  She stated that this month marks the one-year anniversary for COVID response, and that they wish to pay tribute to those who have lost their lives and the families of those who suffered lost during the pandemic.  She indicated that hospital beds are seventy-four percent full, but only ten percent are due to COVID.  She indicated that vaccine efforts are still going smoothly, the State is at 26.8% of the population vaccinated and that Navajo County is at 27%.  She further indicated that they have administered 29,606 doses in Navajo County, 19,214 are first dose and that 10,893 people have completed their vaccine series.  She advised that in Navajo County if you are 18 and over you are eligible for a vaccine, that you can visit the Navajo County COVID dashboard to make an appointment through the link and that several of the facilities you can call and make an appointment directly with them.  She reported that they have been able to serve the minority and under-served population as well as the high-risk individuals.
Chairman Seymore thanked Ms. Linn and her team for their efforts.  He requested that everyone pause for a moment of silence.

Supervisor Peshlakai made a motion to return to regular session, motion seconded by Supervisor Whiting .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
