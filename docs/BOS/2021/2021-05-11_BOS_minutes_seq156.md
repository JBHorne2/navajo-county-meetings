CALL TO THE PUBLIC :  Brent Meyer spoke about a possible property tax relief for disabled Veterans.
Steven Slaton talked about Voter integrity, the ES&S technology and recommend doing an audit of the machines.  He submitted a document regarding the issues he spoke on.
Chairman Seymore directed staff to see if we have any connection with Dominion in the reporting system and report back.
Wes Hamrick talked about having an audit of the elections, voter integrity and encouraged an audit of the election after Maricopa County has completed their audit.
David Smith spoke about the Constitutional Sheriff's and Peace Officers Association and educating the public on the constitutional role of the Sheriff.
David Adams spoke about 2 nd Amendment rights and requested that the Supervisors do what they can to protect and support the 2 nd Amendment.
Betsy Standage spoke about concerns with their guns and ammunition being taken and indicated that she thinks it is important to keep the 2 nd Amendment intact for protection.
Robert McKown spoke about the 2 nd Amendment and protecting our civil liberties.
Jim Vance spoke about the 2 nd Amendment and how our rights are dwindling.  He stated that the State has passed a statute saying that they support the 2 nd Amendment and addressed the reasons why they are requesting that the Supervisors pass something for Navajo County.  He stated that they have petitions signed by hundreds of people requesting that the Board adopt an ordinance regarding the 2 nd Amendment and that they are requesting that it be put on agenda for the next meeting.
Lisa Green spoke about the Board of Supervisors implementing an ordinance to protect the Navajo County citizens 2 nd Amendment rights and requested that the matter be put on an agenda for a future meeting.  She indicated that they don't want to rely on the State or Federal government to protect our rights.
Pete Ellis stated that he is in support of an ordinance for 2 nd amendment rights and indicated that we need layers to protect us.
David Peelman spoke about 2nd amendment rights.  He stated that they want to be on Board agenda for the next meeting and addressed the reasons for the request.  He indicated that rural America needs to take a stand.
Sheriff Clouse addressed the Board and citizens regarding their involvement with government issues. He stated that it is the Sheriff's responsibility to ensure that they defend our rights and that they don't take it lightly.  He indicated that they don't want to see citizens afraid of the government.  He stated that they want the citizens to know the positions of our elected officials and know that they have access to our Elected Officials.
Brad Carlyon stated that he understands why this proposal is being promoted but that it doesn't have any legal effect for a county.  He indicated that the legislative power is centralized in the State Legislature which creates cities, towns, counties and that they decide what powers and limits that they have.  He further indicated that the local governments only have the powers that are delegated to them.  He stated that the Arizona Legislature has addressed the issues and that they have not given any express powers to the counties when it comes to guns rights and laws.  He further stated that they have it set up where they can take away the state shared sales tax revenue if the local governments get involved in areas where they have said they can't.  He advised that this year they have passed HB2111, the 2 nd Amendment Firearm Freedom Act, which is what everyone is asking for and that it was signed by Governor Ducey.  He indicated that our county can make a political statement stating that we believe in 2 nd amendment but that we can't pass an ordinance or law that does that, due to not having the authority to do so.  He indicated that all county employees have all taken an oath to uphold the constitution and that we do that every day and will continue to do it.
Chairman Seymore directed staff to look into a possible study session and determine if this issue should be placed on an upcoming agenda.

BOARD OF SUPERVISORS :

a.

Update on Navajo County Fair Board Events :  Michael Sample, the Navajo County Fair Board President, addressed the Board regarding the item.  He stated that it has been busy this year.  He advised that last year a lot of events and the fair were cancelled due to COVID.  He indicated that the Junior Livestock Show and auction went forward last year, that it was one of the biggest auctions they have had and thanked everyone for coming out to support it.  He stated that currently there are only eight weekends open and that all the other weekends are filled with events, such as barrel races, team roping's and gymkhana's, for this year.  He stated that some of the big events coming up are the SAILA show and the Livestock Camp.  He further stated that they hosted the High School Rodeo a few weeks ago and that they have booked to come back in September this year.  He advised that it will be 90 th Annual Fair this year, that the theme will be 90 Years of Making Memories at the Navajo County Fair, that the dates are September 15-18, 2021 and that all of the Elected Officials will be invited to opening ceremonies on Wednesday.  He stated that there will be a new event this year for the Elected Officials, that they will be having a pig showmanship contest and that they can participate and possibly win a buckle.  Mr. Sample thanked the Board and Navajo County for all of their support.  Michelle Stock, the Navajo County Fair Event Director, thanked everyone for all of their support and indicated that they are looking forward to seeing everyone at the Fair.
Chairman Seymore thanked them for all they do with the Fair.  He stated that it is important for our County to be able to show what we are made of and that the youth can be involved in in the Fair and indicated that they will do what they can to support the Fair in the future.

b.

Presentation on Population Health and Quality of Life :  Allison Hephner spoke about how population health effects each resident in Navajo County.  She indicated that when thinking of population health, you have to consider what goes into the quality of life of an individual, such as school, income, community safety, family social support and physical environment.  She spoke about issues with self-medicating and substance abuse which leads to poor behavior and then a poor quality of life.  She addressed the number of children that live in poverty and different things that are going into helping with that.  She talked about the comparison with the different health issues in Navajo County and stated that the problems are not being addressed, they are getting worse.  She stated that we have a high diabetes population and that there is not a local endocrinologist.  She further stated that we need to look at ways to address these issues.  She talked about how in 2018 we had the highest alcohol rate and indicated that liver dieses go along with alcoholism.  She spoke about mental behavioral health and the high rate of suicide deaths, especially in children.  She indicated that mental health and substance abuse are the top issues in Navajo County.  She addressed the numbers for each disease in 2019 with information provided by Summit.  She addressed sexually transmitted disease and stated that this was the only thing in Navajo County that has been reported as improving. She indicated that there are protocols in place for such things fire, but that there was nothing when it came to the pandemic.  She stated with the pandemic there were lives lost to things other than COVID such as substance abuse, overdoses, and suicides.  She spoke about the numbers that will be reported over the next few years after the impact from COVID in Navajo County.  She stated that during COVID there was a majority of patients with mental health issues due to social isolation.  She spoke of the impact this will have in the future.  She requested that ARPA funding be used to help with population health, especially for the children.  She stated that due to the lack of funding it effected the grants during COVID, and that Public Health needs to be funded to help those working on Mental Health.  She talked about different programs and indicated that we need to come together to find a solution.  She addressed the homeless population and how it relates to public health.  She indicated that if we can't invest in Public Health, we need to find partnerships or provide them with networking and resources.
Chairman Seymore thanked Ms. Hephner for her presentation and encouraged her to continue to work with Health Department.  He indicated that they understand it is an important topic for our County.
Supervisor Peshlakai thanked Ms. Hephner for the presentation.  He indicated that he will be reaching out to her to do a presentation to the Indian Health Care Board of Directors for the work on tribal lands.
Susan Molloy spoke about supporting the Public Health issues and asked that the Board support it in any way they can.  She indicated that this is an urgent issue for those who live in rural areas and in the small towns in Navajo County.

SHERIFF'S OFFICE :

a.

Presentation and update on recent activity by the Navajo County Major Crimes Apprehension Team (MCAT) :  Sheriff Clouse gave an update on the Major Crimes Apprehension Team.  A presentation was shown.  He indicated that he appreciates the support to MCAT for over 25 years and that it has been a priority to the Sheriff's Office for over 25 years.  He stated that he wants to give an update on statistical data from 2020.  He advised that there is a Board for MCAT, which consists of him, Brad Carlyon and the five city police chiefs within the County.  He stated that they are working in each of the district on all major crimes in Navajo County regardless of the jurisdiction and that it is a true partnership of law enforcement.  He advised that last year during one of the Board meetings that members of the Board made a motion that they go to the City Councils and Board of Supervisors to give a report.  He introduced Brian Swanty, the Chief Deputy, and indicated that he will give some statistical data, Wylie Williams, the Task Force Commander and Kyle Esparza, the Task Force Commander.  He spoke about the different locations that they have been working in, stated that they get calls at all times, even in the middle of night and that they are dedicated to making the County safe and great place to live and visit.  He stated that they work to seek and destroy drug organizations and that they work with agencies to find the source and root of the problem. He spoke about working with the victims and how the County Attorney's Office is working with the Courts on different programs.  Brian Swanty addressed the board and indicated that it makes him proud to talk about the positive actions of the task force.  He addressed the different agencies that make up the MCAT team.  He talked about the designation of a HIDTA County, High Intensity Drug Trafficking Area, in 2009, which brings in additional support.  He reviewed statistics from 2020 and indicated that 166 pounds of methamphetamine; 15.6 pounds of heroin; 16.9 pounds of cocaine, which they don't see as much of but that it is still popular of the Northeast coast; 449 pounds of marijuana and 53,000 fentanyl pills were seized by the team in Navajo County.  He stated that the fentanyl pills are coming cloned as hydrocodone, which are blue.  He stated that they are coming from south of the board and that in the past three to four years it has really increased in Navajo County.  He advised that $48,000 of US currency was seized which is directly related to drug trafficking organizations. He spoke about proactive enforcement and indicated that the team had 96 arrests during 2020, that it was slow at the first part of the year due to some revamping, they had 323 agency assists, 12 assists were just to Snowflake Taylor Police Department and 96 search warrants.  He addressed some difference incidents that they assisted with in Snowflake and Heber-Overgaard.  He indicated that it was a big success and that they had a great impact on the community.  He advised that they have now shifted over to the Winslow area to help with drug problems there.  He stated that they are committed to Navajo County and the citizens.
Brad Carlyon addressed the comparison in street values and the amount of drugs and indicated that taking them off the streets helps the communities.  He addressed the issue of fentanyl pills with juveniles and overdoses.
Chairman thanked for all they have done and make our communities safer.
Sheriff Clouse stated that they appreciate the support from board, the County Attorneys and County Management to be able to make this successful.

TREASURER :

a.

Consideration and possible approval of a sole source agreement with HARRIS GOVERN for Licensed Software, maintenance, and provide professional services for data conversion, implementation, and training for a new Treasurer's System :  Kari Lopez addressed the Board regarding the item.  She stated that she knew going in that they would be faced with challenges of old system, that it has continued to deteriorate and that they have been working to come up with a solution that would not only help their office but also the county and the public. She further stated that currently the Treasurer's Office is in jeopardy of losing vital data if the system goes down.  She talked about working to try to develop a new system and indicated that they are no closer to getting it completed. She stated that the Assessors Office is using Harris Govern and that they have decided to go through them for the Treasurer's system.  She indicated that there is no other company that could provide an integrated system for the Assessors and Treasurer's Offices.  She stated that they would be able to access each other's systems, that right now they have to use the internet for up to date information from the Assessors, but that this software would allow them to integrate into one, which would be more helpful to the public as well.  She indicated that right now it takes time for information to populate when answering questions for the public.  She stated that the procurement department has determined that the Treasurer's request is justified and has approved it.  She further stated that staff recommends approval.
Supervisor Whiting thanked Ms. Lopez and stated that he knows that there have been problems and concerns for a while.
Chairman Seymore inquired that if during the transition there will be anything lost or delayed.
Ms. Lopez advised that it will be a year and a half to two years to get the conversion completed but that the Harris team is ready to go.
Chairman Seymore inquired if there is an area for the training sessions and if there will be any issues with the day to day operations during training.
Ms. Lopez talked about the need for more staff.  She stated that they are running two systems, a tax and banking system.  She indicated that they are shorthanded will need extra support and help.  She advised that they will have a team at Harris to help fix issues.  She further stated that the other counties using the Harris system can work together for software additions and improvements.
Chairman Seymore inquired as to the cost to maintain the current system.
Ms. Lopez stated that the system is not maintained right now, that they have someone in IT that has to come to fix it and try to keep it going.  She indicated that they have issues that need to be fixed each day, since they are not able to go back to previous days in the system, it has to be fixed when the issues come up.
Chairman Seymore inquired if Harris is local or would it be remotely fixed.
Ms. Lopez advised that Harris is out of Colorado, but that they have a people based in Arizona.
Chairman Seymore thanked Ms. Lopez for the research on the system and for the information provided.

Supervisor Peshlakai made a motion to approve a sole source agreement with HARRIS GOVERN for Licensed Software, maintenance, and provide professional services for data conversion, implementation, and training for a new Treasurer's System, motion seconded by Supervisor Benally .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

Consideration of a Declaration of Emergency due to extreme fire danger and to enact Section 7 of County Ordinance 01-19 Emergency Fire Restrictions, to enter into Stage 1 fire restrictions :  Catrina Jenkins addressed the item.  She stated that they are requesting the declaration due to extreme fire danger at this time, that it is above normal for this time of year but that we are in an extreme drought for the entire state and that it is not getting better.  She advised that last year we didn't have a good monsoon season or a good winter and that there have been several fires start in Navajo County. She stated that within the last week there have been twenty-two illegal or abandoned campfires within the Apache Sitgreaves Forest.  She requested that they approve the declaration of emergency to enact Section 7 of Ordinance 01-19 for emergency fire restrictions.
Supervisor Whiting inquired as to what the Forest Service is currently saying, if they are looking at shutting down the forests.
Ms. Jenkins addressed all of the entities that are in Stage One restrictions, stated that they are trying to avoid closures and that it would be the most extreme circumstance in their restrictions.  She further stated that closures are hard to control because people will go deeper into the forest than they want them to and they are at place where it is the most dangerous.

Supervisor Benally made a motion to approve Declaration of Emergency due to extreme fire danger and to enact Section 7 of County Ordinance 01-19 Emergency Fire Restrictions, to enter into Stage 1 fire restrictions, motion seconded by Supervisor Peshlakai .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Supervisor Peshlakai made a motion to return to regular session, motion approved by Chairman Seymore .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
