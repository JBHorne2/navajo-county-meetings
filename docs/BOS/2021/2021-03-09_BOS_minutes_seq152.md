NAVAJO COUNTY PUBLIC HEALTH SERVICES DISTRICT: Board of Directors Session:

REGULAR AGENDA:

1.

COVID-19 Update :  Janelle Linn reported that we are up to 15,765 cases in Navajo County and that 6,612 cases have been worked by the Navajo County Public Health nurses.  She stated that numbers remain low, that there were eight cases reported within last 24 hours and only two cases were reported the 24 hours prior.  She further stated that they are monitoring sixty-nine households at this time.  She advised that 1,594 people have been hospitalized in Navajo County; that there have been 505 COVID related deaths and 577 school related cases.  She indicated that to date there is an eighteen percent hospital bed capacity, which is still a little concerning, but that only eleven percent of those beds are being used due to COVID cases.  She advised that for vaccines they are continuing to steadily receive around 2,000 to 2,500 doses each week for Navajo County.  She stated that the nations vaccine coverage rate is at 9.2 percent.  For Navajo County she advised that 23,958 individuals vaccinated; 16,174 are people who have started their first dose and 7,841 have completed their two-dose series, which is a 14.3 percent vaccine coverage rate.  She further advised that there has been almost zero wastage in Navajo County and that we are above 100% for doses utilized due to being able to get extra doses from some of the vials.     She stated that the 55 and over population has been opened to vaccine eligibility, that they would continue to register through the website and that some of the facility partners have waiting lists that you can call and get on.  Ms. Linn stated that there is a new vaccine that has come into the market this week and last week, it's the Johnson and Johnson product and that it's a little bit different from ones they have been using.  She indicated that it is a refrigerated vaccine and is a one dose series.  She advised that there is some confusion on efficacy rates and that there have been a lot of questions.  She addressed the efficacy rates for the different vaccines.  She indicated that to compare one against the other is not really an adequate assessment.  She stated that the CDC is not making a preference on one or the other, that their recommendation is if you have access to a dose and want to be vaccinated you should take it.  She advised that there was guidance released yesterday from the CDC for fully vaccinated individuals regarding wearing a mask and quarantining.  She stated that some things that have not change are that while in public you should continue to wear a mask and that you should use caution with large gathering of unvaccinated individuals, especially if you are high risk.  She indicated that there are many unknowns about COVID, that it's being monitored all the time as far as vaccine efficacy, how long the antibodies are carrying people and how long you can go before you may need a booster dose of vaccine or if they will continue all the time.  She advised that on Friday the Governor issued a new Executive Order, that there has been some confusion around it, it lifted occupancy limits for some facilities such as gyms, theaters and restaurants, but that the mitigation strategies that were given in August and October 2020 are still in place which includes social distancing of six feet or greater and wearing masks.  She stated that for many of the restaurants and business like that it won't change things because in order to maintain social distancing you can't go to your maximum occupancy.  She advised that they are wanting to stress to especially the restaurant partners or business like that to take those things into consideration, that the Executive Order doesn't mean that everything was lifted and returned to normal day to day business.  She stated that this may impact things such as sporting events, that there are no more occupancy limits but that you still need to maintain social distancing, the plans and authority for local levels and for events larger than fifty people you would still need to submit your mitigation plan for your local town or city.
