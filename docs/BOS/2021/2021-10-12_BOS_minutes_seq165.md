REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

Consideration of a Lease Agreement between the City of Show Low and Navajo County for the lease of modular buildings at the Show Low Regional Airport :  Bryan Layton addressed the item.  He stated that the building is located on the airport property, that the lease would be for two years with option of two additional one-year renewals and that the cost would be $2,000.00 per month.  He further stated that this location would house Emergency Management and Public Health Code Enforcement services.   He indicated that the need for space is due to Public Health receiving new funding and staff soon for programs that are being mandated by the State and that with the new staff coming in they don't have room to accommodate them at the Show Low Public Health building.  He addressed the different options that they looked into and indicated that the City approached them with this opportunity.  He stated that Facilities and IT have checked the building, and they have found that it will meet the needs well.  He further stated that this will allow them to place staff at the location, create some flexibility and keep the new grant programs, as well as Code Enforcement and Emergency Management functions in one location.

Chairman Seymore inquired as to the snow removal addressed in the lease agreement and asked if there is a way to negotiate with the City to do the snow removal when they clear the airport parking lot at the location.
Mr. Layton indicated he will talk to the City regarding that to see what arrangements can be made.  He stated that there wouldn't be public access to the building, that it would only be for staff.

Chairman Seymore made a motion  to approve the Lease Agreement between the City of Show Low and Navajo County for the lease of modular buildings at the Show Low Regional Airport, motion seconded by  Supervisor Peshlakai .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

9:25 Chairman Seymore left the meeting.

2.

Consideration of a Participation Agreement for Overdose Detection Mapping Application Program (ODMAP) between Washington/Baltimore High Intensity Drug Trafficking Area and Navajo County :   Rhonda Krouse addressed the Board on the item.  She indicated that the HIDTA program was created by Congress alongside the anti-drug abuse act in 1988 in response to the large increase of drug activity in the United States.  She stated that they created an overdose detection mapping application program that will help detect and add real time surveillance for communities both on and off tribal land.  Randy Moffitt, who is a HIDTA Drug Intelligence Officer, stated that ODMAPPING is a program that has been implemented throughout the State of Arizona and that he has reached out to the Sheriff, the Health Department and to Emergency Management about brining the program to Navajo County.  He stated that it is real time accurate data related to suspected fatal and non-fatal overdoses. He indicated that today they are requesting that the Board sign the participation agreement which states that you will comply with the ODMAPPING guidelines and won't share data with anyone not allowed to have access.  He stated that the ODMAPPING project is a tool to provide resources to people in crisis and save lives.  He further stated that we are experiencing a fentanyl crisis not only in Arizona but also in Navajo County.  He indicated with the mapping law enforcement will be able to research where there are multiple overdoses and be able to apply resources in the high crisis areas.  He advised that there are twenty-two different agencies in the State that are now using this tool.  He showed an example of how the map works.  He stated that the map shows the total suspected overdoses, the fatal overdoses and the Naloxone administration and saves.  He indicated that there are programs out there to give information but that the information they provide is old and dated.  He stated that this program is very detailed, that you can see the exact neighborhoods.  He further stated that this is non-hipaa complaint, meaning that it has no information specific to a person.  He advised that the program is free to Navajo County, that he would assist the Sheriff's Office and Health Department to make sure that it is up and running.  He further advised that the long-term goal is to get with Health Department, the Sheriff's Office and Emergency Management to help assist people.
Vicci Solomon with NEXIS Coalition stated that they track the deaths in Navajo County, and she talked about the number of overdoses from 2020.
Mr. Moffitt addressed the number of overdoses and the number of lives saved with Naloxone.

Supervisor Peshlakai inquired as to the tribal communities having this implemented and inquired if the tracking will be only off reservation or if it will include tribal lands.
Mr. Moffitt indicated they are willing to share with anyone who is willing to share the data back with the program.  He indicated that they are working to get the information to other agencies.  He indicated that he has meet with the Sheriff's Office and that they are supportive of the program.
Supervisor Whitesinger thanked Mr. Moffitt and indicated that she appreciates them bringing this information to our communities.

Supervisor Whiting made a motion  to approve the Participation Agreement for Overdose Detection Mapping Application Program (ODMAP) between Washington/Baltimore High Intensity Drug Trafficking Area and Navajo County, motion seconded by  Supervisor Benally .  Vote passed 4-0 approving the motion, with Chairman Daryl Seymore being absent for the vote.

COUNTY ATTORNEY :

a.

EXECUTIVE SESSION pursuant to A.R.S. § 38-431.03(A)(3) for the Board to be briefed on the National Opioid Litigation :  Glenn Kephart stated that they may not need to move into Executive Session.  He requested that Jason Moore be able to start his presentation and if needed they could then move into Executive Session.

b.

RECONVENE : Consideration and approval to finalize the "One Arizona Plan" which will control how opioid settlement proceeds are to be divided between the counties, cities, and towns that came as a result of settlements in the national multi-district opioid litigation :  Jason Moore stated that they are looking at finalizing the One Arizona Memorandum.  He provided some background of the case, indicating that in 2019 Navajo County joined as a plaintiff in a multi-district litigation in Ohio against several opioid manufactures and distributors and that we are seeking damages for problems caused by the opioid epidemic.  He stated that they are requesting to be compensated for the increased burden that has been placed on the courts, the defense attorneys, the prosecutors' offices, public safety resources, probation, and greater the greater need for treatment as a result of the opioid epidemic.  He indicated that last year the Arizona Attorney General's Office and the Counties talked about forming a coalition of the State, Counties and the Municipalities to talk about an agreement regarding how the State of Arizona would distribute the proceeds if an agreement or settlement was reached.  He stated that at the time the plan was developed due to the defendants expressing a willingness to discuss settlement terms, but that they wanted to reach settlement with as many plaintiffs as possible.  He further stated that during the discussions with the Attorney General's Office they reached an agreement which would give fifty-six percent of any settlement proceeds to the Counties and Municipalities and that forty-four percent would go to the State. He indicated that the settlement money can only be used for drug treatment, prevention, and costs related directly to combat the opioid epidemic.  He advised that no local government in Arizona can receive settlement money unless they sign on to the One Arizona Plan.  He addressed the attorney fees that will be paid and indicated that it will be a shared cost for all the jurisdictions involved. He stated that the agreement provides a bonus from the defendants for as much participation as possible, so that the more municipalities that participate, the more money Arizona will receive overall.  He indicated that the goal of the One Arizona Plan is to get one hundred percent participation to access as much of the bonus as possible to help with the Opioid epidemic.  He stated that the initial plan approval happened last summer, that all 15 Counties and 90 Municipalities joined the One Arizona Agreement but that it was a non-binding agreement and that it is being brought back now because it needs to be finalized.  He advised that the reason they are trying to finalize now is because there are a group of defendants that are willing to enter into an agreement.  He stated that once the numbers are finalized, he will advise the Board.  He stated that the deadline to approve One Arizona Agreement is October 10, 2021.  He addressed the percentages for each jurisdiction within Navajo County once plan is signed.  He stated that the plan is very detailed and that it goes into a lot of detail on how money can be spent.  He stated that he strongly recommends that the Board approve the agreement.

Supervisor Benally inquired if the jurisdictions on tribal land will benefit from the agreement.
Mr. Moore indicated that the Navajo Nation and several other tribal governments have filed their own lawsuits and that they are talking about a settlement as well.  He indicated that their allocations will be more, seeing how they have been more heavily impacted.  He stated that the money that Navajo County receives will be for the non-tribal areas, primarily, due to the tribes having their own settlements that will received.

Supervisor Whiting made a motion  for approval to finalize the "One Arizona Plan" which will control how opioid settlement proceeds are to be divided between the counties, cities, and towns that came as a result of settlements in the national multi-district opioid litigation, motion seconded by  Supervisor Benally .  Vote passed 4-0 approving the motion, with Chairman Daryl Seymore being absent for the vote.
