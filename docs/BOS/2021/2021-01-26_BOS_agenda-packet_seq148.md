Presented By: Glenn Kephart
Subject:
Presentation to Bruce Tucker who was named the 2021 David's Hope Crisis Response Officer of
Distinction
Background: 
Every year David's Hope honors law enforcement officers for exemplary service and leadership in
responding to individuals in mental health crisis. 
The mission of David's Hope is to reduce the numbers of those with mental disorders and
addictions who are incarcerated through Prevention, Intervention and Treatment. David's Hope
promotes and secures treatment for those with mental health disorders and addictions through
increasing collaboration between our mental health and criminal justice systems.  David's Hope
provides education and mentoring, teaching people how to advocate for themselves and others.
Through education and mentoring we hope to create understanding in the community of the issues
facing those living with mental illness involved in the criminal justice system.

Title: Broadband
Submitted By: Melissa Buckley, Clerk of the Board of Supervisors
Department: Administrative Services
Presented By: Bryan Layton and Brandt Clark
Motion before the Board:
Consideration and possible approval of a sole source agreement with Magellan Advisors to
develop a Strategic Broadband Infrastructure Plan for Navajo County. 
Background:
The absence of reliable broadband access is one of the key inhibitors of economic growth in
Navajo County, and the County seeks to find innovative ways to play a regional leadership role in
improving competition, reliability, and resiliency for our communities.  The proposal from
Magellan Advisors will develop a prioritized infrastructure plan for the region.  This plan will
include inventory of existing broadband infrastructure; identification of reliable funding strategies
such as public private partnerships, grants, and other outside sources; documentation of specific
needs within our communities, and development of a conceptual network design to guide
development.  
Recommendation: 
The County’s Procurement Officer has determined that the County’s request for sole sourcing is
justified and approved.  Staff recommend approval of the agreement.

Scope of Work  
Magellan has worked with countless rural communities to explore ways to expand options for broadband 
access  for underserved and unserved populations. Our methodology is designed to gain a clear picture of 
the current broadband environment, leverage existing assets, and find innovative ways  for local 
governments to play a role in  improv ing  competition, reliabili ty, and resiliency for their communities.  
T A S K  1 .  F U N D I N G  A N A L Y S I S 
Our team of broadband funding experts will work with Navajo County  to identify  funding strategies  and 
sources available  for expanding broadband . We will explore funding options including leveraging strategic 
partnerships and the reinvestment of revenues received for the use of infrastructure in the public right -
of-way into the County ’s broadband program. We will also evaluate outside funding sour ces at the state 
and federal levels, providing an analysis of grant and loan opportunities that may be available to Navajo 
County , which may include the use of economic development, transportation, Arizona State Broadband 
Office,  USDA ReConnect, RDOF , and other funding sources.  At the onset of the engagement, our  grants 
team will identify and assess grant opportunities for which to apply.   The final report will include a funding 
action plan  and schedule, identifying key dates and action items for pursuing e ach funding source.  
Because of Magellan’s experience in working with rural clients, our advisors have a deep understanding 
of the challenges that small communities face in deploying new broadband infrastructure to serve their 
populations, including cost ba rriers, attracting investment, and aggregating demand for a successful 
business model. We will work closely with the County’s team to develop innovative solutions to these 
issues based on our knowledge of best practices and Navajo County’s unique situation . Magellan’s team 
will recommend cost -effective build options and funding opportunities that make use of technologies, 
construction standards, strategic joint builds, and other factors that will provide needed last -mile 
connectivity where it is needed.  
TA S K  2 .  A S S E T  I N V E N T O R Y   
We will begin by conduct ing  a comprehensive asset inventory of the County’s  current assets in the public 
right -of-way including conduit, fiber, antennas, poles, towers, abandoned facilities, active facilities and 
other infrastructure  to determine their usefulness for expanding broadband  within the region . This effort 
will provide a realistic assessment of assets available for expanding broadband connectivity.  
We believe that the following components should be analyzed:  
 Underground conduit, innerduct, empty and available conduit 
 Fiber cables, strand counts, splice points, terminations and utilized strands 
 Vault and handhole locations 
 Available and reserved capacity throughout the network 
 Construction and placement method policies 
 Current as-builts and documentation 
 Terminating locations and city facilities 
 GIS maps including County-owned property, right of way, easements 
 Location of capital improvement projects and economic development zones 
 Current and planned locations of public safety cameras and traffic signal interconnect 
Prior to kickoff, Magellan wil l request GIS file s from the County . Using this  data , we propose to first build 
a geo -correct layer of conduit and fiber, identifying placed conduit, type, size, status (occupied/vacant) 
and related information. A second layer will incorporate poles, traff ic signal cabinets and other assets to 
be used for expanding broadband.

We will also work with the County’s  team to assess planned projects that may create opportunities to

T A S K  3 . M A R K E T  A S S E S S M E N T &  G A P  A N A L Y S I S 
In addition to evaluating the existing broadband infrastructure in Navajo County, our team will conduct a 
market analysis to gain an understanding of what service offerings are currently available to the Navajo 
County community. Magellan’s market anal ysis will identify the services that are available, providers, 
service level, pricing, and access. We will document all publicly owned and privately -owned networks in 
the County and research incumbent providers that currently serve the market. This informa tion will 
come from a variety of sources, including our comprehensive  broadband database, third -party research, 
and information obtained from the  providers themselves.  
We will then analyze the current  market and delineate served, underserved and unserved areas. We will 
also work  with the local providers to understand and document their current needs, as  they too are 
stakeholders in the community. These discussions should lead to  potential opportunities for the C ounty 
and private providers to defin e potential  partnership opportunities in delivering new broadband services 
to the region.

T A S K  6 . C O N C E P T U A L  N E T WO R K  D E S I G N 
Based on our assessment of your community’s broadband needs, we will develop a conceptual network 
design that provides a high -level design for components of broadband infrastructure. The conceptual 
routes will be  key to applying for grant fundin g and will be  based on leveraging existing infrastructure to 
meet current and projected needs throughout Navajo County . 
Magellan will assist the County  in developing the most appropriate broadband network design based on 
the needs of unserved and underserv ed sections of the community, municipal services, smart city 
initiatives, businesses, and anchor institutions. We propose to utilize any existing conduit, fiber, facilities 
and other assets including, street light poles, traffic signal poles and traffic si gnal cabinets as a 
foundation to develop an expanded fiber -optic and wireless network capable of serving the needs of the 
County , its residents, businesses, and anchor institutions. The design process will also explore the 
feasibility of collaborating with  regional partners to serve areas outside of the County ’s boundaries 
including Tribal entities and neighboring counties . The conceptual  design will be detailed enough to 
estimate high -level costs to deploy the network, which can be used  for construction gr ant applications.

BOARD ETHICS/CONFLICTS OF INTEREST
• Conflicts of interest for board members and county 
employees are statute driven by A.R.S. §§ 38-501 and 38-
511.
• They are also governed by the Navajo County Personnel 
Policies Manual as found in Section 4.
• Violation statutes can result in criminal charges:
• Unintentional violations are a class 1 misdemeanor.
• Intentional Violations are a class 6 felony.

<!-- page 81 -->
Navajo County - For Official Use Only
WHEN DO I HAVE A CONFLICT OF 
INTEREST?
• You have a conflict of interest any time that you are 
taking action in your official position when you or a 
close relative have a “substantial” personal interest 
in the matter.
• When is my or my relatives interest in a matter 
substantial?  Whether or not you have a substantial 
interest is more easily defined by when you have a 
remote interest in the outcome of a matter.

<!-- page 82 -->
Navajo County - For Official Use Only
REMOTE INTERESTS
• Remote interests are defined in Section 4.25 of the 
Navajo County Personnel Policies Manual.
• Example: You are voting on a general salary restricting plan for the County, and 
have a relative that works for the County and would have her salary impacted by 
the plan.
• No conflict of interest.  You are voting on something with wide application to all 
county employees and your relative is just a member of a much larger class.
Example: County is contracting with a vendor to purchase Ford Trucks.  You know 
that you have some interest in Ford through a mutual fund you invest in that holds 
Ford stock along with a broad array of other company stock.
No conflict of interest.  The percentage of Ford you own is tiny so your interest is 
remote.

WHAT IS A RELATIVE?
• "Relative "means an  elected  official’s,  
officer’s  or  employee’s spouse,  child, child's 
child, parent, grandparent, brother or sister of 
the whole or half blood and their spouses, and 
the parent, brother, sister or child of a spouse. 
Other persons may legally be relatives but are 
not defined as relatives for purposes of the 
conflict of interest laws.

CONDUCT OF BOARD MEETINGS
• Board meetings are governed by the Arizona Open Meeting Law.  
Generally, the law requires:
• Posting notice of the meeting and an agenda 24 hours in advance of the 
meeting;
• Requires the Board to stick to the agenda.  If it is not on the agenda, you 
can’t discuss it;
• Allows for calls to the public, which the Board should generally listen to 
but not discuss;
• Executive Sessions are confidential.
• We do not follow Robert’s Rules of Order. Aspects of it conflict with the 
Open Meeting law.  We want a motion, a second, a discussion and a 
vote.

<!-- page 88 -->
Navajo County - For Official Use Only
CONDUCT OF BOARD MEETINGS
• Caution:  The problem of the “splintered quorum.” Properly understood, it means 
communications about a specific piece of county business between a quorum of 
board members that occurs sequentially outside of a properly noticed public 
meeting.  They can happen accidently.
• Verbal conversations between board members
• Emails
• Social Media
• Third party discussions
• While communicating with one other board member about a specific issue is 
allowed, the best policy is to avoid discussing county business with other members of 
the board outside of a duly noticed public meeting.
• It is best to avoid having a quorum of the board present in the same place, though 
sometimes it cannot be avoided.  If it can’t be, we post a courtesy notice.
