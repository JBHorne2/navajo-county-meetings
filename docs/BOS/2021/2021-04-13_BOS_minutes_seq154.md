PROBATION :

a.

Juvenile Detention Presentation :  Bryan Layton addressed the item.  A presentation was shown.  He talked about consideration of reopening the Juvenile Detention facility, addressed the history and the decisions behind the facility being closed.  He addressed the reasons for considering reopening the facility and indicated that the savings from the past are going away.  He stated that one reason is the economics with the budget and another reason is the changes by Navajo County and the difference in approach as to how to help the Juveniles.  Jason Cash addressed the changes that have been made over the past 3 years in Navajo County.  He stated that the goals to protect the community haven't changed.  He advised that there are two approaches when talking about protecting the community, the first being incarceration, which is a short-term solution, but that studies have shown that someone is more likely to re-offend after release from incarceration.  He stated that they are looking at more of a long-term solution, which would be to change the way that the offender thinks and acts.  He stated that no program has a 100% success rate but that it's much easier to make these changes with Juveniles.  He talked about the changes in the programs since 2018, such as counseling; the respite center, which is temporary housing and the Hub that has after school programs and life skill programs. He stated that the biggest change right now is to have in home housing and not contract it out.  He stated that they are trying to rebuild family support and that if they are close to where the juvenile is, they are more likely to visit.  He talked about some future plans such as Multi systemic therapy, which provides intensive in-home family counseling and the Crisis Center, which would give an alternative to initial incarceration.  Mr. Cash addressed the keys to success, such as the partnerships with Arizona Office of Courts; maintaining a low detention population; Implement Multi systemic Therapy, with would be intensive in-home family counseling and becoming a license Behavioral Health provider, which would allow the ability to bill AHCCCS for eligible services.  He stated that in order to reopen the Detention Center they would need to hire staff and complete the staff training and certifications.  He advised that all facility preparations have been completed.  Judge Ruechel, Presiding Juvenile Judge, addressed the previous closure and indicated that she is excited with the possibility to reopen.  She stated that she feels it serves our community and the youth to have it open.  She indicated that the children need to be near their home, and that when they are placed back in the home, they need the wrap around services, which is hard when they have been housed far away.  She talked about the costs and indicated that this would keep the costs local, they would be able to employee local staff and have people who are dedicated to our children.
Chairman Seymore inquired if there needs to be a motion to move forward.
Mr. Layton advised that today was just a presentation and education, that staff will continue to work on this, and it will be presented at a later time for approval.
Chairman Seymore stated that this is a great program, that he hopes we are able to make it work and that it would be an asset to our communities to have it local.
Supervisor Whitesinger expressed her appreciation to staff and indicated that it is important not only to our organization but to our community.  She stated that the youth need to have direct connectivity.  She indicated that there have been experiences within the different communities with families having a hard time visiting the children or being able to provide the support that their children need.  She addressed providing services different to help the young people to get the additional support they need to be the citizens they desire to be.

REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

Presentation of the 2021 Fire Season Outlook and strategies and mitigation efforts : Catrina Jenkins addressed the item.  A presentation was shown.  She stated that it is group efforts to provide information and addressed all the agencies that make up the Fire Restrictions Coordination Group.  She stated that they will meet weekly for updates and forest information.  She addressed the pre-annual kick off meeting, which included tribal and non-tribal entities, where they discussed issues, the current outlook and what they are currently seeing.  She addressed the current drought monitor comparison from 2020 and 2021.  She stated that drought concerns last year at this time were non-existent/minimal and that this year it is high.  She addressed the current fire potential outlook and the areas of concern.  She stated that May and June show the entire State at high risk and that in July they predict a wetter than normal monsoon season.  She addressed the outlook summary, stating that there is a higher than normal fire potential, that the winds will be picking up and it will likely turn into a red flag.  She advised that they did have small fire yesterday in the Heber area but that crews were able to manage it and keep it under four acres in a few hours.  She stated that it spread one acre in ten minutes.  She further stated that they ended up evacuating a  few homes due to them being close to fire scene.
Chairman Seymore thanked Ms. Jenkins for her efforts and the great job her team has done.
Neil Traver, with APS, talk about their wildfire safety and protocols to ensure they provide safe and reliable energy to the State.  He addressed the fire prevention and indicated they work year-round to do an integrated vegetation program around control power lines, that they clear out 10 foots spaces around poles in high risk areas and that they do detailed line inspections on over 4500 miles of line to ensure maintenance is kept up on equipment before potential risk starts.  He indicated that they do operation protocol that can sense faults on power lines and addressed how the device works. He stated that during fire season they go out to the devices and turn them off and physical make sure that everything is working.  He talked about the fire protocols for checking all the equipment and indicated that they have done this for several years and that they have had conversations with the cities and customers to let them know about what they are doing.  He stated that they haven't done the operational protocols yet but that they will be in the near future. He indicated that they want to make sure they do customer outreach through meetings, postcards, letters and emails in the effected areas.
Chairman Seymore thanked Mr. Traver for his efforts.

2.

COVID-19 Update :  Janelle Linn stated that there have been 15,373 cases in Navajo County, that 6,779 cases have been worked by the Health Department, and that there have been 8 new cases in the past 24 hours.  She stated that the CDC and FDA has done a press release regarding the Johnson & Johnson vaccine and have recommended that use be discontinued for now due to people having developed blood clots after having that vaccine.  She stated that pending further investigation they have advised discontinued use.  She indicated that meetings will be held and further guidance will be released.  She stated that they have advised partners to discount use until further information is received.  She stated that for those who have received the vaccine, for three weeks after receiving it if they have headaches, severe abdominal pain, calf pain or shortness of breath that they follow up with health care provider.  She advised that 36% of Navajo County has been vaccinated.  She stated that over the next couple of weeks it will have been ninety days from peak of the last surge and that natural immunity starts to decrease after 90 days which puts you at risk for reinfection.  She indicated that they are pleading that everyone consider being vaccinated and if you have concerns talk to your Health Care Provider.  She addressed the possibility of a third surge.
Chairman Seymore  thanked for Ms. Linn for her presentation and all that she and her team does for Navajo County.
