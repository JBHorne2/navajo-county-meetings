REGULAR AGENDA

1.

COVID-19 Update:   Janelle Linn advised that as of this morning Navajo County is at 14,066 cases, with 5,803 of those cases having been worked by Public Health, and that the State of Arizona is at 727,895 cases. She indicated that Navajo County had thirty-six new cases reported in the past 24 hours, that this information is promising, and they are hoping we are on the downhill slide of the second spike. She advised that they are seeing the same pattern across the State and that the three Tribal partners are reporting the same pattern.  She indicated that they are still concerned with the hospital bed capacity, that reports from this morning were hospitals across the State are ninety-two percent full, that Summit reported that their ICU is full, and they have one bed available on the regular floor.  She further indicated that those infected are still requiring higher acuity of care and that hospitalizations are not significantly declining, so they are watching that closely.  She indicated that they are redistributing some of the surge staff that was hired for COVID, that they are pulling them off contact tracing and training and preparing them for vaccine distribution.  She advised that Summit had requested thirty surge nurses from the resource request line, that they were able to secure those nurses for them, that they are on site and they have helped them through the peak of the surge.  She further advised that those nurses are assigned to Summit until March, so they are hoping to have enough vaccines distributed in the communities by then so that they won't feel the impact when those nurses return to their homes.  She stated that they are experiencing some vaccine challenges, that they are currently in Tier 1A and Tier 1B, that 65 and older was added to Tier 1B, which added more people eligible for vaccines but that no more vaccines are being received to accommodate that addition.   She further stated that they are still experiencing supply shortages and that the demands far exceed the vaccine they are being allocated.  She indicated that they are at the mercy of the manufacturer, the federal government, and the State.  Navajo County has been receiving between 400 and 1500 doses a week for  our allotment.  She stated that they are getting a lot of feedback from the public, that they are discouraged with trying to schedule appointments through the vaccine link, that a lot of people think that the link is broken, so they have been trying to share the message that the link is working but that there are not enough vaccines available to meet the demand.  She further stated that they don't schedule an appointment unless there are vaccines available to avoid having to cancel the appointments or giving the public a false sense of security that they will be receiving a vaccine.  She indicated that they are asking for people to be patient with them, that they share in the frustration, that they know it's not a perfect system and that they are working with the vendor they use for the registration system to modify the process and try to add a few new features that may prompt messages to help people understand that the appointments are all full and that they are not having trouble with the system.  She advised that once enough vaccine becomes available, they will be distributing at their pod sites, at local fire departments and opening drive through clinics.  She stated that they are encouraging those who are ready for their second dose to reach out to the facility where they received their first dose and work with them to schedule an appointment for the second dose.  She indicated that when people jump around to different facilities it makes it hard for them in the allocation process to ensure second doses of the vaccine.  She addressed the Johnson & Johnson vaccine and said that they are hopeful within the next seven to ten days that they can have another vaccine product on the market, which is a one dose vaccine.  She indicated that ADHS has received funding from the federal government to assist with vaccine distribution, that they submitted their plan to them, that they have utilized their plan for over 10 years, and she is hopeful that they will receive adequate funding for when enough vaccine is available they can get it out to the communities.

Chairman Seymore thanked Ms. Linn. He stated that they have done a fantastic job and that we are thankful for her and her team.

d.

Consideration and possible approval of a sole source agreement with Magellan Advisors to develop a Strategic Broadband Infrastructure Plan for Navajo County :  Bryan Layton addressed the struggle with high speed internet in our region.  He indicated that businesses of every size depend on it especially now with COVID as more and more people are going online. He also expressed that there are also many opportunities for virtual learning.  He further indicated that reliable high-speed internet is essential for economic growth and development.  He stated that where we are at right now, we don't need another study, we need a detailed plan for getting a reliable network and how we can afford it.  He indicated that they reached out to our counties across the state for referrals and recommendations, and after some good discussions, they have selected Magellan, because they feel that there established processes and approach most closely match what they are looking for.
Jory Wolf and Melanie Downing with Magellan addressed the proposal for Navajo County.  A presentation was shown.  Mr. Wolf gave a history of Magellan and addressed previous areas that they have worked in.  He indicated that the only thing they don't do is the construction work, but that they do construction management and assist with inspections, grant writing, gap analysis, financials, and all the designs to make it work.  He advised that the West Coast team would be the ones working with Navajo County and addressed the different positions that will make up the team.  He further advised that the West Coast team has been focusing on the Navajo Nation and that they have been working them for eight months.  He addressed other agencies in rural areas that they have been working with on implementing broadband.  Melanie Downing spoke about the focus and proposed work plan for our local and regional project.  She indicated that the project is expected to take approximately six months.
Chairman Seymore thanked them for the presentation and staff for the efforts they put forth.  He inquired if Ms. Downing is familiar with Arthur Scott from NACo, provided her with his contact information and indicated that he just presented to NACo and that he is familiar with the bills and the grants available for broadband.
Bryan Layton advised that Keith Watkins with the Arizona Commerce Authority is also present, that they have been important partners in this effort, and requested that he be able to speak about the State's position.
Keith Watkins with the Arizona Commerce Authority addressed broadband in rural Arizona and the necessity for updating the broadband infrastructure.  He indicated that they are focused on getting the middle mile out to the various regions in Arizona.  He indicated that they have been successful in working with Arizona Department of Transportation and that they have now established a Broadband Division.  He further indicated that the Governor is very supportive of advancing this initiative for rural Arizona.  He stated that they have had discussions with the County Supervisors Association and that they have been terrific partners in supporting this initiative.

Chairman Seymore thanked him for his time and support.
Supervisor Benally inquired if this plan would include the Navajo Nation.  She addressed the poor connection in many places, especially for many of the students with remote learning.
Chairman Seymore indicated that they are working with all of the reservations within the County.
Supervisor Whiting indicated that they have done studies before, he inquired how Magellan helps this to go further than just doing a study.
Jory Wolf addressed the phased plan that they have in place.

Supervisor Whiting made a motion for approval of the sole source agreement with Magellan Advisors to develop a Strategic Broadband Infrastructure Plan for Navajo County; motion seconded by Supervisor Benally .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

He went over the financing calendar for this item.

SHERIFF'S OFFICE:

a.

Moved from item 4a  Presentation to Bruce Tucker who was named the 2021 David's Hope Crisis Response Officer of Distinction :  Bryan Swanty addressed Bruce Tucker being named the 2021 David's Hope Crisis Response Officer of Distinction.  He advised that David's Hope is a program through the Arizona Mental Health Criminal Justice Coalition.  He indicated that every year David's Hope honors Law Enforcement Officers for their exemplary service and leadership in responding to individuals in a mental health crisis.  He stated that the mission of David's Hope is to reduce the number of those with mental disorders and addictions who are incarcerated through prevention, intervention and treatment.  He advised the Dawn Wilson is the one who brought this to the Sheriff Office's attention, that he took great pride in submitting this nomination for Bruce Tucker and addressed the basis for submitting the nomination.

Chairman Seymore congratulated Mr. Tucker.

b.

Consideration of an Intergovernmental Agreement with the White Mountain Apache Tribe for utilization and leasing of the vacant Juvenile Detention Facility :  Sheriff David Clouse addressed the item, indicated that they have had an agreement with the White Mountain Apache Tribe for many years for holding some of their overflow.  He indicated that their facility needs some repairs, that they are asking for fifty to eighty inmates to be brought in, that with COVID they are trying to avoid adding additional inmates to our jail, and with the juvenile detention facility being vacant they have agreed that the tribe can use it up to July 1, 2021.  He stated that they would bring in their own detention staff and that the only thing we would be providing is meals and if they need medical attention.

Chairman Seymore thanked Sheriff Clouse.

Supervisor Whiting made a motion to approve  the Intergovernmental Agreement with the White Mountain Apache Tribe for utilization and leasing of the vacant Juvenile Detention Facility; motion seconded by Supervisor Peshlakai .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
