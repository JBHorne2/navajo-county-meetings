NAVAJO COUNTY BOARD OF SUPERVISORS' MINUTES
Tuesday, May 25, 2021

Present:
Fern Benally, Supervisor District I; Jason Whiting, Supervisor District III; Daryl Seymore, Chairman; Dawnafe Whitesinger, Vice-Chairwoman

Staff Attendance:
Bryan Layton, Assistant County Manager; Glenn Kephart, County Manager; Brad Carlyon, County Attorney; Melissa Buckley, Clerk of the Board

10:05 a.m. Call To Order :  Chairman Seymore welcomed everyone.

Item 4b, Update on ARPA Funding :

Rochelle Lacapa addressed the status of the ARPA funding, the rules that have come out and the timing for receiving the funding. A presentation was shown.  She indicated that they hope to receive the first allocation this week and then a year from now the second half will be received.  She spoke about some of the important dates for the ARPA funding.  She advised that the Treasury's guidelines have narrowed on how the money can be spent and that the areas include support for Public Health, replacement of public sector revenue loss, investing in water and sewer infrastructure, assisting with the economic impact to businesses and 501c3 partners, paying premium pay for essentials workers, and investing in broadband infrastructure.  She spoke about the guiding principles that had been discussed at the Board Workshop in April and different ways to determine what projects to move forward with.  She stated that we need to determine how to engage our community leaders and service organization leaders to make sure that our priorities are aligned for possible partnerships.
10:17 Supervisor Alberto Peshlakai is present.
Ms. Lacapa addressed the discussions at the Department Directors meeting where Glenn Kephart has asked what they need to be able to improve the way they work and to provide a better service to those who engage with the County. She indicated that they plan to meet with the elected leaders to determine the resources they need to support the work they do and better serve the County.  She further indicated that they are trying to determine the best way to get the cities, towns and the business community involved in the process.  She talked about short term goals, possible investment opportunities and having discussions on proposed projects and priorities.  She addressed some of the needs that had been discussed and if they are allowable under the legislation.
Supervisor Whitesinger inquired what would be the best way to have conversations with the cities, tribal communities and potential partners to determine if the priorities would best be meet with shared dollars.  She stated that if there is an investment that impacts the region or if there are nonprofits with the same requests that the county and cities can possibly partner on those.   She stated that there is a lot of support needed within our own organization, so that investment wouldn't be lost.
Ms. Lacapa addressed the Town Managers Meeting that she attended with Mr. Kephart and indicated that the managers were supportive of forming a committee or task force to identify priorities and projects in the region.  She indicated that they are requesting guidance on how to best partner with the tribes, organizations and businesses and how to make those meeting productive.
A discussion was held regarding the possible ways to distribute money, different uses to improve quality of life and different ways to partner with other organizations.
Supervisor Whiting inquired if we are asking the other organizations where to spend the money or if we are demonstrating leadership by saying here are some areas that we should be focused on.
Ms. Lacapa addressed the vision/needs based on the April 5, 2021 meeting that will be used when having the conversations to determine what needs the partners have that could align.
Supervisor Whiting addressed being able to look at resources, such as CSA, ACCO and NACO to determine what the other counties are looking at doing that can be innovative.
Ms. Lacapa indicated that everyone is still figuring out the rules and haven't started to make definite plans.
Mr. Kephart stated that other counties and organizations are in the planning stages for how the money will be spent.  He addressed the possibility of needing to add resources to the team to help once the program gets going and addressed it being funded by the ARPA funds.
Ms. Lacapa indicated that this will be a short-term big project that may require one or two additional team members, especially when the reporting requirements start.
Supervisor Whiting addressed having a meeting to address timelines and conversation points that can be addressed with our partners.
Ms. Lacapa addressed the project with Magellan and being able to use other resources that are available.
Chairman Seymore addressed looking at the different business structures for possible ways to help those struggling and also looking at the education system to help further education for our youth.  He stated that broadband has been a high topic but addressed the satellites that could also be a possibility.
A discussion was held regarding broadband versus satellites for the rural and tribal areas.

SHERIFF'S OFFICE :

a.

Budget Presentation and Overview : Chief Deputy Brian Swanty addressed the Board regarding the current partnerships with the Board of Supervisors and County Staff.  A presentation was shown.  He talked about the accomplishment of being able to increase the Detention Officers, Dispatch and Deputy salaries to make some of the lower paid positions more competitive. He addressed the Master Deputy position and stated that if they keep up their training and certificates they get higher pay.  He advised that it has worked well so far and gives them a goal to look forward to.   He addressed the vehicle replacement plan that is now in place allowing them to have better quality vehicles.  He talked about the new body scanner, indicating that it is state of the art.  He stated that they had issues with fentanyl coming into jail, and now they are having great success with keeping out of jail.  He talked about the radio technician that they now have in the jail.  He addressed the Hay Hallow radio site that has been funded and indicated that it is now in the process of going live.  He advised that they have been remodeling the Headquarters and that they now have a location in South County for the Sheriff's Department at the Pine Lake Facility, which is being remodeled and made into a resource center.
He spoke about current projects, such as, increasing the Sheriff's staff by two positions.  He advised that it will be the first time since 1990 that the Sheriff's Office has increased staff.  He stated that they are getting ready to increase the Deputies in the detention center by four, that they had lost five positions previously, but that they are now fully staffed.  He addressed the fleet management plan and indicated that they will cap at vehicles at 150,000 miles.  As to technology in the Sheriff's Office he indicated that the system is outdated, that they are in the process of looking for a new body cam system, which would provide good retention records and would also allow for redacting.  He addressed the mobile data computer system and the need for replacement.  As to the facilities, he indicated they are looking at a staff training room remodel, that right now it will house about 12 people and that they are looking at expanding it to allow for 20-25 people.  He advised that the long-term plans are to continue with competitive salaries.  He stated that they want to increase deputies by 11, which would be a full squad.  He indicated that it would allow for time off and would also help them with being able to get their response times down from an hour to possibly minutes.  He stated that they are looking at getting an in-house IT Technician due to the need for additional support. He stated that they want to get starting deputy salaries up to $50,000 and make it attractive for lateral transfers and that currently they have numerous positions that are just above minimum wage.  He talked about long term plans for MCAT, the possible cuts with state funding and the funds needed to continue the MCAT program.  He addressed the long-term technology plans for different computer software/programs, the MDT replacement, and a Power DMS for document signing and tracking.  He stated that Dispatch and the GIS/911 systems are doing well but that there are issues with the capacity that it can handle. He stated that they had a major outage in May and that the system was down for 6 hours and 49 minutes.
Chairman Seymore inquired what caused the outage.
Chief Deputy Swanty stated that they are still waiting on a report from Frontier as to the cause.  He addressed computer replacements, including the live computer in the cars, which is critical to their work on the road.  He stated that a Power DMS is possibility something that ARPA funding could be used for.  He addressed purchasing IFAK kits, which are first aid kits used for victims in shootings.  He addressed facility projects such as expanding the Heber-Overgaard office and the possibility of a new building for the Sheriff's Office Headquarters at the County Complex, with the other County Departments.  He stated that the property and evidence department is not at standard and that they need to focus on space, security and accountability within the facility.  He addressed the long-term plan with radio upgrades and radio coverage areas.
Chairman Seymore stated that he appreciates all that the Sheriff's Office does and that it's great to see the improvements and the plans for going forward.

PUBLIC WORKS :

a.

Budget Presentation and Overview : John Osgood stated that their team has two primary objectives, to manage the county's valuable transportation assets and to improve response time and customer experience in the Community Development program.  A presentation was shown.
Jeanine Carruthers addressed the Highway User Revenue Funds, which is the primary revenue source for Public Works.  She addressed the amount in the funds, indicated that with COVID they had anticipated a 1.5-million-dollar short fall in the budget and stated that they discussed projects that could be pushed off if they didn't have the funds.  She addressed the changes now and stated that it is unknown what the revenue will do.  She stated that there is not much of a change this year, that personal services are staying steady and there has been a slight increase in funding for personnel due to some adjustments with the road maintenance workers and mechanics.  She indicated that there has been an increase in services.  She stated that the Board of Supervisors special roads funds will continue to be $200,000 per District.
Mr. Osgood talked about the concept for the road maintenance progression plan for salaries based on skills.  He stated that they had a modest increase and thanked the Board and Management for the support in their efforts to upgrade the Equipment Mechanic classification and also the Road Maintenance classification.  He addressed how there was very little progression possibility and the steps that they will now have to meet to be able to move up.  He stated that the salaries will increase as their job skills expand.  He talked about how snow response is crucial and indicated that they have to be available all times of the day and night, with bad roads and bad visibility.  He addressed their snow policy for public safety in regard to response on the main heavy traveled roads.
Mr. Osgood spoke about the HURF expenditures and indicated that approximately 1/3 are capital outlay, which directly relates to or supports the primary objection of preserving roads.  He addressed vehicle needs and replacements for the fiscal year.  He talked about pavement management operations for paved and unpaved roads and indicated that it is a big asset.  He addressed capital project requests and showed a map with the project areas outlined for Fiscal Year 2022.  He further addressed how they determine the budget for pavement preservation and why the program is important for the road system.  He stated that they drive all paved roads every year and assign condition ratings by categorizing the roads as good, fair or poor.  He addressed the reasons for the ratings and the current overall conditions of the paved roads.  He stated that with continued annual funding their goal is to have ninety-nine percent of the paved roads in good or fair condition by 2026.  He gave a summary of the projects from last fiscal year that are currently under way or will be shortly and should be completed by June 30, 2021.
Chairman Seymore addressed the seal used in the Torreon area and indicated that he hadn't seen it used in this area before.
Mr. Kephart addressed the seal used for that project and indicated that it was paved with 3-inch pavement and chip sealed on top.  He indicated that they haven't seen chip seal on top of pavement before, that they are debating the pros and cons of that and will see if the method is good.  He stated that he can see some potential benefits, but they will see if it works.
Mr. Osgood addressed the salt product they have used and indicated that they are encouraged about it especially on the gravel roads.  He stated that when ADOT did their most recent resurface they did a cape seal.
Supervisor Seymore talked about road deterioration in the State of Arizona and how Navajo County is keeping up with our roads.
Mr. Osgood addressed Community Development and indicated that the primary objective is to identify the resources necessary for response time and customer experience in the permitting process.  He indicated that the purpose of the Community Development team is to protect the quality of life and ensure the safe and orderly growth of unincorporated areas in Navajo County.  He stated that in the key responsibilities in each of the work sections a large amount of interaction with the public is required and that they have looked at gaps in their program to find ways to fill the gaps with key resources.  He talked about new permits increasing over time and indicated that they anticipate a significant increase over the next few years.  He addressed the different types of permits.  He stated that the septic permits are going up which is an indicator of upcoming building permits.  He stated that the increases and decreases make it hard when determining a budget for needed staff.  He further stated that inspections have continued to increase over time.  He advised that in order to meet the needs and expectations of homeowners and builders they track several workload indicators.  He indicated that one thing they track is the processing of building permits, that the current time frame for processing them is up to six or seven weeks for some of the more complicated projects, which is not acceptable, and that the target time frame is two to three weeks at most.  He stated that another one they track is the building inspections, that they are keeping up with those and that the current and target time frame is twenty-four hours.  He talked about how Code Enforcement has suffered due to limited staff being focused on the building process and the building inspections.  He addressed some recent and current initiatives.  He talked about their COVID protocols.
Jeanine Carruthers talked about the budget proposal and expenditures for Planning and Zoning.  She indicated that the building permit revenue exceeded what was budgeted for this year, that they weren't anticipate the high increase but that they expect it to level out some.  She stated that they are still increasing the budget some to continue with the upward trend but at a conservative approach. She further stated that they are requesting two new positions, a Building and Zoning Inspector and a Permits Application Technician.  She indicated that they will have a slight increase for supplies to cover tools and supplies that will be needed for the new positions.  She stated that they are requesting an increase for services to help with the Code Enforcement Administration and to start working with a consultant on the comprehensive plan.  She advised that they are requesting two new vehicles which is in line with the replacement plan and a new database. She indicated that the supplemental budget for personnel is currently at four and a half positions for the Building Inspectors and that going into next year they are requesting the additional Building Inspection, the Permit Technician and the seasonal temporary wages to help when the building permits spike.  She stated that by obtaining these positions they are hoping to get back to the two to three-week turnaround time for building permits.  She addressed the seasonal wages, for Code Enforcement and plan reviews and the Comprehensive Plan, which needs to be updated every ten years and the need to work with a Consultant to update it.  She stated that they are requesting three laptops, two trucks and a database to handle highways, pavement preservation, GIS, building permits, planning and zoning and flood control.  She stated that they are looking for a system streamline their process and improve their reporting requirements.
Chairman Seymore stated that he thinks this is key to the development of our County and addressed the time frame for permits and inspections.

NAVAJO COUNTY PUBLIC HEALTH SERVICES DISTRICT : Board of Directors Session :  Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion to enter into the Navajo County Public Health Services District Board of Directors Session, motion seconded by Supervisor Whiting .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

a.

Budget Presentation and Overview : Janelle Linn introduced Sue Hilgart, the Deputy Director.
Ms. Linn thanked the Board for all the support over the past last year, indicated that it has been a challenging year for Public Health and that they appreciate the support.  A presentation was shown.  She stated that Public Health is so much more than shots, WIC and a resource for COVID.  She indicated that they are the foundation of the community and that it impacts everything from economic growth and poverty to different areas related to the quality of life.  She stated that this past year a big percent of the employees and Elected Officials were directly impacted by COVID from being infected, exposed and isolated or that they lost loved ones.  She talked about a solid and well-supported Public Health system in our community and indicated that there are many public health topics that are handled through the health department.  She addressed the many organizations that reached out to Public Health for help on how to navigate COVID and many other health issues.  She talked about the divisions within Public Health, the responsibilities for each of the divisions and the services that they provide.  She indicated that they work with different partners in the communities to meet the needs of the public and talked about some of the different programs that they assist with.
She stated that they are requesting a second Vital Records position be funded, that they currently have one person covering that division and addressed the difficulties for that person due to the workload, increase in requests and the time it takes to research and provide different records.  She further stated that they are requesting two Environmental Health Aids, which is more cost-effective that hiring Sanitarians, and will allow them to complete one hundred percent of the inspections required in their Delegation Agreement.  She indicated that they have a Delegation Agreement with the State that requires every restaurant be inspected at least twice a year and that in 2020 they were unable to complete fifty-eight percent of the inspections required.  She stated that with the two positions it will help to be able to meet the responsibilities of the agreement to keep the community safe, allow the Sanitarians to complete plan reviews for new establishments, allow for additional inspections, provide them the ability to respond to local food-borne outbreak and to alleviate stress on existing staff.
Chairman Seymore inquired as to how often food-borne illness happens in Navajo County.
Ms. Linn stated that they investigate 2200-2500 cases a month and addressed the process for the investigations.
She stated that they are also asking for a Public Health Nurse II position, that it is a position that has been vacant for approximately five years due to the budget, but that it would reduce the burden on current staff while moving into the recovery stage post COVID, help them to return to the more normal services, the ability to assess the areas that have been neglected during COVID and to plan and implement intervention strategies once the needs are identified.  She indicated that it will continue to allow them to provide clinical services in the Holbrook Office and that without the position they will be looking at reducing services there and at the off-site locations such providing vaccines/shot clinics at the schools and different businesses.  She spoke about the overload on current staff and about staff turnover since COVID.  She stated that this position would allow them to improve their community health assessment and improvement strategies.
She further stated that funding is becoming available due to the public health emergency, but that due to the emergency they are seeing an increase in other problems, such as mental health issues, poverty, chronic conditions, substance abuse, STI's and access to care issues plus the increase in the population for the area.  She indicated that they need to plan ahead and stated that not all public health emergencies are on a national scale and that no funding is typically there to help cover the costs.  She addressed some health outbreaks in different areas and the amounts that it cost the Public Health departments.  She further addressed taking those things in to consideration and investing in our Public Health Department with the budget, through grants and the ARPA funding.  She stated that most grants don't support capital funding and addressed what it takes to be able to provide the needed services in Navajo County.  She talked about office space and the need to be HIPA compliant to best serve the community.  She addressed the needs for large storage areas and indicated that they need additional building spaces, either new builds or by renting/buying existing structures.  She addressed the needs for vehicles and continuing education needs.  She stated that it will benefit the community through improved quality of services and better trained staff.  She spoke about how the ARPA funding could help assist in continuing education projects and also help in staff retention.  She stated that they want to keep the standards that they have and indicated that they were looked at by other counties as a model for response during COVID.
Sue Hilgart addressed how the Workforce was able to continue to provide services and assistance to customers during COVID.  She stated that they added fourteen people to the Electrical Apprentice Program, that they have added ten registered nurses to the local Workforce this year and that they provided twelve on the job training experiences.  She stated that they have been partnering with the Health Department in Navajo County and were able to provide services in Holbrook and Winslow Health Departments.  She addressed the Arizona@Work Job Center that will be opening in Holbrook.  She stated that they have a great partnership with the Probation Department in Winslow and that staff meets with probationers to work on job skills and do workshops for those being released from incarceration.  She talked about a few success stories in the County with assisting people with skill trades and helping with getting license for employment opportunities.  She addressed the youth program and indicated that staff that has been added to assist with the program.  She addressed the partnership with the White Mountain Institute which is the alternative high school in Show Low.  She addressed collaborations with organizations such as ChangePoint, Behavior Health, Veteran's Housing Program in Holbrook and different School Districts.
She stated that the WIC and Workforce staffs are providing outreach services in Pinetop and that they are providing services to the Veterans' community through the Northeastern Arizona Roundtable.  She talked about how they provide outreach in the local communities.  She addressed possible partnerships with organizations to provide services and indicated that they can reach out to other departments with same demographics for cross referrals.  She addressed serving as a leader to bring community partners together to identify areas where they can provide resources, referrals and partnerships in community events. She talked about the Ruby Payne's Bridges program to help with poverty and homeless the issues.  She talked about the partnership with the Arizona@Work Program and Workforce Development Program.  She indicated that we can use the funding from the pandemic to make our communities stronger and more viable for the future.
Chairman Seymore stated that they are excited about the partnerships that they have to further our communities.  He addressed the challenges to take care of all the needs that are in the communities.
12:54 p.m. Brad Carlyon was excused from the meeting.
Supervisor Whiting made a motion to return to regular session, motion seconded by Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
