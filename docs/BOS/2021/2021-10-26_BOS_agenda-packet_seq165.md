Proposal for Planning & Zoning Services – Navajo County, AZ 
 
 
 
 
Jon Froke, AICP, has more than 30 years of experience in planning and has worked in both the 
public and private sectors representing a range of diverse community planning and land 
development issues. He volunteers his time through several pro-bono speaking engagements 
on a range of topics including land use planning, public policy, economic development, and 
transportation. He is also active in the Arizona Planning Association and was elected by his 
peers as Chapter President serving from 2009-2013.   
 
Considered a consensus bu ilder and team player, h is past work includes overseeing and 
managing master planned communities, redevelopment plans, site plan review, annexation, 
design review, preliminary and final plats, development agreements, ordinance amendments, 
historic preservation efforts, and downtown area planning. 
 
After a n illustrious 27-year career with the City of Glendale, the last 17 years as Planning 
Director, Jon formed his own planning consulting firm and is available to assist the public 
sector and private sector on assignments.  Most recently, Jon worked for the Arizona State 
Land Department, City of Goodyear and the City of Avondale, on a contract basis, to assist in 
managing land development projects .  This work was gratifying and helped each of these 
agencies when they had limited staff resources.    
 
Adam Froke , AICP , is a Project Manager  / Community Planner and works with Jon on all 
aspects of providing excellent customer service to clients.  Adam has over 5 years of planning 
experience working with long-range planning and current planning projects in Arizona, New 
Mexico, Rhode Island, South Dakota and Tennessee.

The anticipated planning and zoning services is identified below. 
 
2011 Comprehensive Plan Update With Area Plan Review. 
Jon and Adam will initiate work on updating the County Comprehensive Plan. The Navajo 
County Comprehensive Plan was last updated in April 2011 and is due for an update. As the 
guiding document for the County, the Comprehensive Plan is of vital importance. We will work 
with staff to identify the strengths and weaknesses of the existing plan and use that 
information to inform the update.  
 
As Comprehensive Plan updates are often multi-year endeavors, it is likely that the update will 
not be complete by the proposed end of the contract date in June 2022. Our team will work 
with the County on a potential contract extension for this task or a succession plan to ensure 
that the Plan can be completed after our contract expires  and / or work with the consultant 
who is engaged to complete the Comprehensive Plan Update.  
 
Comprehensive Plan Updates include a thorough community outreach component. We will 
design a public participation plan that fits the needs of the community, ensuring all residents 
of the county have the opportunity to have their input on the Comprehensive Plan update.  
 
Additionally, the County has adopted the Aztec Area Plan. Our team will perform a concurrent 
review of this existing Area Plan and work with planning staff on identifying the potential 
need for additional Area Plans throughout the county.  
 
Deliverable: Initiate the Update to the Comprehensive Plan with Concurrent Area Plan 
Reviews.  Prepare Citizen Participation Plan. 
 
Zoning Ordinance Update.  
Jon and Adam will work on developing a list of potential Zoning Text Amendments with 
Planning & Zoning Staff.  As part of this endeavor, we will review the existing Zoning 
Ordinance and identify areas that may need to be updated to reflect current conditions.   
 
Stakeholders from the community will be engaged as part of this endeavor  and we will use 
best practices to ensure successful updates to the Zoning Ordinance. 
 
Deliverable: Prepare Draft Zoning Ordinance Text Amendments and Work Schedule.

Agency Problem/Attempts to Solve Problem: 
 
The Heber-Overgaard Fire District services, directly under the  Arizona Department of Health Services CON 
#4, 55 miles of state highways and under mutual aid and automatic aid agreements with neighboring districts, 
covers another 35 miles of state highway 60 mph and higher, those coming from the Valley are up for the 
recreational rim country and second homes and are unfamiliar with the weather impacts and wildlife hazards. 
Currently, the District's first-out ambulance is equipped with the older technology tool and for the staff of two 
on the apparatus, its weight (over 70 pounds) makes it difficult for one person to operate for any length of time. 
With the newer technology and ca pabilities in a lighter weight tool, a majority of any accident requiring a 
powered extrication tool will benefit and thus have shorter on- scene to at -hospital times.  The unit on the 
ambulance has been a critical resource in the past, but it is nearing the end of its service life. The tool is of old 
technology and capabilities and the maintenance and upkeep is becoming difficult.   
 
Agency Funding: 
 
Federal 402 funds will support Capital Outlay: Extrication Tool Package to enhance Emergency Medical 
Services throughout Navajo County. 
 
How Agency Will Solve Problem with Funding: 
 
Heber-Overgaard Fire District will utilize the funds to replace a 15 year old combination extrication tool kit 
positioned on their first-out ALS ambulance. This ambulance responds to all motor vehicle accident in and 
surrounding the communities of Heber-Overgaard. As a quicker response vehicle to the remote area of both 55 
miles of State Highway and remote forest areas, used for recreational vehicles and camping transports, this unit 
can be ahead of the responding resources for extrication by 20-30 minutes.
