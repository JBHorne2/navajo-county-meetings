SHERIFF'S OFFICE:

a.

Presentation on the New Text to 911 Capability:    Sheriff Clouse addressed the Board regarding the New Text to 911 capability.  He advised that Katelin Montoya, the Dispatch Supervisor, is also the regional 911 representative for the State of Arizona.  A presentation was shown.  He stated that Navajo and Apache County now have the option to text to 911 when they need emergency services and are not able to call and that this service went live on December 21, 2020.  He advised that there was a grant that Navajo County Sheriff's Office, Apache County Sheriff's Office, Show Low Regional Medical Center and White Mountain Apache Tribe applied for in 2019.  He indicated that Katelin Montoya implemented a six-step plan in rolling this out and addressed the different steps.  He indicated that this will offer many benefits, including enhanced access for people with disabilities and an alternative means of emergency communications and the availability and ease of use for the public.  He further indicated that this is intended for those who are in a medical situation where they are unable to speak or may have a disability or for those who need to get emergency services quietly, such as domestic violence or home burglary or for those who are deaf or hard of hearing.  He addressed the steps for text to 911.  He advised that the text location is not the same as current location technology.  He further advised the 911 messages can take longer to receive or may not be received due to poor service areas.  He stated that if the text to 911 is not available in the area a person is in they will be notified of that.  He further stated that calling is always more beneficial but that the text to 911 is a benefit in the event you are unable to call.  He indicated that they are excited to be able to offer this new service.

Chairman Seymore indicated that they appreciate the information on this new technology and that they appreciate those who worked on the grant to make this possible.

REGULAR AGENDA:

1.

COVID-19 Update:   Janelle Lin advised that there have been 3619 new cases since that last meeting, that today Navajo County was at 12,675 confirmed cases and Arizona is at 627,541, with 8995 being in the last 24 hours.  She further advised that currently off tribal land the public health nurses are monitoring 787 households, which are either infected cases or isolated direct contacts.  She advised that the are still encouraging wearing a mask, social distancing, washing your hands and staying home when you're not feeling well.  She indicated that they feel we still haven't reached the peak of the second surge.  She stated that they have been working closely with the schools.  She indicated that yesterday the Governor made an announcement in relation to schools and funding and that they are waiting to hear from ADHS as to how it will impact the benchmarks relating to schools to determine if anything needs to be adjusted.  She advised that Tier 1B opened on Monday for the vaccine.  She addressed who the tiers cover for the vaccine.  She indicated that they are being very upfront in letting people know that they are experiencing vaccine shortages and that they are asking everyone to be patient.  She further indicated that they put the requests in to the State health department but that they are at the mercy as to what is allocated to them from the State.  She stated that they are asking that people register for the vaccine and to not just show up to the vaccination sites due to the limited supply.  She addressed the different sites in Navajo County that have the vaccine.  She advised that right now in Public Health they are focusing on contact tracing and patient monitoring.  She stated that you can go to the County's COVID dashboard for information on the vaccine and registering.  She indicated that they have been experiencing concerns with people not being able to register, especially the senior population that don't have access or struggle with using technology.  She advised that the State is utilizing the 211 hotline to help register people if there are clinic and appointment slots open.  She indicated that they have also reached out to the local libraries and are working with them. She addressed the second dose allotments for the vaccine.
Chairman Seymore indicated that he appreciates the report and congratulated Ms. Lin on her new position.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and possible adoption of Resolution Number 5-21 approving/denying a Special Use Permit to construct and operate a 105' wireless communication facility on APN 212-20-147D, a portion of Township 9 North, Range 22 East, Section 22 of the Gila and Salt River Meridian :  Cody Cooper addressed the Board regarding the item.  He indicated that there is currently a 75' tower on the property that was approved.  He advised that the applicant did hold the voluntary outreach meeting on March 12, 2020; that four people did attend that meeting; that one attendee has concerns regarding health issues and was provided additional information by the representative as to health concerns in relations to these towers; the other three in attendance were noted to be in favor of this proposal.  He advised that there were comments given to staff both in favor and against this project, 2 adjacent property owners indicated that they were in support and one adjacent property owner was against this. He indicated that one concern was having to look at the tower in the area; that the applicant did make some visual simulations ahead of time and that those have been included in the packet as to what the tower would look like from the surrounding area.  He addressed the fall zone and indicated that the application requested that this requirement be removed and has submitted a letter from a civil engineer, that Navajo County Engineering Department has reviewed the letter but feels that the fall zone should remain the same size as the size of the tower for public safety.  He indicated that the Planning and Zoning Commission has voted 3-1 in favor of bringing this special use permit to the Board, subject to conditions at their November 19, 2020 meeting.
Chairman Seymore inquired if the 75' tower would be removed.
Mr. Cooper indicated that the 75' tower would remain.

Chairman Seymore opened the meeting up for public comment.  There being no response, Chairman Seymore closed the public hearing.

Supervisor Peshlakai made a motion to adopt Resolution Number 5-21 approving a Special Use Permit to construct and operate a 105' wireless communication facility on APN 212-20-147D, a portion of Township 9 North, Range 22 East, Section 22 of the Gila and Salt River Meridian; motion seconded by Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
