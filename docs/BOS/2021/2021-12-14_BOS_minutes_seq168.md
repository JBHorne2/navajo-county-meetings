Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 35-21 , adopting the Broadband Strategic Plan for Navajo County, Arizona and authorizing the County Manager to approve additional work under the terms of the existing contract for the purposes of preparing grant applications consistent with this strategic plan :  Glenn Kephart addressed the item and addressed the process for grant applicants that they are looking to submit for. Melanie Downing, with Magellan Advisors, addressed the Board regarding the project and the process used to get to the findings and recommendations for the plan.  A presentation was shown.  Ms. Downing reviewed the findings for existing fiber in the area indicating that it is minimal.  She stated that there is new fiber planned for the area and talked about those planned projects.  She addressed the different providers that are in Navajo County and the services that they currently provide.
The reviewed some of the key issues that were brought up during discussion regarding the broadband needs, such as public safety concerns, healthcare, education, working at home, economic development and people wishing to relocate to more remote areas.  Ms. Downing talked about the survey that was conducted and the results from the survey.  She addressed the speed test data that was received in different areas of the county.  She reviewed the gap analysis, she indicated that in order to address the issues their recommendation would be a middle mile county owned fiber network and addressed the benefits of it.  Ms. Downing talked about the modal and high-level design that they have come up with to create a middle mile network and address the needs in the area.  She addressed some of the possible partnerships opportunities for building the network and funding opportunities.

Chairman Seymore addressed some of the key considerations such as connecting county communities not just county facilities.  He inquired as to the commitments from our Tribal partners.
Glenn Kephart addressed the community understandings moving forward and indicated that they have had meetings with Town and City Managers to discuss the potential projects, that there is unanimous support and that they understand the benefits to each of the towns and cities in Navajo County.  He stated that they have had conversations with NPC who has agreed to do a letter of support and that he believes Supervisor Peshlakai has talked with President Nez who is in support.  He indicated that the grant application is due January 31, 2022 so they have time to finalize the support letters and make sure the communities understand what they are working to accomplish.
Chairman Seymore indicated that he appreciates the effort that staff and Magellan has but forth to put us in the position that we are in.  He addressed concerns with funding and with being able to complete the project once it's started.
Mr. Kephart stated that they are not asking for commitment of funds at this time and addressed the concerns with funding, partnerships and completing the project.
Melanie Downing addressed the possible partnerships.
Supervisor Benally addressed concerns with this project not benefiting the northern part of the county.
Chairman Seymore addressed the concerns with the project extending to the last mile.
Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked Magellan for the work they have done.  She talked about the impact and limitations that broadband has in the rural areas.  She addressed having the partnerships and funding opportunities that will make finishing the project to the last mile possible.  She addressed the importance of working with the Tribal communities to make sure that they also will benefit in the future.

REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

COVID-19 Update:   Janelle Linn stated that COVID case counts continues to surge.  She indicated that the hospitalization and mortality rates, especially off tribal lands, are higher than they have been since the start of the pandemic.  She stated that last week there were 345 new cases off tribal lands, 25 new hospitalizations and that thirty-nine percent of the cases were in people under forty.  She indicated that so far this week there are 117 new cases, 6 new hospitalizations and 2 deaths reported.  She advised that there were 10 deaths last week off tribal lands.  She stated that they are still encouraging people to be aware, be cautious, to consider taking precautionary measures and that they are encouraging people to get a vaccination and their boosters.  She advised that the Delta variant continues to be the primary variant circulating in our communities and that it's making people very sick.  She further stated that they are still seeing very good efficacy rates for the vaccine preventing hospitalization or death.  She indicated that the new Omicron variant is circulating through many states and that there are some cases in Arizona.  She further indicated that so far that variant is not making people as sick but that it is still very infectious.
Chairman Seymore thanked Ms. Linn for the update.

ASSESSOR :

a.

Consideration of the Agreement between Pictometry and Navajo County for a flight of specific areas in the county :  Cammy Darris addressed the item.  A presentation was shown.  Ms. Darris indicated that Counties throughout the United States use this without having to go out to visit the properties.  She indicated that it provides accurate data for remote assessments, that it saves travel time, that it provides measuring tools, that they can see changes from previous flights and be able to determine if need to go get new data.  She indicated that it can be used by anybody and that they are willing to share with other departments.  She stated that the program allows you to view all sides and to use a measuring tool.  She stated that in person measuring is very time-consuming but that with the tool you can do it in minutes.  She indicated that there are several Counties in Arizona that use it and that it helps with staffing and funding issues.  She stated that in some remotes areas it is dangerous for staff to go out, so this would help with staff safety, that it saves driving time and allows staff to be in the office and that you can get an overview without having to go on people's property.  She indicated that this would help with the State requirements for visiting properties.  She stated that staff requests approval.

Chairman Seymore inquired as to the two different projects.
Ms. Darris indicated that it will be flown now and then again in two or three years.  She stated that they could change the area for the second flight for the same cost.  She indicated that most areas for the flights are open areas.

Supervisor Peshlakai made a motion  to approve the Agreement between Pictometry and Navajo County for a flight of specific areas in the county, motion seconded by  Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Consideration of Task Agreement Number 25 to the Intergovernmental Agreement between the Bureau of Indian Affairs- Navajo Region and Navajo County that authorizes funding assistance for the Chinle and Western Agencies to provide material and parts which is included in "Part A" of the agreement :  John Osgood addressed the work and coordination that goes into these agreements.  He indicated that this request addressed multiple road maintenance priorities in the Black Mesa Region of District One and throughout the District One.  He further indicated that the BIA has requested that Navajo County to provide maintenance materials for stock piling in Pinon in preparation for winter response and for use continuing repair work on areas of the roadway that were eroded during the monsoons.  He stated that this request also includes funding assistance for heavy equipment part maintenance, including grader tires, tire chains, and cutting edges for motor grader blades.  He indicated that the source of funding is the Highway User Revenue Fund and that the fiscal impact is $50,000.00.   He stated that staff recommends approval.

Supervisor Peshlakai made a motion  to approve Task Agreement Number 25 to the Intergovernmental Agreement between the Bureau of Indian Affairs- Navajo Region and Navajo County that authorizes funding assistance for the Chinle and Western Agencies to provide material and parts which is included in "Part A" of the agreement, motion seconded by  Chairman Seymore .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

b.

Consideration of Task Agreement Number 26 to the Intergovernmental Agreement between the Bureau of Indian Affairs- Navajo Region and Navajo County that authorizes continued funding assistance for the Fort Defiance and Chinle agencies to provide heavy equipment operators to assist on maintenance of roadways :  John Osgood stated that this item continues a very successful partnership between Navajo County and the Bureau of Indian Affairs Department of Transportation that features County hired equipment operators working under the direction of BIA Road Maintenance staff to provide the Navajo Nation with road maintenance assistance of local public roads and school bus routes throughout District One and District Two.  He stated that the current task agreement is set to expire at the end of the month and that the new agreement is authorized would extend the partnership for a year.  He indicated that the source of funding is the Highway User Revenue Fund and that the request amount is $120,000.00 with the specific fiscal impact being $60,000.00 each from District One and Two. He stated that staff recommends approval.

Supervisor Peshlakai made a motion  to approve Task Agreement Number 26 to the Intergovernmental Agreement between the Bureau of Indian Affairs- Navajo Region and Navajo County that authorizes continued funding assistance for the Fort Defiance and Chinle agencies to provide heavy equipment operators to assist on maintenance of roadways, motion seconded by Supervisor Benally .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
