Consideration and adoption of the proposed Tentative Budget for Fiscal Year 2021-22 for Navajo County :  Jayson Vowell talked about the meetings held in preparation of the budget for Fiscal Year 2022.  A presentation was shown.  Mr. Vowell addressed a flat property tax, revenue growth, and having a good financial reserve.  He talked about the budget foundation including having a balanced budget, financial reserves and the operations of the County.  He advised that Navajo County has the fourth lowest property tax rate in Arizona and addressed changes for the upcoming year.  He stated that the County only receives about eight cents out of every dollar and the remainder goes to schools, fire district, flood control, the health district and other special districts.  Mr. Vowell indicated that sales tax this year will be the best over the past five years.  He addressed the projected amount for state shared sales tax and expenditures for Fiscal Year 2022.  He talked about some of the added positions in Planning & Zoning, the Sheriff's Office, Attorney's and other areas for state mandated services.  He indicated that 38.5% of the budget is dedicated to law enforcement and criminal justice, that 6% is Arizona State costs and payments, 9.5 % is for mandated services such as the Treasurer, Public Fiduciary, and the Recorder's Office, that Administration is approximately 21.5% and the contingency amount is around 24.5%.  He addressed the operating budget by category indicating that 67% is designated to staff and employee related expenses, that 14% is for supplies, 8% is state costs, 5% is for insurance, software and legal fees and & the other 6% is for capital outlay.  He talked about some of the special revenue funds such as APRA Funds, Health Trust Savings, the Jail District, and Public Works.  He addressed the employee health care and stated that this is the fourth year in a row that there has been no increase in premiums for the employees and that there are expanded options at no cost to employees.  He indicated that five years ago made changes to the health plan and that they have estimated that it has saved approximately fourteen million dollars.  He addressed the ARPA funding that the County will be receiving, and the discussions held regarding the process for spending the funds.  He advised that the County received the first half of the payment on June 1, 2021.  Mr. Vowell stated that staff recommends approval.

Supervisor Whitesinger stated that it has taken several months to get to this point.  She addressed previous years when Departments were asked to cut back and eliminate positions.  She stated that one thing that Navajo County prides itself in is serving public well, allow them to have positive interaction with the Departments and for them to walk away having had a good experience.  She stated that it is different to be able to give rather than take away and talked about understanding the Departments with what they had to give up and still be able to best serve public.  She addressed being fiscal responsibility.  She indicated that she hopes to leave a legacy that will continue to support the communities positively for future generations to provide best services they can provide.  She thanked everyone who worked to get the budget where it is.
Supervisor Whiting thanked the team for all they have done and thanked all the Supervisors for their time.  He stated that there are a lot of things being done to get the budget where it is today and that he appreciates the leadership and outstanding work from everyone.
Chairman Seymore indicated that this has been a team effort and addressed all the work that has been done.  He stated that we have a great team and thanked the prior leadership as well as the current leaders for their efforts to get to this point.  He talked about having an opportunity to enhance the county to improve the lives of citizens.

Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion to adopt the proposed Tentative Budget for Fiscal Year 2021-22 for Navajo County, motion seconded by Supervisor Peshlakai .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

COVID-19 Update :  Janelle Linn stated that things have been improving.  She advised that there have only been two new cases in past twenty-four and two new cases the twenty-four hours prior.  She stated that there have been 15749 total cases in Navajo County, that Public Health has worked 7075 and that there are seventeen households currently on isolation.  She advised that there have been 1704 cases in Navajo County requiring hospitalization and that 504 of those cases are non-tribal.  She further advised that there have been 542 deaths, with 188 of them being non-tribal cases and that 692 off reservation cases have impacted a school.  She stated that 54,388 vaccinations have been given in Navajo County, that there has a drastic decline in the interest of vaccinations in the past several months and that they are still pleading with the communities to consider being vaccinated.  She indicated that for those who have had COVID the natural immunity is only ninety days and then your immunity starts to decrease.  She advised that they are starting to see reinfections for those who were previously infected during the last spike.  Ms. Linn further advised that have identified 571 variant strains in Navajo County from random samplings.  She talked about how there are several more strains circulating and that those variant strains are more infectious to children and cause more severity in dieses process in children.  She addressed the percentage of cases in children over the past several months and indicated that there are now vaccines available for children.  She stated that we could experience another spike in the fall, that they hope it won't happen but that they are prepared just in case.  She indicated that they have mostly moved back into recovery mode, that they are preforming all public health services as normal other than the walk-in shot clinics but that they are looking at resuming that service in July.
Chairman Seymore stated that there is a great amount of gratitude for all that Public Health has done during this time.

PUBLIC WORKS :

a.

Consideration of potential nuisance abatement of property at 3397 Buckhorn Bend - APN 206-11-063, in Heber, Arizona :   John Osgood addressed the item. A presentation was shown.  He stated that this has been and will continue to be a nuisance condition for the surrounding area without the intervention of the County.  He indicated that consideration of the Board to authorize staff to begin the abatement work to clean up the property.  He stated that the property is located in Heber in the area of SR277 and Tonto Drive and that it is in the middle of a residential area.  Mr. Osgood addressed the violation history and indicated that over the past four years there have been Notices of Inspection to the property owner by staff and promises by the property owner to clean it up.  He further advised that Public Works Staff and the Sheriff's Office have visited the property, talked to the owners, worked with them and given them numerous chances to comply and that beyond the verbal promises by the property owner there have been more formal consent agreements signed by the owner to clean up the property.  He indicated that there have been brief periods of time where progress would be made but then it would go back to the unsanitary condition it is in today.  Mr. Osgood showed pictures taken two weeks ago that accurately represent the current condition of the property and addressed the unsanitary accumulation of junk, garbage and discarded appliances.  He talked about the process for abatement.  He advised that they plan to reach out to the property owner with the Board's decision.  He stated that if they do move to abatement due to the circumstances and under the non-compliance with the owner, they would either hire a contractor to come in clean up the property or get a team from the County.   He stated that they are trying to keep the amount for clean-up under $9,000.00 and that the source of funding is the General Fund.

Supervisor Whitesinger stated that she appreciates the efforts to work with the property owner and mitigate the situation.  She indicated that there have been multiple attempts to connect to ensure that the property was cleaned up, that there have been multiple complaints and there has been no action from the property owners.  She indicated that she appreciates the documents that were provided.

Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion to approve the potential nuisance abatement of property at 3397 Buckhorn Bend - APN 206-11-063, in Heber, Arizona, motion seconded by Supervisor Whiting .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
