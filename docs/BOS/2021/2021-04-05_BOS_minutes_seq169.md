NAVAJO COUNTY BOARD OF SUPERVISORS' MINUTES
Tuesday, April 5, 2021

Present:
Fern Benally, Supervisor District I; Alberto Peshlakai, Supervisor District II; Daryl Seymore, Chairman; Dawnafe Whitesinger, Vice-Chairwoman

Staff Attendance:
Bryan Layton, Assistant County Manager; Glenn Kephart, County Manager; Brad Carlyon, County Attorney; Brandt Clark, Deputy County Attorney; Jason Moore, Deputy County Attorney; Melissa Buckley, Clerk of the Board

9:10 a.m . Call To Order :   Invocation was given by Supervisor Dawnafe Whitesinger  and Pledge of Allegiance was led by Glenn Kephart.

BOARD OF SUPERVISORS :

a.

Welcome :   Chairman Seymore welcomed everyone and gave a short introduction.  Supervisor Peshlakai and Supervisor Benally introduced themselves and gave brief background information for themselves.

b.

Discussion of Key Pillars of Navajo County :  Discussion of Key Pillars: The Board of Supervisors held an in-depth discussion of possible Key Pillars for Navajo County which included Quality of Life, Public Safety, Economic Development, Transportation, Public Health, Education, Criminal Justice/Justice System and County Services.  (See attached)

c.

Budget Update:   Jayson Vowell gave a budget update reviewing general fund revenues and expenditures. He reviewed the Jail District budget, Highway User Revenue Funds and Vehicle Licensing Tax revenues. Mr. Vowell discussed the Pledged Revenue refunding and the debt service savings year over year. He indicated that we have reduced the interest charge from 3.15% to 1.67% which is an overall savings of 6.73%. He further gave an FY21-22 outlook.
Glenn Kephart spoke about the differences from previous years to where we are at now. He indicated that we have a healthy fund balance and that the economy is doing good.
At 10:08 a.m. the Board of Supervisors took a break.
At 10:25 a.m. the Board of Supervisors reconvened.
Supervisor Whiting now present
Rochelle Lacapa introduced herself and gave a brief introduction of herself.

d.

Broadband Presentation/Discussion (Zoom) :  Magellan representatives Melanie Downing and Jory Wolf gave an update on the broadband project and the ongoing outreach that is currently taking place during this phase in the project. They indicated that they would like to launch a survey in the next two weeks, and they are requesting that the county promote it. Ms. Downing addressed upcoming stakeholder meetings that will be ongoing throughout the county. Discussion was held regarding the survey and being able to fill it out with ease of access. The survey will be disseminated county wide digitally as well as paper surveys for those that do not have access online.  Discussion was further held regarding a Spanish and Dine version of the survey.   Ms. Downing discussed funding connections and community connections.  They discussed the overall process and grant funding opportunities.

e.

American Recovery Plan Act :  Glenn Kephart discussed the American Recovery Plan Act (ARPA) which will bring $21.5 million dollars into Navajo County. He discussed options on how to organize for this money to make Navajo County better for the future.  He spoke about Navajo County's guiding principles. Rochelle Lacapa briefly addressed how the plan is designed, how and when the program will roll out and how the funding will work. Ms. Lacapa indicated that the plan passed the House on February 27th, passed the Senate on March 6th and was signed into law by President Biden on March 11th. Ms. Lacapa explained that the ARPA provides $1.88 trillion in federal investments to defeat the COVID-19 virus and provide workers and families with resources needed to survive the pandemic. Ms. Lacapa addressed the direct funding allocations for Navajo County and the cities and towns. Navajo County would receive $21.5, Holbrook $1.2, Pinetop -Lakeside $1.0, Show Low $2.7, Snowflake $1.4, Taylor $1.0, and Winslow $2.2 million. She indicated that she did not have the information on tribal funding at this time.  It will be a couple of weeks before tribal allocations will be released.  Ms. Lacapa spoke about the allowable uses for the funds to be distributed, including responding to public health emergencies or its negative economic impacts; provide premium pay to eligible workers in the county or to provide grants to employers with eligible workers; provide government services to the extent of revenue reductions resulting from the pandemic; and to make necessary investments in water sewer or broadband infrastructure. All funds must be expended prior to December 31, 2024. Ms. Lacapa addressed the restrictions on the use of funds, addressed key provisions for direct allocations, and reviewed additional investment opportunities. Ms. Lacapa discussed the funding timelines, indicating that the first payment should be released around mid-May. She indicated that for municipalities the funding with be released 30-120 days after the State receives their initial deposit which should be around mid-June-September. Funding to Tribes will be direct funding, and they should receive $1.7 million immediately and the remaining amount should be determined soon.  Grant and other funding will be determined by administering agency as they become available.
Glenn Kephart and Chairman Seymore expressed their gratitude to the County Supervisors Association for being a part of the meetings to ensure county allocations.
A presentation was given by Janelle Linn that addressed the purpose and value of Public Health.  She spoke about the different agencies involved in public health system and how a solid and well-supported Public Health System is the foundation for a stable and healthy community.  She addressed different types of funded programs and indicated that some grants are insufficient and how some programs are not grant funded.  Ms. Linn talked about the one person who does all Vital records, addressed the time frame for processing those request and the summary and projection for vital records from 2019-2021.  She addressed the Environmental Health Summary and Projections, which includes inspections of restaurants, hotel and motels, swimming pools, schools, parks, smoke-free locations and food and truck vendors.  She talked about the Medical Examiners workload over the past couple of years.  She addressed the increasing numbers of different STD's in our region and stated that on a web search it shows the different areas that STDs are the highest in our area.  She advised that they go into schools to provide education on this, that they work with kids and address making better life plans.  She spoke about the economic impact of chronic health conditions; the health care costs and the impact with COVID.  She addressed the local numbers for COVID and stated that a little over 6700 cases were worked by the Navajo County Public Health Department.  She advised that they have reached out to about ten percent of those treated to resurvey them and addressed the different issues for those that are still having symptoms.  She talked about the overtime hours worked during COVID by the regular Health Department staff and the surge staff that was brought in to help assist and stated that the regular staff did this because they are vested in our community.  She talked about the need to invest in our Navajo County staff.  She further talked about the economic impact of investing in public health and prevention.  She stated that we need to invest in our Public Health system and address the needs to ensure the community is healthy long term and the funding priorities to ensure services and education that promote healthy communities by focusing on prevention and mitigation.  She addressed the funding priorities for capital projects; sustainable funding to address access to care issues and chronic conditions; the need for support staff to reduce burnout and for retention and consideration for professional development, education and training for staff.
A presentation was given by Bryan Layton regarding ARPA Funding.  He spoke on the guiding principles that influence you when deciding or considering a matter.  He addressed the objectives to discuss, distinguish and prioritize and indicated that the funding is a limited one-time opportunity, allowing us to do stuff we wouldn't have been able to otherwise to help with generations to come while honoring and preserving our heritage.  Supervisor Whitesinger stated that we are all invested in our community and want to make an impact.  He spoke of the guiding principles for different companies and how it sets the tone for them.  He addressed how to identify and remove barriers that limit positive and healthy growth in our communities such as the need to be affordable, sustainable, and have lasting positive effects, avoid creating new programs which do not have the potential for long term funding, leverage federal and state funding sources, partner with communities and non-profits in the region to create economies of scale and focus on projects and programs that benefit the region.  A discussion was held on different ways that this could happen and how-to incorporate these in the previously addressed pillars.

f.

Public Works Discussion; P&Z, Code Enforcement and Transportation :  John Osgood gave a presentation on Public Works for Community Development and Transportation.  He stated that the purpose of community development is to protect the quality of life and ensure safe and orderly growth of development in the unincorporated areas.  He addressed building safety, planning and zoning and code enforcement.  He spoke about the number of new permits, inspections, and revenues from 2011-2020.  He advised that the current timeframe for processing of building permits is four weeks and that the target timeframe is between two and three weeks.  He further advised that for building inspections the current time frame and target is twenty-four hours.  He indicated that they want to improve customer service and the customer experience.  He addressed recent and current initiatives, including new positions, COVID operational protocols and budget proposals for consideration.  He spoke about future initiatives to develop zoning ordinances, policy amendments for subdivisions and lot splits for quality subdivisions and strategies for addressing chronic nuisance and zoning complaints.  On transportation Mr. Osgood talked about how our roadways are a valuable asset.  He advised that there are 730 miles of roads that the County maintains, 409 unpaved miles and 321 paved miles.    He addressed annual road evaluations and ratings and the overall condition of the roads over the past 10 years.  He talked about the current condition of the roads and the investments for the roads that they will be recommending for maintenance.   He reported on the State and Regional transportation activity.  He talked about the key transportation projects that the County has been supporting for several years and the reason for supporting those projects.  He talked about the projects that they are tracking through the Arizona Department of Transportation.  Chairman Seymore stated that the roads are something everyone sees through the county.  Mr. Osgood indicated that they are always learning and trying to improve.   Mr. Kephart addressed the condition of the roads and the conditions for keeping at a consistent level.  Mr. Osgood addressed complaints on roads that are not county maintained.  Supervisor Whitesinger addressed the constant work on the roadways and stated that she knows there has been a lot of progress.  Supervisor Benally inquired as to the County using the stabilized soil.   Mr. Osgood indicated that it is the first year using it and addressed plans for using some going forward.

g.

Community Engagement Moving Forward :  Bryan Layton gave a presentation on Community Engagement, including priorities to inform and alert, build trust, and educate the public regarding county services.  He indicated that they have seen that interviews and videos have worked well and that the reaction has been good when it's authentic and unscripted.  He talked about working on providing a different prospective by having go pros and drones.
Alicia Deets spoke about a possible Virtual County Tour showing everything that the County has to offer, such as lakes, canyons, parks, etc.  She indicated that Navajo County has everything that people are looking for when traveling.  Mr. Layton indicated that the tour can be very specific with different lakes or hikes.  Chairman Seymore addressed marketing in our area.  Ms. Deets spoke about continuing to produce the Quarterly newsletters and increasing subscriptions.  She talked about continuing to work with Economic Development and the Workforce team to kick off a summer tourism campaign.  She addressed running relevant ads through broadcasts, print and social media to reach residents.  She further addressed building connections and trust by actively engaging with residents, businesses and employees.  Mr. Layton indicated that they are looking at future possibilities such as branding for vehicles and facilities with a more consistent design and style guide.   Ms. Deets addressed having specific apps for the area that tie into the website.  She further addressed how social media is changing based on different generations but that apps are for everyone.  She talked about policy and training for those running a county social media page.  A discussion was held regarding communication on Social Media and the need to be united. She then spoke about changing up the current programming that the County does through County Connections.

h.

Other issues for discussion:

1.

Activities to celebrate National County Government Month - April 2021 :   Supervisor Peshlakai addressed the National County Government Month for April and County Health Day Initiatives.  He spoke about doing something for County Health Day to promote or engage with the public and communities.  He addressed his proposal for engaging the public by doing a 5K or two-mile walk.  Chairman Seymore indicated that we could start with something small this year and move into something more next year.  Ms. Linn indicated that they have a chronic illness team that could assist.  Mr. Scott addressed the time frame and indicated that it would be hard to get is set up for this year.  Supervisor Peshlakai addressed starting small by have something available to County employees to start and then work into a larger scale event that could be County wide as time goes on.  A discussion was held as to possibilities for this year.

2.

Pine Lake Facility :   Supervisor Whitesinger addressed a discussion with Sheriff Clouse regarding the Pine Lake Facility and the possible ways that the facility could be used by the Sheriff's Office.  She further addressed complaints from community members in her district regarding law enforcement and profiling. She spoke of how miscommunication happens and addressed possibly having a Liaison for communication to tribal individuals.   She noted that the Sheriff's Office is supportive of the idea.  She spoke about the negativity by things getting shared with miscommunication.  She talked about an Advocacy with Pinetop-Lakeside, Show Low, the Sheriff's Office and District Five.  She talked about the importance of being able to create a space to try and move forward.  Mr. Kephart indicated that they can have further discussions on this and start working on addressing these concerns.

Mr. Kephart indicated that it's nice being together in person and that he is thankful and honored to work with Supervisors who are willing to work together for their community.

Chairman Seymore thanked everyone for being present today and indicated that they will continue working to set goals.
