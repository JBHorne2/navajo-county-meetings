Open Meeting Laws for Fire 
Districts
• Where can I find a 
copy of Arizona’s 
Open Meeting Law?
– A.R.S. 38-431 et. seq.
– County Attorney’s

<!-- page 7 -->
Who Does the OML Apply To?
• OML Applies to “Public Bodies”
-This includes the State of Arizona and all 
political subdivisions.
-A “Political Subdivision” includes the 
county, school districts, and special taxing 
districts.
*  Applies to Advisory Committees organized 
by the elected Board.

<!-- page 8 -->
When is the OML Applicable?
• OML is Applicable whenever there is a “meeting” of a 
“quorum.”
• What is a meeting?  Defined by Statute:
-A gathering, in person or through technological devices, 
of a quorum of members of the public body at which they 
discuss, propose to take legal action, including any 
deliberations by a quorum with respect to such action.
• What is a quorum?  It’s a majority of the board.  Physical 
proximity is not required.
-Be careful of the “splintered quorum” and e-mail; See 
A.G.’s Opinion I05-004 regarding e-mail.

<!-- page 9 -->
Purpose of the OML
• To ensure that the 
public’s business is 
conducted in public.

<!-- page 10 -->
Requirements of the OML
• Accessible Location 
for Meetings
• Proper Notice of 
Meetings
• Agenda for Meetings
• Meeting Minutes

<!-- page 11 -->
Notice of Meetings
• Notice must be published at least 24 hours in advance.
-Official legal notice must be posted in place Fire District 
has designated with the Clerk of the Board of 
Supervisors. 38-431.02(A)(2)
-Other notice must be given as is “reasonable and 
practicable.”
• Actual Emergencies-24 hour notice can be waived, but 
be sure it is an actual emergency (call your lawyer) and 
make sure to post notice A.S.A.P.
– Can take action in these circumstances provided grounds for 
declaring the actual emergency and need for immediate action 
are noted at the public meeting.

<!-- page 12 -->
Meeting Agendas
• Meeting Agenda should be posted with meeting 
notice 24 hours in advance. 
• Agenda must list the specific items to be 
addressed.
– Each item should contain “such information as is 
reasonably necessary to inform the public of the 
matters to be discussed or decided.”
– Can only discuss the items listed on the agenda.
• -Can’t add items to agenda at meeting or within 24 hours of 
meeting.
• Can change order of items on agenda or table items without 
discussion.

<!-- page 13 -->
Meeting Agendas and Calls to the 
Public
• “Calls to the Public”  (A.R.S. 38-431.01(G))
-Calls to the public are allowed, but not required by OML.  
If allowed, a “call to the public” must be on the agenda.
-If items discussed during call to public are not on the 
agenda, Board can’t discuss them.  Board members can 
only:
-Ask staff to review a matter (fire chief).
-Ask that a item be placed on a future agenda.
-Respond to personal criticism.
-Can impose time, place & manner restrictions.
-Must be content neutral to survive 1st amendment                 
challenges.

<!-- page 14 -->
Meeting Minutes/Recordings
• The public body must keep written minutes or a 
recording of their meeting.  If minutes are kept, they 
must identify:
-Date, time, place of meeting;
-Members of public body present/absent;
-General description of all matters considered;
-An accurate description of all legal actions proposed, 
discussed, or taken, and the names of members who 
propose each motion.  Names of all those who make 
statements to Board must also be recorded.
• Minutes or a recording must be available to public within
three working days after the meeting.

<!-- page 15 -->
Citizen Rights Under the OML
• Citizens have the right to attend public 
meetings
• Citizens can make their own video or 
audio recording of them (A.R.S. 38-
431.01(D)).
• Citizens have the right to participate only if 
the Board allows it (don’t have to open 
discussion to public).

<!-- page 16 -->
Executive Sessions
• The Board can only go into executive session (a session 
from which the public is excluded) for only the specific 
reasons authorized by A.R.S. 38-431.03
(1) Employee matters (would include volunteers);
(2) Legal Advice;
(3) Discussion of records exempt from public inspection 
by law;
(4)  Discussions/Consultations with negotiating 
representative on labor disputes or purchase, sale, or 
lease of real property
(5)  International/Interstate Negotiations (includes tribal 
government)

<!-- page 17 -->
Procedural Rules for Executive 
Sessions
• A majority of a quorum must vote to have an executive session.
• Executive sessions must be noted on the meeting agenda in order 
to have one.  
-Should recite the specific statute authorizing the     
executive session and a general description of the matter for 
which the session is being held.  Discussion about off-topic 
matters are prohibited.
• Executive Session minutes/discussions are generally confidential.  
-Access is allowed to A.G. or County Attorney conducting an 
investigation of OML violation.
-Notice of confidentiality must be given at every executive 
session.
• No voting or straw polls may be conducted in executive session. 
• Only those individuals whose presence is required to carry out the 
ES should be present, with the reason for their presence stated on 
the record.

<!-- page 18 -->
Executive Sessions for Legal 
Advice
• Requirements:
– Attorney must be 
present.
– Discussion must be 
confined to legal 
advice.

<!-- page 19 -->
What happens if we violate the 
OML?
• All legal action transacted by the public body is null and 
void.
-OML violations can be cured, but a “ratification” must 
take place.  In order to ratify:
(1) Ratification must occur w/in 30 days of date the OML 
violation occurs or “should have been discovered.”
(2)  Must be ratified at public meeting, notice for which 
must describe the action to be ratified.  
(3) Must of intent to ratify must be given 72 hours prior to 
meeting at which ratification will occur.
-Call your attorney if you need to ratify.

<!-- page 20 -->
Penalties for OML Violations
• Education
• $500 Fine
• Costs & Attorney’s 
Fees
• Removal from Office.

Why does Navajo County have to 
redistrict?
U. S. Constitution, under the principle of 
“one person, one vote” requires that the 
districts from which we elect 
representatives be equal in population
We must redistrict after every decennial 
census and whenever there is a change in 
the number of districts.

Population Variance
 In the past, district populations could vary as 
much as 10% without explanation 
 A.R.S. 11-212. Supervisorial districts
The board of supervisors shall meet at the county seat on or 
before December 1 following the release of the United States 
decennial census data and divide the county into three or five 
supervisorial districts as provided in this article, which shall be 
numbered, respectively, districts one, two and three or districts 
one, two, three, four and five. The board shall define the 
boundaries and limits of each district and make the division 
equal or with not more than ten per cent difference in 
population. The county may redistrict as often as deemed 
necessary between each United States decennial census.

Race and Ethnicity Issues
To comply with the federal Voting Rights 
Act, Navajo County must draw new 
districts that have “neither the purpose, 
nor the effect, of diluting the voting 
strength of racial, origin, or language 
minority populations”
The County is required to avoid 
“retrogression” as to minority voters.  In 
simple terms, this means the new districts 
cannot impair the ability of minority voters 
to elect candidates of their choice.
