NAVAJO COUNTY BOARD OF SUPERVISORS' MINUTES
Tuesday, May 25, 2021

Present:
Fern Benally, Supervisor District I; Alberto Peshlakai, Supervisor District II; Jason Whiting, Supervisor District III; Daryl Seymore, Chairman; Dawnafe Whitesinger, Vice-Chairwoman

Bryan Layton, Assistant County Manager; Glenn Kephart, County Manager; Brad Carlyon, County Attorney; Melissa Buckley, Clerk of the Board

9:00 a.m . Call To Order: Invocation and Pledge of Allegiance was led by Chairman Seymore .

CALL TO THE PUBLIC :
Dennis Quincey spoke about reducing property tax for disabled veterans.  He addressed some of the disabilities that Veterans have and their financial issues due to not being able to work.  He requested that the County consider reducing property tax by the percentage of the Veterans disability and to help those who have served.
Brent Meyer talked about reducing property tax for veterans to help them be able to keep more of their disability pay.  He addressed different ways that the Veterans help in our County.
Chairman Seymore requested that staff turn this over to the Assessor's Office to look into the request.
Brad Carlyon stated that the State Legislature would have to give authority for changes.
Chairman Seymore stated that they would follow up on it.
Roger Brown spoke about the White Mountain Transitional Housing program, through the White Mountain Coalition Against Homelessness.  He indicated that it is a ninety-day program where they teach them life skills and require them to get a job.  He stated that they bought property in Show Low, that they have been building tiny homes that will house four men/women or a small family, but that the cost has gone up.  He addressed the progress of building this year and the costs they have incurred with it.  He stated that each house is approximately $50,000 and they are requesting funding to help with the project.
Tom Brown stated that they have been working on the Transitional Housing since 2012.  He indicated that they came to the Board about five years ago to receive funding from a grant but that the requirements were too difficult to understand and they couldn't comply.  He stated that they have been faithful to the program and that they have housed over 300 people in rentals.   He indicated that it is a worthy cause, that it is helping people and that it will help meet the needs for housing.
Chairman Seymore requested that staff follow up on this to see if there is grants for funding.  He stated that it has been a good program and that he appreciates what they have done so far.
Molly McGavock spoke about the MAMA Program and indicated that they are requesting financial support for fiscal years 2022/2023.  She indicated that the program provides intervention, focus on improving the health outcomes and financial stability for low income mothers and their children. She stated that they do this through community level changes, that the program consists of 50 curricular hours to help moms for the future of their family.  She further stated that they meet once a week until the curriculum is completed, then the support continues with monthly meetings for moms.  She stated that they partner wish other local resources for services and opportunities within the community.  She indicated that the program helps the moms find a way to a new normal and helps reduce their children's exposure to adverse childhood experiences.  She requested funding in the amount of $53,612 to help with the program.  She advised that the funding will support the curriculum costs, transportation, childcare, meals and more.  She stated that the packet provided has more information.
Chairman Seymore take under advisement and will see if there is any source of funding to help.
Margaret Heath, the CEO of Walking Down Ranch, Inc., stated that they started the Veterans Village in Lakeside in 2018.  She indicated that they are housing, clothing feeding and transporting homeless Veterans and help them get on their feet and become members of society.  She stated that they have taken in 103 Veterans from homelessness and transitioned them into stability and good citizens along with fifteen children and six spouses.  She stated that they have redone the Rainbow Lodge to provide housing.  She addressed the amount they have raised and indicated that they are requesting continued support to help continue the program.  She indicated that the Thrift store helps them to pay the bills but with COVID funding has been cut.  She further indicated that they have drug and alcohol counselling and that they want to increase it to include counselling for the post-traumatic stress.  She stated that the funding would be a help to society, to Navajo County and benefit the Veterans.  She indicated that they solely do Veterans and their families.  She stated that this is a community problem and a community effort.
Chairman Seymore stated that they have done fantastic job in the community.  He indicated that there are some opportunities there and that when looking at the budget they will assist if possible.
Chairman Seymore stated that there are some people present about the 2 nd Amendment and that they will be having something on the agenda at the next meeting to address the topic.  He further stated that if those present have something different to say today, they can.
Byron Lewis indicated that he is present on behalf of Snowflake to request that Navajo County be declare a sanctuary for 2 nd Amendment rights.  He stated that he helped to collect the signatures on the petitions that were turned in.  He further stated that every member of the Snowflake City Council, the Chief of Police, several officers and the Chamber of Commerce signed the petitions as well as many others.  He indicated that there are those who say we don't need a resolution due to the State passing one but that he feels our personal protections should be handled by local leaders.  He addressed other counties that have passed similar resolutions.
William Turley stated that he helped pass the petitions around and that only two people in his neighborhood didn't sign it due to not being a registered voter.  He stated that the 2 nd Amendment is dear to him and without it there is no guarantee of anything.  He talked about the 2 nd Amendment being put in place to protect us.  He indicated that he knows the Governor made an executive order to protect our 2 nd Amendment but that we need layers.  He stated that there is a war on our amendments right now and that we need to take a stand.
Willow Calacino stated her support of the 2 nd Amendment.
Chairman Seymore stated that they have an item on the agenda at next meeting to address this.
Jim Vance spoke about what happens when we forgot about the past.  He spoke about different historical events and how our rights are not always protected. He stated that we need to be able to protect ourselves and that is the purposed of the 2 nd Amendment.

PUBLIC WORKS :

a.

Consideration of Task Agreement No. 23 to the Intergovernmental Agreement between the Bureau of Indian Affairs- Navajo Region and Navajo County that authorizes funding assistance for roadway aggregate, culvert pipes, water transport and stabilizer material .  John Osgood addressed the item.  He stated that this partnership for maintenance on Navajo Route 9806 has been in the planning stages for many years. He advised that the surface material of the public road is primarily clay and that several of the drainage crossing have not been functioning for several years which makes it difficult to travel in rain and snow season.  He indicated that the specific request is for Navajo County to partner with the BIA and NDOT to provide 38 hundred tons, which about two miles worth, of aggregate material, culvert pipes, water transport and road stabilizer for road maintenance activity.  He stated that the project details still need to be finalized, but that Navajo County's role would be to purchase the materials and that BIA and NDOT will provide the labor to place the material.  He further stated that the projects fiscal impact is $150,000, with the source of funding being the District Two Special Road Funds allotted from the Highway User Revenue Funds.  He stated that staff recommends approval.

Supervisor Peshlakai made a motion to approve Task Agreement No. 23 to the Intergovernmental Agreement between the Bureau of Indian Affairs- Navajo Region and Navajo County that authorizes funding assistance for roadway aggregate, culvert pipes, water transport and stabilizer material, motion seconded by Supervisor Whitesinger.  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
