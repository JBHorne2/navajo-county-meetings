APACHE-SITGREAVES FOREST SERVICE:   
a. Report of the modified proposed action for the Public Motorized Travel Ma nagement Program for the 
Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests : Ed Collins, District Ranger for Lakeside Ranger District , introduced 
Acting Black Mesa Ranger District Ranger Jack Green and provided a history of the Travel Management 
Program.  He reported that  during the revision process it became evident the proposed comments were not 
considered in the adoption of the plan and forest  service staff was  charged with considering these 
comments, modifying the plan accordingly and opening up the modified plan for c omments.  He discussed 
presentations they had made with user groups and discussed the major changes to the plan as well as the 
challenges in implementing.  He reported this process is only the starting point and they welcome local 
participation to improve it.  Supervisor DeSpain advised that Supervisors addressed this item in Washington 
and Supervisor Tenney stated he and Supervisor Brownlow hear  from constituents on this issue  on a daily 
basis.  He further stated that even though the Forest Service states they are closing a limited number of 
roads, in actuality roads will be closed to public traffic that on paper may already be closed but historically 
have bee n open to traffic.  He pointed out two people could be talking about the same road with one 
maintaining it is closed on paper and the other person aware that the road is actually open. He stated that 
residents’ lifestyle would be negatively impacted if they could not access grazing, stock tanks and other 
forestry related activities.  Mr. Collins advised  that these same roads were always intended to close 
following the purpose for which they had been established.  There was intense discussion in which all 
Supervisors, Rangers Ed Collins and Jack Green, and County Man ager James Jayne participated. Mr. Jayn e 
discussed the expense and monumental task of putting signs on all forest roads.  Supervisor Deal advised 
that the forest road closures could have an impact on the Native American community  since the forest has 
always been a source for herbs and plants ne eded in their culture.  It was agreed that County staff and 
Supervisor Brownlow and Supervisor Tenney would meet with Mr. Collins and other Forest Service 
personnel to attempt resolution of these issues.
