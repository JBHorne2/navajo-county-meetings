b. PUBLIC HEARING ON THE FINAL 2008 -2009 BUDGET  PURSUANT TO ARS § 42 -17104:  
Chairman DeSpain opened the public hearing for the FY Final 2008 -2009 budget. Mr. Jayne a dvised 
that Mr. Menlove has been critically ill for a couple of weeks; spen ding considerable time at Summit 
Health Care and is now home but will be home for a while.  He expressed staff’s l ove and appreciation 
for Mr. Menlove .  He complimented Lauren Sedil lo on her participation in this process, thanked  Mary 
Springer for taking leadership of the department  and Randy Sullivan  for his willingness to take on 
additional duties and leading the financial team.   Mr. Jayne advised that following preliminary budget 
adoption, there had been adjustments within the budget that did not change the bottom line numbers.  He 
described one change as a $5,000 transfer from the 019 budget to the Building and  Grounds budget to 
help partner with the fairgrounds and that the second change is a $100,000 surprise action coming out of 
the state budget process requiring counties to share the cost of the DPS crime lab .  He stated the 
Sheriff’s office  can fund approximately  $6,000 out of the MCAT budget and the remaining 
approximately $94,000 will come out of carry forward funds.  Mr. Jayne thanked  elected officials and 
department directors in the county for working together to meet county needs without exceeding 
resources.  He stated this budget reflects flat revenue growth with every department absorbing employee 
salary increases.  He stated there has been an increased  demand for services especially in the judicial 
system.  He advised that county managers anticipate additional budget hits from the state.  He stated 
administration will continue to monitor revenues and if they feel revenues are dropping, will come back 
to the board with recommendations for adjustments.  He advised this is a fair and balanced budget.  With 
no response from the public during this hearing, Chairman DeSpain closed the hearing.

ii. Consideration and possible approval of the Feasibility Cost Share Agreement with the Army Corps : 
Homero Vela displayed a map of impacted floodplains in the Winslow area , discussed the success of 
the meetings related to this feasibility  study and thanked staff Teresa Cameron and Trent Larson  for 
their participation in this process .  He discussed the Project Management Plan (PMP) phases and the 
establishment of the Project Delivery Team.  He pointed out the extensive size of the study are a on the 
flood plain map and discussed potential partners —City of Winslow, Little Colorado River Watershed  
Coordinating Council, NRCS, Game and Fish, Hopi Reservation, Navajo Nation and State Parks.  He 
pointed out ruins that would be impacted without  a coordination of efforts.  He discussed the PMP 
study objectives, stating that all would come back to the board for approval of objectives when they 
are closer to action.  He reviewed work activities and cost allocations advising funds must be budgeted 
over a period of years.  He advised he was s urprised by the estimated expense of the feasibility study, 
that the Corps put time and effort into this cost study , and it was a g ood solution to invite the Corps.  
Following further discussion, Supervisor Thompson made a motion to approve the Feasibility Cost 
Share Agreement with the Army Corps ; motion seconded by Supervisor  Brownlow. In response to 
questions, Mr. Vela stated the contribution is limited to appropriations received, that Homlovi Ruins is 
a State park and when they met as a group, Hopi was represented and Hopi would like to see rip-rap or 
basket walls to add structural strengths to the river bank in the future.  Mr. Vela stated that  after the 
feasibility study is done they will  determine who can accrue m oney among the partners, including 
those who do not have money today but may have six years from now.  Partnering possibilities were 
discussed but it was acknowledged that this agreement must be signed for immediate assistance from 
the Corps and additional partnership agreements might not be signed until next year.  Mr. Vela advised 
there is no commitment now from anyone for money  but the project management plan encourages 
Navajo County to go out and secure other partnerships.  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
