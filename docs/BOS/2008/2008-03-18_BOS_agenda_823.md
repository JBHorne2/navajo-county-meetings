a. PUBLIC HEARING:  Pursuant to Arizona Revised Statutes § 11 -391 and as properly advertised, the 
Navajo County Board of Supervisors will conduct a Public Hearing to receive public comment on a 
proposal for the County to in cur long term obligations to finance capital projects :  Chairman DeSpain 
advised that this is a public hearing and turned time to Timothy Pickrell of Squire, Sanders & Dempsey 
L.L.P.  Mr. Pickrell advised that now  is the time and place set for the public h earing to be conducted 
pursuant to Arizona Revised Statutes Section 11 -391, on a proposal for the County to incur “long term 
obligations” in a principal amount not to exceed $8,400,000.  He stated the  purposes of the long term 
obligations are to finance on e or more of the following capital projects: (1) the construction of a new

County administrative building in Overgaard, (2) renovations and improvements to, or replacement of, 
water tanks and facilities at the County Complex in Holbrook; (3) renovations an d improvements to the 
electrical system at the County Complex in Holbrook; and (4) the acquisition of sites, improvements and 
buildings thereon in Show Low, which will serve as a new regional County Service Center.  He advised 
staff will provide an analysis of the need for the projects, the need to use long -term obligations and any 
other available options to accomplish the projects.  James Menlove advised that over half of the projects 
will be at the county complex.  The reported cost estimates of the proje cts are: Mogollon Complex 
$1,600,000.00; Water tanks  $660,000.00, County Complex Electrical upgrades  $2.2 million , and two 
building acquisitions in Show Low which will serve as a regional county services center $2.5 million.  It 
was noted that since some of these costs are estimated these are conservative, not -to-exceed, amounts as 
it may not be necessary to finance the full amounts listed .  In response to questions, Mr. Menlove 
advised the public will not vote on this issue as the Board will take the requi red action, no new taxes 
will be required as the funding will be taken from existing revenue streams, the terms of the debt have 
not been determined yet and at the time the Board is asked to take action on the debt, the terms will be 
established that could include a call of the bond as early as five years or it could go to 25 years.  He 
stated that the alternative to this financing would be using the cash reserve that has built up over the 
years and discussed the negative impact that could have on the county.  Supervisors Deal and Thompson 
expressed a desire to assess needs of all residents in the county and include some projects in the northern 
part of the county.   Supervisor Thompson stated he sees this project as an opportunity to meet goals of 
the county wide Strategic Plan. 
Chairman DeSpain invited members of the audience to speak during this public hearing.  Steve Doncea, 
of 1526 Mainline Road, Heber , expressed concern that with interest the financial impact is o ver 
$11,000,000 asking how it will be paid back and if there will be a property tax to support it .  Chairman 
DeSpain stated this financing is not tied to a tax increase  and County Manager  James Jayne stated the 
county can guarantee property tax  rates will not be raised because the board, constitut ionally and 
statutorily, can not levy more than it currently levies .  He stated that e very year the county tax rate 
decreases and will continue to do so and re-emphasized that this repayment will come from the existing 
county half cent sales tax and state shared revenues.  Chairman DeSpain explained that while county 
taxes have gone down, taxes imposed by special districts and schools have not gone down.  Mr. Doncea 
expressed concern over his sewer taxes and issues related to them.  County Attorney Mel Bowers stated 
this is a public hearing for this bond and the sewer district has no tie to this hearing so the Board can not 
address the issue.  Supervisor Tenney advised the sewer tax is not a county -imposed tax and offered to 
discuss this issue with Mr. Donce a outside the meeting .  Mr. Doncea stated that if the county wants 
these projects done , they should  let the cities pay for the improvements in their towns and not make 
county residents pay for them.  Supervisor Tenney stated the improvements to county infrastructure will 
be distributed evenly among citizens of the county and the buildings will service residents of Navajo 
County. He reiterated this bond will not be paid for by a property tax.  He stated the county is attaching 
it to state -shared revenues and  the county half cent sales tax  which will come from not only residents , 
but also from people who come from other areas of state , stating it makes it fair  for everyone by 
spreading the revenue base.   
Darlene Fraley, Clerk of the Board, reported she did no t receive any written comments.   With no 
additional public comments, Chairman DeSpain closed the public hearing.   
In response to questions from Supervisor Brownlow, Mr. Jayne stated that as this process moves 
forward additional information will be provide d.  He stated that c urrently county bond indebtedness is 
about 5.5 million for the 20 -year jail bond.  He reported that in an effort to keep the public fully 
informed the project amounts listed would be the maximum and they anticipate the actual amount s will
