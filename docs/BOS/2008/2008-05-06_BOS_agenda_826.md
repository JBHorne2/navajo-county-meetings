c. PUBLIC HEARING:  Consideration and possible adoption of Ordinance Number ____ - 08 Prohibiting the 
Importation for Release in to the Wild, or the Release into the Wild, of a Certain Predatory Animals of the 
Genera Canis, Ursus and Felis; and Specifying Criminal Penalties for Violations :  Chairman DeSpain  
referred to a newspaper article about cages built for children catching the bus on the Blue in Reserve, NM to 
protect them from wolves.  He advised the deputy county attorney reviewed t he proposed ordinance, stating 
the Board could pass it but there would not be a lot of enforcement capability.  Jim O’haco, a resident of 
Winslow who ranches north and south of Winslow, stated he wanted to go  on record as being against 
enlarging the wolf area due to  several problems experienced in the  Blue; that the Board’s approach is 
correct addressing this as a safety issue; Game and Fish should be concerned  that wolves are killers ; the 
interstates are artificial barriers; reservations will be impacted  and he  would like to assist in making 
appropriate connections with Navajo and Hopi Tribes as they have concerns.  No one spoke in opposition to 
passing the ordinance.  C hairman DeSpain c losed the hearing.  Supervisor Brownlow made a motion  to 
adopt Ordinance Number 01 - 08 Prohibiting the Importation for Rel ease in to the Wild, or the Release 
into the Wild, of a Certain Predatory Animals of the Genera Canis, Ursus and Felis; and Specifying 
Criminal Penalties for Violations ; motion seconded by Supervisor Tenney.  Supervisor DeSpain stated that 
when he first st arted attending Coalition meetings during the first five years of the wolf program it was 
projected it would cost US citizens $25,000,000 to release 50 wolves into the Blue and at the end of last 
year only 38 had been released.  He stated the funding proje ction did not allow for reimbursement for 
destruction of cattle and human life.  He advised there has been s ome success in Yellowstone and one 
wilderness area in Idaho.  Supervisor Deal inquired if Apache County has a similar ordinance and 
Supervisor DeSpain said that Navajo County has taken the first step of all counties in Arizona. Supervisor 
Deal stated that if the wolf is released in other counties it could easily wander into an area where there is a 
prohibition. He clarified that if the  federal government was releasing wolves, this ordinance would have no 
impact so what the county is  really doing is sending a message.  Supervisor Deal discussed cultural reasons 
why the tribes may hesitate to support this resolution.  He said he understands  the safety issue and related a 
personal experience when his wife felt threatened by wolves.  Supervisor Tenney stated he agrees a 
statement should be made against expanding the area; that he is concerned about spending about $1,000,000 
per wolf that has been released; and advised there have been  sightings of collared wolves in the Snowflake 
Taylor area.  Supervisor Brownlow stated that the White Mountain Apache Tribe  have wolves on  the 
reservation that have proprietary status  but the S an Carlos Apache Tribe did have wol ves on their 
reservation but due to losing livestock have requested all wolves be removed.  Vote unanimous approving 
the motion.   
d. Consideration and possible approval of Resolution Number ___ - 08 in support of the Hardrock Council on
