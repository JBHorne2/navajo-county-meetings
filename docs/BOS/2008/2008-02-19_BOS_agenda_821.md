c. Update on declared emergency and road conditions on the Navajo and Hopi Reservations: Rod Ross advised 
that James Jayne, Dusty Parsons, Mary Springer, he and Supervisor Deal had toured Northern Navajo 
County to review weather related damage to the roads on the reservation. Mr. Ross and Supervisor Deal 
displayed pictures showing sink holes in BIA roads that closed school bus routes, which in turn caused some 
schools to close.  They displayed pictures showing other deplorable road conditions making roads virtually 
impassable and displayed a picture of the Oraibi Wash that crosses a major east/west BIA road artery 
between Hardrock and Pinon, reporting that detour has been there four or five years with no plan for repair.  
They discussed rescues of mired  vehicles and discussed road construction equipment in the area.  They 
reported that  Navajo County, BIA , Hopi, Sc hools NDOT and local contractors are working together to 
address this problem.  Supervisor Deal stated that many jurisdictional lines were crossed to utilize multiple 
resources to address these massive  problems.  He reported there are other locations just as dangerous as 
have been seen here today and the reconstruction has only touched a small part of District I as further north, 
the roads are totally inaccessible. He expressed appreciation to staff who visited  the area to determine first 
hand what communit ies are e xperiencing.  Supervisor Thompson and Supervisor Deal again expressed 
appreciation stating they believe administration, with the support of the board, has done everything they can 
to get the children to school each day.

The following Finance/Purchasing items were brought forward on the agenda. 
FINANCE/PURCHASING:   
a. Consideration and possible approval to set a Public Hearing on a proposal for the County to incur long term 
obligations:  Jay Spector of RBC Capital Markets introduced Nick Dodd, also of Capital Markets, and Tim 
Pickrell, bond counsel of Squire, Sanders & Dempsey L.L.P ., stating the purpose of today’s presenta tion is 
to advise what the county must do to proceed with this process.  Mr. Spector reviewed a presentation related 
to the county possibly issuing revenue bonds for long term leases that would not be considered debt under 
the constitution so is not includ ed in expenditure limit.  Tim Pickrell described bond counsel’s role in a 
pledge revenue obligation advising the agreement would be between the county and the bank as trustee  with 
the obligations to be marketed to investors with proceeds to be used by the county.  He advised a hearing 
must be conducted on the issuance of the obligation with notice as required  by statute.  He responded to 
several questions about this process from Supervisors and what projects the bonds would finance.  After 
considerable discussion, Supervisor Tenney made a motion to set a public hearing on March 18 th pursuant 
to A.R.S. 11-391 and authorize publication of such; motion seconded by Supervisor Brownlow; vote 
unanimous approving the motion. 
b. Consideration and possible approval of p urchase agreement for property located at 550  North 9 th Place, 
Show Low, AZ: Mary Springer reviewed the history of offices in the Show Low area, advising the McNeil 
building lease will expire  in two years and reporting  the property under discussion  is a mu ch more 
accessible property for county residents .  James Jayne stated the anticipated funds for this property will 
come from bonding but should that not move forward the county could still purchase the 9th Street property 
utilizing reserve funds.  In response to a board questions, James Menlove clarified this purchase was not 
specifically covered in the budget but , as Mr. Jayne stated,  could be purchased from reserves if necessary.  
Supervisor Tenney made a motion  to approve the purchase agreement for prope rty located at 550 North 
9th Place, Show Low, AZ; motion seconded by Supervisor Brownlow.  Mel Bowers advised the agreement , 
as drafted by Lance Payette,  is in order and he wanted it to be clear that should the county not go through a 
bonding process the commitment is to make a cash purchase.  Supervisors discussed the financial picture of 
the county, the cost of this building and the need for accessible services for county residents.  Vote 
unanimous approving the motion.   
c. Consideration and possible approva l to solicit vendors to provide 457 Deferred Compensation Plan 
Services: Cris Parisot reviewed the history of the county’s deferred comp plans  stating there are two 
providers with less than 100 participants. In response to a question from Supervisor DeSpai n, Mr. Jayne 
stated that employees  may choose to leave their funds  with their current provider  but no additional 
employees will be signed up with the current vendor unless they are the successful vendor .  Mel Bowers 
advised that the 457 plan is recognized by IRS for public employees and the state adopts the same 
regulations so contributions are pretax dollars.  Supervisor Tenney made a motion to authorize purchasing 
to solicit vendors to provide 457 Deferred Compensation Plan Services ; motion seconded by Su pervisor
