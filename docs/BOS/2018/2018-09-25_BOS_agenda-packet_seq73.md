Title: Byrne Grant DC-19-009 Amendment
Submitted For: KC Clark, Sheriff  Submitted By: Cheryl Powell, Deputy Chief 
Department: Sheriff 
Motion before the Board:
Arizona Criminal Justice Commission Byrne grant DC-19-009 Amendment to comply with
provisions imposed by the Department of Justice
Background:
The Drug, Gang, and Violent Crime Control Byrne grant was Board approved on June 12, 2018. 
The grant assists in the operation of the Major Crimes Apprehension Team (MCAT) by paying a
portion of the salaries of the participating officers.
 
The grant is made up of two funding sources, state Drug Enforcement funds and federal Byrne
grant funds.  Before any federal funds will be paid this Amendment must be executed.  The
Amendment ensures that a practice is in place to ensure federal agents have access to correctional
facilities to meet with individuals who are aliens and to inquire as to their rights to remain in the
U.S.  The Amendment also ensures that when  a correctional facility receivs a request from the
Department of Homeland Security (DHS)seeking advance notice of scheduled release dates and
time for an alien, the facility will comply with the requests.
Al lother terms of the grant agreement remain in full force and effect.

1. Background 
 
The Arizona Department of Health Services (ADHS) Bureau of Nutrition and Physical Activity (BNPA) administers 
funds provided by the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) for the operation of the Special Supplemental 
Nutrition Program for Women, Infants and Children (WIC), Breastfeeding Peer Counseling Program (BFP C), and the 
Women and Children's Farmers Market Nutrition Programs (FMNP) for the State of Arizona. The USDA nutrition 
programs are discretionary, and each provides a specific service to women, infants, and children who are low income 
and at nutrition risk . The overall goal of all the USDA Nutrition Programs is to improve the health status of eligible 
participants through adoption of healthy behavioral lifestyle changes and to help prevent the occurrence of health 
problems. 
 
1.1. WIC Nutrition Services 
 
The WIC Program accomplishes this goal by providing participant -centered services (PCS) including nutrition and 
breastfeeding information and support, specific supplemental healthy foods through the issuance of food benefits 
that can be used at ADHS -approved grocery stores and farmers’ markets, and referrals to other health and 
human services as an adjunct to good health care during critical times of growth and development. Service 
population eligibility for the WIC Program is based upon federal regulations such as  participant category 
(pregnant and breastfeeding woman, infant or child under five years of age), a household income at or below 
185% of federal poverty guidelines, residence within the service area, and nutrition risks. 
 
To be considered as a WIC Local A gency, the organization must be a local public or private non- profit 
organization, County health department, or tribal entity under contract with ADHS to provide WIC services 
according to the WIC Program federal regulations and state policies and procedures. The State awards a WIC 
Contract based on the ability of the organization to provide WIC services, potential eligible population, need, 
response to the Scope of Work, information technology capacity, and available funds. The State determines and 
awards the WIC contract amount based on a funding formula using several factors such as a base level for a 
program to function, the amount of caseload negotiated with each Local Agency, and quality of performance.  
 
1.1.1. Specific objectives for nutrition services (based on Healthy People 2020 goals) are: 
 
1.1.1.1. To increase the incidence of breastfeeding to 82% of women initiating breastfeeding; 
 
1.1.1.2. To increase the duration of breastfeeding to 61% of women breastfeeding for the first six (6) 
months of their baby’s life; 
 
1.1.1.3. To increase the duration of breastfeeding to 34% of women breastfeeding for the first year of 
their baby’s life; 
 
1.1.1.4. To increase the rate infants are exclusively breastfed to 44% at three (3) months and 24% at six 
months; 
 
1.1.1.5. To reduce the proportion of adults who are considered obese to 31%; 
 
1.1.1.6. To reduce the proportion of children ages 2-5 who are considered obese to 10%; 
 
1.1.1.7. To increase the consumption and variety of fruits and vegetables by those two (2) and  older; 
 
1.1.1.8. To increase the consumption of whole grains by those two (2) and older; and 
 
1.1.1.9. To increase the proportion of children ages 0-2 who view no television or videos on an average 
day to 45%.

1.2. Breastfeeding Peer Counseling (BFPC) 
 
1.2.1. In 2003, the USDA Food and Nutrition Service (FNS) entered into a cooperative agreement with Best 
Start Social Marketing (Best Start) to gather information to obtain a clear understanding of the 
components that are necessary to sustain effective peer counseling programs and how to structure these 
programs so they are cost effective and manageable. They developed the program "Using  Loving 
Support to Implement Best Practices in Peer Counseling", which is a training and technical assistance  
project that will be used as a model to aid WIC in designing, building, maintaining, and sustaining peer 
counseling programs that will improve breastfeeding initiation and duration rates. Arizona will be using 
this program to guide and develop the state peer counseling program. 
 
1.2.2. The discretionary peer counseling services are considered an adjunct support to WIC breastfeeding 
services to help achieve the WIC Nutrition Services objectives regarding breastfeeding. 
 
1.2.3. Peer counseling has been a significant factor in improving initiation and duration rates of breastfeeding 
among women in a variety of settings, including disadvantaged and WIC populations representing diverse 
cultural backgrounds and geographical locations . Peer counselors are especially effective in communities 
where role models for breastfeeding behaviors, knowledgeable health care providers, and cultural 
practices that include breastfeeding as the norm, are scarce. Combining peer counseling with the ongo ing 
WIC breastfeeding promotion and support efforts has the potential to significantly impact breastfeeding 
rates among WIC participants, and most significantly, increase the harder to achieve breastfeeding 
duration rates. WIC Local Agencies are strongly e ncouraged to provide peer counseling services in 
addition to the ongoing breastfeeding support to their WIC participants. Contractors must provide a 
breastfeeding friendly environment and have policies to accommodate participants and staff who are 
breastfeeding. 
 
1.3. Farmer's Market Nutrition Program (FMNP) 
 
The Women and Children Farmers' Market Nutrition Program (FMNP) is intended to increase consumption of 
locally grown fresh fruits and vegetables by providing FMNP checks to a limited number of WIC women, ch ildren, 
and infants as ADHS allows to purchase these items directly from growers at ADHS -approved farmers' markets. 
FMNP is also intended to support local agriculture by expanding the awareness, use of, and sales at farmers' 
markets.

Title: Sky Hi - Amendment of Traffic Flow Pattern For Flood Mitigation Purposes
Submitted For: John Osgood, Public Works Director 
Submitted By: Sandra Phillips, Assistant County Engineer/Flood Control Manager
Department: Public Works
Presented By: Bill Bess & Sandra Phillips
Motion before the Board:
Consideration of accepting/rejecting the County Engineer's recommendation to alter the traffic
flow pattern within the Sky Hi Subdivision for flood mitigation purposes.
Background:
Sky Hi Retreat is a subdivision located in the Pinetop area of District IV.  It was recorded in the
early 60's prior to the adoption of Navajo County's Subdivision Regulations and Requirements in
January 1969.  The drainage through a subdivision was not regulated.  Requirements to provide
channels, sized to handle a large or more frequent storm events were not in place.  Consequently,
the existing channel through Sky Hi does not have capacity for a large storm event or even more
frequent smaller events. Over the years the Sky Hi Property Owners Association (POA) has
requested County assistance to resolve significant flooding issues within the subdivision.  Most
recently, over the past eighteen months Navajo County Flood Control has been working closely
with the Sky Hi POA to investigate how to improve flow capacity of the existing channel,
inparticular, at the downstream portion of the channel where it flows into the forest.    Key issues
and recent actions taken by Public Works Engineering and Road Maintenance staff include:
•       Continued efforts to obtain drainage easements from adjacent property owners to allow for
channel widening.  To date, seventeen of the necessary twenty-four easements have been obtained.
•       Hired an engineering consultant to study several options to help mitigate the flooding. 
•       Presented drainage improvement strategy at two Sky Hi Retreat POA Board meetings which
included: 
Exploratory excavation in that area to determine if the channel could be
deepened through the Malapai rock in the channel.  A consistent slope
in the channel and increasing the slope of the channel will both
increase flow capacity and better drain the subdivision storm water and
reduce ponding.
Removal of the existing culverts under N. Skylane Dr. as this is a pinch point
and no longer is at the elevation.  By removing the culverts this helps to
further reduce the drainage backwater up stream of the road, thus reducing
flooding.
•       Requested the Pinetop Fire District review the potential road closure at the channel for the

<!-- page 83 -->
impact of reduced evacuation routes in case of fire; no concerns were raised, see attached letter.
•       Evaluated construction of a bridge over the channel; determined to be cost prohibitive
After receiving agreement from the POA Board and the response of the Fire District, work began
in the first quarter of 2018 to , confirm that excavation could occur through the rock, deepen the
channel, and remove the culverts.  This work resulted in the closure of N. Skylane Drive at the
channel.  Prior to the initial drainage improvements described above, there were
three roads that provided access to N. Baron Circle – N. Skylane Road, N.
Cherokee Drive, and N. Comanche Drive.  Removal of the culverts and deepening
the channel at this location would eliminate N. Skylane Dr. as an access to N.
Baron Circle, thereby altering the traffic flow pattern in this area of the
subdivision.  When the summer residents on N. Baron Circle returned and saw
the completed work they expressed the following concerns:
•       The County did not inform them of the proposed work
•       The alteration of the traffic pattern in the area would cut off one of their fire exits
•       Questioned the legal authority under which the County altered the traffic pattern on Skylane
Dr. at the drainage channel.
A third POA meeting was held on August 4, 2018 at which these concerns were addressed by
Public Works staff.  Staff and POA Board members confirmed that two prior meetings were held
and residents were notified; the POA mentioned the Fire District will work with them on being a
Firewise Community and to develop a community evacuation plan; and staff stated they would
confer with the County’s legal department regarding the legal authority to alter the traffic pattern. 
After conferring with the County Attorney’s Office on the latter issue, it was determined that the
Board of Supervisors should consider the County Engineer’s recommendation to alter the traffic
flow pattern for flood mitigation purposes.
Recommendation: 
The flood mitigation actions stated above and resulting traffic pattern alteration provide
the most benefit to the residents of Sky Hi Retreat. The ongoing flood mitigation efforts
will reduce the year round flooding impact on approximately 80% of the lots.   Staff
recommends acceptance of the County Engineer's recommendation to alter the traffic flow
pattern within the Sky Hi Retreat subdivision for flood mitigation purposes.
