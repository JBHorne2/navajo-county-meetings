BOARD OF SUPERVISORS :

a.

Presentation by Highland Economics on Adverse Effects on Coal Communities:   Paul Watson spoke about the ACC grant, the hiring of Highland Economics and the research involved in preparing the preliminary final report.  He stated the grant was for both Navajo and Apache Counties.  He spoke about the three phases of the study which are, 1, Identify the adverse impacts regarding the coal industry, 2, Look at what other industries we can attract to the area, and, 3, What are the current infrastructure gaps in relation to attracting other industries?  He indicated that the grant was $104,000 and emphasized that in addition to that, we received matching funds from APS and Real AZ and also personnel costs from Paul Watson and Stephanie Ray.  He stated that we chose Highland Economics because they did a similar study for northwestern New Mexico.

Barbara Wyse, Highland Economics, presented the findings from the preliminary final report.  She spoke about the existing contribution of coal-related industries, both direct and indirect.  She stated that there are over 500 jobs at Cholla and Kayenta mine with an associated income of over $68 million, which is 1.5% of the Navajo County economy and 3% to 5% if you take into account those jobs affected by the economic impact.  She spoke about the fiscal impact in terms of the County budget, taxing district budget and the Reservation budget.  She spoke about the determinants of rural economic strength, including skill and education levels, transportation infrastructure, natural attractiveness, proximity to urban areas and institutions of higher education.  She further spoke about growth strategies in rural areas, including capitalizing on natural strengths and geographic location; cultivating industry clusters; attracting tourists, retirees and other residents; and building community partnerships at local and regional levels.  She stated that the best practices and strategies for northeast Arizona, including Image/Brand and Quality of Life; Business Environment; and the local, regional and State-level partnerships.  Travis Greenwalt, Highland Economics, spoke about the positive infrastructure pieces, as well as the infrastructure gaps in the region, including natural gas capacity, broadband reliability and speed, local university, communal workspaces, entrepreneurship and leisure.   He stated that the economic strengths of northeast Arizona include natural resource sector, tourism and recreation, local services, construction and utilities/power production. He spoke about the state employment growth and indicated that Navajo and Apache Counties have not been up to the level of the state of 5% since 2008.  He further spoke about the economic diversification summary and stated that they were trying to ascertain what resources would be required for development in the targeted industries and if the local area would provide a good match.  He stated that they found several industries which would provide a good fit to the area.  He stated that potash is the only single industry which could replace the loss of the coal industry.

Stephanie Ray spoke about the three legs of the proposed strategic action plan, which are how to attract industry, skilled workers and visitors.  She stated that in attracting Industry, the focus will be to further develop Real AZ, an entrepreneurship development program and develop properties and infrastructure to attract industries.  She indicated that we are also looking at what funding resources are available and spoke about grants which are available to help us.  She stated that to attract skilled workers and develop the existing workforce, the plan is to develop the quality of life components and develop the local workforce to meet industry needs.  She stated that the plan to attract visitors includes developing additional visitor services to attract a more diverse tourist demographic to the region and collaborate with local stakeholders to enhance the regional tourism marketing plan and collaborate with neighboring regions to coordinate marketing on tourism initiatives that overlap.  Paul Watson spoke about the case studies in the report which are helpful in putting together the action plan.  He thanked APS and others for helping us do this project.
Supervisor Whiting thanked those who participated in this process for all that they have done in putting together a well-informed game plan and encouraged them to continue the process.  He encouraged Mr. Watson to meet with each of the partners to make sure they are on the same page so we can work forward together.  Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked Mr. Watson for the information.  She spoke about ways we can participate in being able to advocate for our community through local boards on which we serve.  Mr. Watson spoke about Real AZ and encouraged any and all to participate in that.  He indicated that as to tourism, we have the White Mountain Partnership to promote the region for tourism, and it is starting to gain traction, and there are partners interested in coming back and participating.  Chairman Williams thanked Highland Economics, Ms. Ray and Mr. Watson for their work.

b.

Consideration and possible adoption of Resolution Number 07-18 , opposing the "NextGen Climate Action" ballot initiative :  Rod Ross, Government Affairs Representative with APS, stated that they are very concerned about the ballot initiative and are asking policy makers to examine and oppose it.  He stated that this ballot initiative is referred to as the clean energy for a healthy Arizona ballot initiative which would be an amendment to the State Constitution which would require Arizona utility companies to obtain 50% of their energy from renewable sources by 2030.  He indicated that the initiative did not originate in Arizona but is the brainchild of a group that is based in San Francisco, California and is funded by a California billionaire who is running this initiative in Michigan and Nevada, as well.  He stated that he believes that the best place for energy policy is the Arizona Corporation Commission.  He stated that the initiative would have the effect of shutting down a number of power plants throughout the state, including all of the State's coal plants and the Palo Verde Nuclear Generating Station by 2020, which is the largest source of tax revenue for the state.  He indicated that this initiative would significantly increase utility rates.  He spoke about the claims being made that this initiative would improve public health and air quality, but the facts do not support that claim.  He stated that solar energy is intermittent and has to be backed up by flexible sources of generation.  Supervisor Whiting thanked Mr. Ross for speaking to the Board today and indicated that it is not okay for California to be dictating their policies in Arizona.  He stated that the cost to our citizens locally is also concerning.  Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger stated that it is important to keep our community members informed regarding how these ballot measures can impact our community.  Supervisor Whiting made a motion to adopt Resolution Number 07-18 opposing the "NextGen Climate Action" ballot initiative; motion seconded by Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger .  Supervisor Thompson thanked Mr. Ross for the information.  He asked what steps are being taken to explain to people that what they are being told regarding air quality is not the proper answer.  Mr. Ross stated that it is a story we are constantly trying to tell, and it's difficult due to the misinformation out there and preconceived notions.  He stated that when we finish with the installation of the pollution control technology on the four corners plant in New Mexico, we'll be in a place where all of our coal units will emit as much emissions as all of the coal units used to emit together.  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES :

a.

Presentation on the current fire season including entering into Stage 2 Fire Restrictions and Tinder Fire information :  Jeff Lee gave an update regarding the Tinder Fire in Coconino County.  He stated that the fire has burned over 16,000 acres, with 33 residences lost along with an additional 54 outbuildings.  He thanked our partners in Winslow for opening the junior high school gym for an emergency shelter, and the City Animal Shelter opened their doors, as well.  He spoke about the smoke impacts in Navajo County.  He indicated that we worked with the Department of Air Quality and put air quality monitors in Holbrook and Winslow.  He stated that they Issued an air quality advisory for Navajo County due to the smoke effects.  He indicated that Jeff Lineberry is deployed to link the communications with the emergency operations center to the fire camps, the Navajo County Sheriff assisted with evacuations, and the Navajo County Fairgrounds opened up for large animal evacuations.  He stated that there were no deaths or major injuries directly related to the fire; however, there were two fatalities due to a head-on collision which occurred while a family was coming back after evacuations were lifted.  He indicated that this fire was 100% preventable and was started by an illegal campfire and an abandoned campfire.  He encouraged people to call law enforcement or the Forest Service if they see someone starting an illegal campfire.

Mr. Hill stated that Navajo County is moving into Stage 2 fire restrictions, and the risk will get higher between now and monsoon season.  He spoke about the Stage 2 restrictions, which are no fires allowed, including coal, wood, charcoal, campfires and pile burning.  He stated that there are additional restrictions on manufacturing and construction, including no welding over an open flame allowed without special use permits.  He indicated that smoking is prohibited unless you're in an enclose vehicle or a building.  He stated that the message regarding fire restrictions have been shared through social media, media outlets and ADOT through signs.  Supervisor Thompson inquired as to whether all of northern Arizona is united on raising the restrictions, and Mr. Lee stated that all of northern Arizona except the very northern part of the Kaibab National Forest is in Stage 2, and they are very close to entering Stage 2, as well .  Supervisor Whiting thanked Mr. Lee for the information and getting the information out there.  He requested that Mr. Lee explain the process that would take us into Stage 3 and how close are we to going into Stage 3.  Mr. Lee stated that every week there is a fire restrictions phone call, and they look at the data and science, and once it hits 100%, serious consideration will go into going into stage 3.
