Tuesday, September 11, 2018

Present:
Lee Jack, Sr, Supervisor District I; Jesse Thompson, Supervisor District II; Jason Whiting, Supervisor District III; Steve Williams, Chairman; Dawnafe Whitesinger, Vice-Chairwoman

Staff Attendance:
Glenn Kephart, County Manager; Brad Carlyon, County Attorney; Melissa Buckley, Clerk of the Board

9:02 a.m . Call To Order: Pledge of Allegiance and Invocation was led by Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger .

There was a moment of silence held in remembrance of 9-11.  A short video was shown.  Supervisor Whiting spoke regarding the remembrance of 9-11 and expressed appreciation for all those who serve their communities and those who serve our country.

CALL TO THE PUBLIC : Martha Pierce addressed the Board regarding fracking. She spoke about acid stimulation, which goes down below the aquifer and spins acid out into the sandstone. She indicated that that acid creates caverns in the sandstone and in those caverns the helium is released. She spoke about how the BLM and the State have decided that one well every 640 acres was not good enough, so they will put one well per 40 acres. She stated that fracking is not going to bring jobs, and our property values will go down 10-25%.  She stated that BLM released leases last week and sold three leases for $2.00 an acre in Apache County.  She spoke about a movie called Sacred Land Sacred Water, and the biggest point in the movie was that they stopped what was happening at the county level because the native people know you don't rape the land but give back to the land.  She stated that we need to look into hemp. Wayne Pierce addressed the Board and the audience regarding fracking. He spoke about how everyone drinks water from our aquifer, and that aquifer is in potential danger. He stated that a corporation's number one goal is to make a profit, and they will do anything to make that profit, including going way beyond what is ethical. He indicated that they do this by not letting us know what they are doing.  He spoke about July 27th being the deadline for downwinders to make a claim.  He stated that we are counting on the Board of Supervisors to do the right thing and stop the fracking. Ms. Pierce stated that we need to have this on the agenda.  Chairman Williams indicated that the Board of Supervisors have asked staff to keep them apprised of the status of BLM and their actions.

RECORDER :

a.

Presentation and discussion of the August 28, 2018 Primary Election:   Doris Clark discussed the August 28, 2018 Primary Election.  She stated that the Arizona Capitol Times reported that more people in Arizona voted in this 2018 Primary Election than any other primary in the State's history.  She indicated that we had 21,000 voters total, and 12,000 of those were people who voted early and 8,900 went to the polls.  She stated that this year was the first time the County Recorder's Office outsourced mailing out ballots, and she indicated that there were a few glitches.  She spoke about the importance of education regarding ballots being returned early so that they can be counted.  She stated that the drop-off boxes were very effective.  She spoke about early satelite voting on the Reservation this year, and they will include one day in Heber and one week in Show Low at the City office for the General Election.  Rayleen Richards gave an overview of our new election recording system on the Navajo County website.  Chairman Williams stated that the appearance of the website is great.  He inquired regarding what causes the delay for the official results.  Ms. Richards stated that as soon as we get the tablets, they have to be uploaded into the Power Profile and then they will do the early ballots.  She indicated that it then has to be verified that someone who turned in an early ballot did not vote at the polling place and that the signature matches.  She also stated that it remains unofficial until she brings the canvas before the Board, which makes it official.   Chairman Williams suggested having a link to the election results on the County's main web page and the County's Facebook post.   Supervisor Thompson expressed appreciation to Ms. Richards and Ms. Clark for making it easier for the voters.   Supervisor Jack thanked Ms. Richards and Ms. Clark for their presentation.  He inquired regarding how they addressed the parking lot issues for the elders at the different chapters.  Ms. Clark stated that Coconino had a situation where the Department of Justice came in and indicated that a lot of the polling sites were not in compliance with ADA.  She indicated that we were not contacted by the Department of Justice but went ahead and did an overall survey and did what was needed to ensure our polling places are in compliance.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE: Report from County Manager, County Attorney and Board members:  Mr. Kephart sent his heartfelt sympathies to our Treasurer, Debra Kester, who recently lost her sister.  He gave a brief summary about the efforts to educate our community regarding the choice we will be asking them to make on Proposition 419.  He stated that within the last two weeks, there has been educational outreach at the Timber Mesa Fire District Board, Town of Snowflake, Good Morning Winslow Chamber of Commerce, Indian Wells District 7, White Mountain Conservatives, White Cone Chapter, Snowflake-Taylor Chamber, I-40 Response Agencies, Heber-Overgaard Chamber, White Mountain Business Owners Roundtable, staff of the County Attorney's Office and Lead Team and Pinetop-Lakeside Town Council.  He indiated that they will be at the City of Holbrook this evening, Timber Mesa staff tomorrow, and the City of Show Low and City of Winslow City Councils are scheduled.  He stated that many of these are second meetings and are follow-up meetings regarding Proposition 419.  He spoke about how good of a team effort it has been. He stated that this will be Adam Wolfe's last meeting and thanked him for his efforts here at Navajo County and wished him well on his new endeavors.  Brad  Carlyon stated that the Arizona Association of Counties executive director, Jen Larson, will be here at 11:00 a.m. at Public Works to make a presentation on AACo. Supervisor Thompson stated that heattended the District 7 presentation on the Jail District.  He indicated that he made a presentation to Hardrock and Teesto and then will be going to Dilkon, as well.  He spoke about meeting with Tom O'Halleran and former Senator Albert Hale to talk about past legislation that he introduced but wasn't able to get done. He stated that Congressman O'Halleran extended his help again regarding the Winslow Levee.  He indicated that he will be attending a meeting at Twin Arrows with the southwest communities regarding transportation on the Reservation roads. He spoke about the rememberance of September 11 and expressed appreciation to all of the first responders. Supervisor Jack stated that the Little Colorado River water rights hearings will take place this morning.  He indicated that NGS negotiations are still ongoing.  He stated that the Navajo Nation's budget looks okay this year, but we do not know what will happen nest year.  He indicated that even if the two new owners take over the plant, the budget will still be pretty low for next year.  He spoke about visiting the chapters and educating them regarding the County budget.  Supervisor Whitesinger thanked those who helped with the White Mountain Apache Tribal Fair and Rodeo.  She also thanked Alberto Peshlakai in his efforts. She thanked Adam Wolfe for his efforts and stated that she enjoyed working with him. Supervisor Thompson told Mr. Wolfe good luck and stated that he has been very helpful not only to the County but has helped each of the Board members. Chairman Williams thanked everyone for their efforts on the agenda today and thanked those who came to speak in call to the public and wished Mr. Wolfe good luck.  Supervisor Jack stated the Navajo Nation Council addressed the issue of fracking and are in opposition, but the fracking is on Indian allottments, private lands.
