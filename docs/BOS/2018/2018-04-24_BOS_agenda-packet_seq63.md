2.1  COMPENSATION PROGRAM 
       
2.11  Policy:   
 
It is the policy of Navajo County to provide compensation commensurate with the type of 
work and the degree of responsibility required for each job and which is comparable or 
approximately comparable, as governed by budgetary constraints, to rates being paid for 
similar work within and without the organization. 
 
2.12  Coverage:   
 
This policy applies to all classified and unclassified positions.  Nothing in this policy 
modifies or w aives the “at w ill” status of an unclassified employee 
 
2.13 Compensation:   
 
Every County employee shall be paid within the Salary Range assigned to the position 
for which he/she was hired. 
 
A. Hire Rate:   
 
Entry Level (Step 1) :  Generally, initial appointments shall be at Step 1 of the 
assigned Salary Range for the position.   
 
Above Step 1 :  In instances where there is justification in the opinion of the 
Department Director or Elected Official, to hire at a rate above Step 1 of the Range, 
a request and justification for appointment above the minimum may be submitted 
to Human Resources.  A hire rate up to Step 4 of the range can be initiated upon 
prior approval of the Human Resources Director  and the Finance Director .  
Approval is cont ingent upon availability of funds  and appropriateness of the 
justification.  Requests for a hire rate above Step 4 of the Range require approval 
of the County Manager.     
 
Below Step 1:  Applicants whose qualifications are less than those required for the 
position may under-fill a position commensurate with the individual’s qualifications.  
The Elected Official or Department Director may make a request for appointment 
below the minimum.  The position may be under-filled upon prior approval of the 
Human Resources Director. 
 
B. Reappointment:   
 
Appointment of former County employees eligible for rehire shall be the same as 
for a new hire. 
 
C. Promotions:

An employee who is promoted to a position in a higher Salary Range will generally 
receive a salary increase equal to Step 1 of the new Salary Range. 
 
D. Schedule for Progression within Salary Range 
 
Employees shall move to the next Step within the Range currently held every two 
(2) years on their anniversary date.  Employees who reach the maximum of their 
Range (Step 10) will receive a one -time increase equivalent to the difference 
between Step 9 and Step 10 of their current Range every two (2) y ears on their 
anniversary date. 
 
Employees must meet the minimum performance requirements of their position to 
be eligible to move to the next Step  within the Range .  Employees under a 
Performance Improvement Plan or disciplinary action  must return to normal work 
status and meet the minium performance requirements of their position before 
being moved to the next Step  within the Range.  Movement to the next Step will 
be made effective from when the employee meets minimum performance 
requirements of the position, and will not be made retroactive from the employee’s 
anniversary date.  
 
Employees who have received a targeted market adjustment or other form of  
unique reclassification to their individual position which results in an increase in 
salary will not be eligible to progress to the ne xt Step within the Range for 12 
months (Human Resources and the Department will have taken pending  
scheduled Step increases into consideration with the targeted market adjustment).      
 
 (2.13 cont.) 
 
D. Lateral Transfers:   
 
An employee transferred to a position in the same Range or to any other 
classification in the same Range, shall be paid the same salary (sameStep in range 
currently held) that was received prior to the transfer. 
 
E. Demotions – Voluntary/Involuntary: (Shall include reclassifications, departmental 
reorganizations or imminent lay off.) 
 
 1. An employee who is demoted, voluntarily or involuntarily, shall receive a 
reduction in salary but no less than Step 1 of the new Salary Range (grade). 
 2. If a promotion is followed by a demotion, the employee's salary shall not 
exceed the amount that would be due if no promotion had occurred. 
 
F. Declared Emergencies During Holidays

1.     If a declared emergency or other exceptional circumstances fall on a holiday 
or observed holiday, an employee required to work shall be eligible for pay 
in accordance with 2.31 (G).  
2.   Holidays and observed holidays are listed at Section 2.31 (A). 
 
G.     Special Duty 
 
1. An employee who is assigned to special duty on a temporary basis, in a 
higher salary range, will generally receive an increase equal to Step 1 of the 
new salary range.   
2. Prior approval must be obtained from the County Man ager for salary 
increases above (G) (1). 
3. If an employee on special duty has a change in status (promotion, transfer, 
etc.) the employee shall be restored to his/her original position and salary 
before application of necessary adjustment. 
 
H. Return to Regular Duties 
 
An employee returning from special duty shall return to his/her regular salary 
(range and percent) prior to the assignment.  If any salary increases were received 
(i.e. COLAs) by the employee while serving on assignment, such salary increases 
will be included upon return to regular duty. 
 
I. An employee returning from special duty of six months or longer shall retain a 2.5% 
increase in his/her regular salary prior to the assignment.

Recognition of Sheriff KC Clark for being named 2018 Sheriff of the Year by the WETIP
Foundation.
Background: 
Navajo County Sheriff KC Clark has been named “Sheriff of the Year” recognized by WeTip. 
WeTip (www.wetip.com) was founded in 1972. It was created as an Anonymous Crime Reporting
resource for citizens, and as a tool to aid law enforcement. This tremendously successful program
has dramatically impacted crime. It has significantly reduced crime incidents in communities and
schools nationwide. Law Enforcement agencies throughout Navajo County have successfully
utilized the WeTip program to receive tips from citizens on unsolved crimes and payout rewards
on information that lead to arrests. Sheriff Clark was named Sheriff of the year by WeTip in 2013.
Prior to that, former Maricopa County Sheriff Joe Arpio was the only Arizona Sheriff to receive
the national recognition.  Sheriff Clark is now the only Arizona Sheriff to be recognized twice. 
In 2015, WeTip awarded the Navajo County Sheriff’s Office (NCSO) as “Sheriff’s Office of the
Year” At that time, WeTip specifically recognized the community-based relationship(s) that
NCSO deputies established with communities. These relationships allowed NCSO to lead all other
Wetip organizations in the clearance of open cases due to feedback (tips) from the community. 
Sheriff Clark remarked on this 2018 award “our deputies go out every day and make this
recognition possible. Without the work they do in our communities this would have not been
possible.”
The Navajo County Sheriff’s Office reminds people with any information on a crime to contact
WETIP at 800-78CRIME or contact the Sheriff’s Office at (928)532-6060 or (928)524-4050.
Callers may request to remain anonymous.The WETIP Foundation was founded in 1972 as an
Anonymous Crime Reporting resource for citizens and a tool to aid law enforcement.  This is the
second time Sheriff Clark has been recognized by WETIP.  He was also awarded the 2015 Sheriff
of the Year award. He is the first Sheriff in Arizona to receive this award.

Article I  
Authority 
 
1.1 The county is authorized by A.R.S. 11-251 and 11- 952 to enter into intergovernmental 
agreements for cooperative action with agencies of the United States and/or Tribal 
governments. 
 
1.2 A.R.S. 11-251 (4) authorizes the county to maintain all public roads within its 
boundaries. 
 
1.3 A.R.S. 11-251 (29) authorizes the Board of Supervisors of the County to enter into 
agreements for the maintenance of highways, including those which pass through 
Indian lands, with the government of the United States, acting through its duly 
authorized officers or agents pursuant to any act of  Congress. 
 
1.4 The above statutes have been interpreted by the Arizona Attorney General in Opinion 
No. 186-029 to authorize the expenditure of funds for the maintenance of all public 
roads within Indian country (as defined in 18 U.S.C. 1151). 
 
1.5 The code of Federal Regulations provides in 25 C.F.R. 170.6 that "The administration 
and maintenance of Indian Reservation Roads and Bridges is basically a function of 
the local Governmen t Subject to availability of funds, the Commissioner (of Indian 
Affairs) shall maintain, or cause to be maintained, those approved roads on the 
Federal-Aid Indian Road  System." 
 
1.6 25 C.F.R. 170.7 provides that Tribes may enter into an agreement with the State 
(which is defined to include political subdivisions such as county) for cooperation in 
the maintenance of certain Indian Reservation Roads and Bridges, especially at those 
locations where road projects serve on non -Indian land as well as Indian  land. 
 
1.7 The White Mountain Apache  Tribe may retain all funding received pursuant to 
this MOA without depositing the same in the Miscellaneous  Receipts Account of 
the United States Treasury. The White Mountain Apache  Tribe has the authority to 
retain these  funds and use them for the road maintenance  that supplements  but does 
not replace maintenance projects funded by the appropriate funds. Pursuant to the 
broad authorities of the Secretary of Interior over Indian Affairs, the regulations
