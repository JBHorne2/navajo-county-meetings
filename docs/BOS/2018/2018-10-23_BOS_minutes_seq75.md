BOARD OF SUPERVISORS :

a.

Board of Supervisors final approval to the District Administrator of the White Mountain Lakes Fire District (WMLFD) to proceed with the consolidation of WMLFD with the Timber Mesa Fire and Medical District : Jason Moore discussed the item.  He spoke about the legalities of the district consolidation process.  He introduced John Flynn and Chief Brian Savage. He stated that the Timber Mesa Fire and Medical District had their meeting last night and voted in favor of the consolidation. Mr. Moore indicated that we are here today for Mr. Flynn to ask for the Board of Supervisor's blessing to move forward on behalf of WMLFD. He stated that they are going down two parallel tracks at this time because there was no guaranty that the consolidation was going to go through.  He indicated that if the consolidation does not go through, we would be creating a new governing board from scratch, and the statute requires that that be done through an election to determine the highest number of vote getters who would get four-year terms, and the two lowest numbers of vote getters would get two-year terms, which terms do not begin until December 1st.  He stated that if consolidation is approved by both districts, the Timber Mesa Fire District governing board will become the governing board of the White Mountain Lake Fire District, and at future elections, the members of the White Mountain Lake Fire District will have the opportunity to vote for members of the Timber Mesa Fire District's governing board, and anyone from White Mountain Lake Fire District would be eligible to run for a position on the Timber Mesa Fire District governing board.

John Flynn, Administrator for the WMLFD, requested the Board's support for the consolidation of the WMLFD into the Timber Mesa Fire and Medical District.  He spoke about the procedures and consolidation report that was to be  done pursuant to law. He reported on the public engagement process which has been completed.  He spoke about the survey process that was used and indicated that those comments were provided to the Board of Supervisors. He spoke about the October 16 public hearing. He indicated that there were people present both for and against.  He said the results of the survey show more in favor of the consolidation than not. He spoke about what that consolidation would achieve, including fixing a financial problem so that their district has financial sustainability and reliability.  He further spoke about the improvement of services, including going from a 12-hour shift to a 24-hour service, adding paramedic services to that area and an ambulance in White Mountain Lakes. He stated that there will be a drastic improvement provided to that community, and those tangible benefits will be immediate to the White Mountain Lake area.  Mr. Flynn stated that those in opposition spoke about the local control issue.  He stated that the improvement of services will come with 7.7 percent tax reduction for the White Mountain Lake Community.  Brian Savage indicated that Timber Mesa undertook a feasibility study as part of this process, as well, and their financial projections indicate that there will be a slight savings to the Timber Mesa residents.  He stated that there are no operational changes being made within Timber Mesa, so the same number of firefighters responding from the same stations will continue to occur.  He indicated that there is a slight benefit with regard to the coordination of resources across the region.  He indicated that Timber Mesa Fire and Medical District did vote unanimously to consolidate.

Chairman Williams opened up the public hearing. Tony Cass, 8310 Skyline Drive, White Mountain Lake, spoke in favor of the consolidation, especially having an ambulance in the community and 24-hour service. He thanked John Flynn for his work. He also thanked the Board of Supervisors for the dumpsters. Rose Thomas, 1925 Passage Drive, Show Low, stated that she is here representing Juniper Ridge RV Resort and White Mountain Lake Vistas.  She indicated that there are 528 owned lots in Juniper Ridge and 65 lots in White Mountain Lake Vistas.  She stated that in the summertime, over 1,000 people may be in residence and there is getting to be a larger population in the winter, between 150 to 200. She spoke in favor of the consolidation for the emergency medical services that will be provided.  Lyn Misener, 1827 Gutierrez Place, White Mountain Lake, and also owns a lot in Juniper Ridge, spoke in favor of the consolidation for the emergency medical services.  She indicated that they have not done very well out there governing themselves, and she thinks this will be a good opportunity for the community.

Brian Savage spoke about a feasibility study for the current Timber Mesa Fire and Medical District consolidation. He stated that they have outperformed their financial projections every year since the merger, and the communities have been saved more than $8 million over the last four years. He indicated that he he thinks we have found a way to do local government better, and this consolidation with WMLFD is a continuation of that effort. Supervisor Whiting indicated that he believes that the conversation has turned from concern to support with some questions. He indicated that he has read through all of the reports and information provided. He spoke about the improvement of medical services that will be a benefit to the community.
Supervisor Whiting made a motion to approve the recommendation to the District Administrator of the White Mountain Lakes Fire District (WMLFD) to proceed with the consolidation of WMLFD with the Timber Mesa Fire and Medical District. Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked Mr. Flynn and Chief Savage for their continued work on this consolidation. Motion seconded by Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger . Vote unanimous approving the motion.

10:27 a.m. PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 25-18 , approving/denying the request for a Special Use Permit for the installation of a cellular tower in the Aripine area :  Sandra Phillips discussed the item and indicated that it has been duly noticed.  She stated that the parcel is between Clay Springs and Heber-Overgaard.  Ms. Phillips spoke about the Planning and Zoning Commission meetings regarding this item and the stipulations to which the applicant has agreed.  Vice- Chairwoman Whitesinger requested clarification regarding where the tower will be and the neighbors in that area.   She also asked about the measurements regarding the root metrics and what that means with regard to the coverage in the area. Michelle Lamoureux, Pinnacle Consulting, spoke about the coverage maps. She spoke about the neighborhood meeting and that the majority of those who attended were in favor of the cell tower. She stated that the proposed tower has been moved back approximately 736 feet. She indicated that they will paint the tower to try to blend in with the surrounding areas.  She stated that the tower will meet all of the codes for Navajo County.  Steve Kennedy, 2100 North 145th Avenue, Suite 1038, Goodyear, Arizona, spoke about the root metrics map which measures all wireless carriers.  He also spoke about the open signal map. He demonstrated the existing coverage as well as the future coverage with the new tower. He also indicated that because of the fewer number of sites in the area and the fact that they are farther apart, it is a lower noise environment.

Chairman Williams opened the public hearing. Jason Foutz, 1514 Sagebrush Drive, Holbrook, addressed the Board opposing this item due to the loss of peace and tranquility.  He stated that there is coverage existing in the area. Sandy Foutz, 1514 Sagebrush Avenue, Holbrook, addressed the Board in opposition of the cell tower. She indicated that coverage is not an issue in that area. She stated that they go to this area for the tranquility, and the cell tower is not needed and is not wanted out there. She read a letter from Tristan Bell, a property owner in the area of the cell tower, who is also opposing the cell tower. Samantha Morrow, 2161 Camperland Rd, Overgaard, spoke in opposition of this item and further spoke about growing up in the Aripine area. Eric Morrow, 2161 Camperland Rd, Overgaard, spoke in opposition of this item.  He referenced Section 2002 of the Navajo County Ordinance.  He spoke about the Planning and Zoning Commission meeting at which this item was approved.  He stated that the tower will dessamate property values in the area.  Connie Franke, 2212 Camperland Rd., Overgaard, spoke in opposition of this item. She stated that the cell tower is not needed.  She spoke about the flight path and indicated that the air vac does fly through there and therefore a light would need to be put on the tower. She indicated that coverage is better than fair right now, and there are better spots to put this. John Franke, 2212 Camperland Rd., Overgaard, spoke in opposition of the cell tower and indicated that the cell tower is not needed. He further indicated that the people who were at the neighborhood meeting only spent two weeks at the RV park, and all they want is 911 service. He indicated that yes, he does lose service sometimes, however, coverage is fair. He spoke about the concerns regarding property values going down. Chairman Williams closed the Public Hearing.

Sandra Phillips addressed concerns made during public comment.  Ms. Lamoureux indicated that there will be no generator and no lights on the tower.  She stated that as far as safety, the cell tower will withstand many conditions, and they take the safety and viability of the towers very seriously. She further spoke to the property values and the placement of the cell tower.
Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger asked about the advantages of putting the cell tower in a sparsely populated area or area that might not get significant use. Mr. Kennedy spoke about the concern regarding noise and indicated that when he was speaking about noise, he was not speaking of noise within what the human ear can hear, but he was speaking about transmitter receiver noise and how they affect each other. He stated that the advantages of putting the cell tower in this area is coverage along the highway. He further spoke about RF safety. Chairman Williams asked about whether this will increase cell coverage along the highway beyond the ridge, and Mr. Kennedy indicated that it will help bridge the gap within the valley and improve coverage. Chairman Williams indicated that he does drive that highway regularly and drops calls all the time. He stated that there are neighbors that are supportive of it and some that are not. He indicated that he heard the concerns, but from his perspective and for the public interest, he would be supportive of the item. He spoke about this being a balancing act of the private property interests and what is in the best interest of the public. Ms. Phillips stated that this item does meet our special use requirements.
Supervisor Whiting made comments about the stipulations and accommodations that have been made by the applicant. Supervisor Whiting made a motion to adopt Resolution Number 25-18 approving the request for a Special Use Permit for the installation of a cellular tower in the Aripine area; motion seconded by Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger .  Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger made comments about the appeal of Navajo County because of its remoteness and large corporations coming in to impact rural communities. Vote unanimous approving the motion.
