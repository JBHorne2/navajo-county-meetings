b.

Presentation of the current fire season outlook and update regarding moving into Stage 3 fire restrictions:   Catrina Jenkins discussed the current fire season outlook and indicated that we are now in Stage 3 of fire restrictions.  She spoke about the current red flag conditions, meaning low humidity levels and winds are up. She stated that the weather service has told us no moisture yet.  She spoke about Stage 3 restrictions and what is and is not allowed.  She stated that if an individual causes a fire, they will be liable for the suppression and any damages. She encouraged everyone to view firerestrictions.us which is updated regularly with current fire restrictions and closures.   Supervisor Whiting thanked Ms. Jenkins for the information.  Heinquired as to how people know what area is closed and what is not. Ms. Jenkins stated that once they get the closure orders from the General Forest Office, they will update firerestrictions.us.    Supervisor Thompson asked about the liability and fire insurance.  Ms. Jenkins indicated that there is fire insurance; however, fire insurance does not negate an individual's liability who causes a fire. Ms. Jenkins continued to answer questions of the Board.  Mr. Carlyon indicated that there was a young man in Oregon who caused a forest fire and has been assessed restitution in the amount of over $36 million.  He stated that his office intends to prosecute individuals who cause fires to the fullest extent that they can. Mr. Kephart inquired regarding the cause of fires this time of year.  Ms. Jenkins spoke briefly about human caused fires and natural caused fires.

Legislative Update:   James Candland, political consultant, gave a legislative update.  He thanked Mr. Kephart and his management team for their support this last legislative session.  He spoke about the Navajo County presentation to the Small Counties Caucus and indicated that meeting was talked about a lot.  He spoke about a legislator who was taken out of office.  He stated that there were 220 bills  that impacted counties, and CSA got through 5 which were signed by the governor.  He spoke about bills which would have had a negative impact to Navajo County which were killed,  He spoke about HB 2002, which is the Coal Mine TPT bill, which would have taken away a lot of the tax revenue that would have come to Navajo County; however the bill was revised and then signed by the governor.  He spoke about HB 2333 which would have a big impact on local governments which was killed. He indicated that the Cox Communications Bill which also would have had a big impact to local governments was defeated in the end.  He stated we had a very successful year with regard to those bills which needed to be defeated and those which were pushed through the process.

He spoke about the county priorities with respect to the budget, including restoring all of the funding which had been taken away or cost shifts given to local governments.   He stated that three-quarters of the way through the process, the Red for Ed movement happened and teachers' salaries were increased which took away all of the priorities that the counties had and shifted those back to teachers' salaries. He spoke about the restoration of a few of those priorities. He indicated that we had three big champions:  Representative Drew John, Senator Karen Fann and President Yarbrough.  He indicated that in the final weeks of the session, they were able to get most of the priorities added back in.  He stated that the ACJC was a one-time relief and this year they hope to get that added back in as ongoing, SVP and ASH are now ongoing, DUC Pool is now ongoing, and lottery appropriations are now ongoing.  He stated that EORP was a hard issue, and they were able to get that added back in as a one-time relief for the eight small counties.   He stated that the HURF funding is at 5.9 for 2019 and will move to 8.9 in 2020.

He stated that moving into next year, Mr. Yarbrough is retiring from the State Senate and the Speaker of the House will be moving over to his seat.  He indicated that if the Republicans maintain the House and the Senate, Karen Fann may be the next senate president and Rusty Bowers may be the Speaker of the House, which is positive as they are advocates for rural counties. Supervisor Thompson thanked those legislators for their help this year. Supervisor Thompson spoke about Native American issues and the redistribution of TPT.  Mr. Candland stated that Representative Descheenie and Senator Benally made mention of that and it is a topic of conversation at the Capitol.  Supervisor Whiting thanked Mr. Candland for his partnership and efforts this year. He spoke about the Red for Ed movement and the help of those at the legislature. Supervisor Jack commented on the NGS issue and that the Tribe has been meeting with potential buyers. He stated that the power will be sold for super computers. He further spoke about the plant closing down in 2019.  Mr. Candland answered questions of the Board. Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked Mr. Candland for his efforts during this year's legislative process. Mr. Candland stated that when there was a need, Navajo County came.   Chairman Williams thanked Supervisor Whiting for his leadership on the LPC Committee, staff and Mr. Candland.

e.

Information concerning national litigation regarding lawsuits against opioid manufacturers. Pursuant to ARS 38-431.03(A)(4) the Board May move into executive session for legal advice on potentially joining the litigation as a plaintiff:   Judge Higgins introduced Anthony Coury, an attorney who is counsel on this national opioid litigation. Mr. Coury spoke about the consortium of firms across the nation working on this litigation.  He stated that the opioid crisis is a major problem in this country.  He indicated that as many as one in four patients receiving long-term opioid therapy become addicted.  He spoke about the lawsuits pending on a national level as a multi-district litigation suing the manufacturers and distributors of opioids.   He spoke about the theories against the manufacturers and distributors.  He stated there is a database which shows the number of pills which have been distributed in each area.  He indicated that the lawsuit is looking to cover retrospective costs, including law enforcement, healthcare and education.  He stated they are also looking for damages which are prospective. He gave a brief history of the opioid litigation.  He stated there are 650 claimants, and his group represents 300 of them.  He spoke about what would be involved if Navajo County chose to become a plaintiff in this litigation.  Mr. Coury answered questions of the Board about the damages computation.  Mr. Carlyon asked about damages nationally. Mr. Coury indicated that every municipality has their own numbers, but this case is considered similar to tobacco. He stated that the Judge on the case is driving hard for settlements. He spoke about the difference between a multi-district litigation and a class action suit.  Mr. Carlyon spoke about the overlap on the damages, and Mr. Coury advised that the County would make that determination, and each municipality within the County could join as a separate entity.  Mr. Coury continued to answered questions of the Board.
Chairman Williams directed staff to look into the issue that has been presented and possibly put it on a future agenda.
