Consideration and approval of the District Administrator of the White Mountain Lakes Fire District, John Flynn, to explore merger or consolidation options for the White Mountain Lakes Fire District:   John Flynn addressed the Board and indicated that the expenditures of the White Mountain Lakes Fire District will exceed revenues at their current service levels going forward for the next several years.  He stated that he has identified other districts which would be available to either merge or consolidate with White Mountain Lakes Fire District, which includes Taylor-Snowflake, Vernon and Timber Mesa. He indicated that he is looking for guidance and direction on how the Board would like him to proceed. He indicated that he has had preliminary, informal conversations with all three districts to see if they would be willing to explore the option of consolidation. John Hager, White Mountain Lakes, addressed the Board.  He expressed concern regarding not hearing about this item until two days ago when the Board of Supervisors agenda was posted.  He stated that he has not seen any postings.  Mr. Flynn stated that the report to the Board has been on the home page of the White Mountain Lakes Fire District website since August 10th. He indicated that he held a special meeting prior to the regular public meeting to explain the details of the report.  He spoke about the public notice requirements if he receives direction today to explore a consolidation with Timber Mesa Fire District and public hearing requirements at White Mountain Lakes Fire District and the Timber Mesa Fire District, as well.  He indicated that his term will end December 1  when the new board is seated, and so going forward, even with direction from the Board of Supervisors, he would prepare two parallel tracks:  the consolidation of the District, as well as the District transitioning to a new Board of Directors.  Supervisor Whiting made a motion to approve the District Administrator of the White Mountain Lakes Fire District, John Flynn, to explore merger or consolidation options for the White Mountain Lakes Fire District; motion seconded by Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

a.

Reject Abandonment Application # 18-01, the request for the abandonment of right-of-way for a portion of Pinion Place in the Pineview Summer Homesites Number Two subdivision:   Bill Bess discussed the item. Mr. Bess stated that staff is opposed to the abandonment of the right-of-way and requested the Board reject the abandonment application #18-01. He indicated that 72 mailers were sent to the surrounding subdivision with an opinion survey for this abandonment and, to date, only 34 opinion surveys have been returned with 21 supporting the abandonment of the right-of-way, 5 neutral, 7 opposed and 1 did not indicate an opinion. He stated that the utility companies opposed abandoning the road unless they had a 16- to 20-foot wide easement.  Mr. George Phillips, 6175 Sutter, thanked the Board and Mr. Bess for the sewer that has been put in the neighborhood.  He spoke about the interesting relationships throughout the neighborhood. He stated that his main purpose for the application is to be able to build a garage at the side of his home and use the easement for a setback.  He expressed concern about adding extra traffic to the neighborhood.  He spoke about speaking with the Show Low manager and Show Low attorney who indicated that they have no reason or want for there to be a road that connects through there. He stated that this property has been kept by Navajo County for over 40 years, and he was told by Mr. Bess that there are no plans for a road in his lifetime. Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger requested a clarification with regard to utility use. Bill Bess indicated that the right-of-way can be abandoned, but State law states that there still must be an easement in effect that covers those utilities. Chairman Williams inquired whether this will impact the plan to connect Scott Road to Penrod.  Mr. Bess indicated that it does not because the connection is farther east.  He stated that if we abandon this right-of-way, there will never be a way for us to provide additional connection to White Mountain Road at this location at a signalized intersection.  He stated that the plan was that there may be a need in the future for the easement.  He stated that staff is recommending rejection due to emergency vehicles and service vehicles for the utility companies to be able to turn around. He indicated that it also provides another access for evacuation purposes.  Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger asked a question about whether an individual who purchases a property similar to this communicates regarding abandoning that property if they wanted to expand their property, and Mr. Bess indicated that that normally that does not happen.   Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger made a motion to reject abandonment application # 18-01, the request for the abandonment of right-of-way for a portion of Pinion Place in the Pineview Summer Homesites Number Two subdivision; motion seconded by Supervisor Whiting .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

BOARD OF EQUALIZATION :

a.

Discussion and action regarding Personal Property Petition for Review of Valuation on Accounts: P188002101 (Sequoia Pacific Solar I LLC); P188002201 (Solar House I LLC); P188002401 (Visigoth Solar I LLC); P188002501 (Solar City LMC Series II LLC); P188002601 (Solar City LMC Series III LLC); P188002701 (Solar Integrated Fund I LLC); P188002801 (Mound Solar Owner VIII LLC); and P188002901 (NBA Solarcity Commercial I LLC); Discussion and action regarding Personal Property Petition for Review of Valuation on Accounts: P188002101 (Sequoia Pacific Solar I LLC); P188002201 (Solar House I LLC); P188002401 (Visigoth Solar I LLC); P188002501 (Solar City LMC Series II LLC); P188002601 (Solar City LMC Series III LLC); P188002701 (Solar Integrated Fund I LLC); P188002801 (Mound Solar Owner VIII LLC); and P188002901 (NBA Solarcity Commercial I LLC) :  Melissa Buckley, Clerk of the Board, swore in the Assessor and the Attorney for the Petitioners, Mr. Wilhoit.  Mr. Wilhoit indicated that most of the petitions address the same issue and can be handled as one.  He indicated that the properties at issue consist of solar energy devices that are installed on rooftops of either homeowners or businesses that are designed to produce electricity primarily for onsite consumption.  He indicated that in 2006 and a subsequent amendment in 2009, the Legislature enacted ARS 42-11054(C)(2) which dictated how these properties were supposed to be treated for property tax purposes.  He stated that that statute specifically directs that grid-tied solar energy devices that are designed to generate electricity primarily for onsite consumption are considered to have no value and add no value to the property on which they are installed for property tax purposes.  He stated that since the time the statute was enacted in 2006 and amended in 2009, everyone followed that law, and the counties didn't tax it, and nobody tried to centrally assess it.  He indicated that in 2015, the Arizona Department of Revenue decided that it wanted to start taxing that property regardless of the statute, so it assessed and valued that property from tax years 2015 to 2018.  He stated that by doing so, the Department of Revenue said that the counties did not have authority to tax it, and that's why the Department of Revenue was stepping in.  He indicated that the taxpayers filed litigation to challenge the Department of Revenue's ability to tax, which has gone on for four years now starting in the Tax Court, to the Court of Appeals up through the Supreme Court.  He stated that every one of those courts have held that the Department of Revenue has no authority to tax it.

He indicated that since that litigation, the counties have now decided that they want to try to tax it regardless of 42-11054(C)(2) for 2018.  He stated that there are two problems with that:  one is that property has already been assessed and values for that property have already been on the roll for 2018. He indicated that the Department of Revenue sent notices of value out for tax year 2018; therefore, the value for those properties are still on the rolls because it can't be removed as the Department certified those values for this property to the counties at the end of 2017, and the taxes associated with the Department of Revenue's valuations were levied last Monday.  He stated that because it has already been taxed for 2018, the counties cannot come in and try to tax it again.  He indicated that ARS 42-11003 dictates that the same property cannot be taxed twice.  He stated that if the counties want to change the law, they can do that for 2019, but not for 2018.  He indicated that the second issue is the statute itself, 42-11054(C)(2), which is still valid law. He spoke about the history of the litigation and stated that after the Tax Court, both sides appealed to the Court of Appeals, and the Court of Appeals ruled that the Department of Revenue couldn't tax it and that the counties could not tax it, as well.  He indicated that the Department of Revenue then appealed to the Supreme Court for a petition to review, and the Supreme Court granted that petition and struck down the part about the counties not being able to tax it because they said the counties were not involved in that litigation, so they remanded it back to the Tax Court for a determination as to whether the counties have the ability to tax it; and if they do, how does 11054(C)(2) apply, which is being litigated right now.  He stated that the counties cannot ignore that statute, and there is a presumption in the law that any statute enacted by the Legislature is constitutional and to show that it's unconstitutional, you have to prove it beyond a reasonable doubt, and in order to do that, the counties would have to go to Tax Court. He indicated that the only way for the Board to uphold what the county has done would be to determine that that statute was unconstitutional, which is a matter for the Court.

Ms. Darris indicated that although most of the appeals are the same issue, one of the appeals owned by Sequoia Pacific Solar, LLC, Account Number P188002101, is claiming that property should be exempt because it is attached to a Government exempt facility, but it is not attached to the building and it is not owned by the Government facility, so it is not exempt. She stated that the biggest issue on the appeal is the double assessment. She spoke about an email from the Arizona Department of Revenue instructing the counties to add these properties to their tax rolls.   She stated that in December, the Department of Revenue sends the Assessor's Office the centrally valued tax rolls, but since that time and based on the lawsuit, they have been pulled from the Assessor's tax rolls and have been declared inactive, so they will not receive a 2018 assessment based on that centrally valued assessment.  She stated that the email from the Department of Revenue instructed the counties to add them to their tax rolls, and notice was given to the tax payers asking them to file a personal property rendition, Form DOR 85520, which was not received from the taxpayers until the county's notices of values were sent.  She indicated that when they received their personal property renditions, they applied their numbers, processed their renditions, along with the Department of Revenue's appreciation schedule, and came up with Assessor's decision.  She requested that the Board uphold the Assessor's decision for 2018 and see where that litigation goes.

Chairman Williams inquired as to the case number of the current litigation, and Mr. Wilhoit indicated that there are six cases at the trial court level and there is one case which is declaratory relief filed which started in the Tax Court, then went to the Court of Appeals and then went to the Supreme Court and has now been remanded back to the Tax Court.  He stated that the issue that was remanded was to determine whether the counties have authority to tax the property or not.  He stated the case is Solar City vs. Department of Revenue 243 Ariz. 477.  Jason Moore stated that Mr. Wilhoit accurately set forth the pending litigation, and the bottom line is that the Solar City matter was remanded to the trial court, and the trial court has to make certain decisions.  He indicated that we cannot do anything other than what the Assessor has done until we get further clarification. He requested that the Board of Supervisors affirm the Assessor's assessment.  He stated that this most likely will result in a lawsuit being filed by the solar companies in this particular matter, but we think that litigation will probably be placed on hold pending resolution of the remand in the Solar City case.  He indicated that 2015, 2016 and 2017 were all centrally assessed by the Department of Revenue, and 2018 will be the first year with local assessment, and we need to see the outcome of the 2015, 2016 and 2017 cases before we decide how to deal with 2018.  Mr. Wilhoit indicated that he has never seen the email from the Department of Revenue. He stated that there is no provision in the law that the Department of Revenue can take property off the tax rolls by simply sending an email to an assessor.  He indicated that it has been certified to the counties, it's on the rolls and it exists.  He stated that the counties have never taxed this property before.  He indicated that statute was followed from 2006 until 2018, and if you want to preserve the status quo, the way to do that would be to deny their attempts to assess it right now, and they can appeal that if they want to.

Supervisor Whiting inquired whether it is accurate that we have not billed that in the past, and Ms. Darris indicated that we have billed it, but it was centrally valued.  Mr. Wilhoit indicated that those assessments have been determined to be illegal by the Supreme Court.  Mr. Moore stated that one of the issues before the trial court on remand is what happens as a result of being improperly valued by the Arizona Department of Revenue instead of being locally assessed.  He indicated that the question is whether the plaintiffs in those cases get by with paying no taxes for all of those years.  He stated that it is the State's position and the County's position that that's not the result, that you get local valuation; and with regard to 2018, the practical matter is that someone has to be responsible for assessing and taxing that property in 2018. Chairman Williams indicated that he understands the legal argument; however, we have received a recommendation from the County Attorney's Office which he believes needs to be given some weight.  Mr. Moore indicated that he is recommending the Board support the Assessor's decision as the only practical way to move forward.  Mr. Wilhoit stated that the point that somebody has to do this for 2018 is incorrect.  He indicated that ARS 42-11054(C)(2) states this property does not independently have value, it doesn't add any value for property tax purposes on the property on which it's located.  He indicated that from 2006 to 2015, neither the County nor the Department of Revenue valued the property.  Supervisor Whiting made a motion to support the Assessor's decision on the 2018 personal property petition. Chairman Williams inquired whether Supervisor Whiting's motion is to affirm the valuations on those properties set forth in 8a as listed on the agenda, and Supervisor Whiting indicated that that is correct.  Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger seconded the motion.  She thanked the parties for taking the time to educate the Board on this request.  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
