NAVAJO COUNTY BOARD OF SUPERVISORS' MINUTES
Tuesday, October 9, 2018

Present:
Lee Jack, Sr, Supervisor District I; Jesse Thompson, Supervisor District II; Jason Whiting, Supervisor District III; Dawnafe Whitesinger, Vice-Chairwoman

Absent:
Steve Williams, Chairman

Staff Attendance:
Glenn Kephart, County Manager; Melissa Buckley, Clerk of the Board

9:00 a.m . Call To Order: Invocation and Pledge of Allegiance was led by Supervisor Thompson .

CALL TO THE PUBLIC : Dr. Kevin L. Gibson spoke about the dangers of fracking and the health issues from wells and processing. He further spoke about BLM and Black Stone fracking from the areas within Navajo County and split estate law. Dr. Gibson was given an additional three minutes to finish his presentation. He spoke about heart conditions, leukemia and disabilities that can result from fracking and that children are more susceptible to these diseases. He indicated that the risk is much higher than the reward.  Wayne Pierce spoke about fracking and indicated that this is an issue that affects us all because we all drink the same water, and they are trying to protect that water. He stated that he feels that they are not being listened to.  He requested that this issue be on the agenda. Martha Pierce spoke regarding fracking and indicated that she is disappointed because she has sent multiple correspondence to the Board inviting them to see a movie in their own building with no response. She indicated that they would like to show the movie in the different chapter houses. She stated that the fracking is going to start in Woodruff in December. She indicated that she would like to see this on the agenda.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE: Report from County Manager, County Attorney and Board members:   Supervisor Thompson stated that the Arizona Transportation Board was made aware of a sink hole on Highway 89, and he indicated that a quick response was made to that situation, and the road has been repaired. He spoke about the roads on the Reservation which are school bus routes, and they have to find a way to open up these roads for our children. He further indicated that he has a meeting regarding school bus routes later this morning. Supervisor Jack reported on the CSA Legislative Summit in Yuma and indicated that there were many bills discussed and moved forward. He further spoke about a colleague on the Navajo Nation Council who passed away. He spoke about some graveling projects in Kayenta and commended Public Works on their help. Supervisor Whiting had no report. Glenn Kephart reported that the National Flood Insurance Program has exceeded their minimum standards and as a result, residents who need flood insurance receive a 10% discount.  Supervisor Whitesinger reported on the White Mountain Regional Transportation Committee meeting last week to discuss priorities for the region.  She spoke about the effort to prioritize the needs, and the final plan will be in February.  She stated that it is important to support every organization within the region based on road priority.  She thanked Deputy Chief Clouse and Deputy Whiting for their presentation on an active shooter training for teachers and helping them be aware of the steps that are important in being able to support our children in the event of an active shooter.
