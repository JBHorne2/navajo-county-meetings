Consideration and approval of Memorandum of Understanding between the Sheriff's Office and Houston High Intensity Drug Trafficking Area for enhanced interdiction efforts:   Chief Deputy Randy Moffitt discussed the license plate readers (LPR) and how they are used to locate and identify stolen vehicles, wanted fugitives, homicide suspects, interstate terrorism and locally to identify suspect vehicles that have committed crimes.  He spoke about the Memorandum of Understanding with the Houston High Intensity Drug Trafficking Area (HIDTA) and how it will advance our technology.  He stated that we will be able to identify more violent fugitives, more drug smugglers, and more stolen vehicles. ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES :

a.

Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 02-18 , declaring a local emergency exists in the unincorporated areas of Navajo County due to extreme drought conditions:   Mary Springer recognized the President of Navajo County Cattleman's Association, Mark Carlisle; Shelby Johnson, County Executive Director from the Arizona Farm Service Agency; former Supervisor JR DeSpain and his sister, Cindy Machusak. Mary Springer spoke about the extremely dry conditions in this area and requested approval of the resolution. JR DeSpain thanked Supervisor Whiting for assigning Ms. Springer to research this matter. He spoke about the lack of moisture in his area and that his land is dry. Mark Carlisle indicated that everybody benefits from ranchers and farmers receiving help from the federal government to stay in business. Shelby Johnson handed out the U.S. Drought Monitor which indicates that a lot of Navajo County is in the D3 extreme drought and severe drought sections and a fact sheet on the Livestock Forage Program. She indicated that a declaration of drought will not trigger the funding, but the funding is triggered by the Drought Monitor. She spoke about the funding not being available right now because we are not in the growing season; however, funding has been requested, and she expects to hear this week.
Supervisor Whiting thanked everyone who was a part of this item. Heinquired as to whether declaring a drought emergency helps to access any other funding, and Mr. Carlisle indicated that he is not aware of any. Supervisor Whiting indicated that this does not trigger the funding; however, we need to make an official statement recognizing that we are in a state of emergency. JR DeSpain indicated that even though the resolution does not trigger the funding, we need to work with the Secretary of Agriculture and the State with regard to not relying solely on the Drought Monitor. Supervisor Thompson requested that Ms. Springer keep the Board informed of ways we can address the concerns and stated that these drought conditions are on and off the reservation.

Ms. Johnson indicated that until this year, Navajo County has been given a 365-grazing year which was changed by the national office to April 15th. She stated that a request was submitted to have that changed, and the request was denied. She indicated that they have submitted documentation of leases that show we graze for 365 days and are hoping to hear back. She spoke about other programs that can come because of the Drought Monitor, but it is dependent on that grazing season.
Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger asked about acreage on farming. Ms. Johnson indicated that all of Navajo County is included, so if one piece is included, then the entire county is eligible for the programs.
Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger also asked about how many heads of cattle are impacted, and President Carlisle indicated that he is unaware of that number. Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger spoke about the impact to our communities. President Carlisle indicated that there are approximately 100 members in the association. Supervisor Whiting asked for Ms. Johnson's contact information so that we can have a united voice in our efforts. Supervisor Jack indicated that he represents a few chapters up north and indicated that they are faced with the same issue.  He stated that the Tribe bought insurance for drought from the USDA, and that has kicked in, and they are getting $17 million for that.  Chairman Williams inquired as to whether the moisture that is in the forecast would take us out of drought, and Ms. Springer indicated that it would not.
Supervisor Whiting made a motion to adopt Resolution Number 02-18 , declaring a local emergency exists in the unincorporated areas of Navajo County due to extreme drought conditions; motion seconded by Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
