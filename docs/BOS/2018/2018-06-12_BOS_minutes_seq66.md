ASSESSOR:

a.

Consideration to hire outside legal counsel, Roberta Livesay, to represent Navajo County's interests in Solar City v. ADOR, the pending litigation over the valuation of rooftop solar installations in the State of Arizona:  Jason Moore discussed the item. He spoke about the ongoing litigation with various rooftop solar companies regarding how properties leasing solar panels should be valued.   He stated that the Arizona Supreme Court ruled that these properties should be valued locally by the local county assessors.  He indicated that because of that ruling, the Arizona Supreme Court told the counties that we need to intervene in these cases in order to look after the County interests. He stated that there is a list of 16 cases which have been provided to the Board in their packets which have been filed by rooftop solar companies seeking refunds for prior tax years.  He indicated that the second question is how and through what process the county assessors should go through in order to properly value the rooftop solar.  He stated that the Arizona Department of Revenue establishes guidelines for the assessors to follow in assessing properties.  He indicated that the County Assessors group has gotten together and discussed the best course of action, and they wish to be jointly represented by the same legal counsel in all 16 cases.  He stated that Navajo County's proportionate share of solar in Arizona is .03% in the state, and the bill would be divided on the proportionate value and would not be a significant burden.  He indicated that other counties have already signed on to approve this. Supervisor Whiting inquired whether all of the counties have agreed to the percentages which have been outlined, and Mr. Moore indicated that the assessors have had an informal cost sharing agreement through which they calculated the percentage of solar in the counties, and it is perceived that all of the counties with rooftop solar will be on board. Supervisor Thompson inquired as to whether we have the budget capacity for this, and Mr. Moore indicated that he does not believe our contribution would be more than a few thousand.  Supervisor Thompson made a motion to approve the hiring of outside legal counsel, Roberta Livesay, to represent Navajo County's interests in Solar City v. ADOR, the pending litigation over the valuation of rooftop solar installations in the State of Arizona; motion seconded by Supervisor Whiting . Vote unanimous approving the motion.

b.

Consideration and adoption of the proposed Tentative Budget for Fiscal Year 2018-19 for Navajo County : Paige Peterson presented the FY2018-19 preliminary budget presentation.  She indicated that we are recommending a flat property tax levy  plus new construction.  She stated we can maintain services at current levels and have the opportunity to address some of our most pressing capital needs.  She spoke about where we were at this time last year.  She stated that last year, we had to reduce our budget $2.5 million, which included the elimination of two of our programs.  She spoke regarding concerns in FY 2018.  She spoke about our successful partnerships which put us in a great position to finish the year out, which included Supervisor Whiting , Brad Carlyon, Department Directors and County Supervisor Association. She indicated that our delegation heard our concerns and stepped up for us. She spoke about the continued partnership by Sheriff Clark with other agencies for housing federal inmates.  She further spoke about the diligent effort by Elected Officials and Department Directors to stay within their budgets.  She stated that we had a strong year for State shared sales tax and County sales tax, and the State economy continues to grow.  She stated that the County sales tax increase is temporary and will go away when coal energy production declines.  She stated that we have the fourth lowest property tax and property value in the state. She indicated that only eight cents of every dollar comes to the county general fund. She stated that for FY2019, we have a flat levy plus new construction and will maintain services at current levels.  She indicated that we do have a capital replacement plan this year which is new to Navajo County.  She stated that this year, our general fund budget is $43.9 million, all operation budgets are flat, and the increases are due to State Retirement Systems, Healthcare and the cost of doing business. She indicated that Public Safety takes up the biggest chuck of the general fund, and another big piece is Indigent Health over which we have no control.   She spoke about our mandated services and capital needs to  maintain services.  She stated that we have a backlog of equipment which have ended their useful life.  She spoke about the importance of the contingency.   She stated that our IT network is the heart of all county functions, and the plan is to replace three of our servers, many network switches and power supply backups.

Bryan Layton discussed the decline of the coal industry and about our options moving forward, including reducing staff, economic development and new industry, broadband infrastructure and any revenue possibilities.  Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger spoke about the meetings held with Department Directors and Elected Officials and expressed appreciation for us being proactive. Supervisor Thompson spoke about staff doing more to provide services to our citizens and thanked Ms. Peterson and Mr. Layton for all of the information. Supervisor Whiting thanked the Department Directors and Elected Officials for their willingness to work together with our administration team and thanked the administration team for their efforts.  He also thanked Chairman Williams and Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger for the meetings they attended and that they represented the interests of the Board well. Chairman Williams thanked Ms. Peterson and Mr. Layton for the presentation and conversations outside of Board meetings, Department Directors, Elected Officials and also Mr. Kephart's leadership on this.  Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger made a motion to adopt the Proposed Tentative Budget for Fiscal Year 2018-2019 for Navajo County; motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
