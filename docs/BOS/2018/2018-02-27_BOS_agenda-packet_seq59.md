MEMORANDUM OF UNDERS TANDING 
(MOU) BETWEEN 
NAVAJO COUNTY  
AND 
NORTHEAST ARIZONA FAMILY RESOURCE CENTER  DBA FAMILY ADVOCACY CENTER  
 
WHEREAS, Navajo County, Navajo County’s Attorney Office and the Northeast Arizona Family 
Resource Center dba Family Advocacy Center have come together to collaborate and to make an 
application for OVW Fiscal Year 2018 Improving Criminal Justice Responses to Sexual Assault, 
Domestic Violence, Dating Violence, and Stalking Grant Program (formerly known as the Grants to 
Encourage Arrest Policies and Enforcement of Protection Orders Program) Solicitation grant; and 
 
WHEREAS, the partners listed below have agreed to enter into a collaborative agreement in which 
Navajo County Attorney’s Office will be the lead agency and named applicant and the other 
agencies will be partners in this application; and 
 
WHEREAS, the partners herein desire to enter into a Memorandum of Understanding setting forth 
the services to be provided by the collaborative;  
1.0 PARTIES 
1.1 This Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) provides a binding agreement 
between Navajo County through the Navajo County Attorney’s Office 
(hereinafter referred to as “County”) and Northeast Arizona Family 
Resource Center dba Family Advocacy Center (hereinafter referred to as 
“FAC”) for their respective responsibilities and rights in providing services 
to victims  of sexual assault, domestic violence, dating violence, and 
stalking. Navajo County is authorized to enter into this MOU under A.R.S. 
§ 11 -952 for the purposes of exercising joint powers or contracting for 
services. FAC is empowered by A.R.S. § 11-951 to e nter into this 
Agreement and has authorized the undersigned to execute this Agreement 
on behalf of FAC. County and FAC may collectively at times hereinafter be 
referred to as the “Parties,” or each individually as a “Party.” 
1.2 The FAC was established in 2011 to provide a coordinated, multidisciplinary 
and humane approach to cases of child abuse and sexual assault. The FAC 
offers a welcoming homelike atmosphere, and puts children at ease when 
conducting forensic interviews; this in turn increases the effective ness of 
the investigative process. The FAC is a safe, secure, and caring 
environment that focuses on the needs of the victims they serve, and assist 
in addressing and overcoming the trauma of their experience. The FAC staff 
proofread and approved all grant content.   
1.3 The Navajo County Attorney’s Office is an Arizona Constitutionally created 
office and was created when Arizona received Statehood in 1912.  The 
Navajo County Attorney’s Office is the prosecuting age ncy for the State of 
Arizona in Navajo County.  Among the 14-member staff of prosecutors there 
are attorneys who have been specifically trained in the prosecution of Sex 
Crimes, Homicides, Drug Sales and Trafficking offenses . The Navajo

Office Grant Manager, Victim Services Staff, Civil Attorney wrote and

2.0  PURPOSE 
2.1  This Agreement outlines the responsibilities of each of the Parties as to their 
respective responsibilities and rights in providing victims, across their life 
span, with the protection and services they need to pursue safe and healthy 
lives, while improving communities’ capacity to provide justice for victims 
and hold offenders accountable. The Parties agree to cooperate and assist 
each other in meeting the mutual obligations and duties of each office 
because it is good public policy and benefits the citizens of Navajo County 
and of the individuals who utilize the services of the FAC. 
3.0  PARTNERSHIP 
3.1  The primary project partners for this grant would be between the Navajo 
County Attorney’s office, and Family Advocacy Center. A key role the 
Navajo County Attorney Office will provide is that of two Spec ialized Sex 
Crimes Prosecutors. The County Attorney’s Office is also contracted with 
six City and Town entities within the county, including Holbrook, Kayenta, 
Pinetop-Lakeside, Show Low, Snowflake -Taylor, and Winslow, Arizona to 
prosecute all misdemeanor crimes. At a misdemeanor level approximatel y 
80% of these cases are Domestic Violence related.  
3.2  As part of the relationship, the Navajo County Attorney’s Office  Victim 
Services Department (Victim Services) will be an intricate project partner. 
Victim Services provides the key roles of Grant Manager, Victim Services 
Director, Victim Compensation Coordinator, SART team coordinator, South 
County Misdemeanor Court Advocate, and Superior Court Criminal Justice 
Advocate. The Navajo County Victim Services Department was established 
in the 1990’s.  By the e arly 2000’s, and with the assistance of grant funds, 
the program expanded to include criminal justice court advocacy for victims.  
Currently, the program consists of five advocates total, providing victim 
compensation services, crisis response services, an d criminal justice 
advocacy.  All advocates who are currently employed have attended the 
Basic Arizona Victim Assistance Academy through the Arizona Coalition of 
Victim Services. This 40 -hour basic academy curriculum meets 
requirements for the National Adv ocate Credentialing Program and the 
Military Advocate Certificate Program pre -service training through the 
National Organization  for Victim Assistance (NOVA). This training 
addresses topics such as victimology, crisis theory and response, criminal 
justice system, victims’ rights, and cultural awareness.  This training also 
provides education and examination about various crime types including 
Homicide, Domestic Violence, Sexual Assault, Stalking, Drunk Driving, and 
Vulnerable Adult Abuse.  Advocates will also undergo extensive local direct 
service training by shadowing and working alongside the Victim Servic es

4.1 Agreement shall become effective upon approval and execution by the

5.0 RESPONSIBILITIES OF PARTIES 
5.1 COUNTY Responsibilities: 
5.1.1  Provide all upfront expenses to be reimbursed.  
5.1.2  Provide two dedicated County Attorney Prosecutors, that specialize 
in sexual offenses, to oversee and prosecute sex based offenses.  
5.1.3  Provide onsite personnel to monitor in-person interviews while they 
are conducted.  
5.1.4  Coordinate investigatory efforts between multi-jurisdictional law 
enforcement agencies for sexual assault, domestic violence, dating 
violence, and sexual stalking.   
5.1.5  Provides office space utilized by the FAC and the Prosecuting 
Attorneys and project staff.  
5.1.6  Provide training to law enforcement agency personnel as it relates 
to sexual assault, domestic violence, dating violence, and sexual 
stalking.  
5.1.7  Ensure the information obtained from forensic interviews is used by 
law enforcement in investigation efforts.   
5.1.8  Assure that the services provided at the FAC are coordinated 
with and integrated into the overarching purpose of this MOU. 
5.2 FAC Responsibilities: 
5.2.1  Provide one forensic interviewer who will work with victims of 
sexual assault, domestic violence, dating violence, and sexual 
stalking.  
5.2.2  Provide office space and interview rooms for members of law 
enforcement, social services, and the County Attorney’s office to 
watch forensic interviews live, when possible. Alternatively, provide 
viewing rooms for these individuals to watch the recorded interview

Re: Improving Criminal Justice Responses Program Dear Director: 
 
Dear Director: 
As Chairman of the Navajo County Board of Supervisors, I submit this letter to certify 
to the following: 
1) the laws or official policies of Navajo County encourage or mandate arrests of 
domestic violence offenders based on probable cause that an offense has been 
committed; 
2) the laws or official policies of Navajo County encourage or mandate arrests of 
domestic violence offenders who violate the terms of a valid and outstanding protection 
order; 
3) the laws, policies, or practices and the training programs of Navajo County discourage 
dual arrests of offender and victim; 
4) the laws, policies, or practices of Navajo County prohibit issuance of mutual 
restraining orders of protection except in cases where both parties file a claim and the 
court makes detailed findings of fact indicating that both parties acted primarily as 
aggressors and that neither party acted primarily in self-defense; 
5) the laws, policies, and practices of Navajo County do not require, in connection with 
the prosecution of any misdemeanor or felony domestic violence, dating violence, 
sexual assault, or stalking offense, or in connection with the filing, issuance, 
registration, modification, enforcement, dismissal, or service of a protection order, or a 
petition for a protection order, to protect a victim of sexual assault, domestic violence, 
dating violence, or stalking, that the victim bear the costs associated with the filing of 
criminal charges against the offender, or the costs associated with the filing, issuance, 
registration, modification, enforcement, dismissal or service of a warrant, protection 
order, petition for a protection order, or witness subpoena, whether issued inside or 
outside the state, tribal, or local jurisdiction; and

<!-- page 13 -->
6) the laws, policies or practices of Navajo County ensure that: 
(A) no law enforcement officer, prosecuting officer or other government official shall 
ask or require an adult, youth, or child victim of a sex offense as defined under 
federal, tribal, state, territorial, or local law to submit to a polygraph examination or 
other truth telling device as a condition for proceeding with the investigation of, trial 
of, or sentencing for such an offense; and  
(B) the refusal of a victim to submit to an examination described in subparagraph 
(A) shall not prevent the investigation of, trial of, or sentencing for the offense.
