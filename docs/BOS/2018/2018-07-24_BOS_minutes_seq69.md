CALL TO THE PUBLIC :  Martha Pearce, PO Box 153, Show Low, Arizona, stated that she has had the opportunity to learn more about what we need to do to find a resolution to the fracking issue.  She expressed gratitude for the rain.  She stated that we need to address the matter of infrastructure if the fracking starts happening and the leases are put out.  She thanked the Board for opening up dialog, but indicated that we need to slow the process down.  She stated that she feels that we have an obligation to be involved in the dialog.  Wayne Pearce, PO Box 153, Show Low, Arizona, thanked the Board for the opportunity to speak.  He spoke about water being life.  He stated that there are some who will gamble with our water.  He indicated that he is not against gas and oil and understands that we need jobs, but he does not feel it is worth the risk to our water. He urged the Board of Supervisors to do what they can to influence others that fracking is a bad idea.

REGULAR AGENDA:

1.

Presentation of the current status of the 2018 Fire Season and Restrictions:  Catrina Jenkins presented a final fire season update.  She indicated that our region is in moderate to low danger and that our energy release components are under the 50th percentile.  She stated that fire restrictions were lifted last week, and the weather forecasts indicate more monsoon weather coming.  Chief Deputy Chevalier, Timber Mesa Fire and Medical, spoke about how it takes a whole community to get through fire season and how residents listened to the warnings.  He spoke about the fine fuel that exist for fires and how we mitigate that through prevention efforts and cleaning those fuels.  He thanked everybody who came together to make this happen.  He spoke about recreational activities and how we have to continue to operate in a safe manner.  He stated that we need to use safety measures when operating ATV's and UTV's.  He spoke about defensible space zones and how now is the time to get outside.  Ms. Jenkins stated that we are focusing on our communities and restoring our resilient landscapes and becoming a fire-wise adapted community.    She spoke about monsoon safety.  She stated that there was a flood incident in Joseph City and encouraged the citizens to not play in the washes.  She spoke about heat warnings and advisories.  Chief Deputy Chevalier stated that we need to make sure that we stay sufficiently hydrated during the heat.  Ms. Jenkins thanked all those who helped during wildfire season.
Supervisor Thompson stated that flooding has occurred in the Joseph City area before, and he was worried about the road being washed out.  He indicated that he has spoken to the Arizona Department of Transportation Board to prevent this from happening.  He expressed gratitude for the rain in most of the communities but indicate that there are communities in the northeast area which have not received rain and inquired how much more rain we can expect.  Ms. Jenkins indicated that we have several more weeks of monsoon season left.  Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked Ms. Jenkins for her efforts in working with our partners across the region.  She stated that she has seen an increased vigilance within our community, and our citizens are more aware of the impact of their particular property and community.  Chairman Williams thanked Ms. Jenkins and Chief Deputy Chevalier for the presentation.  He indicated that he read that the dryness was comparable to 2002, and Ms. Jenkins indicated that it was actually worse at certain points.
