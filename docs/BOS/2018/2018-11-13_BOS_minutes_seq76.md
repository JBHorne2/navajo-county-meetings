a.

Presentation on Earth Day : Supervisor Thompson introduced Leon Nuvayestewa.  Mr. Nuvayestew stated that he is a retired health service administrator and a veteran. He spoke about noticing a lot of trash in his community during his morning walks and expressed his concern to his family.  He introduced his team:  his wife, Eva; his daughter, Marissa Nuvayestewa  and Jack Pongyesva. He gave a short power point on the Earth Day project.

At 9:16 a.m. Supervisor Jack is present.

Mr. Nuvayestewa and Marissa Nuvayestewa continued with the Earth Day project presentation. They stated that 13 tons of trash was collected, and they used a zip line to get some of the trash down the steep hill. They spoke about the help they received from the Emergency Response Team on the reservation.  Mr. Nuvayestewa indicated that they had 233 volunteers, and over 60 people donated food to feed the volunteers. Ms. Nuvayestewa stated that the project took over 6 months of planning to the day of the event, including raising awareness among the villagers to make sure that they do not continue to illegally dump their trash.  She indicated that the budget for the one-day event was about $120,000.  Ms. Nuvayestewa thanked the community sponsors and spoke about the donations they received, including Navajo County Board of Supervisors. Mr. Nuvayestewa stated that he believes it will take 6 years to complete the cleanup of this area.  He thanked the County for their help with this project. Ms. Nuvayestewa indicated that the next Earth Day project with be a 3-day event. Jack Pongyesva spoke about the recycling component of the Earth Day project. He stated that he is trying to combat the illegal dumping problem on the reservations. He indicated that about ½ ton of solid waste was diverted from the landfill with up to 20 volunteers who worked on this part of the project. He stated that he plans to continue his work. Supervisor Thompson thanked the team for their efforts. Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger expressed her appreciation for the team for being great stewards and providing an example to other communities. Chairman Williams expressed his appreciation for the team and the work that was done.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE: Report from County Manager, County Attorney and Board members :  Glenn Kephart spoke about Proposition 419 coming up short by about 156 votes.  He stated that the turnout for this election as about 45% greater than it would historically have been for a mid-term election.  He thanked Doris Clark, Rayleen Richards and their team for working around the clock to make sure that all the votes were counted.  He stated that as a result of the election, we will need to redefine the services and the way we provide them. He indicated that this can be a stressful time for people and especially staff as we work to refine the organization and the services we provide, but we will get through this successfully by adhering to some important principles:

First and foremost- The public interest, as it has always been and should be, will be our guide.

We will need to think strategically in prioritizing the services we provide and how we do so; our work teams are already very lean and we cannot continue to just cut in areas without redefining the expectations.

We need to find new ways of delivering some of the services we provide. We will be creative in exploring new ways to deliver services, and we will need to be open to reimagining traditional boundaries between departments/offices so we can more effectively leverage each other and achieve better economies of scale.

We will work inclusively and collaboratively to find the best solutions and to prioritize our decisions. And to that end we have scheduled an elected officials and department directors meeting to continue this process.

We will work openly with our communities and partners.

We will be compassionate and understanding that this can be a stressful time for people.

We will be supportive of each other.

Mr. Kephart stated that we may be embarking on a difficult period, but we are a dedicated and resilient team, and, together, we will get through this successfully. He indicated that he did attend the VFW event and spoke about the moving experience that he enjoyed.  Mr. Carlyon did not have an update. Supervisor Thompson said we fell short on 419, but we worked hard and we need to keep our heads held high as we are Navajo County and we can deal with the challenges ahead. He spoke about what he could have done more to educate his people. He stated that he attended several prayer services for this Veteran's Day. Supervisor Jack spoke about NGS being the same, and Navajo Nation is working diligently to get a new buyer.  He spoke about former Supervisor Jonathan Nez being voted in as Navajo Nation Tribal President. He said that there will be a whole new council, as well. He spoke about the Red Dog Project moving slowly. Supervisor Whiting spoke about meetings with the Corporation Commission regarding requirements for biomass to be part of the portfolio the State has and although it lost 3-2; they have instructed staff to continue the conversation recognizing the importance to the watersheds we have here.
Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked those who exercised their right to vote. She spoke about the challenges ahead, and indicated that we will not shy away from those challenges, but work through them together through hard work and dedication and looking towards our communities to help guide us during this process. She indicated that we have tried to be very transparent, and it is important that we continue to educate one another about the efforts we make on behalf of the County.  She stated that the Board of Supervisors work very hard to understand the finances and ensure that we are serving our community members. Chairman Williams expressed appreciation for those who worked on the items in the agenda. He spoke about the sixth annual Community Fast of Compassion going on in Show Low for one this, which began on Sunday and will end this Saturday.  He indicated that the money raised during the fast stays locally to help people in need. He spoke about the different organizations which have received funding.  He stated that in the past, $65,000 has been collected. He indicated that on Sunday at 6 p.m. will be the wrap-up celebration. He spoke about some of the donations and the way the money was spent.
