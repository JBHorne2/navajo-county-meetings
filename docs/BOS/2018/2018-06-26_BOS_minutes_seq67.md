b.

Presentation of the current status of the 2018 Fire Season :  Catrina Jenkins introduced Darrel Craig and Kurt Schneider with the Department of Forestry and Fire Management and thanked them for being here and helping to support us.  She gave an update of the current status of the 2018 Fire Season. She spoke about the 30-day temperature and precipitation outlook.  She stated that fireworks are never allowed on Federal Forest or State Lands.  She indicated that unincorporated areas of Navajo and Apache Counties do not permit consumer permissible fireworks when fire restrictions are in place.  She stated that Sunrise and Alpine have canceled their fireworks display, and Show Low is evaluating the conditions and will let us know early next week. She indicated that there are still displays yet to be determined, and other jurisdictions have canceled outside of Navajo County.  She stated that although there may not be fireworks, there is still much to do during the 4th of July to celebrate our Country. Ms. Jenkins further discussed the issue of children playing with matches.  She spoke about a fire yesterday in the Forest Lakes region.  She stated that one less spark means one less wildfire.  Supervisor Thompson commended Ms. Jenkins on the work that she is doing.  Chairman Williams thanked Ms. Jenkins for her work.  Ms. Jenkins stated that no one in Navajo County has canceled to date.  She indicated that Snowflake-Taylor is having their displays this Saturday, and Show Low is evaluating and making decisions the early part of next week.

PUBLIC WORKS :

a.

Consideration of Adopting Ordinance Number 01-18 , approving the Minor Land Division Ordinance with associated application forms and fees :  Sandra Phillips discussed the item.  She indicated that the Planning and Zoning Commission unanimously recommended approval of this item.  She further indicated that staff has the support and encouragement from the local jurisdictions.  She stated that staff has worked with the Recorder and the Assessor and that this has been a combined effort.  She indicated that this ordinance was compiled from other ordinances in and around the State of Arizona.  She stated that this ordinance will protect existing residents, as well as new residents.  She requested that the application form not be included in the motion before the Board.  She stated that land splits will still happen, but the ordinance would require a paragraph that would be recorded with the splits where there is no legal or physical access or zoning regulations are not met. She spoke about the fees associated with this process and the work that goes into the investigation to support this fee.  Cammy Darris, County Assessor, spoke in support of the proposed ordinance. Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger expressed appreciation to staff investigating this issue and bringing it forward. Chairman Williams also thanked staff for their work.  Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger made a motion to adopt Ordinance Number 01-18 , approving the Minor Land Division Ordinance with associated application forms and fees; motion seconded by Supervisor Thompson .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE: Report from County Manager, County Attorney and Board members :  Glenn Kephart thanked the Board, staff and those involved in getting the Minor Land Division Ordinance put together and passed.  He spoke about the fracking concerns raised by residents at the last meeting, and advised the Board that we have been following up on that and are engaged in the conversations, but it is premature to bring that issue forward as an agenda item.  He spoke about the BLM issue and the process which would happen if that occurs, which includes a public comment period. He indicated that we are staying involved, and this issue will be headed up by Paul Watson.  He stated that there will be a blood drive on July 3rd at the Navajo County Complex in Holbrook.  Melissa Buckley spoke about the CARE Committee backpack drive.  Supervisor Thompson spoke about a meeting with Holbrook Unified School District regarding their plan if an emergency should happen on campus.  He stated that their plan is well thought out, and they are prepared. He spoke about the Winslow City Council meeting regarding the budget update. He spoke about the Arizona State Transportation Board meeting where they passed the five-year transportation plan. He stated that any community which wishes to have a project that needs to be considered that they keep pushing it. He spoke about the Korean War Veterans Memorial Highway dedication.  He indicated that he participated in the NACo Transportation Steering Committee teleconference and stated that certain resolutions  will be presented in Nashville, and he has one regarding encouraging the consideration of improving funding for Reservation roads. He spoke about the TPAC, Transportation Policy Advisory Committee, teleconference where he was updated on federal and state issues. Supervisor Jack spoke about the dedication of the Korean War Veterans Memorial Highway.  He further spoke about projects which will utilize the HURF funding. He introduced Mr. Bill Yazzie with Pinon Chapter. Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger spoke about the White Mountain Apache Tribal Council meeting and thanked those who were able to join at which we shared the message about the financial challenges that Navajo County is facing. She spoke about the White Mountain Regional Transportation Committee and the public comments which were gathered through the survey. She stated that she attended the Pinetop Lakeside Town Council meeting and was happy to see the partnerships between Pinetop-Lakeside and the White Mountain Apache Tribe and the police departments developing a Memorandum of Agreement for shared resources. Chairman Williams expressed his appreciation to his fellow members of the Board of Supervisors and the work that each of them do in their districts which benefits the County as a whole.  He further expressed appreciation to our management team. He thanked Paul Watson for taking the lead regarding the fracking issue, Sandra Phillips for the taking the lead in the Minor Land Division Ordinance and Bryan Layton for his work with the budget. He stated that he is feeling grateful and wished everyone a safe and happy 4th of July.
