ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES :

a.

Presentation of the latest fire season information:  Catrina Jenkins indicated that we have seen some change in our forest conditions.  She stated that we still have some danger for our region, but we are slowly transitioning out of that.  She spoke about the energy release components (ERC) sitting just below the 97th percentile in Arizona and below the 90th percentile in our region.   She indicated that fuels are still out there and moisture has been scattered, therefore, we will remain in Stage 3 and keep current restrictions across the board.   She stated that with these storms, we get lightning, and lightning-caused fires have been a huge component the last few days with 12-15 lightning fire strikes.  She indicated that monsoon season is looking really good.  She spoke about monsoon safety, including encouraging citizens to not cross roadways that are flooded, seek shelter if you see lightning or hear thunder, and pull completely off the road and turn off your lights if you're in a dust storm.  Supervisor Thompson inquired how restrictions are determined.  Ms. Jenkins stated that because the storms have been scattered and isolated, restrictions have remained the same.  She indicated that once we get rain across the board and we get a continuous wetting rain, then they will be more secure in lifting those restrictions.

SHERIFF'S OFFICE :

a.

Consideration of Resolution No. 12-18 to establish a Jail District in Navajo County for the purpose of providing a stable funding source to counteract the loss of revenues from pending closures of coal power plants:   Bryan Layton discussed the item.  He stated that this is a continuation of an ongoing discussion regarding the effects to the County with the loss of the coal power industry.  He spoke about what has happened over the last decade starting with the recession, including the loss of personnel, vehicles, tools, our network and increases in healthcare and salary costs.  He stated that the County adapted during this time, and the public did not see the changes.  He indicated that that changed when we were forced to cut programs last year, including the juvenile detention facility and the County Attorney Child Support Program.  He stated the general fund budget coming into this year looks good due to mild increases in sales taxes, but that will change starting December of 2019 due to the closure of the Navajo Generating Station and the impact on revenues that will have to Navajo County, which will be another $2.5 million on top of what was cut last year.  He spoke about mandated services.  He stated that any job loss would have to include public safety at this point, and there would be a concern regarding meeting the needs, including vehicles and equipment for public safety, and case loads on both the detective/investigator side and prosecution side.  He spoke about the impact to other departments within the County and how services would slow down.   He spoke about options moving forward, including forced reduction, economic development and revenue possibilities.    He distributed a revenue analysis to the Board.  He spoke about Navajo County having the fourth lowest property tax in the State and the fourth lowest property values.  He spoke about the current revenue sources to the County and how some are stable but we can't change them, stable but they cannot bring the volume we need to make up the loss we are facing or unstable.   He indicated that when the coal power plants go offline, we will go out of balance by $2.5 million.

Mr. Layton stated that one option open to us to replace those revenues is a jail district, which is a special district  that's created by the voters, and it goes to the daily operations of the jail.  He indicated that it is a 1/3 cent equitable sales tax.  He stated that the jail cost is over $5 million, and the jail district would fill the gap of what we lose from the loss of the coal industry.  He indicated that a jail district is currently used by eight other counties in the State.  He stated that another benefit of a jail district is that it would eliminate the jail fees paid by cities and towns, which would allow those funds to then stay with the cities and towns.  He spoke about the impacts of a 1/3 cent sales tax to residents in everyday life.  Sheriff Clark spoke about how the loss of the coal industry is another devastating blow to this county, and we have to do something.  He spoke about the expense of incarceration.  He indicated that jail staff is always looking for ways to decrease costs.  He spoke about the struggles of renting out bed space.  He stated that this was a tough decision, but it is something that we as leaders have to do is make those hard decisions.  He spoke about the possible effects of cutting back on public safety, including losing the civil division, drug task force and helping out other jurisdictions.  He requested that the Board pass this item and let the voters decide.

Supervisor Thompson stated that he believes that this request definitely has to go to the citizens of Navajo County.  He stated that he knows there are deputies going from community to community on the Reservation and spoke about the effects if that position is vacated.  Supervisor Whiting inquired whether a jail district would eliminate jail fees for municipalities and allow them to use that money in their general fund.  Sheriff Clark stated that by law, if you have a jail district, you cannot charge jail fees.  Supervisor Whiting thanked Mr. Layton and Sheriff Clark for the detailed report.  He stated that it has been a constant cut for every department throughout the years.  He thanked the Sheriff's Department for their partnership.  He indicated that we're at the point where losing these coal mines would result in cutting services to our citizens.  He spoke about the citizens being a part of the conversation and being educated.  Supervisor Jack thanked Mr. Layton and Sheriff Clark for the report.  He expressed support for this item and indicated that one of these days coal is not going to be there, and we need to find other ways to bring in more revenue.  Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger inquired about when we would see the impact on our community if we do not have a jail district.  Mr. Layton indicated those effects would be next budget year.  Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger stated what those effects would look like in public safety and other departments. She expressed appreciation to staff for being proactive in looking down the road and being able to provide public safety to our community.   She spoke about the community being a part of the discussion regarding the services which are provided and the quality of those services and what does it look like if those services are not continually there.  She expressed appreciation for the investigation and keeping the Board informed and being able to understand the history of the County and how we got to this point and how industry impacts us and our neighbors and the services which are provided.
Chairman Williams thanked Mr. Layton for the presentation and for our management team.  He expressed appreciation to the Sheriff for his history of being a great partner.  He inquired if the Board voted to approve the resolution, there would be a  public hearing in a month or so, and, depending on the outcome of the public hearing, the jail district would go to the voters of Navajo County who would get to decide whether that district would be funded.  Brad Carlyon indicated comfirmed that that is correct.

Supervisor Thompson made a motion to adopt Resolution No. 12-18 to establish a Jail District in Navajo County for the purpose of providing a stable funding source to counteract the loss of revenues from pending closures of coal power plants; motion seconded by Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

PROBATION :

a.

Presentation regarding Navajo County Juvenile Probation working to keep local youth out of Detention by teaming up with the Navajo County Accommodation District to help at-risk youth :  Jason Cash discussed the item.   He spoke about how this is a program to keep kids here as opposed to being housed in Pinal County, and the kids have named it H.U.B., Hope, Unity and Belief.  He stated that this is not just a youth center, but the idea is to have pro-social activities and people which would ultimately lower the risk of youth to reoffend.  He stated that they have a partnership with NCIS which allows the youth to go to school and get a full high school education, and it is also a credit recovery model, which helps kids who are falling behind in school.   He stated that there are counseling services on site, as well as security.   He spoke about the Life Skills and Enrichment Program.  He indicated that they have many volunteers who will be coming in and teaching the youth.  He spoke about the current staff and stated that he is looking to recruit teachers to come in and teach life skills lessons.  He spoke about some of the benefits of the program to Navajo County, including this is an alternative to detention, it's less expensive than detention, there's an extra level of supervision, it's a solid foundation for add-on programs, other options for diversion, it's easily replicated, and it develops the strengths of our local youth.  Chairman Williams congratulated Mr. Cash and Jalyn Gerlich for this program.  He inquired whether this program is post- or pre-disposition, and Mr. Cash stated that right now, it is post-disposition, but there are opportunities for diversion programs.  He indicated that youth can be ordered to participate by a judge, or a probation officer can order them to attend the program as a term of probation.  He stated that they had 16 kids there yesterday, and they could go up to 25.  Chairman Williams thanked Mr. Cash for presenting the program to the Board.  He directed staff to schedule a time for members of the Board to tour the facility.  Supervisor Thompson thanked Mr. Cash for the presentation and recognized Mr. Cash's compassion for the program.  Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked Mr. Cash for the efforts and thinking alternatively about the services we provide.  She stated that this program addresses the issue of our youth being taken out of the community, and it also will provide an alternative resource to empower our youth.  Supervisor Whiting thanked Mr. Cash and everyone involved.  He indicated that this will not only cut costs but will provide a better service to our community.
