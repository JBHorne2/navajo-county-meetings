b).    Discussion and possible action regarding Petition for Review of Proposed Correction for Fitzgerald 
Living Trust APN: 105 -10-002D and 105 -10-002F Assessor Cammy Darris distributed information 
regarding this hearing, stated the  parcels were located in the Sun Valley area and further explained the 
parcels were removed from agriculture status in 2001.  She reported that a courtesy letter was sent to Mr. 
Fitzgerald asking him to re-apply for agricultural status.  Assessor Darris read the statute and stated that  
notices of value have been sent every year , advising  that her office recommend s no change .  The 
assessor introduced Mr. Heimbach, who is representing the Fitzgerald family.  Mr. Heimbach stated he  
agrees that  the assessor does not have the authority to gr ant agriculture status. He reported that 
numerous multi -page letters have been written by Mr. Fitzgerald’s daughter  explaining that Mr. 
Fitzgerald is in very poor health and didn’t understand filing the paperwork.  He stated h is office has 
filed a Notice of Claim and handed out copies of his presentation to the board.  He indicated that vacant 
land is valued at $60.00 per acre but with agricultural designation  it would be valued at only $2.00 per 
acre.  Mr. Heimbach distributed statutes, stating they do all ow for actual use of the property and the 
board can establish agricultural status.  He stated this is an actual use issue and he is requesting 
agricultural classification for these two parcels. Assessor Darris clarified the appeal process.  Mr. 
Heimbach stated that the statute was amended this spring and the bill was approved by the governor f or 
the litigation process.  County Attorney Bowers said this appears to be a technical legal issue and that he 
did not see where the Assessor’s office made any factual  errors.  Mr. Heimbach stated that the new

 COOPERATIVE EXTENSION:  Discussion of drought impact on Navajo County ; Mr. Steve 
Campbell addressed the board and introduced Gregg Norton with the Farm Service Agency. Mr. Norton 
reported on some of the issues regarding the drought conditions , stating they are considering different 
management practices since the drought conditions are ongoing. Mr. Norton distributed fact sheets and 
advised that Congress passed and the President signed the Agricultural Assistance Act, adding that at the 
very bottom of the legislation they included some drought help.  He advised there may be some funding, 
a crop disaster program , all for losses for 2005, 2006, and 2007  and that the l ivestock compensation 
program requires a Presidential or Congressional declaration of disaster.  Mr. Norton stated that this will 
be an assistance payment  and that som e payments are limited to different areas of the country.  
Supervisor DeSpain inquired if the crop disaster program is different from the previous program, stating 
he is on a  drought monitoring committee and so far the drought cond itions have actually worsened and 
that in 2006 there was a drought declaration.  Mr. Norton stated  the Governor’s Office is currently 
working on a declaration.  Supervisor Deal stated he appreciated this information and inquired about 
assistance for the repair of wi ndmills providing water for livestock . Mr. Norton stated there is some 
assistance under one program, but it must be directly related to drought.   He added the Natural Resource 
Conservation Service has an assistance program and there may be some new programs  of which he is 
not yet aware.  Supervisor Deal said he hopes there is some relief because in some areas the animals are 
trying to get water which causes damages to th e facility  and that  it is the same with families ; that 
sometimes the facility is sabotaged because of frustration.  Mr. Norton said  there are some programs  
where they can cost share on drilling new wells and deepening wells.  He said he will check with NRCS 
to see if they have any assistance  available.  Supervisor DeSpain said it was his experience that most 
programs are geared to large operations  with no  opportunity to apply for smaller grants  and that  
assistance is needed for the mini grants  to help the small ranchers and farmers.  Mr. Norton stated the 
1995 farm bill changed to the total operation conservation plan, but he knows there are some mini grants 
and he will check with NRCS to see what might be available.  Supervisor DeSpain stated that  when 
there is a drought situation and overgrazing, there is an immediate need for rotation and water 
production.  Supervisor Thompson reported his concern is that it may take several months to go through 
the grant process, asking  if there is any immediate relief av ailable.  Mr. Norton said that with the  
emergency conservation program, the funding is such that may have to wait.  He indicated he is h oping 
that livestock water funding will be available sooner.  The board thanked Mr. Campbell and Mr. Norton 
for attending and sharing information.
