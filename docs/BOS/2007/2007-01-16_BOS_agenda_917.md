featured the posse and their ride.  He said the article generated tremendous public interest; they receive  hundreds of 
letters and emails  a day and money is sent.   The Hashknife Posse dinner will be held  Tuesday, January 30 th at the 
Holbrook Elks Lodge .  Jack Young  thanked the board for allowing the  posse to address them , stating several 
members in their 60’s and 70’s are still riding.  He advised that an important part of the posse is the search and rescue 
portion.  Supervisors thanked the posse for their presentation.

SHERIFF’S OFFICE: Consideration and possible approval of Sheriff’s Office proposal for distribution of Arizona 
Criminal Justice Commission grant funds of $92 ,475.00: Deputy Commander Clark reviewed the ACJC budget as 
presented to the board.  He distributed information regarding the Partnership for a Drug Free America, discussing the 
comprehensive approach the program is taking.  He discussed educational events  at various sites across the county.   
Officer Bill Murray discussed the canine program as related to drug related stops and arrests, providing statistics 
regarding stops, arrests and drug and cash seizures.  Commander Clark displayed video clips from the P artnership for 
a Drug Free America.  He explained this program has the same basic concepts as the Montana Meth project and it 
will be here specifically for Navajo County.  He said the ACJC supports the Partnership for a Drug  
Free America.  He advised that the task force has made several educational presentations and over 3,000 arrests in the 
last 13 years.  He said they started Drug Court and are active in the Community Coalition.  He discussed prior grants 
received as well as funding level cuts.  Mel Bowe rs stated that he echoes what Commander Clark has said and 
supports the Partnership for a Drug Free America.  He advised funding was cut through the Byrne Grant process and 
he hopes to make that up through Enhancement and Fill the Gap funds.  He discussed Drug Court and Juvenile Drug 
Court, stating he a ppreciates consideration of directing some of these funds to  the County Attorney’s office.  James 
Jayne recognized Kirk Grugel and Debe Campbell, Coordinator of the Coalition for the county, for their participation 
in education al opportunities around the county. Supervisor Thompson  stated he appreciates Mr. Bowers and 
Commander Clark going up to Hardrock when they brought the dog out , stating that two to three weeks later the dog 
appeared in paper and that is creating a positive relationship on reservation.   Commander Clark said that after they 
did the presentation at Hardrock, Wal-Mart donated a system that they can take into the field that runs off of a 
generator for presentations where there is no  electricity.  Mel Bowers advised there has been a cultural shift, as up 
until a few years ago law enforcement ’s job was to arrest and prosecute  but now they are  trying to address crime 
through treatment, drug court and education.  He said they have not been winning the war against drugs but now they 
understand the three pronged approach. Supervisor Brownlow stated he supports this breakdown of funding , 
recognizing that the  Drug Free America Partnership is a good program but that he would also like to support the 
Montana Meth Project.  He stated Supervisors have seen presentations on this program and lean toward the Montana 
Project because it targets the younger age group.  Commander Clark and Chief Eavenson responded to questions 
regarding confidential funds and the di gital camera used for covert operations. Supervisor Deal inquired if the Tribes 
received funding in addition to the county funding  and Commander Clark said he would try to obtain that 
information.  Supervisor Deal requested a copy of the letter sent to the  schools. He inquired if this project will be  
reservation inclusive and Commander Clark said they have worked on the reservation and are planning a project now.  
Supervisor Deal announced they are putting together a meth conference on February 14 & 15  and they want a section 
for the children in the  schools around the Pinon community. He said they are i nvolving Navajo County in planning 
for the conference , he would enjoy meeting with Kirk Grugel and Debbie Campbell  and he is i nviting the Navajo 
County S heriff to have someone attend the planning meeting every Wednesday for this conference. Commander 
Clark stated he has a presentation that can run 2-4 hours that is very well received and he would be honored to be part 
of that.  Chief Eavenson advised that the st ate received $3,000,000 from ACJC  and each  county received a base 
allotment of $50,000 , then they used a percentage formula based on population to provide an a dditional allotment.  
He said the l argest portion went to Maricopa County, the smallest to Greenlee and n othing was listed for tribal
