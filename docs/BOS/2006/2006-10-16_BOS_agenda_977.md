NAVAJO COUNTY BOARD OF SUPERVISORS MINUTES 
MONDAY, OCTOBER 16, 2006 
 
At 9:28 a.m. Chairman Thompson called the meeting to order :  Vice- chairman Percy Deal led the Pledge of 
Allegiance and offered the invocation. 
 
PRESENT: Jesse Thompson, Chairman; Percy Deal, Vice-C hairman; David Tenney, Member; James G. 
Jayne, County Manager, and Darlene Fraley, Clerk of the Board.  J.R. DeSpain, Member, and Jerry 
Brownlow, Member, not present. 
 
Chairman Jesse Thompson introduced the board members.  Blaine Baker, Director of the Kayenta 
Unified School District Human Resources, welcomed th e elected officials and representatives of Navajo 
County.  Chairman Thompson thanked Mr. Baker fo r hosting this occasion and recognized audience 
members requesting self-introductions : Dr. Kartchner, County Direct or of Health; Judge Tom Wing, 
Superior Court; Mary Sp ringer, County Purchasing and Finance;  Dusty Parsons, County Public Works 
Director; Judge Evelyn Bradley, Justice of the Peace; Lillian Smith, community member; Juanita Mann, 
County Clerk of the Superior Court; Laurie Justman, County Recorder; Cammy Darris, County 
Assessor, Mary Fontes, HAVA business analyst representing the Secretary of State; Manny Hernandez, 
County Treasurer; Linda Morrow, County School Superintendent, Leo Sheppar d, County transportation 
liaison servicing District I and 2; Daniel Peaches, Kayenta Township and previous legislator; Alfred 
Yazzie, Kayenta Chapter; Helen Bonnaha, Kayenta Ch apter and Health Board for seven chapters plus

County Manager, Chairman Thompson, Vice-Chair man Deal and Supervisor Tenney expressed

Internet auction information:  Mary Springer reported the internet auction for seized and forfeited items 
by the County Sheriff and County Attorney, including several vehicles, was launched this morning.  She 
provided the internet address and the procedures to submit bids stating the auction will remain open to 
the public for two weeks. In response to a ques tion from Helen Bonnaha, Ms. Springer advised that 
following the auction a clear title will be issued for vehicles purchased.  In response to a question from 
Judge Wing, Ms. Springer advised that  the successful bidder has ten days to pick up their purchase, 
paying with a cashier’s check, cash or money order and if the item is not picked up, it will go to the next 
bidder. 
 
Proposition 400 presentation:   County Manager James G. Jayne ex pressed appreciation to Supervisor 
Deal for the opportunity to provide  information regarding Proposition 400.  He conducted a PowerPoint 
presentation that outlined general fund expenditures;  general fund revenues; limitations to expenditures 
as related to the constitutional limit;  expenditure limit definition; expenditure limit history; that Navajo 
County is the 3 rd lowest in per capita on expenditures for basic services;  problems that include public 
safety, transportation issues, access to  services, quality work force; a proposal to take this issue to a 
public vote on November 7 to request a general election increase through Proposition 400; a “yes” vote 
will not authorize any new taxes and will allow existing revenues to  be spent on improved county 
services addressing count y priorities, increased law enforcemen t coverage, additional investment in 
transportation infrastructure, more accessible government services, and reduced operational and training 
costs to make a more effective and efficient workfo rce; that the issue will not authorize any additional 
taxes; that if the measure passes it will provide an avenue for funding to invest additional resources in 
county transportation, public safety and accessible count y offices.  All supervisors spoke to this issue, 
stating they would like to see incr eased services for all county resi dents to include the four areas

Helen Bonnaha discussed a recent li ghtning storm where lightning struck a trailer and it burned to the

locate road yards around the reservati on and he is working with the BIA to gravel the road near Jeddito.

Unified School District, additional taxing entities received some of  that funding and a small portion

Local Government:  Kayenta Township Commission:  Daniel Peaches, Township Manager, thanked the 
supervisors for attending.  He spoke for an exte nded period of time, discus sing road project needs 
advising the Township does not have the funding to pay for engineering and studies; the Nation’s EPA 
requires upfront payment that is expensive and then if payment is made, their schedule is full.  He 
discussed collaboration between agencies to fund pr ojects and the need for a vehicle license fee in 
Kayenta.  He stated the airport is not sufficient a nd described related difficulties, stating they need a 
regional airport for transportation and medical needs.  He discussed the need for planners to determine 
their future; necessary infrastructure such as water and sewage lines to meet growing community needs, 
the movement away from traditional ways in the tribe, pressure on students to get good grades, the 
drought, the need for a flood manage ment study, and the desire to have community college services 
here. 
 
Early voting information : Recorder Laurette Justman thanked t hose present to address the board and 
county staff.  She advised that an ea rly voting site is set up at this school district today for Kayenta, 
Shonto and Chilchinbeto.  She discu ssed voter registration requirements,  election time lines and that 
those that vote early will not have to go through the ID at the polls process.
