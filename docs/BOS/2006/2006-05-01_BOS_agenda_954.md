BOARD OF SUPERVISORS:  Environmental and economic impact of Cholla Power Plant improvements:  Supervisor DeSpain 
introduced Conrad Spencer, Cholla Power Plant Manager, who discussed the history of the APS Cholla Power Plant in Navajo 
County and its current and future impact on the Navajo County economy.  Mr. Spencer introduced Rick Taylor of APS.  Mr. 
Spencer reviewed planned plant upgrades, explaining that when the upgrades are complete the plant’s emission control standards 
will exceed proposed regulatory standards. He reported the plant entered into a long-term coal supply agreement with Peabody 
Coal with the El Segundo mine as the future primary fuel source. He advised that AMEC and Zachary Construction will be the 
construction companies with Zachary Construction doing some local hiring.  He stated the average construction labor force will 
be 100, will peak in 2007 to approximately 250, then taper off to a final count of 30 new permanent employees.  Chairman 
Thompson and Supervisor Deal inquired about  the possibility of a future coal contract with Peabody at Black Mesa and Mr. 
Spencer advised that it doesn’t appear to be economically feasible as it would cost about $400,000,000 to put in a railway to 
transport the coal. Supervisor DeSpain advised that transportation needs for the plant are being reviewed with Public Works staff 
especially as related to the freeway interchange at Love’s Travel Center at Joseph City.  In discussion of increased rail traffic, 
Mr. Spencer stated that the rail site will be expanded to accommodate longer trains. Chairman Thompson and Supervisors 
expressed the Board’s appreciation for this presentation and to APS for their visionary emission control plan.

SHERIFF OFFICE EMERGENCY/MANAGEMENT SERVICES:  Discussion and possible approval to implement Open 
Fire Ordinance 01-06 on May 10, 2006:  Larry Dunagan discussed the prohibited activities under the ordinance as previously 
adopted.  He advised that this request is for the implementation of Section 6 of the ordinance, exempting Section 7.  He reviewed 
the letter from the Fire Chiefs’ Association requesting the enactment of the ordinance on May 10.  Chief Mineer addressed the 
Board on this topic outlining the necessity of having a controlled environment during the upcoming fire season and stating it is 
the consensus of all fire chiefs to enact the ordinance.  Discussion of this topic centered on the five day difference between this 
implementation and the Apache Sitgreaves May 15 implementation,  the necessity for extensive public education, a report of 
residences that have already been lost or threatened due to increased fire danger, the risk to all zones as the northern parts of the 
County have dry grasslands and tamarisk, and a no-cost burn permit that can be obtained on a case-by-case basis from the local 
jurisdiction.   Mr. Mineer advised that this was a difficult process because different areas require different strategies yet some 
areas could have controlled burns year-r ound with no problem, so the permit allows necessary flexibility.  Supervisor Deal 
advised that during the education process, it would be appropria te to advise adjoining counties of the implementation of the 
ordinance.  Steve Campbell provided information of where County residents can find breaking news regarding fires and an 
explanation of contributing factors to fire conditions. Supervisor Tenney made a motion to activate the Open Fire Ordinance 
01-06 as presented on May 15, 2006 with a ppropriate public notification and educa tion; motion seconded by Supervisor 
Brownlow; vote unanimous approving the motion.

ARIZONA ASSOCIATION OF COUNTIES (AACo):  AACo staff presentation of legislative update:  Supervisor DeSpain 
introduced the Executive Director of AACo Nicole Strickler, Michael Jeanes, second vice-president and Public Affairs Officer, 
and staff member, Jennifer Sweeney.  Ms. Strickler provided an overview of AACo and its goals.  She discussed educational 
outreach programs through meetings, conferences, publications and research programs, and the AACo website.  She stated they 
promote collaboration for cost savings and increased employ ee benefits and act as a source for benchmarking and Best 
Management practices.  She reviewed policy statements that address county authority, preserving quality in County Services, 
identity theft and public records, methamphetamine abuse and control, and immigration reform and local impact. 
 
Jennifer Sweeney reviewed this session’s legislative packag e that impact Counties.  Extending the Canvass deadline; 
Independent and Youth Poll Workers; Election law revisions (at Department of Justice); GIS Development surcharge; creating 
flexible schedules for Deputies (unsuccessful); Securing funding for Constable Ethics and Training (still going through the 
process); raising maximum salary for Constables (still going through process); competitive bidding for County Bank selection; 
County Treasury procedural improvements; Adjust County Equalization for Jail program (unsuccessful).  She reported there is 
no budget yet but there seems to be agreement on projected revenues.  AACo has not heard anything on the tax cut issue.  She 
discussed SB 1557 that requires hand counts for state-wide elections if the margin of error is greater than the allowable, stating 
that AACO proposed that political parties provide three people to assist in this process and requiring the Superior Court to 
appoint a Master of Review of source code on the electronic tabulating machine.   She reported strike everything amendments 
have resurfaced and this strike everything brought back the misdemeanour charge for being in Arizona illegally, which shifts the 
issue to local jails. 
 
Nicole Strickler reported both CSA and AACo are actively lobbyi ng the legislature on the danger of tinkering with tax cuts 
involving the property tax rate and/or valuation freezes.  She stated the county equalization rate elimination would impact 
supplemental funding for jail education, juvenile detention, and small schools.   
 
Nicole Strickler advised that at the June conference AACo will present a wrap up of all legislation.  Michael Jeanes, Maricopa 
County Clerk of the Superior Court, discussed future issues that will include Counties submitting endorsed legislative issues by 
September 1st; the AACo membership survey to identify desirable future services, research, training; new cost-savings programs; 
updated and new publications; and continued legislative advocacy. 
 
Supervisor Deal advised that in northern Arizona, the Constable resided 60-70 miles away from the Justice Court in Kayenta and 
requested that reimbursement be addressed. Ms. Strickler stated that during negotiations they are reviewing reimbursement and 
compensation statutes. Supervisor Deal encouraged further communication regarding Native American issues.
