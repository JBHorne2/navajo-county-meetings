ii. Update on conversion of Flood Insurance Rate Maps to digital format: Homero Vela reported 
this digital formatting will provide an avenue for multi-layering of maps.  He reviewed the status 
of the project, advising the maps will be released in December of this year.  He advised there will 
be one map for all of Navajo County including the cities and towns and that meetings will be 
held with all governmental entities to explain th e process.  He said the process will take 
approximately one year and will culminate in the adoption of the flood control maps.  He 
discussed outreach activities that include newspaper publication, cities and towns, and meetings 
with community residents.    Supervisor DeSpain inquired if there are other levees that must be 
re-certified.  Mr. Vela stated that certain levees, such as for the railroad, will not be considered 
unless they have been certified; they are talking about 20 structures.  Mr. Vela, in response to a 
question from Supervisor Deal, advised that FEMA expects Navajo County to bear the cost of 
outreach activities and the appeal, and does not believe funds will be available from FEMA for 
the implementation. Supervisor Deal requested that Mr. Vela explore the possibility of 
connecting FEMA with reservation staff to have such designations on the reservation.  Chairman 
Thompson stated he would be more interested in learning more about reservation flood plain 
maps if they exist and Mr. Vela advised he would investigate this. 
Supervisor Brownlow made a motion to return to regular session;  motion seconded by Supervisor
