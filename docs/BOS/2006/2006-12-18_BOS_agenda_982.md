NAVAJO COUNTY BOARD OF SUPERVISORS MINUTES 
MONDAY, December 18, 2006 
     
9:03 a.m.  CALL TO ORDER: Supervisor Thompson led the Pledge of allegiance and offered the invocation. 
 
PRESENT:  Jesse Thompson, Chairman; Percy Deal, Vice- Chairman; J.R. DeSpain, Member; David Tenney, 
Member; Jerry Brownlow, Member; Mel Bowers, County Attorney; James G. Jayne, County Manager, and Darlene

INDUCTION TO OFFICE CEREMONY FOR ELECTED OFFICIALS:  The Honorable Dale Nielson, 
Presiding Judge, administered the Oath of Office to the recently elected officials:  Superintendent of School Linda 
Morrow; Clerk of the Superior Cour t Juanita Mann; Justices of the Peace Ralph Hatch, Alison Kolomitz, Fred 
Peterson, Evelyne Bradley, Steve Price and David Widmaier; Constables  Madeline Pauline Hernandez, Ray Brown 
and Ryan Reinhold.  Juanita Mann thanked everyone present, expressing appreciation to the board, elected officials,

CONSTABLES: Consideration and possible approval to establish a writ fee for all writs issued by Justice Courts 
pursuant to ARS §22-136 (F) and §11-445 (A) (17): Constable Ryan Reinhold discussed the implementation of the 
statute that allows writ fees to be charged, stating revenue generated by these fees can be used to offset charges for 
mandated constable training and the Constable Ethics Comm ittee.  He requested that a public hearing be set for 
January 16th to consider adopting a $5.00 fee for each writ issued and direction was given to staff to schedule this 
hearing. 
 
PETRIFIED FOREST NATIONAL PARK:  Consideration and possible approval  of letter of support to establish 
the Petrified Forest National Park as a World Heritage Property:  Superintendent Lee Baiza of the Petrified Forest 
National Park discussed the intent of the Park to nominate that entity to be designated as a World Heritage Property. 
He discussed the history of the program as being intr oduced in 1973 to present the day when there are 812 sites 
world wide.  He reported there are 20 national parks designated World Heritage sites and that this program promotes 
these sites internationally to benefit academia, research, and preservation of the site.  Supervisor DeSpain thanked 
Mr. Baiza for providing this information and for his service at the park and intensely promoting it.  Supervisor 
DeSpain made a motion to approve a letter of support to nominate the Petrified Forest National Park as a World 
Heritage Property; motion seconded by Supervisor Br ownlow.  Supervisor Deal inquired why only the 93,000 
original acres were included and Mr. Baiza stated that only 160 acres of the additional authorized lands have been 
acquired.  In response to a question from Supervisor Deal regarding whether letters of support had been obtained 
from the Navajo Nation, Mr. Baiza reported he did not yet have a letter of support and listed the agencies who have 
already offered support.  Chairman Thompson requested that informational booklets be provided to the schools 
regarding the Petrified Forest.  Mel Bowers inquired how many research projects are going on at the Park and Mr. 
Baiza stated that they constantly get applications from researchers who want to conduct research within the park and

CHARACTER FIRST PRESENTATION:  James G. Jayne and Shalice C ook discussed the Character First 
program, providing information that this is an internationally known program that has been implemented in schools, 
cities, and businesses.  Mr. Jayne advised that Kent Fahrenbruck had conducted a two day training of several staff 
members and had also presented a brief overview of the program to department directors and elected officials.  He 
said he anticipates intensive training of key personnel in the county.  Shalice Cook advised the program positively 
impacts the work environment through the reinforcement of 49 character qualities, discussing how this program can 
benefit the County by:  helping to address workforce challenges such as absenteeism, low productivity, and quality 
problems; improving working relationships; and facilitating better communication.  She specifically addressed the 
quality of sensitivity, the December character trait. Ms. Cook discussed ways that the future training will be 
implemented in the county.  Mel Bowers stated that in the presentation he attended it struck him that by teaching 
these qualities you develop a common language in the office environment.  He stated he was favorably impressed 
with the program.  Supervisor Tenney stated that he would like to see questions on job interviews developed around 
these character traits.  The audience viewed a video of the December character trait Sensitivity.
