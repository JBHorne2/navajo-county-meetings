d. Consideration and possible approval by Resolution No. ___-06 accepting Cherry Hills Drive, Meteor Drive, 
Continental Drive, Mercury Lane, Wellfield Roa d, and South Sagebrush Drive as County Highways : Carol

e. Consideration and possible approval of acceptance of road easement on Territorial Road from the Hopi tribe, for 
the purpose of construction, operation and maintenance of a public roadway: Carol Fraley advised this easement 
has been offered by the Hopi Tribe for the board’s acceptance.  Supervisor Brownlow inquired if there were 
other easement issues and Carol Fraley advised that in 1896 the road was accepted without deed action.  Dusty 
Parsons advised they have a verbal agreement on the other easements with Aztec and will complete that written 
process.  He advised they started with the section around the bridge to begin the rehabilitation efforts.  
Supervisor DeSpain reported the county has maintained this right of way for many years.  Mr. Parsons advised 
that the county had an enhancement grant of $500,000 and bids came in at $1.2 million so they are currently re-
evaluating the project and also applying for additional grant money.  Supervisor DeSpain made a motion to 
approve acceptance of the road easement on Territorial  Road from the Hopi Tribe for the purpose of 
construction, operation and maintenance of a public roadway; motion seconded by Supervisor Brownlow.  
Supervisor Deal asked where the referenced ranch is lo cated and where the road leads.  Supervisor DeSpain 
stated that for over 100 years this bridge has been in place on Territorial Road which was considered part of the 
main route going to the coast until Route 66 was built.   Vote unanimous approving the motion.

JUVENILE PROBATION:  Consideration and possible approval to hire a former probation officer with salary 
range to reflect applicant’s training and experience:  Arno Hall advised that this former employee has outstanding 
experience and the recommended salary would be a conti nuation of what she was rece iving when she left the 
county. He stated they spoke with administration management and justified this salary.  Chairman Thompson 
clarified that the salary would be funded by 75% budget and 25% Diversion Intake funds.  Supervisor DeSpain 
made a motion to approve the employment of a probation office at a 96% rate to reflect applicant’s training and 
experience; motion seconded by Supervisor Brownlow.  Supervisor Deal inquired how long the position has been 
open and Arno Hall stated it has been open six months due to an officer’s illness with the position filled by other 
officers and safe schools’ officers and they have now run out of resources to fill the position.  He stated the person 
they would like to bring in is already trained and there would be a savings in that area to the county; that the 
position was recently advertised and there were three applicants with this person being the most qualified.  
Supervisor Deal inquired why this one is singled out for board action and Mr. Hall responded it was due to the 
recommendation for employment at the 96% level. Vote unanimous approving the motion.

BOARD BUSINESS: 
Supervisor Brownlow reported four supervisors were in Prescott for the CSA legislative summit.  He thanked the 
chairman for the meeting held on the Hopi Reservation that provided a good discussion forum.  Supervisor DeSpain 
thanked Representative Brown for his representation of the rural and small counties, stating he is pleased to be on 
the Governor’s SWAG committee for water and appreciates the Brown law firm guidance on this critical issue to 
create a plan for water use.  He thanked David Newlin for his efforts on getting the detox center. 
 
Chairman Thompson acknowledged the presence of Representatives Jack Brown and Ann Kirkpatrick, Attorney 
Brown, City Manager David Newlin, and City Councilmen Spike Simmons and Charlie Houseman. He advised that 
the supervisors have met with the Hopi Tribe and will meet with the Navajo Tribe this month and later the White 
Mountain Apache Tribe.  He encouraged the public to  learn more about Propositi on 400.  Supervisor Deal 
welcomed all those present.  He stated he enjoyed the Hopi Tribal meeting and appreciates the County Manager’s 
assistance in setting up meetings outside these various communities.   
 
LEGISLATIVE UPDATES: Comments and Legislative updates by attending legislators: 
Representative Jack Brown, Representative Ann Kirkpa trick and Senator Jake Flake addressed the Board, 
expressing appreciation for the board’s support.  They provided information about themselves and their interaction 
with other legislators on behalf of th e rural counties.  They discussed the need for the recently approved detox 
centers.  Supervisors expressed appreciation for the effo rts of these legislators on behalf of Navajo County.  
Supervisor Deal thanked the legislators for their representation of the tribes and their support of the legislation to 
return shared revenues to the tribes and urged their continued support when it is re-introduced.  He thanked 
Representative Kirkpatrick for her recognition of the me thamphetamine problem on and off the reservation and 
requested the legislators’ continued support in that area.   
 
PRESENTATION:  Presentation by the Honorable Janet Napolitano, Governor of Arizona:  Governor Napolitano 
invited Director Owens, Representative Brown, Senator Fl ake, and Representative Kirkpatrick to join her in 
presenting a $35,000 check from WIFA to the residents of  the Fawnbrook Wastewater Improvement District, a 
district that serves residents in Show Low, Lakeside and the county.  Governor Napolitano discussed projects that 
are ongoing that will enhance water resources in rural Arizona.  Chairman Thompson expressed appreciation to the 
Governor for Summer Youth Employment, Senior Centers and many other projects including the return of HURF 
dollars to the county.  Supervisor DeSpain thanked the Governor for Director Owens’ hard work on behalf of this 
county, stating that 21 orphan tanks have been removed in Holbrook at no cost to the City of Holbrook.  He 
expressed appreciation that the Governor’s staff worked with AACo on integrating records so county treasurers can 
provide better services to our residents.  Supervisor Brownlow thanked the Governor for coming to Navajo County 
and for her leadership on the budget.  He expressed appreciation for the Governor’s staff including Brent Brown 
who acts on rural concerns.  Governor Napolitano stated that Brent Brown has been invaluable on issues related to 
rural areas.  Supervisor Deal expressed appreciation to the Governor for travelling throughout state and particularly 
Navajo County, stating she has been on the reservations  on numerous occasions.  He expressed appreciation for 
funding to communities and chapters directly to address the drug problems.  He stated the system to work directly 
with rural communities is outstanding and he commended her on that.  He congratulated her on getting the Navajo 
Nation endorsement because she understands tribal issu es and addresses those issues.  Governor Napolitano 
reported they are establishing local amphetamine prevention centers including sites on the reservations and they 
secured additional funding during the budget process this year for meth prevention, interdiction and treatment.
