BOARD OF SUPERVISORS:  Consideration and possible action related to emergency response to BIA route 
issues: Chairman Thompson advised that the recent major rainfall caught the reservation off-guard.  Supervisor Deal 
expressed appreciation to Dusty Parsons and James Jayne regarding their response to the emergencies in Blue Gap, 
Low Mountain; and Whippoorwill areas and in the Pinon Unified School District.  He advised that some roads have 
gone beyond repair.  He stated the purpose of the discussion is to immediately re-open these roads so emergency 
responses can be made for public safety, so that school and public travel will not be interrupted.  He said it is hard to 
justify what has occurred as an emergency since it might not fit the definition yet people in the situation do see it as 
an emergency.  He stated he would like the board to au thorize the county manager and public works director to 
consider these requests without having to enter into emergency sessions of the board.  He advised that the BIA, who 
has primary responsibility, has expended this fiscal year’s resources and they are now utilizing next fiscal year 
personnel account to pay for maintenance in these areas.  He stated he is not looking for a resolution today but is 
looking for direction of how to resolve this issue without  violating a State statute. Supervisor Deal expressed 
appreciation to Pinon Unified School District for accommodating county staff through use of equipment, housing, 
meals, and guides and also expressed appreciation to Jack Latham of Congressman Renzi’s office.  He introduced 
Evelyn Meadows of Pinon Unified School District and invited her to address the board.  Ms. Meadows conducted a 
power point presentation that depicted resulting damage from recent storms to the roads, washes, culverts and land 
erosion and discussed the related costs including manpower and diesel.  She displayed pictures of buses stuck in the 
mud resulting in children not returning home until late in the night.  She advised that when the roads become 
impassable there is no school.  She displayed pictures of equipment that needs repair and the lack of appropriate 
equipment.   Supervisor Thompson discussed the past agreement with the schools to maintain their roads and how 
they can no longer do so and inquired if the county has a couple of graders available that can be taken to a problem 
area for immediate maintenance.  Mr. Bowers advised that discussion would be limited to those roads that are BIA 
maintained and those that are not and compared this situation to the south end of the county where similar 
restrictions would exist unless there is an immediate da nger to life.  Mr. Bowers stated that only this body can 
declare an emergency and that authority can not be delegated.  In response to a question from Supervisor Deal about 
the school bus route roads, Mr. Bowers advised the county can only expend funds on non-BIA routes in the event of 
a true public emergency.  He stated that numbered roads are eligible for the expenditure of county funds but the 
county can not maintain others except in an emergency to go in and address serious public safety issues.  Chapter 
Officials, Bessie Allen,  Leo Shepherd, and Ms. Meadows reviewed pictures of all the bus routes for Pinon Unified 
School District.  Supervisor Deal advised most culverts pictured are culverts the county has purchased in the past.

PUBLIC WORKS:  :  Lone Pine Dam Bridge Design Concept Report, project update and consultant presentation 
to hear public comments: Dusty Parsons introduced the consultants Karl Obergh, Josh Nies of Ritoch Powell, Dan 
Heller of T.Y.-Lin International, David Phelps of J2 E ngineering, and  Mike Langley of EcoPlan & Associates.  
Karl Obergh reported they have been involved in the Lone Pine Dam Bridge project and the Arizona Department of 
Water Resources indicated that another 100 year event could washout the dam and classified it unsafe.  He stated 
that Southwest Traffic is conducting the traffic impact analysis and upon completion of all studies, a 400’ bridge 
will be constructed to the south over Show Low Creek in addition to approximately 0.6 miles of new roadway with 
a total budget of $5.5 million.  He stated they are currently in Design Concept Report and Environmental Design 
Report stage and reviewed the Design Concept Report process advising the goal is to minimize the length and width 
of bridge to minimize expense.  He displayed a digital rendering of the project, discussing criteria used to arrive at 
this rendering. He reported they are ahead of the original DCR schedule with the original contract time 300 days.  
He stated that two public meetings are required by FHWA; one prior to the initial design concept report and another 
prior to final design concept report.  In response to a question from Supervisor DeSpain, Dusty Parsons advised they 
are still working on the traffic impact analysis but his estimate would be over 500 vehicles per day use the bridge.  
Karl Obergh stated they must look at 25 years down the ro ad when this could be used as a bypass form SR 77.  
Supervisor Tenney inquired when three alignments would become the recommended choice.  Karl Obergh stated 
they designed alignments according to ADOT requirements and in the DCR process they need a minimum of three 
considering feasibility, constructability and the ability to  be maintained.  Supervisor Tenney inquired if an 
alignment chosen is the one Pete Shumway wanted and was told that was correct.  Supervisor Tenney requested that 
gratitude be conveyed to Congressman Renzi for his help on this project as it would not have happened without his 
support.  Dusty Parsons advised they will have a gr ound breaking and dedication ceremony and will invite the 
Congressman to the ceremonies.  Chairman Thompson invited public comment and there was none.
