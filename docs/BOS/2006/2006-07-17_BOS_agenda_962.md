a. 9:00 PUBLIC HEARING for comments as related to the possible increase in the Navajo County expenditure 
(spending) limit: James Menlove advised the board there are considerable challenges in meeting basic services 
in the county and staying within the existing expenditure limitation.  He conducted a PowerPoint presentation 
that outlined general fund expenditures; general fund re venues; limitations to expenditures as related to the 
constitutional limit;  expenditure limit definition; expenditure limit history; that Navajo County is the 3rd lowest 
in per capita on expenditures for basic services;  probl ems that include public safety, transportation issues, 
access to services, quality work force; a proposal to take this issue to a public vote on November 7 to request a 
general election increase through Proposition 400; a “yes” vote will not authorize any new taxes and will allow 
existing revenues to be spent on improved county serv ices addressing county priorities, increased law 
enforcement coverage, additional investment in tran sportation infrastructure, more accessible government 
services, and reduced operational and training costs to make a more effective and efficient workforce; that the 
issue will not authorize any additional taxes; that if the measure passes it will provide an avenue for funding to 
invest additional resources in county transportation, pub lic safety and accessible county offices.  Supervisor 
Deal pointed out that there are no deputies on the reservation and requested that this information be reflected in 
the statistics listed.  Mr. Menlove advised that if the Board of Supervisors calls for an election, further public 
presentations will be made.  Supervisors discussed the services that the county would like to provide but has 
been unable to do so because of the expenditure limit.   Mel Bowers inquired wh ere Navajo County was in 
relation to other counties when tax rates were frozen in 1980.  Mr. Menlove advised that the relationship was 
similar and that several small counties are now at thei r expenditure limit.  He discussed mandated costs that 
negatively impact the budget.  Mel Bowers advised that to take this issue to the pubic, it would require that four 
of the five supervisors vote in favor of the issue.  Mr. Menlove advised another public hearing will be held in 
Show Low tomorrow evening on this issue and an acti on item will be brought to the board. Supervisor Deal 
expressed concern that it was inconvenient that the pl ace to vote was being taken further from his area and

PROBATION:  Consideration and possible approval of creation of a Drug Court Probation Officer position: James 
Menlove advised this is a request for a probation officer to serve the Drug Court and it will be funded through two 
grant sources. He reported this item was included in the tentative budget.  Supervisor Tenney made a motion to 
approve the creation of a Drug Court Probation Officer position; motion seconded by Supervisor Brownlow.  In 
response to a question, Mr. Bowers stated the program has been successful to-date and he supports this proposal.   
Mr. Bowers described the Drug Court process, stating that those who violated intensive probation can participate 
when otherwise the next step would be prison.  He said if they successfully complete drug court, the violation of 
probation will be dismissed and the client will be terminated from probation.  He said if the client is not successful, 
they go back to court for sentencing. He reported to this point no one has failed the program and many have used 
drugs for majority of their adult life.  He reported on two instances where there were mild disruptions, stating that 
since this is the toughest target, he has been pleasantly surprised.  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
