CALL TO THE PUBLIC:  Jim Ryff, Chairman of the Heber Overgaard Fact Finding Committee,

lobbied the board for over a year but they are self appointed and do not represent the whole community.

vote.

EMERGENCY MANAGEMENT:  Larry Dunagan, Steve Campbell:  Public Hearing:  Discussion and 
possible approval of County Ordinance 01-06 (Outdoor Fire Ordinance) with repeal of County Ordinance 
01-02 (Open Outdoor Fire Ordinance):  Chairman Thompson advised those present that this is a public 
hearing and invited Mr. Dunagan to address the Boar d. Mr. Dunagan advised that Lance Payette of the 
County attorney’s office drafted and approved the proposed fire ordinance.  He discussed the fire zones 
within the County, boundaries of such, the proposed fire restrictions and that violation of these restrictions 
will include a criminal penalty.  Steve Campbell of Navajo County Cooperative Extension stated that there 
is a Community Wildfire Protection Plan(CWPP) throughout Zone 3 that will help with enforcement and 
may facilitate grant awards.  He said the next step would be a plan for Zone 1 and 2 and there will be a 
four hour retreat to address this with the Board and key figures in county invited.  There was no public 
comment.  The Board and Mr. Dunagan discussed the ordinance making the following points:   
• Energy Release Components determine the danger level. 
• The Forest Service may not impose restrictions as soon as the County would due to their mandate 
to keep the forest open as much as possible for recreation. 
• The communities are currently working on their ordinances and in an effort to limit confusion it is 
desirable to have the County and communities to have mirror ordinances. 
• If a local (County) fire chief moves the pointer up higher than has been designated by the County, 
it is not enforceable. 
• No permit fee has been set. 
• It would take Board action to put restrictions into effect and those restrictions would stay in effect 
until the danger passes.  In the event of an emergency, the Board Chairman can declare an 
emergency and then put the fire restrictions into effect.   
• Notifications of restrictions would go out th rough local media, TV, radio, newspaper, the 593 
network and the local number 928-594-4780 or 4792. 
Supervisor DeSpain made a motion to repeal Ordinance 01-02 (Open Outdoor Fire Ordinance; motion 
seconded by Supervisor Tenney; vote unanimous approving the motion.  Supervisor Brownlow made a 
motion to adopt County Ordinance 01-06 (Outdoor Fire Ordinance); Supervisor DeSpain seconded the 
motion.  During discussion Supervisor DeSpain reque sted fees and notification be drafted.  Vote 
unanimous approving the motion.

requests regarding political activity.  The current policy allows an employee to continue employment while 
running for an office and the proposed policy does not  allow continued employment.  She reviewed 
personnel-requested changes, inclusion of policies that the Board had passed since the last personnel 
policy revision, statutory changes that impact policy, formatting changes to make the document clearer as 
well as clarification of specific processes that were incorporated into this document. She advised that the

The following addressed this issue as related to  4.11 C in the proposed Personnel Policy manual:  Dan 
Hinz, Chairman of the Personnel Commission, Ma rk Reynolds, Vice-chairman of the Personnel 
Commission, Marlin Gillespie, previous Supervisor, all sitting Supervisors, James Jayne, Gail Calisen,  
Deputy Commander Sutton, Carolyn Castillo, Commander Ebert. In that process the following points were 
discussed: 
• The Personnel Commission took the position to stay neutral in this process. 
• This issue has come to the Board in the past and action had not been taken because it would appear self 
serving for the policy to require an employee to re sign or take a leave of absence when the sitting 
elected official does not have to do so. 
• If the top administrators in a department are absent from their job to run for an office the County loses 
the benefit of that experience and the County will have to hire new people to fill those spots for what 
may be just a few months. 
• It will create a financial hardship for the employee to resign to run as it is difficult to find short term 
employment within the County. 
• It may come down to the situation that only those who are wealthy or are not County employees will 
be able to run for a County office. 
• Those County employees who are funded by State and Federal funds are governed by the Federal 
Hatch Act and Little Hatch Act.  They are required to take a leave of absence or resign to run for an 
office and it seems it would be fairer if all County employees were subject to the same rules. 
• There were several problems with the last election that were not good for the County organization or 
the employees. 
• It would be difficult to campaign against a supervisor in the evenings and on weekends and come back 
to work during the week and have a good working relationship.  Or, a supervisor could assign an 
employee-candidate to a remote post to make it difficult for that person to campaign. 
 
Supervisor Tenney made a motion to the approve the draft of the personnel policies as submitted in their 
entirety; Supervisor Brownlow seconded the motion with the request to include the language where an 
employee could use their annual leave or personal l eave toward the leave of absence and Supervisor 
Tenney agreed to that amendment of his motion. Mr. DeSpain asked for and received confirmation that if a 
person took an approved leave of absence their position would be held.  The vote was 2-2 with Chairman 
Thompson and Supervisor DeSpain voting against the motion. 
 
Supervisor DeSpain requested that this policy be sent back to Human Resources for clarification regarding 
what leave could be taken and to obtain a recommendation from the Personnel Commission.  Mr. Jayne 
emphasized that if we continue with the existing policy, we have two classes of employee—those who can 
continued to be employed while running for an office and a co-worker who is funded differently required 
to resign to run.  Supervisor DeSpain made a motion  to send this issue (4.11 C) to the Personnel 
Commission to bring a recommendation to the next Board of Supervisors meeting; Supervisor Brownlow 
seconded the motion.  The motion carried 3-1 with Supervisor Tenney voting against the motion.
