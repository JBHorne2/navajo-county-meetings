SPECIAL MEETING  
NAVAJO COUNTY BOARD OF SUPERVISORS 
 TUESDAY, SEPTEMBER 26, 2006 
HOPI TRIBAL HEADQUARTERS 
 
The meeting was called to order by the Hopi Tribal Council at 9:00 a.m.    
 
Introductions: Council Members were introduced as well as members of the Navajo County Board of 
Supervisors and staff.  Attending from Navajo County were: Jesse Thompson, Chairman; Percy Deal, Vice 
Chairman; J.R. DeSpain, David Tenney and Jerry Brownlow, Members; James Jayne, County Manager; 
June Foard, Board Secretary. 
 
Proposition 400 presentation:  James G. Jayne presented information regarding proposed Proposition 400 
and how it will affect county residents. He explained that taxes would not be raised. Discussion followed 
with several questions from council members regarding the affect the proposition will have on services 
including Law Enforcement availability and voter registration on the reservation. Mr. Polacca thanked Mr. 
Thompson for the help.  He indicated they were fee ling a financial crunch since the coal mine has shut 
down.   
 
Presentation of voter registratio n and early voting information : Doris Clark reported on several  
problems on the reservation with voter registration and information dissemination.  
 
Voter Identification Requirements:  Bernalda Navasie provided information regarding identification 
requirements at the polls explaining that there were  not many problems.  There was some confusion as 
people did not realize there is a conditional provision that identification can be provided up to 5 days after 
the election. 
 
Discussion of items of mutual interest to the Hopi Tribal Council and Navajo County :  Hopi Tribal 
Council Members and Supervisors discussed the proposed Detox center in Holbrook.  Chairman 
Thompson reported that Supervisor DeSpain and he were attending the meetings regarding the proposed 
center.  Other general discussion was held. It was suggested that more joint meetings be held as they are 
very beneficial. 
 
Adjourn: The Hopi Council recessed for lunch at 12:15 p.m. and the Navajo County delegation departed 
at that time.
