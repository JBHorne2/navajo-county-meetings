2.

Broadband installation and commercial marketing progress
Grant Evans provided an update on the Broadband Installation stating that it is 100% complete. Fiber has been run to all County buildings, and they are in the splicing and testing phase now. Show Low Municipal is building a network, and a connection to the Taylor, AZ airport has been completed.  Navajo County is targeted to take full ownership of the fibers towards the end of the 2025 calendar year, variables such as completing testing and the holidays could push that date into early January 2026.  The grant closeout with the ACA is coming up, Navajo County has received 90% of the grant funds already and the remaining 10% of funds has been submitted for.  Grant stated that the next lifecycle of the project infrastructure would be operation, maintenance and sustainability.  The annual maintenance fee will be approximately $196,000.  He stated that an IRU is currently being negotiated with a local internet provider and is planning to bring it to the board in early 2026, expecting the IRU to be for 4 fibers. He also said that they are actively engaged with Municipal Partners on how they can leverage the network to fit their needs, agreements with municipalities are expected to be executed under IGAs.  Grant stated they have high confidence in BEAD allocations being awarded.  Grant also stated that EX2 has engaged with local wireless providers, local ISPs and RDOF awardees and is in discussions with Tribal Connections, in particular the Navajo Nation as BEAD allotments, and has reached out.  It is estimated that Navajo County has 192 fibers available to market and 96 fibers set aside for Public Safety Partners.  There was some discussion about the fibers expected to be leased out moving slower than anticipated.  It was stated that a variety of factors contributed to this such as a delay in BEAD funds being allocated, and prohibitive pricing. Rochelle LaCapa spoke next about planning celebration in the 1st quarter of 2026.  There was discussion about what kind of celebration would be ideal and guests to invite.

County Complex Maintenance and Future Planning
Bryan Layton led a discussion on modernizing and updating the Board Chambers, Code Talkers Meeting Room, and the Hallway/Foyer in between the 2 rooms.  Some photos of potential design changes were shown and discussed.  He stated that new 85-inch monitors have been purchased to be installed in the Board Chambers that will make it easier for everyone to see the presentations at Board of Supervisors meetings. Discussion centered on emphasizing the history of the area and Code Talkers when revitalizing the spaces. Discussion then moved onto current problems with roof drainpipes, sewer pipes, and roof drains on the flat roof.  The main complex building is 50 years old, and the current roof is original. Grant Evans is looking into options; the entire roof may not need to be replaced but will need repairs, as well as some of the drainpipes which are old and cracking and in need of replacement or relining.
Staff stated the bids have gone out for a new security system, consolidating a system to be used by the main complex and jail. It is estimated that a contract will come before the board in June.

The last item discussed was the conceptual plans for the main complex that was approved last year. The updates are a low priority, but still on the list of things coming up.  It is anticipated that the results of the next census will state that Navajo County will be required to add an additional superior court.

e.

Data Center Industry Inquires / Development Agreements and Impacts
Discussion focused on Navajo County's approach to negotiating and regulating development agreements for renewable projects and preparing for anticipated data center developments.
Staff stated that they are working on their first Development Agreement with a solar industry using a methodical approach, calculating the cost burdens after researching factors such as property taxes, impacts of jobs created and sustained, public health concerns and public safety concerns.
Cody Cooper then discussed the rising interest in data centers coming to Arizona and highlighted the different types of data centers. Cody went over the pros and cons of data centers. Highlighted pros being, economic and fiscal benefits, high property tax, construction jobs upfront, low demand for public services, usage of fiber, potential upgrades to roads, low traffic impact after initial construction, potential for Brownfield redevelopment, no reclamation, and less peak noise than other industrial uses. Some of the challenges discussed were fewer long-term jobs, low wages, risk of overly generous tax incentives, and environmental impacts such as energy usage. The biggest concern is water usage.  Looking forward, it was suggested to explore other cooling technologies when developing agreements. Other impacts discussed were land use conflicts and competition with agriculture.
The planning team expressed that they have been studying a county in Virginia and how they have navigated development agreements for data centers and recommended that Navajo County seek to adopt discretionary rather than by-right permitting. The supervisors asked questions about self-sustaining centers and water usage.  It was suggested that when developing agreements, aquifer studies be conducted and consider setting baseline water usage guidelines.
Discussion moved on to regulatory and legal pathways, clarifying that the county must allow a pathway for such projects but does not have to approve every proposal and reasonable regulatory standards can be enforced. Cody stated that the technologies are rapidly moving and suggested that the county be forward-thinking but flexible, balancing the need for local control, long-term planning, environmental responsibility, and transparency.
