a. Presentation of the Navajo County Rural Diversion Program.
County Attorney Brad Carlyon introduced Erin Escobar, Managed Care
Coordinator for Complete Care Partners, who provides services to those
who participate in the Misdemeanor Diversion Program. He stated that
Erin will give a presentation on the services that Complete Care Partners
provides.  Erin stated that she has managed Complete Care Partners
since their doors opened approximately for 4 ½ years ago and stated that
they have been in Navajo County for 2 ½ years now. She read the
Mission Statement.  “The Navajo County Attorney’s Office has
established a court diversion program to provide an alternative method of
holding low level offenders whose offense often has a nexus to substance
abuse or mental health conditions accountable for violations of the law

<!-- page 11 -->
through participation in an evidence-based education or treatment
program that targets factors related to recidivism. The program effectively
redirects limited criminal justice resources, brings about participants
rehabilitation, and gives rise to safer communities.  The program diverts
criminal cases from formal court proceedings and allows for the dismissal
of charges if the program is successfully completed.”
Erin stated that one of the things that makes the program unique is that it
couples accountability and mental health and other factors that play into
success. They provide very detailed behavioral health screenings and
work with local providers like CBI or Change Point and offer remote
services, providing the technology for those series.  Erin also stated that
they do alcohol and drug testing requiring soberness for the program,
helping participants to get sober through different methods of treatment
and support.  Participants have weekly meetings with their care manager
in person, over the phone, or via Zoom. Local care managers in Winslow
do travel to see their clients. Erin spoke about GPS monitoring and
electronic check ins. She also spoke on how the support groups and
guest speakers provide important encouragement. Erin moved on to
provide program statistics, there have been 165 clients enrolled in the
program and 93 have completed it, giving it a 68% success rate.  Some
clients have returned to the program; she stated that they are always
welcomed back for continuation whether they have previously completed
the program or not. Erin stated that fees for the program are collected on
behalf of the Navajo County Attorney’s Office, however they are based on
a sliding scale using the National Poverty Guidelines additionally, they
have been able to waive fees through the Navajo County Attorney’s
Office.  Erin continued her slide presentation by speaking about some of
the participants who have completed the program.
County Attorney Brad Carlyon mentioned that the Arizona State
Legislature funded this program three years ago for three rural counties.
 He stated that Navajo County has focused on misdemeanants, most of
whom would have been seen in Municipal Court, these are citizens that
would be frequent fliers and Mr. Carlyon stated that they wanted to focus
on this group before they rose to the felony level.  Mr. Carlyon stated that
the program has had a very high success rate because they provide
mental health services, food, and housing. He stated that the program
started in the Winslow Justice Court, Judge Little and Judge Pro-Tem
Penrod, worked out kinks and brought the program county wide.  Mr.
Carlyon mentioned how Jeanie Boyce, who runs the five diversion
programs for Navajo County and Co-Chairs the APAC Diversion Program
got everyone on same page for reporting.  Mr. Carlyon went on to explain
that funding for this program is running out, and he plans on asking the
Arizona State Legislator to continue the funding and asks for the support
of the Navajo County Supervisors as well as the Municipalities.
Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked Attorney Carlyon, his staff, and
Erin for sharing information about the program and thanked Jannie for the
services she provides.  Supervisor Whitesinger stated that she works
with young people and recognizes that this program gives youth and
clients caring relationships to support and help them and get out of these
types of cycles, adding that if we build strong families, we build strong
communities, and in turn a strong nation.
