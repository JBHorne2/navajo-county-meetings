BOARD OF EQUALIZATION :

a.

Discussion and action regarding Petition for Review of Real Property Valuation of APN 411-52-136 (Mulligan); Swearing in of Assessor, Appraisers and Petitioners :
Assessor, Michael Montandon, Laurie Velde and the Petitioner, Tim Mulligan, were sworn in by Melissa Buckley, Clerk of the Board. Ms. Velde provided a packet for the Board.  The petitioner, Tim Mulligan, addressed the Board regarding his opposition to the proposed increase, indicating that it appears excessive given the actual value of their property. He spoke about data from multiple real estate sources, indicating that they all estimate the home's value to be significantly lower than the county's full cash value listed on the 2026 Residential Notice of Value. He spoke about inconsistencies in valuations compared to nearby properties, including one directly across the street. He acknowledged a 33% increase in their home's living space, indicating that their taxes are set to increase to more than double, which he feels is unfair. He indicated that they are willing to pay their fair share but urged the Board to reconsider the amount of the proposed increase.  Ms. Velde stated that the subject property, located in Pinetop Country Club and bordering forest reservation land, underwent a 728-square-foot addition and construction of a 725-square-foot garage in 2023, increasing its livable space by roughly 33%. She indicated that these improvements brought the total living area to 2,184 square feet and updated the home's effective age to 1991. She noted that following inspections in 2023, the county initially assigned the property a full cash value of $622,129 and a limited property value (LPV) of $370,997 for 2026.  She indicated that the property owner appealed, presenting comparable sales data, to argue for a lower valuation, estimating the full cash value at $502,320. She further indicated that the Assessor reviewed the appeal and applied a $20,009 reduction to the improvement value, supported by three sales and two equity comparisons, bringing the revised full cash value to $602,120. Ms. Velde stated that the property's location backing forest land adds value and recommended that the amounts remain as proposed, noting that the LPV is calculated in accordance with A.R.S. § 42-13301, and is not subject to appeal.  Mr. Mulligan addressed the Board regarding an error with the math in the report by Ms. Velde.  A discussion was held regarding the error.  Mr. Mulligan indicated that he doesn't fully understand the county's process and spoke about his observation of the property valuations, based on Zillow and comparable sales in their neighborhood, appearing significantly higher than what neighbors are paying per square foot. Michael Montandon explained that Arizona's current property tax system, established by a 2012 proposition, creates two values: the full cash value, estimated by the assessor, and a limited property value (LPV) set by statute, noting that the LPV increases by 5% annually unless there's a major change like a home addition over 15%, which causes a larger adjustment. He indicated that this is why the neighbor's value is lower, since they didn't have a recent large addition. He indicated that while the assessor tries to be fair with market value estimates, the LPV is legally fixed and cannot be changed at their discretion.
Mr. Mulligan indicated that he understands the legal explanation but expressed hope for some leniency or flexibility in the process, noting that the average homeowner isn't familiar with real estate laws and may feel unfairly impacted. Mr. Carlyon indicated that taxes are based on the limited property value, which increases steadily and is set by statute, noting that even if the market value is adjusted, it won't affect the limited value used to calculate taxes.   Vice-Chair Whitesinger inquired if the discrepancy in the dollar amount would be corrected.  Mr. Montandon indicated that the discrepancy was only on presentation and that it is correct in the system.   Vice-Chair Whitesinger acknowledged Mr. Mulligan's concerns, expressed appreciation for their investment in the community, and emphasized that the county must follow the law in these matters.
Vice-Chair Whitesinger made a motion  to uphold the Assessor's recommendation, motion seconded by  Supervisor Seymore .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to return to regular session, motion seconded by  Supervisor Peshlakai .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

* PUBLIC HEARING:  Convene a Special Meeting of the Navajo County Board of Supervisors: FINAL 2024-25 BUDGET PURSUANT TO A.R.S. §42-17104 : Consideration and possible adoption of  Resolution Number 23-2025 , approving the proposed final budget for fiscal year 2025-26 for Navajo County :
A presentation was shown.  Jayson Vowell stated that the proposed final budget remains unchanged from the preliminary version presented in July. He indicated that the county's property tax rate will remain flat, with a continued focus on maintaining essential services and investing in regional projects such as broadband infrastructure, the Winslow levee, and the Taylor medical examiner's office. He spoke about the proposed adjustments to employee compensation, including a 2.5% salary increase, the introduction of sabbatical leave, and a health incentive program.  He stated that the general fund budget is set at $68.4 million, with $50.7 million allocated to departmental expenditures across public safety, criminal justice, mandated services, and administration, and an additional $17.7 million is reserved for contingencies. He spoke about special revenue funds which support specific restricted-use programs such as health services, public works, and ARPA-funded initiatives, totaling $140.6 million.  He provided an update on the Jail District, noting that since 2020, the district has relied on a dedicated sales tax, which is projected to generate $6.9 million in fiscal year 2025. He indicated that fund balances have declined due to higher expenses, especially following the county's transition to a third-party medical provider for inmate healthcare in 2022, noting that the change was necessary due to insurance limitations and rising liability concerns. He indicated that, although medical costs remain high, grant funding from the Arizona Border Security program and general fund transfers have helped stabilize the budget. He noted that inmate housing revenue is expected to provide further support in the upcoming fiscal year.  He spoke about the debt service and capital project budgets for fiscal year 2025–2026, highlighting strategic financial decisions and long-term savings. He indicated that the debt service funds total $2.3 million, with two major components, a 2021 pension obligation bond and a refinancing of earlier facility improvement bonds, noting that the pension debt restructuring has already saved $628,000 and is projected to save over $11 million by 2038 and that the facility bond refinancing has yielded $409,000 in savings so far, with total expected savings of about $769,000 through 2031.  He indicated that the county also maintains a capital projects fund of $35.1 million, with approximately $30.5 million being allocated for the Winslow Levee project and additional funds being designated for broadband expansion, the Taylor medical examiner facility, code enforcement, and regional law enforcement communications.
He stated that, in total, the county's budget across all funds includes $68.4 million in the general fund, $140.6 million in special revenue funds, $2.3 million in debt service, and $35.1 million in capital projects, bringing the overall fiscal year 2026 budget to $246.6 million. He noted that this reflects a 3.3% decrease from the prior year, and future budgets are expected to decrease further due to the phasing out of one-time funding sources like CARES and ARPA. Chairman Whiting opened the meeting for public comments.  There being no comments, the public hearing was closed.
Supervisor Seymore indicated that since the jail tax was implemented, jail district spending has increased by about $4 million, largely due to rising medical service costs. He expressed hope for finding ways to reduce those costs, possibly through legislative support. He thanked staff for their work on the budget.  Vice-Chair Whitesinger expressed her appreciation for the collaborative work on the budget, emphasizing the importance of long-term planning to maintain quality public services. She spoke about the complexity of the budgeting process and shared her appreciation for the transparency and responsible use of funds. She thanked all departments involved and highlighted the team's efforts in preparing for the future while maintaining a flat tax rate.  Chairman Whiting thanked all county departments and offices for their collaborative efforts during the lengthy budget process. He acknowledged the Administration team's responsiveness to feedback and praised fellow board members for their serious, thoughtful engagement and commitment to understanding and planning the budget responsibly.
Supervisor Benally made a motion  to adopt  Resolution Number 23-2025 , approving the proposed final budget for fiscal year 2025-26 for Navajo County, motion seconded by  Vice-Chair Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

* Consideration of  Resolution Number 24-2025 authorizing an application for the AZ SMART grant in the amount of $215,000 for the construction phase of Heber Sidewalk Improvements project :
Madhav Mundle stated that the proposed resolution is to authorize the county's application for a SMART grant to cover the local match required for the construction phase of the Heber Sidewalk ImprovementProject in District 4. He indicated that the county previously received a SMART grant for the design phase and has applied for a federal SS4A grant for construction, noting that since SS4A requires a 20% local match, the new SMART grant would help cover that amount. He stated that ADOT now accepts a resolution and RFGA instead of a full IGA to expedite the process. He stated that staff recommend approval.   Supervisor Seymore inquired if the county does not receive the SMART grant to cover the local match if the county would be responsible for paying that amount to proceed with the project.  Mr. Mundle indicated that if the SMART grant isn't awarded, the county is not obligated to pay the match, that the requirement is simply to apply. He further indicated that Public Works has also proactively applied for a TA grant with a lower match rate as a backup plan.
Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to adopt  Resolution Number 24-2025 authorizing an application for the AZ SMART grant in the amount of $215,000 for the construction phase of Heber Sidewalk Improvements project, motion seconded by  Supervisor Peshlakai .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
