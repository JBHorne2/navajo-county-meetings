COUNTY ATTORNEY:

a.

Consideration of a Proclamation recognizing November as Operation Green Light Salute, honoring the men and women who served in the Armed Forces, maintaining the freedom and way of life enjoyed by our community:
County Attorney Brad Carlyon stated that this is the 5th anniversary of the National Association of Counties, project called Operation Green Light.  Operation Green Light is a project to honor and recognize Veterans by lighting up government buildings green, to send a message to veterans that they are seen, appreciated, and supported. Mr. Carlyon stated that too many veterans have fought wars overseas and when they come back, they have changed, usually not in the most positive way, he recognized that there is an extremely high suicide rate among our veterans and a high substance abuse rate as well.  Too often they get involved in our criminal justice system because of mental health and substance issues that come with being on battlefields. Mr. Carlyon stated that in Navajo County we have a robust veterans court to make sure they can access the benefits they have earned and resources the county has, to get them back on the right track and overcome the challenges they have. Mr. Carlyon stated that Navajo County will be hosting an event at the old Navajo County Courthouse, where the courthouse will be lighted green.  Mr. Carlyon expressed his thanks to the Building and Grounds crew that worked to make the event happen he also thanked Alicia Deets, who works to take photos and promote events in Navajo County.  Mr. Carlyon asked that the Navajo County Board of Supervisors on behalf of the County Attorney's Office and all employees of Navajo County adopt the proclamation of Operation Green Light and formally announce to the public and to the veterans of our communities that we are there for them if they ever need help or support.  Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Carlyon for his leadership and thanked all that were involved honoring and recognizing the veterans, he stated that the County Attorney's Office has done a good job of supporting Operation Green Light.
Vice Chairwoman Whitesinger   made a motion  to approve a Proclamation recognizing November as Operation Green Light Salute, honoring the men and women who served in the Armed Forces, maintaining the freedom and way of life enjoyed by our community, motion seconded by  Supervisor Peshlakai .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
Supervisor Seymore commented that veterans are sometimes overlooked and forgotten.  He told of his Aunt who recently passed; her husband was a WWII veteran who received the distinguished flying cross, he was able to see his uncle's journal and letters from WWII, he stated that in the letters he couldn't write down what really happened because he didn't want his wife at home to see, but he was able to record in his journal. He recognized veterans' strength, they have fought and given their all and some have given their life.  He thanked veterans for their service.

BOARD OF SUPERVISORS

a.

Consideration of the County proposal to transfer ownership of the Clay Springs Community Center to the Clay Springs Heritage Foundation (CSHF), a Arizona non-profit, for $1.
County Manager Bryan Layton thanked the board and shared the background of the building, stating that the property known as the Clay Springs Community Center was purchased by Navajo County approximately 10 years ago from the Show Low Unified School District and has been used by the residents of Clay Springs as a gathering place to hold events, community meetings, celebrations and other activities.  He stated that the building is located next to the community playground and acts as a hub for that community.  Mr. Layton explained that at this time, Navajo County leases the building to a non-profit organization, organized by the residents of Clay Springs, called the Clay Spring Heritage Foundation.  This group has requested the opportunity to purchase the building from Navajo County and continue operating it as a community center.  Mr. Layton went on to explain that the Heritage Foundation currently uses proceeds from their thrift store, volunteer support, and other community proceeds to pay for the operation and maintenance of the building. He stated that the building has a lot of history and holds a lot of significance for the residents of Clay Springs, and they are excited to take ownership of the building and continue its operations.  Mr. Layton noted that staff recommends the transfer of the building.
Chairman Whiting thanked the residents of Clay Springs for the good job they do taking care of the building, he acknowledged that the community uses the building frequently as a gathering place and stated that it is an important resource with an interesting history. He stated that Navajo County wants to be good partners and work with citizens. He expressed his thanks everyone who has facilitated this transaction.
Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to approve County proposal to transfer ownership of the Clay Springs Community Center to the Clay Springs Heritage Foundation (CSHF), a Arizona non-profit, for $1, motion seconded by  Vice Chairwoman Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
Eleanor Malcolm, President of the Clay Springs Heritage Foundation thanked the board on the behalf of the Clay Springs Community.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE:
County Attorney Brad Carlyon reported that he had been in the Phoenix area October 9-10, 2025, for the 2050 Criminal Justice Service-Infrastructure Symposium as a presenter and participant. The event was convened by the Arizona Prosecuting Attorney Advisory Council and the Administrative Office of the Courts to look ahead 25 years from now and see what the needs will be for the criminal justice system.  He stated that the purpose is to study the growth in Arizona in our counties and municipalities and to evaluate the needs of the criminal justice system, including law enforcement, courts, prosecutors, and public defenders. This was the second symposium in the ongoing study, and the focus was on the needs of crime labs, jails, and data needs that help project what is working and what is not working to keep people out of the criminal justice cycle. He recalled that Navajo County is one of two counties in Arizona projected to have no or negative growth in the next 25 years so our needs may be somewhat different.  However, Arizona overall is projected to have growth driven by people relocating from other states which means the average age of our population will increase.  He stated that violent crimes may be reduced because statistics say that they are committed by those aged 35 or less. However we may see a significant increase in constituents that are victims of crimes, those that are over 65 tend to be the victims of elder abuse and financial fraud.  It is also projected that there will be more medical examinations upon death, and these factors will change some of the needs of our Criminal Justice System.  Mr. Carylon expressed that he is happy that the state is looking at what the Criminal Justice needs are moving forward.
County Manager Bryan Layton reminded everyone that after the Board of Supervisor Meeting today, Navajo County is hosting the employee Health and Safety Fair which has a focus on wellness, our health plan and the partnerships we have in place for our employees.  He thanked the board who have been extremely supportive of the initiatives as we focus on the health, wellness, and physical safety of Navajo County employees.
Supervisor Seymore spoke on the recent death of the President of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints, a world-renowned heart surgeon, 101 at his death. He helped develop open heart surgery and performed the first open heart surgery in Utah.  Supervisor Seymore recommended the book "The Heart of the Matter". He reminded everyone to take care of their heart physically and spiritually and wished that we may share love and kindness.
