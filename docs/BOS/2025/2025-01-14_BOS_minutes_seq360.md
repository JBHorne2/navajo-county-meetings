PLANNING & DEVELOPMENT SERVICES :

a.

PUBLIC HEARING:  Consideration and possible adoption of  Resolution Number 01-2025 , approving/denying an update to the Navajo County Comprehensive Plan :
Cody Cooper provided clarification on the Navajo County Comprehensive Plan update, explaining that the plan is not a regulatory document but rather a guiding one. He indicated that it serves as a foundation for future amendments and the adoption of regulatory documents, including those related to renewable energy ordinances. He emphasized that while the plan provides a pathway for these changes, it does not directly implement regulations.
A presentation was shown. Terry Hogan, Consultant from Logan Simpson Design, shared her appreciation for the collaboration with the county's planning and development team. She indicated that the team consisted of experts in transportation, water resources, and economics, essential for addressing various planning areas. She reviewed the purpose of the comprehensive plan, which is required by state law for every Arizona county, noting that the plan provides a blueprint for growth and development, guiding coordinated, efficient, and sustainable growth while conserving natural resources and promoting public welfare. She stated that the plan is forward-looking, with the goal of aligning future development with the community's vision.  She outlined how the comprehensive plan serves as the foundation for other planning tools like zoning codes and subdivision regulations. She stated that the team worked on the entire county, knowing that two-thirds of the county is in sovereign nations but that there's connectivity with roadways for open spaces, and coordinated with tribal governments and incorporated cities to ensure compatibility with their plans. She indicated that the process, lasting over 12 months, involved six phases, including public engagement and feedback.  She indicated that that the draft was reviewed during a 60-day public period, and that the Planning and Zoning Commission recommended approval. She stated that if adopted, the plan would become effective immediately, serving as the governing land use document for Navajo County.  She reviewed the Public Participation Plan , indicating that it is a statutory requirement and was presented to the board in March of last year. She stated that it aligns with state regulations and the county's existing public participation efforts.  She spoke about the process and Strategy, noting that the goal was to maximize exposure and gather public input, which included setting up a project website that has had over 9,100 views and 74 direct communication sign-ups. She indicated that a Comprehensive Plan Committee (CPC) of experts was established to guide the process at key milestones.  She spoke about community outreach, indicating that multiple methods were employed to engage the community, including a community questionnaire, in-person and virtual interviews, and eight in-person events with over 900 participants. She stated that social media and press releases helped publicize the process and encourage participation.  She spoke about the public review, indicating that a 60-day public review period was held, during which the plan was posted online, advertised through various media, and open house meetings were held. She stated that feedback was collected and reviewed, with most comments being positive.  She reviewed the content of the plan, indicating that it addresses nine key topics, including land use, transportation, economic growth, housing, water, and energy with guiding principles and goals for each. She stated that a future land use map and policies for future growth were developed.  She spoke about implementation, indicating that while not required by statute, an implementation plan has been included to help guide the execution of the plan. She stated that it includes 87 action items covering short-term, mid-term, and long-term goals.  She stated that the project team recommends approval of the Navajo County 2050 Comprehensive Plan.
Supervisor Benally expressed dissatisfaction with the Comprehensive Plan, specifically highlighting the exclusion of input from the Navajo Nation and Hopi Tribe, which cover two-thirds of the county. She spoke about the plan primarily focusing on wealth and development for towns and municipalities while neglecting the rest of the county, leaving out vast areas with Indigenous populations. She questioned the rush to push the plan forward without involving these groups, raising concerns about the fairness and inclusivity of the plan.
Cody Cooper acknowledged the concerns raised, explaining that the comprehensive plan is limited by statutory authority, which excludes sovereign nations from the process. He spoke about efforts to engage with the Navajo Nation, conducting outreach to various chapters and community representatives, noting that despite reaching out to 40-50 entities, feedback was limited. He indicated that they had done their best within the legal constraints of their planning authority.
Chairman Whiting inquired if the plan only applies to areas within the county's statutory authority, excluding cities or incorporated areas, as they cannot plan for those regions.
Mr. Cooper confirmed that the plan only governs unincorporated, non-reservation areas of the county, as towns are required to create their own plans. He indicated that there is ongoing collaboration with towns, cities, and neighboring counties to ensure consistency and compliance with legal requirements. He spoke about Winslow doing their General Plan, noting that they reached out to the County for the a 60-day review period, which allowed the county to provide feedback and address any potential conflicts between their plans. He stated that the county continues to collaborate with all agencies within its authority but cannot regulate anything outside the unincorporated areas.
Supervisor Benally expressed frustration that the Navajo Nation was not properly involved in the planning process, despite the claim of reaching out to all jurisdictions. She criticized the exclusion of the two-thirds of Navajo County covered by sovereign nations, stating that the county's plans seem to prioritize certain areas while neglecting others. She spoke of a constituent's perception of prejudice in the area and felt the county was not being inclusive in its approach, despite claiming the plan was comprehensive.
Supervisor Peshlakai expressed his appreciation for the comprehensive plan and the efforts behind it, acknowledging the differences between county and tribal planning processes. He explained that the county's plan focuses on land use, infrastructure, and economic growth within county boundaries, while tribal nations prioritize cultural preservation, self-determination, and economic development within their sovereign lands. He clarified that tribal nations are not bound by state regulations in the same way as counties, which operate under state authority. He noted that tribal nations receive federal funding for community planning, unlike counties, which rely on local taxes.

10:26 a.m. Supervisor Peshlakai stepped out of the meeting.

Supervisor Seymore expressed gratitude for staff's hard work in developing the comprehensive plan, emphasizing its importance for future community growth, zoning, and economic sustainability. He spoke about the plan's potential to guide the county's development over the next 20-30 years, addressing challenges like population decline and shifts in the economy. He commended the plan as a necessary update, noting the county's last comprehensive plan was in 2004, and it was only recently revised to remain compliant. He acknowledged that while the plan isn't perfect, it provides a strong foundation for growth and will be refined over time through regulations and ordinances.

10:30 a.m. Supervisor Peshlakai returned to the meeting.

Chairman Whiting opened the Public Hearing. There being no comments, the Public Hearing was closed.

Vice-Chair Whitesinger asked for clarification on the approval process and inquired about the procedure for making amendments or modifications to the plan if needed in the future, specifically in the context of the recommendation to review the plan every 10 years.
Mr. Cooper explained that, by state law, the comprehensive plan must be updated or readopted every 10 years, but that the plan is a "living document," meaning it can be amended or modified earlier if significant trends or shifts are identified. He indicated that any changes would follow a process similar to the one already completed, allowing for updates as needed at the local level.
Vice-Chair Whitesinger expressed appreciation for the effort and professionalism in developing the comprehensive plan, highlighting its importance in meeting state requirements and its impact on local communities. She commended the outreach efforts to educate the public about the plan's significance and its potential effects on property, land, and future projects. She acknowledged the importance of considering renewable energy and the involvement of tribal communities in the planning process, emphasizing that tribal partnerships are a valued asset to Navajo County. She expressed gratitude for the work put into the plan and clarifying the county's commitment to valuing and supporting tribal communities.
Chairman Whiting thanked staff for the efforts made to connect with various communities and gather feedback on what citizens want for Navajo County. He acknowledged the thorough outreach efforts and expressed their impression of the process.

PUBLIC WORKS :

a.

Presentation of Navajo County's Waste Tire Program and notification of the receipt of the Arizona Department of Environmental Quality (ADEQ) Voluntary Environmental Stewardship Program (VESP) Bronze Award :
A presentation was shown.  Madhav Mundle stated that Navajo County received the VESP Bronze Award from ADEQ for maintaining sustained compliance with environmental laws for three consecutive years, recognizing the county's waste tire program. He stated that the program, managed by Navajo County Public Works in collaboration with ADEQ, involves two tire collection sites in Holbrook and Show Low. He indicated that the county also organizes community cleanups and works with a contractor to haul and dispose of tires, noting that in fiscal year 2024, over 54,000 tires were collected. He stated that the program is funded by the state, with annual funding varying but typically exceeding $210,000. He indicated that the county's outreach includes informational materials available on their website.
A presentation was shown.  Manny Bowler, Community Liaison for Navajo, Apache, and Gila counties, spoke about Navajo County's waste tire program, which received the VESP Bronze Award for sustained compliance with environmental laws. She stated that this recognition is part of the Voluntary Environmental Stewardship Program (VESP), created by the Arizona State Legislature. She indicated that the program encourages organizations to improve their environmental practices, with recognition levels ranging from Copper to Platinum, noting that Navajo County's tire program caught up on six years of overdue reporting and has maintained compliance. She praised the county's efforts, noting that the program has become a model for other areas, including Apache County, where tire disposal options are limited. She thanked the county for its continued support of waste tire disposal events.

b.

Consideration of an Intergovernmental Agreement with the State of Arizona, Department of Transportation for the Silver Creek Drive and Silver Lake Boulevard Crossing :
Madhav Mundle stated that Navajo County received funding through AZ Smart Funds for the design of a bridge replacement project in District 3, specifically in the White Mountain Lakes Community. He indicated that the project would involve designing a new bridge over Rocky Arroyo and creating a new storm drain crossing at Silver Lake Boulevard. He stated that Public Works applied for and secured $890,000 for the design phase, which will be administered by ADOT. He indicated that the project aims to improve access and infrastructure in the area. He noted that they would apply for separate funding to do the construction work.  He stated that staff requests approval of the IGA.

Chairman Whiting inquired about the plan for managing traffic disruptions during construction, emphasizing the importance of minimizing the impact on the public and ensuring smooth traffic flow during the project.
Mr. Mundle confirmed that managing traffic disruptions will be a key consideration during the project, assuring that access to the community will not be cut off and that detour routes will be planned. He indicated that the goal is to avoid impacting both routes simultaneously, ensuring the community's access is maintained during construction, which will be addressed as part of the design and development phase, focusing on traffic maintenance.
Chairman Whiting encouraged them to communicate effectively with the public about the upcoming construction, including timelines and alternative routes.

Vice-Chair Whitesinger made a motion  to approve an Intergovernmental Agreement with the State of Arizona, Department of Transportation for the Silver Creek Drive and Silver Lake Boulevard Crossing, motion seconded by  Supervisor Peshlakai .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
