b. Consideration and approval of the proposed initial Navajo County Middle
Mile Fiber Pricing (Pricing Number 2025-001), the proposed Indefeasible
Right of Use (IRU) agreement template, and a proposed pre-sale
campaign :
Grant Evans discussed the item indicating that the presentation covers
two key items for consideration: the initial pricing structure for Navajo
County’s middle mile broadband infrastructure and the Indefeasible Right
of Use (IRU) agreement, which serves as the long-term fiber lease
mechanism. He introduced Kyle Hildebrand from Ex2, the county’s partner
in design, construction, and marketing, who will present updates on
leasing efforts and pricing. He stated the construction is progressing well
and is approximately 75% complete. Mr. Evans stated that the project
faced early delays during the permitting phase, which slowed the initial
construction ramp-up. He stated that originally, the Arizona Commerce
Authority grant deadline was June 30, 2025, but it has been extended to
June 30, 2026, due to those delays. He again emphasized that
construction is 75% complete, and the project is expected to finish by the
end of this year. Mr. Evans stated that Ex2 has begun initial lease
discussions with potential users, making it a timely moment to present the
pricing structure and lease agreement to the Board to support final
negotiations and marketing efforts. Kyle Hildebrand, Co-Founder and
Chief Revenue Officer of Ex2, was again introduced as a key partner in
the project. He oversees business development, project development, and
marketing, and has been an integral and instrumental part of guiding the
broadband initiative. Mr. Hildebrand indicated that they have a dedicated
team focused on commercialization and monetizing Navajo County’s
broadband infrastructure, including experts in business, legal, and
technical areas. He stated that efforts began once permitting was
completed, starting with a marketing analysis to identify potential
commercial users and network connection points. Negotiations were
initiated with entities like APS, ADOT, NTUA, and private carriers. He
stated that this led to the development of a formal commercialization and
marketing plan tailored to Navajo County, prioritizing community
benefits—broadband access, emergency services, economic
development, healthcare, and education—over profit. The plan includes a
pricing strategy for leasing dark fiber and uses Indefeasible Right of Use
(IRU) agreements, offering commercial users 20-year access with certain
ownership-like benefits. Mr. Hildebrand then spoke about the pricing
component. He stated that the proposed pricing structure for Navajo
County’s broadband infrastructure includes a 20-year lease (IRU) for dark
fiber only, with no lit service or conduit included. The pricing is

competitive, based on market research, and designed to lower barriers to
entry, encourage last-mile competition, and attract a broad range of users,
including ISPs, healthcare providers, wireless companies, and energy
firms. He stated that the key terms are a one-time, upfront payment, an
annual maintenance fee: paid yearly, adjusted for inflation via CPI, longer

mirrors similar models used by ADOT and APS, where Ex2 also acts as

negotiations. The focus remains on enabling broadband access for
residents and supporting state initiatives like the BEAD program, with
marketing efforts planned to further drive interest. Grant Evans spoke
about the IRU agreement, the telecommunications industry standard for
executing fiber lease deals. He stated that the agreement outlines the
terms and conditions of the leases and will support Ex2 in final
negotiations with end users. Mr. Evans stated that staff recommended
approval of both the pricing structure and the IRU agreement template.
Supervisor Whitesinger raised a question about the IRU pricing
structure, specifically why the IRU fee is a one-time payment rather than
an annual fee. She then clarified that the question refers to the initial IRU
fee (not the ongoing maintenance fee) and sought further explanation to
better understand the reasoning behind this industry-standard approach.
Mr. Hildebrand stated that the one-time upfront IRU fee is standard in the
dark fiber and telecommunications industry because it allows carriers to
capitalize the cost as an asset. While some asset owners offer annual
payment options, that structure is more typical of standard lease
agreements rather than traditional IRUs. Given the nature of this
infrastructure, the recommended model is the one-time IRU fee plus
annual maintenance. Supervisor Seymore raised a question about the
potential for cost recovery on the broadband infrastructure investment. He
expressed concern that, given the current pricing, there seems to be little
chance of recovering the $22 million spent on the project. He cited a rough
calculation—800 multiplied by 192 fibers, totaling around 153,000—as an
example of the limited revenue potential under the proposed pricing
structure. Mr. Hildebrand stated that its per fiber per mile, so you would
take that 153,00 then times that by 105.
Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion to approve the proposed initial
Navajo County Middle Mile Fiber Pricing (Pricing Number 2025-001), the
proposed Indefeasible Right of Use (IRU) agreement template, and a
proposed pre-sale campaign, motion seconded by Supervisor Seymore.

1. Presentation regarding the current wildfire conditions and fire restrictions:
Catrina Jenkins discussed the current wildfire conditions and fire restrictions
indicating that they are starting to see more activity with a new fire starting
this morning. Mrs. Jenkins spoke about the White Mountain Fire
Coordination Group that meets weekly to discuss and determine fire
restrictions in the local region. Mrs. Jenkins indicated that the group consists
of the National Forest Service partners, State Forestry partners, the BIA Fort
Apache Agency Bureau of Land Management, Emergency Management
from Navajo and Apache Counties, as well as a representative from the
Northeastern Arizona Fire Chiefs Association as well as the Northeastern
Arizona Police Chiefs Association. The group receives weekly updates and
reports on the weather forecasts. Mrs. Jenkins stated that the group
considers several factors, including available resources, fuel conditions, the
number of fires starts, and the number of people in the region. For instance,
during busy times like Memorial Day weekend, they anticipate an increase
in activity. Mrs. Jenkins reported that the drought conditions have only
slightly changed since last week. The region remains in severe to extreme
drought, though not at the highest levels. Recent moisture, especially near
I-40, slightly improved conditions, but a lack of winter snowpack remains a
concern. Temperatures have been below normal, and precipitation has been
above normal in late April and early May, which has influenced the fire
outlook. The short-term drought is expected to continue, as it does annually.
Mrs. Jenkins continued to report that the updated fire outlooks for May show
improvement due to recent rain, while June still shows high fire potential.
Restrictions have increased in some areas like the Tonto National Forest
which is in Stage 2 restrictions. The dryness is typical, progressing from
east to west, as do monsoons. July is expected to bring increased
monsoonal activity. Mrs. Jenkins stated that currently, the area is in Stage 1
fire restrictions, but that may change. She emphasized that most fires this
year have started in residential areas, not forests, often due to human
activity, like a charcoal grill fire that destroyed a home. The Coyote Fire,
which burned over 1,000 acres, started from a structure and spread rapidly.
Mrs. Jenkins strongly stressed community responsibility, especially in
wildland-urban interface areas, and urged locals and visitors alike to stay
vigilant. Finally, Mrs. Jenkins highlighted community outreach efforts,
including a successful expo in March and the upcoming Firewise Block
Party, which aims to educate residents on fire safety and preparedness.
Chairman Whiting thanked Mrs. Jenkins for her presentation and for
consistently keeping the Board informed, both during board meetings and
through emails.
