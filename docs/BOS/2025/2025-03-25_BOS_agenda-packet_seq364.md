1. PUBLIC HEARING: Consideration of the amendment of Ordinance 01-19
Outdoor Fire Ordinance:
Catrina Jenkins discussed the annual review of the fire season ordinance.
She spoke about aligning local regulations with federal and state
guidelines, especially as many properties border federal forests. She
indicated that the changes made to the ordinance are minor, including
adding definitions for "explosives" and "firearms," and reorganizing the
exemptions for prohibited items, for example, the exemption for using
propane or LPG fuel for fires has been moved to be directly under the
prohibited item to make it clearer for the public and partners. She stated
that she is available for any questions of the Board.
 
Supervisor Seymore shared his appreciation to Ms. Jenkins and team
for the efforts made, noting that there are concerns out there.  She
thanked them for making the changes to the ordinance so that it’s clearer
and easier to understand.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE: Report from County Manager, County Attorney
and Board members :
Rochelle Lacapa provided an update on the listening sessions for the new
Renewable Energy Ordinance, indicating that the sessions are scheduled and
available on the website for public access, where people can also submit
comments online. She stated that the in-person listening sessions will take place
in Show Low on March 24, 2025; Heber on March 26, 2025; and Joseph City on
March 27, 2025, all from 6 to 8 PM.
Supervisor Seymore stated that he shared a handout with the Supervisors, which
was presented by CSA to state legislators to highlight the gap between rising state
revenue shares and the slower growth of county revenue. He indicated that the
handout aims to educate new state leaders on the financial struggles of counties,
especially when state mandates burden local budgets. He spoke about ongoing
efforts to oppose legislation that could negatively affect Navajo County, including
changes to voting precincts and subdivision regulations that could undermine local
control and planning.
Supervisor Benally reported on attending the NACO Legislative Conference in
Washington, DC, noting that she attended several of the committee meetings,
such as Transportation, RAC, WIR, and Immigration.  She stated that she met with
Senator Mark Kelly and his staff about issues affecting Navajo and Hopi lands,
including roads, water rights, and education.  She spoke about Leonard Peltier,
who was a political prisoner released on clemency after nearly 50 years, noting his
connection to Indigenous rights and activism.  She further spoke about the death of
an Elder from the Big Mountain region, offering condolences and thanking the
Navajo County Sheriff's Office for their efforts in the search.  She indicated that the
Navajo Nation Chapters are having elections today and spoke about times when
the polls will close due to being on Daylight Savings Time.
Vice-Chair Whitesinger shared her gratitude for the ongoing work in local
communities.  She spoke about the recent moisture that we have received and
addressed concerns about the drought and the potential for wildfires, noting that
the White Mountain Apache Tribal community has held meetings with local
partners to address fire preparedness. She reminded everyone about the Fire
Preparedness Expo on March 29, 2025, in Pinetop Lakeside and emphasized the
importance of advocating for federal funding for wildfire mitigation. She thanked
everyone who has supported these efforts.

The new Code Enforcement Facility has a septic system that is under the jurisdiction of the Arizona Department 
of Environmental Quality.  ADEQ has reviewed and approved the design for the system.   W ith that approval,

this service and requires it from the applicant. 
 
Here are the requirements per R18-09-A309(C)(2) to receive a Discharge Authorization that will need to be 
completed during inspection:  
• As built plans showing changes from construction quality drawings, 
• A certification of tank watertightness, 
• A final list of equipment and materials showing changes from the original list. 
• Visual inspection of the septic tank manufacturer and capacity on the outside of the tank. 
• Visual inspection of the piping, d-box, and disposal field components to ensure proper components used 
and proper installation. 
 
Additional items needed to submit for a Discharge Authorization include: 
• A final O&M manual, 
• A certification of a service contract, 
• A certificate of completion signed by the person responsible for assuring that the installation conforms 
to the CA, 
• The name and license number of the installing contractor,

Motion before the Board:
Resolution Number 06-2025, approving the exchange of an existing Navajo County Highway Easement
over Parcel 202-05-005G
Background:
The Town of Taylor is requesting the exhange of a public highway easement recorded as a "Navajo
County Highway Easement" over and across Assessor's Parcel Number (APN): 202-05-005G and is
associated with Papermill Road and within the town's limits.
The parcel owner supports the exchange and the town is seeking the exchange for the clarification of the
easement and the town's ability to utilize it on behalf of the public for projects. 
This exchange will:  
Provide a larger public highway easement over property from 33' to 50'
Clean the legal description so that the language will clearly address the easement location over and
across APN 202-05-005G
Change the public agent name from Navajo County to the Town of Taylor
Allow the Town of Taylor to create a public trail 
Clarify legal concerns with the Town of Taylor's project within City Limits and Navajo County
Clarify any conflicting public highway easements over APN 202-05-005G
This exchange will not: 
Change the location of the road
Extinguish utility rights to the old easement description
Change access
Apply to any other properties.
The Navajo County Staff have worked with the Town of Taylor and the property owner and support the
easement exchange.
The Town of Taylor has accepted the new easement dedicated by the property owner pending the Navajo
County Board of Supervisors' approval of the exchange.
With the nature of the exhange as being clerical and not removing public rights, public notice has been
excluded as allowed for in the Abandonment Policy at the discretion of the County Engineer with legal
concurrence.

Title: April 2025 Victims' Rights Week Proclamation
Submitted For: Brad Carlyon, County Attorney  Submitted By: Lynda Young, Office Manager
Department: County Attorney
Presented By: Roxanne Pergeson
Motion before the Board:
Consideration and approval of a Proclamation declaring April 6th through April 12, 2025, as National
Victims' Rights Week.
Background:
National Crime Victims’ Rights Week (NCVRW) has been observed since 1981 to challenge the Nation
to confront and remove barriers to achieving justice for all victims of crime. During NCVRW, individuals
and organizations that support victims and victim services are recognized. The exact date of the
observance varies year to year, but it is always in the month of April. The theme for this year is
KINSHIP, which calls for recognizing shared humanity in supporting all survivors and victims of crime.
Recommendation: 
Staff recommends acceptance and declaration of National Victims' Rights Week for April 6, 2025 to
April 12, 2025.

ProclamationNational Victims' Rights Week
WHEREAS, crime affects individuals, families, and communities in profound and varied ways, often leaving lasting
physical, emotional, and social challenges; and
WHEREAS, the concept of kinship underscores the connections that build bridges to support, services and shared
strength among survivors, advocates, and communities; and
WHEREAS, kinship is the foundation of victim advocacy, inspiring trauma-informed care that meets survivors where
they are; and
WHEREAS, through kinship, we build peer networks and community alliances that empower survivors, foster
resilience, and offer pathways to healing;
   WHEREAS, KINSHIP reminds us of the strength that comes from connection, the importance of listening to every
   voice, and the power we have to create lasting change when we work together;
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT PROCLAIMED, by the Board of Supervisors of Navajo County and the County Attorney’s
Office of Navajo County, the week of April 6th through the 12th, 2025, as “National Victims’ Rights Week” in Navajo
County, Arizona, in honor of victims of crime within Navajo County.
