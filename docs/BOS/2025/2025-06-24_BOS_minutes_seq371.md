PUBLIC HEARING:  Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 17-2025 , approving/denying a comprehensive update to the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance :
Cody Cooper stated that over the past year staff received significant community feedback regarding renewable energy and existing renewable energy ordinances, noting that the Board adopted a new Comprehensive Plan in January, which recommended a full zoning ordinance update, including renewable energy regulations.  He spoke about the proposed updates to the Comprehensive Zoning Ordinance.  He reviewed the revisions to the zoning districts which updated administrative procedures, code enforcement, and compliance processes, clear lists of permitted and special permitted land uses, updated development standards and definitions, and countywide standards and clarified language throughout.  He indicated that key improvements included
zoning districts being simplified by removing complex cross-referencing between zones by consolidating use tables and rural districts being combined with a unified use list and separate development standard charts.  He stated that administrative procedures would be streamlined, noting that violations, enforcement, and penalties were previously spread across multiple sections would now be consolidated into one article, Article 21, and that users can now clearly understand what constitutes a violation, how it's enforced, and potential penalties.  He spoke about the land use clarifications, including clear distinctions being added for permitted, administrative, and special use permits, and administrative permit requirements now being listed within zoning articles to avoid confusion during the permitting process.  He spoke about the general Provisions & Development Standards which include items like yards, open spaces, and height regulations, being moved to simplified charts for easier interpretation.  He indicated that special development zoning would be expanded to include all development types (e.g., site plans, master plans, tentative/final plats), noting that process expectations and submittal checklists were added to improve transparency and reduce planning delays.  He further indicated that parking requirements would be simplified into a chart and aligned with residential and building codes to avoid conflicts and plan revisions.  He stated that the sign ordinance would be updated to comply with Reed v. Town of Gilbert Supreme Court ruling and that all sign regulations would be content-neutral to avoid First Amendment violations.
Chairman Whiting shared his appreciation for the efforts to view the zoning ordinance updates through the lens of the citizens by focusing on clarity, accessibility, and usability. He thanked staff for proactively working to simplify processes, reduce confusion, and better serve the public.
Mr. Cooper stated that as part of this zoning ordinance update, staff significantly streamlined the document, reducing it from 180 pages to 100, without cutting meaningful content. He indicated that the goal was to simplify and condense information to make it more accessible and user-friendly for the public, noting that they aimed to remove barriers and create a more approachable and consumable resource for the community.
Mr. Cooper reviewed the proposed revisions to the renewable energy ordinance.  He spoke about the integration of wind and solar standards which would allow for unified standards under renewable energy development, cover items like decommissioning, bonding, and road use agreements and would apply to both wind and solar.  He spoke about Development Agreements and  performance standards which would allow for development agreements alongside or instead of special use permits and would include preference criteria for lower-impact or reclaimed land projects.  He further spoke about Fire Department involvement, indicating that they hosted listening sessions, resulting in language that now meets their needs and clarifies roles in permitting and inspections.  As to road use and maintenance, he indicated that there would be a requirement for developers to maintain roads during and post-construction and that there would be road condition assessments required before and after the project.  He spoke about Municipal & Tribal Coordination, noting that developers must coordinate with entities within 5 miles of project boundaries, which is aimed to increase awareness and transparency.  He spoke about technology and safety requirements, that would mandate companies to use the best commercially proven technology, to minimize wildlife impacts.  He further spoke about the mandate for site studies, including environmental, cultural, wetland, and photo simulation studies.  He spoke about wind-specific revisions, that would allow for waivers from neighboring properties within setbacks and preserves property owners' right to negotiate.  He reviewed the solar-specific revisions, noting that compliance with zoning setbacks are required and that waiver and screening provisions were added. Mr. Cooper spoke about the public engagement, noting that there was extensive public feedback, including 21 comments in opposition to the staff report related to renewal energy prior to the development of the ordinance, 51 online submissions, 5 letters/emails, and over 80 comments from the 3 public listening sessions.  He indicated that 7 additional comments were received since the release of the draft ordinance.  He stated that common concerns included clarifying language within the ordinance, noise and setback standards for wind energy, excessive public outreach standards, roll of fire within review, transference of Special Use Permits requiring hearings for renewable energy and decommissioning procedures.  He indicated that some of the items have not been addressed, noting that some items have existed since the ordinance was first adopted.  He reviewed some of the modifications that have been made.  He spoke about proposed minor clarifying edits which include adding "desktop" in front of wetland delineation report, as well as the cultural resources report to clarify level of detail they expect; correcting language to include public outreach will be required regardless of the process used; and changing "consult" to "coordinate" with municipalities/tribes to avoid implying federal-level consultation.  He clarified some points, noting that solar panels are not considered impervious surfaces because they allow water to run off and do not completely cover the ground, so they aren't treated like buildings when calculating lot coverage. He indicated that the ordinance also allows for fencing up to eight feet high in residential areas tied to renewable energy projects, which is more typical of commercial or industrial standards, noting that it is intentional, not an oversight, as the more specific renewable energy provisions take precedence over general residential regulations. Mr. Cooper stated that staff would recommend approval of the Renewable Energy Ordinance Update, noting that the Planning & Zoning Commission unanimously recommended approval at their last meeting, with the three minor clarifying amendments.
Chairman Whiting opened the meeting for public comments.

a.

FY26 Tribal Transportation Projects :
Chairman Whiting thanked everyone for being present. Rochelle Lacapa stated that this work session's goal is to encourage strategic budgeting for tribal transportation projects funding Highway User Revenue Funds. She stated that since many of these projects are made successful through partnerships with tribal transportation departments and the Bureau of Indian Affairs, this session will aim to inform and give context to the County's procurement process, provide an overview of characteristics of successful partnerships and breakdown annual HURF allocations. Nicole Reske discussed that the priority is understanding your budget responsibilities, including clearly defining the county's financial contribution and ensuring it aligns with the available budget capacity. She stated that it is important to avoid overcommitting to projects beyond your financial limits. She requested that focus be placed on identifying projects that exceed the $100,000 threshold, as these may streamline the budgeting process and help avoid delays or unspent funds. She stated that priority should be given to shovel-ready projects that align with both county and partner goals, helping to prevent redundant spending on previously completed surveys or studies. Lastly, she requested that each project should have a clearly defined scope to support efficient planning and execution. Nicole discussed the packet of information that was handed out indicating that the packet provided to the Supervisors includes all necessary documentation for the Navajo Road Project N8065, such as scope of work, cost estimates, material quotes, and supporting letters. She stated that for projects with Navajo Nation without vendor quotes, documentation is included to show due diligence. She further stated that larger or more complex projects may also require resolutions from local governing entities or chapters, especially for partnership efforts. Nicole stated that it is important to prioritize projects that can realistically be completed within the first three years of procurement timelines. Nicole noted that a valid and current IGA (Intergovernmental Agreement) is critical, as delays in renewing or signing it, especially if legal review is needed, can push projects into the next fiscal year and impact others. Rochelle indicated that we currently have IGA's in place with Fort Apache Agency BIA, WMAT, NDOT and Western Agency BIA and Chinle BIA/Fort Defiance. Rochelle stated that we are waiting for the IGA to be signed by the Hopi Chairman. Rochelle further stated that Nicole has been tasked with being the steward of all these IGA's so that we know their expiration dates, so that we can start the renewal process early, ensuring it becomes standard practice rather than a last-minute scramble. Rochelle indicated that since gathering signatures from partner agencies can take time, early action helps avoid delays. Rochelle thanked Nicole and stated that a major achievement has been Nicole's success in getting all IGAs updated and in place, which reinforces and renews the county's partnership with the tribal community, including with the transit agency. Supervisor Benally asked if there is an IGA in place with the Chinle Agency. Rochelle indicated that they would make sure and get back to her. Jeanine Carruthers indicated that we do have an IGA in place with the Western Agency. Nicole stated that in addition, all IGAs must be submitted to the Board of Supervisors for approval before their first meeting in April to meet procurement deadlines. She further stated that missing this deadline increases the risk of project delays and the possibility of it being pushed into the next fiscal year. Nicole indicated that it is important to remember that projects exceeding the $100,000 threshold typically add two to three months to the project timeline. She further indicated that equally, if not more important, is understanding your partners' priorities to ensure alignment and mutual investment in projects. She stated that engaging with agency partners about their goals helps build successful, collaborative efforts while respecting the sovereignty of each department. She listed the key partners as often included are the Navajo Department of Transportation, Hopi Department of Transportation, White Mountain Apache Tribe Department of Transportation, and the five BIA jurisdictions. Nicole wrapped up her comments by reviewing the highly visible projects with immediate impacts, examples include cost-sharing partnerships, where the county funds materials and partner agencies contribute labor. She stated that these would be projects such as culverts, gravel, or sign fabrication. She further stated that another example is partnering to reimburse agencies for hiring personnel, such as heavy equipment operators, to work on roads owned by those agencies. Nicole stated that these collaborative efforts demonstrate effective use of resources and strengthen interagency relationships and visible results. Jeanine Carruthers spoke about the county's bid process for contracts, noting that anything over $100,000 has to go out for bid.  She explained the bid process, including preparing a comprehensive bid package that follows specific statutes, publishing the bid in the county newspaper, allowing 21-28 days for vendors to review and download bid materials, publicly opening bids after the specified period, the Board approval of the contract, and within ten days they have to get the contract in place, including obtaining certificates of insurance, if needed.  She indicated that the entire process takes about 8 weeks, and for more technical projects like engineering services, it can take even longer. She further indicated that the goal is to give vendors sufficient time to review and prepare their bids while following legal requirements.  Rochelle spoke about the importance of early communication and joint priority setting between the county and tribal transportation departments.  She indicated that the county priorities often don't align with transportation department priorities, necessitating negotiations. She further indicated that staff would like to start conversations early to prepare projects for Q2-Q3, avoiding last-minute rushes and ensuring projects can be completed within the fiscal year. She stated that by engaging with the Supervisors about their district priorities now, they hope to prevent project rollovers and maximize fund utilization. She reviewed the budgets specific to the districts.  Supervisor Benally inquired about the rollover costs.  Rochelle stated that rollover costs are projects that started in the current fiscal year but would not be able to be finished in the current fiscal year, so they are charged to the next fiscal year.  She continued to review the district budget worksheets.  She spoke about the budget components, including fixed costs, rollover projects, and proposed new projects. Supervisor Benally expressed frustration about repairs on a motor grader, indicating she was disappointed with guidance from public works. She stated that there was enough money in her HURF funds to buy a new grader at the time due to there being no projects.  Rochelle indicated that for Supervisor Benally's district, there are currently no rollover projects from the previous year, noting that the only proposed project so far is an N41 pothole repair, though an estimated cost has not yet been determined. Supervisor Benally spokeabout several proposed projects she has already requested, including culverts projects and gravel work.  Rochelle advised Supervisor Benally to include Nicole in project conversations to help manage documentation and procurement processes.
