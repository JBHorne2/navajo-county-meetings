PLANNING & DEVELOPMENT SERVICES :

PUBLIC HEARING:  Consideration and adoption of  Resolution Number 26-2025,  approving/denying an update to Section 1916 (assisted living home administrative procedures), Sections 502, 602, 702, and 1917 (guest houses/accessory dwelling units), and Article 23 (definitions) of the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance :
A presentation was shown.  Cody Cooper spoke about proposed updates to Navajo County's zoning ordinances to comply with recent Arizona state laws. He indicated that the first relates to assisted living homes, requiring counties to provide a process for granting variances to the 1,200-foot separation requirement between group homes for individuals with disabilities.  He further indicated that this change ensures the county complies with the Americans with Disabilities Act and the Fair Housing Act by allowing reasonable accommodations for qualifying homes.  He stated that they recommend adding a section name reasonable accommodations under group homes for disabled.
He spoke about the new legislation regarding accessory dwelling units, also known as guest houses. He indicated that the new law mandates that counties must allow one attached and one detached unit on any single-family residential lot, and two detached units on lots that are one acre or larger. He stated that Navajo County is required to adopt regulations by January 1, 2026, otherwise, accessory dwelling units will be permitted without any county oversight.  He indicated that they are proposing ordinance revisions that allow guest houses in all zoning districts where single-family residences are permitted, noting these changes include removing minimum lot size and design similarity requirements, and standardizing a five-foot setback from property lines to meet fire safety standards. He further indicated that the county may no longer impose restrictions like requiring additional parking, owner-residency, or design matching for accessory dwelling units. He stated that definitions for guest houses and accessory dwelling units will be updated to align with state law, and language related to manufactured and RV parks has been clarified to avoid unintended permitting conflicts.
He stated that staff would recommend approval of these updates to ensure timely compliance with the state requirements and preserve the county's ability to regulate accessory dwelling units. He indicated that no public comments had been received on the proposal at the time of the presentation.  Chairman Whiting opened the meeting for public comments.  There being no comments, the public hearing was closed.  Supervisor Seymore inquired if you are in a subdivision and the subdivision has CC&R's, would this override the CC&R's?
Mr. Cooper stated that it would not override CC&Rs or HOA rules. He indicated that counties cannot require restrictive covenants, but they also cannot regulate or interfere with private agreements like CC&Rs, noting if a property is subject to such restrictions, the owner is still civilly responsible for complying with them, as there is no state legislation that invalidates or overrides those private agreements.  Supervisor Seymore stated that he agrees with the assisted living provisions but indicated that HB 2928 is an overreach, noting that the law fails to consider the strain it could place on infrastructure like roads, water, and sewer systems. He indicated that it must be followed since it's now law, but noted that it was poorly written, potentially allowing a dramatic increase in housing density without adequate planning. Vice-Chair Whitesinger inquired if there are any regulations limiting the number of accessory dwelling units in a neighborhood. Mr. Cooper indicated that it's hypothetically possible for every neighbor in a subdivision to add accessory dwelling units within the allowed limits.  He further indicated that counties still retain authority to regulate safety-related issues such as wastewater capacity and flood control, noting that permits can be denied if septic systems lack capacity or if construction violates floodplain rules.  He stated that sewer connections still require that there's a will serve, showing that there is capacity for use of the system.  He indicated that the only area where counties cannot impose restrictions is road impact or traffic volume, unless related to construction damage.
Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to adopt Resolution Number 26-2025, approving an update to Section 1916 (assisted living home administrative procedures), Sections 502, 602, 702, and 1917 (guest houses/accessory dwelling units), and Article 23 (definitions) of the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance, motion seconded by  Vice-Chair Whitesinger .  Motion passed 4-0, with Supervisor Benally being absent.

CALL TO THE PUBLIC :
Cindy Allen spoke about the proclamation for Patriotic Week from the 8th to the 14th and presented a Certificate of Honor to the Board for properly flying the U.S. flag, on behalf of the Holbrook Elks Lodge.  She noted that they have 12 certificates that will be awarded, with the Board being the first recipients.
Nani Jacobsen with the Civil Integrity Alliance, addressed the Board to express concern and frustration regarding the adoption of Resolution 17-2025. She highlighted ongoing community grievances, including repeated unanswered requests to be placed on meeting agendas and a lack of transparency or support regarding wind farm projects. She cited multiple formal requests and communications dating back to 2023, including a letter from Senator Wendy Rogers urging the Board to listen to constituents. She questioned the Board's continued silence and apparent disregard for community input.
Evangeline Adkins, with the Civil Integrity Alliance, continued outlining concerns related to Resolution 17-2025, emphasizing procedural and transparency issues. She indicated that the resolution, though presented as a zoning ordinance update, should have been treated as a major amendment due to its significant land use implications, triggering a more rigorous public review process under state law. She accused the county of abandoning principles of proximity fairness and shared benefit, with no systems in place to include or protect adjacent landowners. She indicated that the public notices were inadequate, with little time for residents to prepare or participate meaningfully. She spoke about failures to notify affected landowners, exclude the topic from meeting agendas, and avoid public discussion, particularly through channels like the county's Facebook page. She indicated that these actions are a breach of the county's own 2025 Comprehensive Plan.
Lisa Vincenti expressed frustration over the lack of acknowledgment or response from the Board of Supervisors regarding a formal grievance and repeated requests for public discussion about zoning ordinance changes. She indicated that despite over a year of effort to be included in the process, residents were consistently ignored or told it wasn't the right time, only to see Resolution 17-2025 passed with little notice. She expressed concern and feelings of disenfranchisement, citing social media engagement and recent executive orders that deprioritize wind and solar energy, which they believe were overlooked. She urged the Board to pause implementation of the zoning changes and conduct a proper major amendment process, noting that she has been in contact with Representative David Marshall and the Arizona Ombudsman's Office.  Ms. Vincenti requested that she be allowed to read a statement on behalf of Roger Gorres, noting that he was running late.  The Board held a discussion regarding the matter and agreed to allow Ms. Vincenti to read the statement. Ms. Vincenti read a statement on behalf of Roger Gorres, with the Civic Integrity Alliance, urging the Navajo County Board of Supervisors to reconsider Resolution 17-2025 and Zoning Ordinance Article 12. She spoke about the flawed public notification process and the impracticality of placing massive wind turbines close to residential homes. She questioned why such projects aren't being developed on reservation land, suggesting the financial benefits could better serve Native communities. She asked the three Native American supervisors to use their majority vote to reopen discussion, revise the ordinance, and consider environmental, cultural, and community impacts.
Chairman Whiting encouraged the residents to speak directly with county staff regarding community concerns about Resolution 17-2025 and the zoning ordinance process. He indicated that all legal requirements for public notice were followed and that the county went above and beyond to provide opportunities for public input over the course of a year. He urged them to seek out the facts for themselves to better understand the process.
