BOARD OF SUPERVISORS :

a.

Presentation to Snowflake High School Girls Basketball Team for winning the 3A State Championship :
Chairman Whiting stated that they are excited to recognize the Snowflake High School Girls Basketball Team for winning the 3A State Championship, which is an incredible achievement that hasn't happened in 28 years.  He indicated that the team showed resilience, teamwork, and heart throughout the season, and that despite losing many seniors last year and facing injuries, they never doubted themselves. He stated that they truly embodied a "next woman up" mentality, and it was inspiring to watch them grow and succeed together.  He congratulated the team, noting that they made their school and community proud.
Coach Brogan stated that many may have underestimated this year's team, after graduating seven seniors last year, but that a busy summer with nearly 40 games gave them early confidence and chemistry.  He indicated that a key moment came early in the season with a big win over defending the state champion team, Page, which set the tone. He noted that throughout the season the team faced 11 of the top 12 teams in 3A, which made them ready by the time of the tournament.  He praised the players for their energy, effort, and dedication, highlighting their commitment in practice, academic excellence, and great sportsmanship, including two and a half years without a technical foul.  He stated that despite some injuries and setbacks, they finished the season 29-5, showing resilience and strong team unity throughout.
The two team captains shared that from the start, the team believed in their potential and worked hard all season, noting that everyone stepped up when needed, especially during tough moments and injuries. They indicated that they played for each other, supported one another, and their unity and effort were key to winning the championship.
Chairman Whiting stated that it was inspiring to see the team play with heart and determination and that it was a true team effort that showed on the court. Coach Brogan shared some moments from the championship game.
Supervisor Benally c ongratulated the team for coming back strong after graduating seniors and winning again after 28 years. She indicated that their determination was clear, and more success is surely ahead.
Supervisor Seymore stated that teamwork was key to the championship, noting that everyone contributed, whether on the court or from the bench. He indicated that it's the unity and support that led to success, and that win or lose, coming together as a team is what matters most. He congratulated them on their accomplishment.
Chairman Whiting presented the team with medals in recognition of their state championship.

b.

Recognition of Snowflake D-3 - 215 lb. Wrestling State Champion Devin Kinlicheenie :
Chairman Whiting  stated that Devin is being recognized for his outstanding accomplishments in wrestling, noting that he is a three-time state champion, with a remarkable high school career record of 199 wins and only 9 losses, 8 of which occurred during his freshman year. He indicated that wrestling runs in his family, that his brothers were also state champions, and his father, who is also his coach. He stated that Devin has shown leadership on his team and continues to set a strong example for others.
Devin's Father/Coach reflected on a tough but rewarding season coaching his youngest son. He indicated that, despite early setbacks, the team pushed through with hard work and grit. He spoke about Devin's success with becoming Snowflake's first three-time state champion and All-American, noting that he was driven by his exceptional work ethic and support from his brothers. He indicated that the future of Snowflake wrestling looks promising.
Devin shared that his success this season wasn't just about him, that it was thanks to his teammates, coaches, community, and faith. He emphasized the team's bond, prayer, and support from Snowflake, giving credit to those around him for helping him grow and compete at the highest level.
Supervisor Seymore recognized Devin for his hard work and dedication to wrestling, noting that it's one of the toughest sports. He spoke about the difficulty and skill involved, commending Devin for his outstanding performance.
Supervisor Benally congratulated Devin on his championship.

c.

Recognition of Winslow Division IV - 144 lb. Wrestling Champion Michael Romero :
Supervisor Benally  stated that Michael, a senior at Winslow High, was the Division 4 state wrestling champion in the 144-pound class, noting that he earned four pins on his way to the title, including a final match victory over the top seed. She indicated that Michael finished his career with 189 wins, 59 in a single season, and now holds two school records.
Michael's Uncle stated that he didn't like wrestling at first but grew into it through hard work and support from teammates and family. He indicated that Michael became his family's first state champion, placing at state all four years. He stated that his dedication and training paid off and expressed pride in his growth and success.
Michael stated Michael shared that his favorite takedown was taking down Devin Kinlicheenie the first time. He indicated that winning state felt great in the moment, but he quickly set new goals, that he is aiming to become a national champion or All-American, with plans to keep working hard in the future.
Supervisor Seymore praised the spirit of collaboration in wrestling, highlighting the value of friends sharing their best moves to help each other improve.

d.

Recognition of Mogollon D-4 - 165 lb. Wrestling Champion Hadley Porter and 106 lb. Wrestling State Champion Anthony Pimentel :
Supervisor Seymore  stated that Anthony won the 106-pound state championship for the Mustangs in an overtime match, noting that he dominated earlier rounds with quick pins and technical falls and credited his coaches and drill partner for his success. He indicated that last year he helped his team win a title but fell short individually, which motivated him this season.  He stated that Hadley finished the season 40-3 and capped his high school career with a state title after placing in state four times and coming up short in previous finals. He indicated that with a 199-18 career record Hadley credited his cousin and drill partner, his daily hard work, and his faith for finally achieving his long-time goal.
Coach Porter spoke about the importance of strong teammates and drill partners in shaping great wrestlers, noting that the toughest competition often happens in the practice room. He praised the dedication and hard work of the team, especially Anthony and Hadley, while also highlighting their strong character. He indicated that though the team placed second this year, their effort, discipline, and values reflect the strength of their community and prepare them for success beyond wrestling.
Anthony spoke about his tough but rewarding season, crediting his teammates and coaches for pushing him to improve. He shared that the hardest battles were in the practice room and joked about struggling to cut weight due to his love for food. He expressed gratitude for the support that helped him grow as a wrestler.
Hadley reflected on a successful year, expressing gratitude for the support from coaches, teammates, and his mom. He acknowledged that his success wouldn't have been possible without their help and emphasized that all glory goes to Jesus Christ for guiding him through it.
Supervisor Seymore spoke about the significance of these young men's achievements, noting that they compete in a highly competitive division that includes a mix of smaller and larger schools. He stated that despite coming from smaller schools, they managed to rise to the top in a large and challenging field, making their victories even more impressive.

The Board presented all the wrestlers with medals in recognition of their championships.

REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

Presentation regarding the current wildfire conditions, fire restrictions and mitigation efforts throughout the region :
A presentation was shown. Catrina Jenkins presented a drought and fire conditions update, noting that severe and extreme drought persists across the region, with increased fire risk forecasted in the coming months. She indicated that Stage 1 fire restrictions remain in place, including a ban on debris burning and suspended permits. She stated that residents and visitors are urged to exercise caution, especially regarding vehicle-related fire risks. She shared her appreciation for support of the recent Wildfire Preparedness Expo, which had a strong turnout. She stated that the community is encouraged to attend the upcoming Firewise Block Party on July 12, 2025.  She shared her appreciation to APS for their significant support of the Expo.
A presentation was shown.  Janet Dean, with APS, provided an update on APS's wildfire mitigation efforts in response to increasing drought and fire risks. She highlighted their comprehensive strategy, including vegetation management, grid hardening, advanced monitoring with AI-powered fire detection cameras, and operational changes like public safety power shutoffs (PSPS). She indicated that the shutoffs are a last-resort safety tool to proactively cut power during extreme fire conditions. She stated that APS is also working closely with local partners and communities, including Navajo County, to prepare and inform customers, emphasizing early warnings, communication, and public safety.
Supervisor Seymore expressed gratitude to APS for sponsoring the event, and thanked Catrina, Supervisor Whitesinger, and county staff for their efforts in organizing it. He indicated that the event was a great success and much needed in the community.
Supervisor Benally thanked APS for its community outreach and emphasized the importance of continued education on fire mitigation. She spoke of the recent brush fires on tribal lands and stressed the need to keep communities informed. She thanked Katrina for providing timely emergency information to residents and visitors.
Ms. Dean explained that while only a few power lines in Navajo County are currently impacted and more will likely be added in the future due to worsening drought conditions. She emphasized the importance of ongoing communication to help the public understand the necessity of the program.  She indicated that unlike California, they do not expect multi-day outages, estimating most shutoffs to last around 20 hours.
Chairman Whiting expressed gratitude to APS, Catrina, Vice Chair Whitesinger, and others involved in wildfire preparedness. He acknowledged the growing threat of natural disasters and commended APS for proactively working to mitigate wildfire risks. He shared his appreciation to APS for their collaborative approach and commitment to educating and informing partners and the community.
Supervisor Seymore expressed appreciation for the support and help that Catrina's husband provides to her, acknowledging his constant readiness to assist.
