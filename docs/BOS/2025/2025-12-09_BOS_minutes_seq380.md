PUBLIC WORKS:

a.

Consideration of award of B25-08-007 to Brimhall Sand Rock and Building Materials, Inc. for the supply and delivery of roadway materials to the Navajo Nation.
Madhav Mundle greeted the board and explained that the contract up for consideration is for materials only, for the purchase of aggregate-based material or gravel to be used on Navajo Nation Route N9806 within Navajo County District II.  Mr. Mundle stated that the purpose of the aggregate material or gravel is to improve surface conditions and durability of the road so that it will have less frequent maintenance work.  Mr. Mundle stated that with this contract, Navajo County will purchase the aggregate-based material for placement on a two-mile section of the roadway and that Navajo Nation Department of Transportation will perform the work.  Madhav explained that the contract was obtained following the procurement policies of Navajo County, two bids were received, and the bid opening was held on November 13, 2025. Brimhall Sand and Rock was the low bidder, and the details of the contract and pricing were included in the agenda packet.  Mr. Mundle stated that staff recommended the awarding of the contract to Brimhall Sand and Rock.
Supervisor Peshlakai thanked Mr. Mundle for the presentation and extended appreciation to Nicole Reske and Jeanine Carruthers for their administrative work on the project.  He explained that the project started with former Supervisor Thompson in 2018.  He expressed his thanks to the team for their efforts to advocate for the residents that utilize the road.
Supervisor Peshlakai made a motion to approve, motion seconded by  Vice Chairwoman Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
Supervisor Peshlakai  made a motion to enter into the Navajo County Flood Control District Board of Directors Session, motion seconded by  Vice Chairwoman Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES:

a.

Presentation to Jeff Lineberry on his Retirement from Navajo County after 25 Years of Service
Eric Scott greeted the board and recognized Jeff Linberry, who has been with Navajo County for 25 years.  Eric stated that Navajo County is made up of employees who dedicate years of their lives to serving the residents.  He invited Jeff to the podium and said that keeping the network and IT secure and safe often goes on behind the scenes and that Jeff has done an amazing job at keeping Navajo County safe. Jeff Lineberry then spoke, recognizing that the leadership and elected officials, past and present, as well as engaging with the other departments, have made Navajo County an exciting place to work at and that he will miss everyone he has worked with over the years. b.

Presentation to Melissa Buckley on her Retirement from Navajo County after 29 Years of Service
Chairman Whiting approached the podium and invited Melissa to join him. Chairman Whiting read a prepared statement recognizing Melissa, stating that she has meant a great deal to the Board and Navajo County.  The Chairman gave the history of Melissa's 29 years with Navajo County, stating that she started in 1994 and over the years has worked in many departments, the Assessor's Office, the Clerk of the Superior Court, Public Health, Administration, and eventually stepping into the role of the Clerk of the Board in 2009.  Chairman Whiting said that Melissa has brought professionalism, precision, and genuine love for public service into every position she has held.  Chairman Whiting further explained that Melissa's leadership has strengthened policies, streamlined processes, and bought a level of consistency and integrity that has benefited every office, department, and the community we serve. Chairman Whiting said that beyond work, Melissa is a remarkable member of the community, a devoted wife, a wonderful mother and daughter and an influence to the community.  On behalf of the Board of Supervisors, Chairman Whiting expressed heartfelt gratitude for Melissa's decades of service, friendship, and the example that she sets, wishing her joy, rest, and adventure in retirement.
Melissa Buckley read a prepared statement, saying that she has been blessed to work beside the most dedicated Department Directors, County Managers, and Elected Officials in the state. Melissa thanked everyone who showed up for her today.  She said that serving Navajo County has been part of who she is. Melissa thanked her husband, her family, the Arizona County Clerk's Association, and her team.  Melissa said that over the years her team members have made her hard days easier, her good days better, and memories unforgettable, stating that what she will miss the most is the work.  Melissa wrapped up by thanking God and asking God to bless the work, compassion, and calling in each of the Board and County Employees in service to the communities.
Vice Chairwoman Whitesinger said that she is grateful for Melissa's example and thanked her for all that she has shared with her and all that she has taught her.  She said that she has been inspired by Melissa and that Melissa has impacted her life.
Supervisor Benally said that she is grateful for Melissa and appreciates her professionalism, responsibility, and that Melissa has always gone over and above.  She wished Melissa the best and said that she thinks Melissa has made a great decision to be with her family and that everyone at Navajo County will miss her beautiful smile and well wishes of the day.  She thanked Melissa for all that she has done in service over the years.
Supervisor Seymore stated that Melissa has made the job of being a supervisor so much easier with the knowledge she's shared.  He told Melissa to keep scattering sunshine and thanked her for her friendship and devotion to the work.
Supervisor Peshlakai thanked Melissa for her friendship, reminiscing on how they started out as co-workers. He said that her forgiving heart strengthened the trust he had held in her and that he would miss her. He read a prepared statement thanking Melissa for her collaborative efforts to navigate three Native American Tribes, fostering strong relationships and mutual understanding, contributing to mutual understanding and the community's progress. CALL TO THE PUBLIC :
Chairman Whiting invited Boyd Barhoe to speak.  Boyd thanked the Board for letting him speak and expressed thanks to Will Flake, Bryan Layton and the team for trying to help with the flooding situation in his neighborhood, but that unfortunately it was unsuccessful.  He stated that he knows that the board cannot make a statement today but wanted to request that he and some neighbors be able to put ornamental aesthetic rock back in place to enhance the aesthetics of their homes as well as help with the sediment building.  He referred to photos that he provided to the Board and invited them to come to their neighborhood to see the situation firsthand.  He also brought up a section in his area where there was a shoulder and then a 5-foot drop off on the edge of the pavement that needs to be addressed.  He wished everyone Merry Christmas and appreciated the Board of Supervisors invoking scripture and prayer in their meetings.
Chairman Whiting invited Steve Bennett to speak.  He explained that he lives on Buckskin Court in Linden Trails and wants to speak on the same matter as Boyd. He said that he is a new resident in Linden Trails and shared specifics about the damage done by the culvert ditch process, referring to some photos he provided to the board. He said prior to the ditches being dug out there was no erosion on his specific property and now there is significant erosion.  He stated that the erosion is coming out through the ditch, now filling up the culvert of his neighbor. He asked the board to allow this topic to be discussed further, possibly presented with a future agenda item.  He also congratulated Jeff and Melissa on their retirements and stated that he was impressed with the fanfare given to the outgoing employees.
Supervisor Seymore commented that the area of Linden Trails is in his district and while he cannot comment on the Call to Public, he would like to continue the discussion with Public Works and the homeowner's association through a study session, agenda item, or meeting to rectify the situation.
