ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES :

a.

Update on the impact of changes in federal funding sources on Navajo County :
Jayson Vowell indicated that this item is to provide an update regarding how recent federal changes with funding is affecting Public Health and WIOA. He stated that they will highlight the impacts of these changes and outline how their departments are adjusting operations to continue providing services to citizens.
Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion  to enter into the Navajo County Public Health Services District Board of Directors Session, motion seconded by  Supervisor Benally .  Motion passed 4-0, with Supervisor Seymore being absent.
A presentation was shown.  Janelle Linn indicated that public health is undergoing fundamental, uncertain changes, that service reductions are directly affecting individuals and local economies, and advocacy is urgently needed to protect rural public health.  She stated that since January a series of major disruptions have taken place, including the withdrawal from the World Health Organization, workforce reductions, and multiple federal grant suspensions which have significantly reduced the department's ability to operate.  She indicated that in March key grants supporting immunization, health disparities, and disease surveillance were abruptly stopped resulting in the loss of staff and critical services. She further indicated that the restructuring of federal health agencies has further destabilized funding and ongoing threats to programs like WIC, overdose prevention, and emergency preparedness remain.  She stated that the elimination of vital funding has already led to reduced services including school immunization clinics, nutrition programs, community outreach, and mental health first aid. She spoke about the consequences of the Big Beautiful Bill, which cut $1 trillion from Medicaid, potentially affecting over 12,000 residents in Navajo County and placing more than 1,400 local healthcare jobs at risk. She indicated that these changes could also result in rural hospital closures, increased emergency room costs, and widespread loss of healthcare access.  She indicated that politically driven decisions are undermining science-based public health strategies and that programs proven to reduce chronic disease, substance use, and incarceration rates are being cut, leading to worsening community health outcomes and long-term economic consequences.  She further indicated that food insecurity is rising, chronic disease rates are expected to grow, and school absenteeism linked to hunger may increase.
She requested support for flexible public health funding, participation in workgroups distributing rural health funds, advocacy for science-based policies, and stronger public engagement to help tell the story of public health. She indicated that without immediate and sustained advocacy the damage to the public health infrastructure could have generational consequences, particularly for rural communities like Navajo County.
Vice-Chair Whitesinger shared her appreciation for the information provided during the presentation.  She spoke about the importance of data-driven messaging over broad assumptions to effectively convey the challenges facing healthcare in Navajo County.  She thanked the Public Health staff for their dedication, creativity, and sacrifices during difficult budget decisions, including volunteering reduced hours to support one another. She committed to continuing to support the Health Department and rural healthcare more broadly and offered to be a voice for advocacy while working collaboratively to strengthen community health efforts.
Brad Carlyon spoke about how federal health funding cuts will strain not only the health department but also the criminal justice system, leading to more cases, higher costs, and greater demand on courts, law enforcement, and public defenders. He indicated that public health and justice are deeply connected, and broad advocacy is needed to protect vital services.
Chairman Whiting thanked Ms. Linn and her team for their continued efforts and for offering solutions. He indicated that they hope to stay involved in the conversation and ensure all county leaders and citizens are informed and engaged in advocating for essential services.
Supervisor Peshlakai made a motion  to return to regular session, motion seconded by  Vice-Chair Whitesinger .  Motion passed 4-0, with Supervisor Seymore being absent.
A presentation was shown.  Adam Gerrard spoke about the current state WIOA, which supports job training, youth employment, and economic development across Navajo, Apache, and Gila counties, including tribal lands, noting they have seen a 12% budget cut and the loss of key personnel and services. He explained that staffing reductions have forced the consolidation of multiple roles into single positions, and the number of job coaches have been cut in half, from six to three, severely limiting the department's ability to connect individuals with training programs. He stated that one workforce center has already been lost, and the remaining three are at risk due to increasing costs and reduced funding, noting that these centers are vital for services like job searches, resume building, and unemployment assistance.  He spoke about how training accounts were cut making it impossible to fund essential career programs like CDL training and that youth funding has either been eliminated or drastically reduced, directly affecting young people's ability to access education and career development, especially in rural areas where alternatives are limited.
He spoke about the impact of these cuts on local economic growth, tax revenue, and the region's ability to meet demand in sectors like healthcare, transportation, and IT. He requested support from local leaders and citizens to ensure Congress understands the vital need for continued support.
Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Gerrard and his team for all they do.  He requested that staff identify areas, from both presentations, that the Board can advocate and provide support, noting that they want to make sure to help in any way they can.

ELECTIONS :

a.

Consideration of Resolution Number 27-2025 , adopting the vote precinct maps pursuant to A.R.S. §16-411 :
Rayleen Richards stated that in October 2023, following the most recent approval to precinct boundaries, there were two precincts created in error, Precincts 19 and 20, due to a misalignment between a Justice of the Peace boundary and a legislative district boundary. She indicated that the precincts are extremely small, only 0.16 miles wide and 983 feet long, and contain no registered voters.  She stated that to correct the issue staff recommend combining Precincts 19 and 20 into neighboring Precinct 16, which is the most geographically and demographically appropriate, noting that the adjustment will have no effect on voter access or election outcomes.  She further stated that to maintain consistent precinct numbering, Precinct 21 will be renumbered to Precinct 19, preventing a gap in the sequence. She indicated that no other changes will be made to precinct boundaries or names. She stated that the realignment ensures the integrity and accuracy of Navajo County's precinct map moving forward.
Vice-Chair Whitesinger inquired if there are any homesteads or a chance of having anyone that would register to vote in that area.
Ms. Richards indicated that there are no homes or voters in the area.
Vice-Chair Whitesinger made a motion  to approve  Resolution Number 27-2025 , adopting the vote precinct maps pursuant to A.R.S. §16-411, motion seconded by  Supervisor Peshlakai .  Motion passed 4-0, with Supervisor Seymore being absent.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE: Report from County Manager, County Attorney and Board members :
Supervisor Peshlakai reported that he has been attending regular chapter meetings and providing quarterly reports instead of monthly ones, which has been well received by the communities. He shared his appreciation to Will and Alicia for their updates at the Joseph City Chamber of Commerce meeting regarding street lighting and related projects.  He indicated that coordination has been ongoing with a New Mexico nonprofit, La Planta, to distribute fresh produce, mostly from California farms to various locations in Navajo County, including healthcare centers and chapter houses, noting that this effort aims to introduce new and nutritious foods to local residents, including the Navajo and Hopi communities.  He stated that he will be participating in the Hopi Health and Wellness Fair, noting that it will be an opportunity for community engagement.  He spoke about his involvement with the National Association of Counties, focusing on federal budget impacts, state health steering committees, and local healthcare boards. He indicated that collaboration with the University of Arizona Cooperative Extension is ongoing to address staffing cuts and ensure continued community service, with local healthcare providers stepping in temporarily to fill gaps.
He stated that he remains active in attending community events, senior centers, veterans meetings, and other responsibilities in District Two.
Supervisor Benally reported on attending the Western Navajo Agency Council meeting. She indicated that she participated in welcoming horse trail riders crossing the reservation, encouraging them to engage in advocacy.  She further indicated that work continues on road projects primarily in the Black Mesa region, including grader and gravel work, noting that they are awaiting the Navajo Nation President's signature on an intergovernmental agreement (IGA) for a bridge project in Kayenta. She spoke about them looking into grants for several road projects along US Highways 160, 163, and State Route 89.  She indicated that she has been working with the chapters, attending veterans meetings by invitation, and delivering senior center checks.
Vice-Chair Whitesinger reported on the recent transportation meeting with the White Mountain Apache Tribe to discuss transportation partnerships and how to effectively support the small communities within her district.  She indicated that the focus for the upcoming year will be supporting Cibecue and its existing projects. She stated that the partnership has taken time to build but has grown stronger, allowing better collaboration on transportation needs, such as a recently completed bus turnaround project.  She spoke about attending the Small Counties meeting where rural healthcare was a major topic, noting that efforts are ongoing to build a collective voice advocating for rural healthcare, with outreach to lawmakers including Senator Mark Kelly's office. She spoke about the hard work by local advocates aimed at raising awareness about rural healthcare's impact on communities.  She stated that she will be a featured speaker at the First Things First Early Childhood Summit on October 10th, focusing on the importance of preserving traditional language in early childhood services and how providers can support this in communities.
