Department: Facilities Mangement
Presented By: Grant Evans
Motion before the Board:
Consideration of entering into a Dark Fiber Indefeasible Right of Use (IRU) Agreement with
Frontier Communications of America, Inc.
Background:
On May 13, 2025, the Board of Supervisors (BOS) approved the pricing structure and
associated IRU agreement template for leasing of the Navajo County Middle Mile broadband
fiber. Utilizing those documents, staff and our commercialization partner, eX2 Technology,
LLC, have negotiated this Dark Fiber IRU Agreement with Frontier Communications of
America, Inc. This IRU agreement sets forth a 20-year indefeasible right to use four (4) fibers
on the county's middle mile network for the entire 106-miles of system routes. Approval of this
IRU is a major step forward in the development and commercialization of the County's Middle
Mile fiber project and will improve and enhance broadband availability, reliability and capacity
throughout our County.
 
Recommendation: 
Staff recommends entering into this Dark Fiber IRU Agreement with Frontier Communications
of America, Inc.

Testing Procedures and Standards 
 
1. All splices will be performed with an industry-accepted fusion splicing machine. 
 
2. Splices will be qualified during initial construction with an Optical Time Domain Reflectometer 
(OTDR) from only one direction. The profile alignment system or light injection detection system on 
the fusion splicer may be used to qualify splices in lieu of an OTDR, provided both parties agree a 
close correlation to the OTDR values can be established.  
 
3. Bi-directional span testing will be done from either fiber termination panel to fiber termination panel 
or via bare fiber adaptor at route endpoints.  
 
3.1 Installed loss measurements at 1550 nm will be recorded using an industry -accepted laser 
source. 
 
3.2 OTDR traces will be taken at 1550 nm and splice loss measurements will be analyzed. GN 
Nettest Laser Precision or EXFO format will be used on all traces, unless another OTDR 
format is agreed to by both parties. Launch reels will not be used for bare fiber OTDR traces, 
if applicable. 
 
3.3 OTDR traces shall be saved in a standard file naming convention. 
 
3.4  Traces will be evaluated and stored on Grantor’s file storage. 
 
4. The splicing standards are as follows: 
 
4.1 The objective for each fiber within a span shall be an average bi -directional loss of 0.30dB 
or less for each splice . For example, at each splice location, all fiber splice losses will be 
averaged with the objective of the overall average splice loss for each location being 0.30 
dB. 
 
5. All splices will be protected with heat shrinks or may be stored in trays with bare fiber manifolds. An 
industry-accepted non -encapsulated splice enclosure will be used on all splices CommScope 
FOSC-450D.  
 
6. The entire fiber optic cable system shall be properly protected from foreign voltage and grounded 
with an industry-accepted system. 
 
7. Connectors shall be Ultra polished SC with conventional single mode glass.
