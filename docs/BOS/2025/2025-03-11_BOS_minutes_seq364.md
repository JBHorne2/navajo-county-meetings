NAVAJO COUNTY PUBLIC HEALTH SERVICES DISTRICT: Board of Directors Session :

REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

PUBLIC HEARING:  Consideration of the amendment of Ordinance 01-19 Outdoor Fire Ordinance :
Catrina Jenkins discussed the annual review of the fire season ordinance. She spoke about aligning local regulations with federal and state guidelines, especially as many properties border federal forests. She indicated that the changes made to the ordinance are minor, including adding definitions for "explosives" and "firearms," and reorganizing the exemptions for prohibited items, for example, the exemption for using propane or LPG fuel for fires has been moved to be directly under the prohibited item to make it clearer for the public and partners. She stated that she is available for any questions of the Board.

Supervisor Seymore shared his appreciation to Ms. Jenkins and team for the efforts made, noting that there are concerns out there.  She thanked them for making the changes to the ordinance so that it's clearer and easier to understand.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to approve amendment of Ordinance 01-19 Outdoor Fire Ordinance.

Vice-Chair Whitesinger opened the meeting for Public Hearing.  There being no comments, the Public Hearing was closed.

The motion was seconded by  Supervisor Peshlakai .  Motion passed 4-0, with Chairman Whiting being absent.

NAVAJO COUNTY FLOOD CONTROL DISTRICT: Board of Directors Session :

REGULAR AGENDA :

a.
Consideration of an Intergovernmental Agreement with the Town of Taylor for their request for financial assistance for the Rail Road Grade drainage project in the amount of $68,000 :
Will Flake stated that this is an intergovernmental agreement with the Town of Taylor, located in District Three, regarding a FEMA grant for the Railroad Grade Drainage Project. He indicated that the grant requires a 25% local match, which the county will contribute up to $68,000. He stated that the project involves constructing a detention basin near Paper Mill Road and directing water through the Railroad Grade Wash, crossing Centennial Boulevard, noting that the project is nearly complete.

Supervisor Seymore expressed concerns about the use of Flood Control funds, mentioning recent contributions to projects with the City of Show Low and the current project with Taylor. He indicated that, while these are valuable projects, there are areas in the county without city participation where the county has to fund flood control efforts entirely on its own, noting that he would like to see more flood control projects in the county using local funds, especially in areas that don't benefit from FEMA grants or city assistance.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to approve an Intergovernmental Agreement with the Town of Taylor for their request for financial assistance for the Rail Road Grade drainage project in the amount of $68,000, motion seconded by  Supervisor Peshlakai .  Motion passed 4-0, with Chairman Whiting being absent.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE: Report from County Manager, County Attorney and Board members :
Rochelle Lacapa provided an update on the listening sessions for the new Renewable Energy Ordinance, indicating that the sessions are scheduled and available on the website for public access, where people can also submit comments online. She stated that the in-person listening sessions will take place in Show Low on March 24, 2025; Heber on March 26, 2025; and Joseph City on March 27, 2025, all from 6 to 8 PM.
Supervisor Seymore stated that he shared a handout with the Supervisors, which was presented by CSA to state legislators to highlight the gap between rising state revenue shares and the slower growth of county revenue. He indicated that the handout aims to educate new state leaders on the financial struggles of counties, especially when state mandates burden local budgets. He spoke about ongoing efforts to oppose legislation that could negatively affect Navajo County, including changes to voting precincts and subdivision regulations that could undermine local control and planning.
Supervisor Benally reported on attending the NACO Legislative Conference in Washington, DC, noting that she attended several of the committee meetings, such as Transportation, RAC, WIR, and Immigration.  She stated that she met with Senator Mark Kelly and his staff about issues affecting Navajo and Hopi lands, including roads, water rights, and education.  She spoke about Leonard Peltier, who was a political prisoner released on clemency after nearly 50 years, noting his connection to Indigenous rights and activism.  She further spoke about the death of an Elder from the Big Mountain region, offering condolences and thanking the Navajo County Sheriff's Office for their efforts in the search.  She indicated that the Navajo Nation Chapters are having elections today and spoke about times when the polls will close due to being on Daylight Savings Time.
Vice-Chair Whitesinger shared her gratitude for the ongoing work in local communities.  She spoke about the recent moisture that we have received and addressed concerns about the drought and the potential for wildfires, noting that the White Mountain Apache Tribal community has held meetings with local partners to address fire preparedness. She reminded everyone about the Fire Preparedness Expo on March 29, 2025, in Pinetop Lakeside and emphasized the importance of advocating for federal funding for wildfire mitigation. She thanked everyone who has supported these efforts.
