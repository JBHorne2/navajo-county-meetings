Consideration and adoption of the proposed Tentative Budget for Fiscal Year 2025-26 for Navajo County :
A presentation was shown. Jayson Vowell presented an overview of the county's budget priorities and financial outlook for the upcoming fiscal year. He indicated that the focus has been a balanced budget approach ensuring recurring revenues meet or exceed recurring expenditures, noting that the county is seeing slowing growth in sales tax revenue and is preparing for a projected future decrease in property tax revenue due to the closure of the Cholla Power Plant, though this won't impact the current year.  He stated that the focus has also been on employee compensation and benefits, which includes recommendations for a modest 2.5% Cost of Living Adjustment for all employees, continued funding for good job pay to reward exceptional performance, the introduction of a sabbatical leave program for employees and a proposal to use previously one-time payments as incentives for completing annual physicals, promoting preventive healthcare and reducing benefit costs.  He indicated that that they would not be recommending the anniversary step program, which was the biannual 2.5% increase, but would be looking transition toward a performance-based merit pay system going forward.  Mr. Vowell spoke about the new sabbatical leave program being proposed, noting that it offers additional leave every 5 years of service as follows, 5 years – 2 extra days, 10 years – 1 extra week and 15 years & every 5 years after – 2 weeks, with the option to cash out 1 week.  He indicated that this one-time leave, per milestone year, aims to boost employee morale, retention, and offer flexibility, including the option to fund special vacations or personal time. Mr. Vowell provided an overview of the ongoing capital replacement efforts, which are budgeted annually to maintain essential equipment, infrastructure, and facilities.  He stated that each year, approximately 10 patrol vehicles are replaced at a cost of $70,000 to $80,000 each, funded through the Inmate Housing Fund, in addition to approximately 10 non-patrol vehicles are also replaced annually, averaging $32,000 each, with funding coming from the General Fund.  He indicated that technology replacements are also a regular part of the budget, noting that the county follows a four- to five-year replacement cycle for employee computers and laptops due to wear and obsolescence. He further indicated that additional IT upgrades are planned, including network improvements, server updates, and new systems like the Treasurer's system and NeoGov for HR.  He stated that for the current year, the county plans to invest approximately $1.5 million to upgrade the security system, which would include doors and cameras, at the county complex and jail, due to aging infrastructure and limited availability of replacement parts. He indicated that another $50,000 is allocated for bathroom remodels in areas near the courts, continuing the refresh work that has already taken place and that $112,000 is set aside for remodeling the Silver Creek Facility in Snowflake to create more usable office and meeting space as departments relocate to the new Taylor facility.  Mr. Vowell stated that, looking ahead, two significant projects are on the horizon, including a roof replacement and an HVAC system upgrade for the main facility, each estimated at around $1.5 million, noting that these planned investments are essential for maintaining operational continuity and improving the county's long-term infrastructure.  Mr. Vowell reviewed the proposed FY26 county budget totals of approximately $246.6 million, reflecting a decrease from the $255 million budget in FY25, noting that the reduction is largely due to the drawdown of one-time funds, such as ARPA and broadband grants.  He indicated that for FY26, the General Fund includes estimated expenditures of $68.4 million, with projected primary property tax revenue of $8.8 million and other revenues contributing the rest. He further indicated that about $11 million is set aside for future financial stability, roughly 25% of estimated actual FY25 expenditures.  He stated that special revenue funds are budgeted at $140.6 million, with estimated non-property tax revenues of $62.5 million, and debt service remains stable at $2.3 million.  He indicated that capital projects are budgeted at $35 million, with ongoing investments continuing across county infrastructure.  Mr. Vowell indicated that the FY26 county budget projects just over $49 million in General Fund revenues, with 77% of that coming from state-shared sales tax, county sales tax, and property tax, noting that the property tax rate will remain flat at 0.8114, though collections will rise modestly due to increased property values. He further indicated that the county continues to levy below the maximum allowable amount, with FY26's levy projected at $8.8 million, about $564,000 under the cap.  He stated that sales tax collections have been strong in recent years, exceeding budgeted expectations and that FY26 projections are set at $18.1 million for state-shared sales tax and $10.6 million for county sales tax, however, revenue estimates have been scaled back slightly in anticipation of slower growth.  He indicated that the general fund expenditures are projected at $50.7 million, excluding contingency and that spending is primarily allocated to public safety, criminal justice, and mandated services like elections, indigent health, and courts, noting that about 65% of general fund expenses go toward salaries and employee benefits, with additional costs including supplies, services, capital outlay, and required state payments.  He stated that capital investments from the general fund include IT systems, building improvements, vehicles, and equipment, with key recommendations such as a new part-time custodial position, market salary adjustments in select departments, IT security enhancements, and new sheriff staff positions.  He further stated that in the Special Revenue Funds, the Public Health, Flood Control, and Library Districts will maintain flat secondary property tax rates, noting that the only increase is for the Joseph City Street Lighting District, which will see its levy rise to catch up on overdue costs. He indicated that the Health District expects to receive $2.8 million in property tax revenue and another $3 million in grant funding, which is down from $5 million last year due to reduced federal support.  He further indicated that the Flood Control District will take in $2.3 million in property tax and fund projects such as drainage basins and partnerships with local municipalities and that the Library District will receive around $1 million to support countywide library services and resources.  Mr. Vowell stated that the Jail District, funded by a dedicated sales tax, expects $6.9 million in revenue and that approximately 47% of its spending will go to salaries, with another 22% dedicated to medical services. He indicated that a contingency reserve of nearly 20% is built into the budget.  He stated that Public Works is supported mainly by Highway User Revenue Funds (HURF) and vehicle license taxes, with a total FY26 budget of $27 million, including contingency and that projects include road maintenance, tribal partnerships, and the replacement of aging equipment.  He stated that debt service totals $2.1 million for FY26, split between pension bond payments and a refinancing bond, noting that the refinancing is expected to save taxpayers over $11 million.  He stated that capital projects for FY26 total $35.1 million, with the bulk of it, approximately $30 million, being reserved for the Winslow Levee project. He indicated that other significant efforts include broadband infrastructure expansion, funded through ACA and ARPA grants, facility improvements at the Taylor site, and upgrades to the sheriff's communication systems.
Chairman Whiting expressed appreciation to staff for the detailed budget presentation, noting the extensive preparation, including individual meetings and a prior work session.

PUBLIC HEARING:  Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 20-2025 , approving/denying a Special Use Permit to expand an existing storage unit facility to an adjacent, 5.15-acre parcel in the Lakeside area :

A presentation was shown. Katherine Serna presented on the item, indicating that Ironside Engineering is requesting a special use permit to expand the EZ Storage facility in Lakeside onto an adjacent 5.15-acre parcel. She indicated that the project includes approximately 10 storage unit buildings of varying sizes with fencing, noting that some buildings will be used as borders in lieu of fencing. Katherine stated that no public comments have been received and that staff recommend approval with the 16 conditions listed in the staff report.

Chairman Whiting opened the meeting for public comments.  There being no comments, the public hearing was closed.

Supervisor Seymore spoke about the fencing, hours of operations and storage restrictions.  He proposed the following additional conditions, decorative or permanent fencing, not chain link with slats, to protect neighboring areas; non-restrictive business hours, allowing flexibility for customers to access the facility outside of regular business hours; and prohibiting storage of mobile homes or tiny homes.

Zack Barlow, with Ironside Engineering, clarified that the proposed hours reflect existing office hours (9–5, Mon–Sat), while facility access extends beyond those hours via key card or code. He expressed openness to any specific fencing conditions and to prohibit mobile home or tiny home storage as requested.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to adopt
Resolution Number 20-2025 , approving a Special Use Permit to expand an existing storage unit facility to an adjacent, 5.15-acre parcel in the Lakeside area, with conditions listed by staff and additional conditions that the fencing must be decorative and suitable to future neighborhood developments and that manufactured or tiny homes storage is prohibited on the property, motion seconded by
Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

b.

PUBLIC HEARING: Consideration of possible adoption of Resolution Number 21-2025 approving/denying an amendment to the fee schedule for services provided to the public by the Planning Division of the Planning & Development Services Department in connection with Planning related reviews and permits :
Cody Cooper stated that following last month's adoption of the zoning ordinance, staff is proposing minor updates to the fee schedule, as follows, Increase the fee cap for zone changes with comprehensive plan amendments and special use permits for renewable energy projects from $10,000 to $25,000 due to the significant staff time required; Clarify fees for renewable energy facilities in both the ordinance and fee schedule, given their long-term permitting and tracking needs; and Make minor adjustments to seasonal RV permit fees. He indicated that no other fee increases or changes are proposed and that this update is primarily to align the fee schedule with the new zoning ordinance.
Chairman Whiting opened the meeting for public comments.  There being no comments, the public hearing was closed.
Vice-Chair Whitesinger inquired how the proposed fee increase was determined, specifically whether it was based solely on increased staff time or if comparable fees from other jurisdictions were also considered.
Cody stated that the proposed increase was based on both staff time and a comparison with similar fees across Arizona, noting that the new fees are mid-range compared to other jurisdictions, where some fees reach $50,000–$100,000.  He indicated that the goal is to remaining competitive and encourage development.
Vice-Chair Whitesinger made a motion  to adopt  Resolution Number 21-2025 approving an amendment to the fee schedule for services provided to the public by the Planning Division of the Planning & Development Services Department in connection with Planning related reviews and permits, motion seconded by  Supervisor Peshlakai .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

NAVAJO COUNTY FLOOD CONTROL DISTRICT : Board of Directors Session :

1.

Project update for the Winslow Levee at the Little Colorado River:
A presentation was shown.  Will Flake shared an update on the Winslow Levee Project, highlighting key milestones reached in advancing the project's design with the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers. He indicated that the total design cost is estimated at $7 million, with the county contributing approximately $2.5 million, some of which is covered through in-kind work such as survey and aerial mapping.  He reviewed the portions of the project that have been completed, including survey and aerial mapping by HDR and subconsultants, establishment of control and benchmark points for use during design and construction, and a geotechnical investigation conducted by the Army Corps, involving extensive soil testing and borings to assess levee and borrow areas.  He reviewed the preliminary timeline, including 35% design by July 2026, 65% by August 2027, and final 95% design by late 2029, noting that they will have a more accurate projections following the completion of the project management plan. He indicated that further updates will be provided as the project progresses.
Supervisor Peshlakai shared his appreciation for the update on the Winslow Levee Project, recognizing it as a long-anticipated effort. He thanked staff and stakeholders for maintaining momentum and emphasized the importance of continued progress and communication.
Supervisor Peshlakai made a motion  to return to regular session, motion seconded by  Vice-Chair Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
