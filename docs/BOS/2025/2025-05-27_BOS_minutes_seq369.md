SHERIFF'S OFFICE :

a.

Presentation of a Medical Aid Award to Navajo County Sheriff's Office dispatcher Kendal Webster, and Sheriff's Life-Saving Award(s) to Navajo County Sheriff's Office Deputy Austin Huddleston and Show Low Police Department dispatcher Amber Price :
This item was moved to the end of the agenda at the request of Sheriff Clouse. At 10:11 a.m. Sheriff David Clouse honored three employees; Deputy Austin Huddleston, dispatcher Kendall Webster, and dispatcher Amber Price, for their heroic efforts in saving the life of Gary Mitchell, a community volunteer, who suffered sudden cardiac arrest at home. Sheriff Clouse stated that Kendall quickly and calmly handled the 911 call, coordinating with other agencies. Amber provided CPR guidance over the phone to Mitchell's wife, and Deputy Huddleston arrived within minutes to continue life-saving CPR until paramedics arrived. Sheriff Clouse credited their teamwork and professionalism with saving Mitchell's life and praised their actions. Each employee was presented with an award. Gary Mitchell emphasized that he experienced a sudden cardiac arrest, which typically has only 10% survival rate outside of a hospital. He expressed deep gratitude to the responders, stating they are the reason he is alive today. Sheriff Clouse thanked the Board of Supervisors for allowing him to make the presentation today. Supervisor Seymore praised Mrs. Mitchell for her excellent efforts performing CPR without prior training, guided by dispatcher Amber Price for nearly six minutes. He further thanked everyone involved for their quick, skilled actions that saved Mr. Mitchell's life, noting how his survival is a blessing and a testament to the value of service and teamwork. Supervisor Benally commended the team for their outstanding lifesaving efforts, emphasizing how difficult CPR is from personal experience as a former nurse. She expressed gratitude to the deputies, dispatchers, and all involved for their swift action and community service, and thanked the sheriff for sharing the story, noting it was a well-deserved honor. Vice-Chair Whitesinger expressed deep appreciation for the opportunity to recognize the individuals involved, praising them as exemplary public servants with compassionate hearts. She thanked everyone, including the sheriff and chief, for their dedication to community safety and for allowing this recognition to take place.

SUPERINTENDENT OF SCHOOLS :

a.

Consideration of approval to enter into an agreement with Legacy Learning LLC and NCAD (Navajo County Accomodation District) to provide Dropout Recovery services for students in Navajo County :
Superintendent of Schools Rachael Lewis stated that she is excited to introduce a new Dropout Recovery Program to the Accommodation District. She reported a 14% dropout rate in Navajo County and stated that the program aims to re-engage students who have left high school, addressing not just personal and societal impacts, but also significant economic consequences. She estimated at over $400,000 per dropout. Superintendent Lewis stated that after reviewing three potential providers, the district chose Legacy Learning Community, a local organization based in Show Low, due to their strong commitment to personal student support and understanding of local needs. She stated that the program will use the district's accreditation while Legacy provides curriculum and support services. Superintendent Lewis requested approval to move forward with this partnership. Supervisor Seymore thanked Superintendent Lewis for taking a proactive role in trying to help students as he knows that it does make a difference.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion  to approve entering into an agreement with Legacy Learning LLC and NCAD (Navajo County Accommodation District) to provide Dropout Recovery services for students in Navajo County, motion seconded by  Supervisor Peshlakai .  Motion passed 4-0, with Chairman Whiting being absent.

PLANNING & DEVELOPMENT SERVICES :

a.

Update on Renewable Ordinance Discussion :
Mischa Larischintroduces the presentation stating that Mr. Cody Cooper will provide an overview of the timeline and changes related to the upcoming renewable ordinance. He further stated that after Cody's presentation, he would explain the proposed development agreement being recommended as part of the process. Cody Cooper provided a comprehensive overview of Navajo County's proposed Renewable Energy Ordinance, which encompasses both solar and wind energy regulations. Mr. Cooper stated that this was developed through public outreach and board work sessions, the ordinance establishes general development standards for both energy types, along with specific sections tailored to each. He further stated that it introduces a development agreement option that may be used alongside or instead of a special use permit and includes preferred criteria such as placing projects on less desirable land. Mr. Cooper reported on the stronger fire safety language that has been codified, ensuring local fire agencies are involved in the review and construction processes. He reported on the enhanced road use agreements, stating that they will now cover both pre- and post-construction phases. Mr. Cooper stated that the ordinance will also require project developers to consult with nearby municipalities or tribal entities within a five-mile radius and further mandates the use of the best commercially available technology to minimize environmental impacts. Mr. Cooper reported on the environmental, cultural, wetland, and visual impact studies that will be required for all renewable projects. He stated that additional provisions include setback waivers for non-participating properties, adjusted setback and screening standards for solar developments, and a fee structure specific to renewable energy projects due to their complexity. Mr. Cooper closed by stating that originally Planning and Development expected that this item would be brought forward to the Board of Supervisors at the July meeting, however the ordinance is now set to be presented on June 24, ahead of schedule, with the potential for adoption by the end of next month.   Supervisor Seymore thanked Mr. Cooper for working hard on the item and expressed his appreciation for all the efforts put into the ordinance. Mischa Larisch provided an overview of development agreements, which are contracts between a county and a developer or landowner that define specific terms and conditions for a development project. Mr. Larisch stated that these agreements are common across several Arizona counties, and often address aspects like construction phases, infrastructure requirements, and alignment with the county's comprehensive plan. Mr. Larisch reported that the proposal includes amending the zoning ordinance to allow the development agreements to either in place of or alongside special use permits. He stated that development agreements would be used for larger projects and special use permits would be used for smaller ones. Mr. Larisch explained that a development agreement becomes legally binding once approved by the Board of Supervisors and recorded with the County Recorder's Office. He further reviewed key components of a development agreement, which include identification of involved parties, property descriptions, terms and conditions (such as duration, permitted uses, density, infrastructure needs, and development phases), and any other project-specific requirements. Once recorded, development agreements offer benefits like certainty, stability, predictability, and improved collaboration between developers and the county. Mr. Larisch stated that the proposed process would involve legal counsel in developing a standardized template, and developers would work closely with county staff, administration, and legal teams to finalize agreements. Mr. Larisch indicated that Supervisors from the project's district may participate in negotiations, and public involvement will be preserved through required community input meetings and opportunities for public comment at Board meetings before adoption. Supervisor Seymore asked whether development agreements, like special use permits, which are typically reviewed every two to three years, can include similar review conditions, and whether the process for approving a development agreement still requires going through the Planning and Zoning Commission or if it bypasses that step and goes directly to the Board of Supervisors through internal collaboration? Mischa Larisch explained that while the development agreement process still includes public input, it does not go through the Planning and Zoning Commission. Mr. Larisch indicated that instead, it is streamlined directly through the Board of Supervisors, making the process more efficient. Vice-Chair Whitesinger expressed her gratitude and echoed the sentiments of Supervisor Seymore, appreciating the thorough and thoughtful development of the process. She emphasized the importance of collaboration, especially in addressing unexpected needs and ensuring all aspects, such as emergency access and partnerships with tribal and non-tribal communities, are considered. Vice-Chair Whitesinger commended the effort to create a strong, inclusive policy that supports investment and partnership within Navajo County.
