BOARD OF SUPERVISORS:
a. Consideration and approval of a proclamation declaring September 10
through September 13, 2025, as "Navajo County Fair Days" :
Rusty Despain, Navajo County President, introduced Navajo County Fair
staff, Mina Henning and DJ Beatty.  He thanked the Board for the
partnership with Navajo County.  He stated that they were able to do an
economic study this year and spoke about the amount of money
generated by the Navajo County Fair for their facility as well as the
region.  He indicated that they hold approximately sixty-two events a year,
not counting the fair, which is the biggest event.  He stated that the fair will
start on Wednesday, September 10 at 5:30 p.m., indicating that he hopes
everyone will be able to attend.  He thanked Bryan Layton for the
relationship that has been established this year, noting that they have had
a lot of new developments this year.  He spoke about the joint city/county
asphalt project that was done this year, noting that it will be nice especially
during inclement weather.  He stated that they have a new sky box in the
arena, which was sponsored by Mr. Hendricks, that they have a new grain
bin in the pig barns, and have upgraded to LED’s, which was also a joint
project with the county.  He talked about how the partnership has been
beneficial in place of requesting funds.  He spoke about the need to
upgrade the grandstands, as well as expand them, which would be more
economical for them.  He thanked the Board for all that they do, stated that
the fair starts Wednesday and invited everyone to attend.  
Chairman Jason Whiting shared his appreciation to Mr. Despain and the
Fair Board for their leadership and effective partnership. He commend the
organization for operating with a business-minded approach, balancing
growth with financial responsibility, and for prioritizing service to citizens
over personal gain. He indicated that the success of the recent county fair
is acknowledged as a reflection of this strong leadership and
collaboration. He shared his appreciation for the forward-thinking mindset,
especially in plans like expanding the grandstands, and emphasized how
such actions benefit the community and ensure long-term sustainability.
Supervisor Seymore expressed his appreciation for the continued legacy
of past leaders.  He spoke about the importance of teamwork and thanked
the many volunteers who make events possible, highlighting the value of
youth programs like 4-H, sharing a personal story about how it helped
shape his own life. He spoke about the positive impact on the community
and commend the current leadership’s vision.  The thanked the Fair Board
and noting that future collaboration is welcomed. 
Supervisor Benally thanked the Fair Board and acknowledged the work
they’ve done. She spoke about the timing of the Navajo County Fair in
relation to the Navajo Nation Fair, noting that many of residents in her
district attend both fairs.  She expressed appreciation for the
improvements made, noting that she is looking forward to the County Fair.
Mr. Despain stated that they’ve contracted with LA Williams, a well-known
voice in the Navajo Nation and active social media presence, to help
promote the fair. He indicated that the goal is to reach residents on the
reservation, reminding them that they are part of the county and
encouraging their participation in the fair.
Chairman Whiting read the proposed proclamation.

6. Is the project evidence based or evidence informed? Please detail the evidence 
based/informed practices the project will utilize to establish an effective process, as well 
as detailed project evaluation to measure effectiveness. 
 
Research demonstrates that peer support programs in correctional settings are a vital 
evidence-based practice for addressing opioid use disorder (OUD) and that they 
demonstrate effectiveness in promoting recovery and reducing recidivism. Studies 
published in the Journal of Addiction Medicine (Hamilton et al., 2022) highlight how 
peer support specialists, drawing from their lived experience with substance use, 
recovery, and justice involvement are uniquely positioned to build trust and 
engagement with incarcerated individuals with OUD. This trust-based relationship 
proves especially powerful in jail settings, where individuals may be resistant to 
traditional treatment approaches. Research by Martin et al. (2021) in the International 
Journal of Prisoner Health indicates that when peer support is integrated into 
correctional healthcare services, there are measurable improvements in treatment

<!-- page 83 -->
adherence, particularly with MAT/MOUD services (Medication Assisted 
Treatment/Medications for Opioid Use Disorder). 
 
The evidence supporting peer support programs goes beyond the duration an individual 
is incarcerated, with studies showing their crucial role in successful community 
reintegration. According to a systematic review by Gormley et al. (2021), individuals 
who engage with peer support specialists while incarcerated are more likely to continue 
their recovery journey post-release, with higher rates of treatment engagement and 
lower rates of relapse. This effectiveness stems from peers' ability to navigate both the 
correctional environment and recovery landscape, providing practical guidance while 
offering hope through their own recovery stories. Stack et al. (2022) particularly 
emphasizes how peer support programs create a bridge between jail-based treatment 
and community services, helping to close the critical gap where many individuals 
traditionally fall through the cracks of care. These evidence-based outcomes have led 
correctional facilities nationwide to increasingly recognize peer support as an essential 
component of comprehensive OUD treatment strategies. 
 
By using peer mentors, we bring real life specialists who have their own story of 
recovery and utilize their own experience to help those by promoting recovery-oriented 
environments. Investing in peer support specialists is a valuable addition to correctional 
facilities, providing both immediate support for incarcerated individuals and potential 
long-term benefits for successful reintegration into society.

for treating women with substance use disorders (SUD), especially opioid use disorder 
(OUD), in correctional settings. 
 
The program will primarily use the SMART Recovery curriculum for group sessions. A 
2018 longitudinal study found that SMART Recovery is a viable alternative to 12-step 
groups, with comparable outcomes in abstinence and self-efficacy, and may be 
particularly accessible for those who do not connect with traditional abstinence-only 
groups (Zemore et al., 2018). Recent protocols for longitudinal studies continue to 
reinforce the program’s real-world effectiveness across diverse populations (Kelly et al., 
2023), particularly for justice-involved women who may not connect with abstinence-
only approaches. SMART Recovery’s emphasis on self-management, coping skills, and 
relapse prevention aligns with best practices for incarcerated women, many of whom 
have complex trauma histories or co-occurring disorders. 
 
All participants will be screened using the Texas Christian University Drug Screen V 
(TCUDS V) and its Opioid Supplement. The TCUDS V is a validated screening tool widely 
used in criminal justice settings, with strong reliability and validity for identifying SUD 
and opioid-specific risk among justice-involved populations (Knight et al., 2018). Its use 
is recommended in correctional settings for ensuring treatment is provided to those 
most in need (Knight, Simpson & Hiller, 2002). These tools are recommended by the 
Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA) and the National 
Institute on Drug Abuse (NIDA) for correctional settings. Routine, validated screening 
ensures no one falls through the cracks and allows for tailored treatment planning from 
the outset. 
 
The program will support participants who are taking MAT/MOUD medications in 
custody, including buprenorphine, methadone or naltrexone Extensive research has 
shown that MAT reduces opioid cravings, overdose risk, and recidivism, and is 
particularly effective when paired with behavioral therapy and peer support. National 
guidelines from SAMHSA (SAMHSA, 2020), the American Society of Addiction Medicine 
(ASAM) (ASAM, 2025), and the National Commission on Correctional Health Care 
(NCCHC) (NCCHC 2025 MAT Guidelines) all recommend MAT as the standard of care for 
OUD in correctional environments. 
 
Participants will have access to peer support specialists through our community partner 
HOPE, Inc. Peer Support is a practice backed by strong evidence for improving 
engagement, reducing relapse, and supporting successful reentry. Peer-led support is 
especially powerful for women who may feel isolated or stigmatized by their substance 
use and justice involvement.

<!-- page 90 -->
The program will track and evaluate: 
• Number of women screened with TCUDS V and Opioid Supplement 
• Number identified as needing SUD/OUD treatment 
• MAT participation  
• Participation and completion rates in the 90 day in custody treatment program. 
• Linkage to community-based treatment post-release 
• Recidivism rates of participants  
 
Participant feedback will be collected regularly to inform program adjustments. Data 
will be shared with county stakeholders and used to drive ongoing quality improvement. 
 
This program combines validated screening tools (TCUDS V and Opioid Supplement), 
evidence-based curriculum (SMART Recovery), national best practices (MAT for OUD), 
and ongoing programmatic evaluation. This integrated, evidence-based approach 
maximizes the odds of recovery for women in the Navajo County Jail and aligns with the 
best available evidence on what works to support justice-involved women who suffer 
from a substance use disorder.

The program implemented would not have a completion date. The program is designed 
to be on going, based on funding availability.

1.0 Introduction 
The purpose of this request is to extinguish specific interior right -of-way (ROW) easements within the 
designated solar array area of the Red Hills Solar Project to enable construction permitting and project 
commencement. The Red Hills Solar Project Special Use Permit was approved under Case Number SUP 
24-007. All easements located along the Project boundaries will remain intact to preserve legal access to 
surrounding properties. This request pertains exclusively to the removal of interior easements on Navajo 
County parcels owned by Langley Nz Ranches LLC and identified by APNs: 202- 27-003J, 202-27-003L, 
202-50-002, 202-50-003, 202-50-007, 202-50-006, 202-50-005, 202-50-009, 202-50-010, 202-50-012, and 
202-50-011.  
2.0 Justiﬁcation 
The proposed extinguishment will have no adverse impact on surrounding properties or access. New roads 
will be constructed along the property boundaries, as shown in the current site plan, to ensure seamless 
connectivity and access to all adjoining parcels. The planned roadways have been designed to meet 
applicable County transportation and safety standards, mitigating any potential concerns regarding public 
access or utility services. 
3.0 Arizona State Statue Considerations 
This request complies with A.R.S. §§ 28-7205 and 28-7206 regarding the extinguishment of ROW and the 
vesting of title: 
1. Interior easements subject to extinguishment will revert to the current abutting property owner per 
statutory requirements. 
2. The extinguishment will not result in any landlocked parcels, as all adjoining properties will retain 
access to established public roadways via new or existing easements. 
3. Utility easements will remain unaffected, ensuring continued provision of utility services as per 
A.R.S. § 28-7210. 
4.0 Notice Requirements 
The applicant respectfully requests a waiver of the notification requirements for the extinguishment of the 
easements. All easements affected by this request are interior easements located entirely within the 
applicant's property boundaries. Consequently, no other parties will be impacted by this request. 
5.0 Easements to be Extinguished 
All interior portions of the following easements and rights that are not located along property boundaries  
are proposed for extinguishment as found in the following recorded documents: 
1. Easement recorded in Book 40 of Deeds, Page 376 (Roadway). 
2. Easement recorded in Docket 461, Page 953 (Roadway). 
3. Easement recorded in Docket 469, Page 720 (Public Roadway).

4. Easements, restrictions, reservations, conditions, and setback lines as set forth on the plat recorded 
in Book 30 of Surveys, Page 21. 
5.0 Conclusion 
The extinguishment of these interior ROW easements is necessary to facilitate the development of the Red 
Hills Solar Project. All legal requirements under Arizona statutes and County regulations have been met, 
and measures are in place to ensure there are no adverse effects on neighboring properties or public access.

Project Overview
The Woodruff community in Navajo County (County) experiences frequent nuisance flooding, mostly along 
1st Street and County Road, due to the lack of drainage infrastructure. Currently, runoff flows along roadway 
alignments in undersized roadside ditches or adjacent properties, and ponds at intersections. Runoff 
generally flows south to north through the community. These conditions create impacts to resident vehicular 
ingress/egress and parking. The County would like to develop construction documents based on the 
drainage evaluation completed by Kimley-Horn in March 2025. This feasibility study developed drainage 
project alternatives with phasing and cost analyses. The County would like to develop construction 
documents for the selected alternative, and for both phases per the finalized evaluation memo dated 
3/12/25. 
Project Understanding and Approach
Plans, special provisions, and an engineer’s estimate of probably cost will be developed for the Phase 1 
and 2 improvements identified in the drainage evaluation. The final design effort will include field survey to 
support design, utility locating services, and utility coordination. Services not included in this scope of work 
include public involvement, geotechnical evaluations, and FEMA map revisions for work in the Little 
Colorado River floodplain.
Scope of Services
Kimley-Horn (KH) will provide the services specifically set forth below.
Task 1 Environmental Services
Cultural Resources
Because the Woodruff Drainage project area is located on Navajo County right-of-way (ROW), the project 
is required to comply with the Arizona Antiquities Act (ARS 41-481 et seq.) and the State Historic 
Preservation Act (ARS 41-861 et seq.). The involvement of the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers in the 
issuance of a Section 404 permit will also require compliance with Section 106 of the National Historic 
Preservation Act (NHPA).
Kimley-Horn holds an Arizona Antiquities Permit (2025-038bl) for conducting non-collection surveys within 
the State of Arizona. Kimley-Horn will conduct a Class III Cultural Resource Survey. A team of 
professional archaeologists will visit the project area to conduct an intensive pedestrian survey of those 
portions of the project area that have not been surveyed within the last 10 years. Cultural resources 
encountered within the project area will be mapped using a professional grade Global Positioning System 
with a sub-meter accuracy. Where appropriate, cultural resources will be photographed and measured. 
Kimley-Horn will prepare a cultural resource survey report compliant with the State Historic Preservation 
Office (SHPO), and Arizona State Museum (ASM) standards. The report will include the results of the 
Class I records check and the Class III cultural resource survey. The report will include sections on 
cultural context, results of the fieldwork, including descriptions of cultural resources identified in the 
project area, recommendations of any cultural resources for inclusion in the Arizona Register of Historic 
Places, and recommendations regarding the need for further archaeological work.
