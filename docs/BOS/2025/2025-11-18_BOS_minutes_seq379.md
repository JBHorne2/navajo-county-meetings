Consideration of an Intergovernmental Agreement between Navajo County and the incorporated municipalities of Holbrook, Pinetop-Lakeside, Show Low, Snowflake, Taylor, and Winslow to improve efficiency in the operation of the Magistrate Courts .
Chelle Ewald presented the one-year renewal of the intergovernmental agreement. She stated that the agreement was originally established in 2011 and amended in 2019. The underlying arrangements have remained in practice, but the formal agreement has been expired for several years. Administrative Services is proposing to renew the agreement for an additional 1-year term to bring it back into alignment. The agreement will authorize the County Court Collection staff to continue providing collection services on behalf of the Municipal Magistrate Courts, continue the practice of having Municipal Police Officers and other law enforcement personnel file criminal misdemeanor cases in the appropriate justice courts where they are then prosecuted by the County Attorney and Public Defender's Offices, and outlines the compensation each municipality pays to the county to help offset the costs of providing criminal justice services. She stated that there are no changes to the scope or responsibilities from prior years. Ms. Ewald stated that staff recommends approval of the intergovernmental agreement. COUNTY ATTORNEY:

a.

Presentation of the Navajo County Rural Diversion Program.
County Attorney Brad Carlyon introduced Erin Escobar, Managed Care Coordinator for Complete Care Partners, who provides services to those who participate in the Misdemeanor Diversion Program. He stated that Erin will give a presentation on the services that Complete Care Partners provides.  Erin stated that she has managed Complete Care Partners since their doors opened approximately for 4 ½ years ago and stated that they have been in Navajo County for 2 ½ years now. She read the Mission Statement.  "The Navajo County Attorney's Office has established a court diversion program to provide an alternative method of holding low level offenders whose offense often has a nexus to substance abuse or mental health conditions accountable for violations of the law through participation in an evidence-based education or treatment program that targets factors related to recidivism. The program effectively redirects limited criminal justice resources, brings about participants rehabilitation, and gives rise to safer communities.  The program diverts criminal cases from formal court proceedings and allows for the dismissal of charges if the program is successfully completed."
Erin stated that one of the things that makes the program unique is that it couples accountability and mental health and other factors that play into success. They provide very detailed behavioral health screenings and work with local providers like CBI or Change Point and offer remote services, providing the technology for those series.  Erin also stated that they do alcohol and drug testing requiring soberness for the program, helping participants to get sober through different methods of treatment and support.  Participants have weekly meetings with their care manager in person, over the phone, or via Zoom. Local care managers in Winslow do travel to see their clients. Erin spoke about GPS monitoring and electronic check ins. She also spoke on how the support groups and guest speakers provide important encouragement. Erin moved on to provide program statistics, there have been 165 clients enrolled in the program and 93 have completed it, giving it a 68% success rate.  Some clients have returned to the program; she stated that they are always welcomed back for continuation whether they have previously completed the program or not. Erin stated that fees for the program are collected on behalf of the Navajo County Attorney's Office, however they are based on a sliding scale using the National Poverty Guidelines additionally, they have been able to waive fees through the Navajo County Attorney's Office.  Erin continued her slide presentation by speaking about some of the participants who have completed the program.
County Attorney Brad Carlyon mentioned that the Arizona State Legislature funded this program three years ago for three rural counties.  He stated that Navajo County has focused on misdemeanants, most of whom would have been seen in Municipal Court, these are citizens that would be frequent fliers and Mr. Carlyon stated that they wanted to focus on this group before they rose to the felony level.  Mr. Carlyon stated that the program has had a very high success rate because they provide mental health services, food, and housing. He stated that the program started in the Winslow Justice Court, Judge Little and Judge Pro-Tem Penrod, worked out kinks and brought the program county wide.  Mr. Carlyon mentioned how Jeanie Boyce, who runs the five diversion programs for Navajo County and Co-Chairs the APAC Diversion Program got everyone on same page for reporting.  Mr. Carlyon went on to explain that funding for this program is running out, and he plans on asking the Arizona State Legislator to continue the funding and asks for the support of the Navajo County Supervisors as well as the Municipalities.
Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked Attorney Carlyon, his staff, and Erin for sharing information about the program and thanked Jannie for the services she provides.   Supervisor Whitesinger stated that she works with young people and recognizes that this program gives youth and clients caring relationships to support and help them and get out of these types of cycles, adding that if we build strong families, we build strong communities, and in turn a strong nation.

CALL TO THE PUBLIC :

Jessie Fisher requested to speak on proposed Zoning Ordinances.   Vice-Chairwoman Whitesinger invited Mr. Fisher to the podium and explained that he has 3 minutes to speak. Mr. Fisher introduced himself and stated that he lives in Riverbed Ranch, an off-grid community in Utah.  Mr. Fisher stated that his non-profit has been meeting with Navajo County staff to explore how they could eventually bring $275,000 per year in new property tax revenue without triggering long-term infrastructure obligations. Mr. Fisher introduced Philip Gleason, who started the project called Operation Self-Reliance. He also introduced his wife, who was born and raised in Navajo County.  Mr. Fisher said that eventually they will ask Board of Supervisors to consider a zoning tool called the Community Land Cooperative Ordinance.  He said it would be a help to areas in the north and south parts of Navajo County. This ordinance would allow their non-profit to privately fund and maintain infrastructure. In their model, members build and sustain their own off-grid systems and internal roads.  Their members take full responsibility for water systems, power, wastewater treatment, and they use catchments like berms to keep water onsite.  They are a resident owned cooperative and build a strong culture of mutual respect and accountability. At this time, the 3 minutes allotted ended. However, the Board of Supervisors let Mr. Fisher read the last paragraph of his statement which stated that in a future Call to the Public segment they would like to share a presentation on their Utah model of the Operation Self-Reliance Community.
