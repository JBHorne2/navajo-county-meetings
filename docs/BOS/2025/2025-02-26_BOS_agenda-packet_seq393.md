Submitted For: Catrina Jenkins, EMP Director 
Submitted By: Catrina Jenkins, EMP Director
Department: NC Public Health Services District Division: Emergency Management
Presented By: Catrina Jenkins
Motion before the Board:
Consideration of a Declaration of Emergency due to extreme fire danger and to enact Section 6
of the Navajo County Ordinance 01-19 Emergency Fire Restrictions, to enter into Stage 1 fire
restrictions beginning Friday February 28, 2025 at 8:00 a.m.
Background:
The White Mountain Fire Coordination Group has initiated discussions regarding the prevailing
conditions throughout the region. This year has been one of the driest on record, with negligible
precipitation during the winter months and throughout last year’s monsoon season. Weather
forecasts indicate that no significant precipitation is expected in the near future; long-range
projections also anticipate below-normal rainfall and above-normal temperatures during the
spring and into the summer months, thereby exacerbating Arizona’s drought
conditions. Furthermore, multiple fires of human origin have occurred in various areas
throughout the region. These incidents, coupled with the catastrophic fatal fires in California in
January, have substantially heightened regional concern. 
In light of these factors and other elements of our operational plan, the WMFCG recommends
that Stage 1 Fire Restrictions be implemented in coordination with our regional partners on
Friday, February 28, 2025, at 8:00 a.m. This measure represents the earliest implementation of
fire restrictions in our region in the past 12 years.
All residents, visitors, and businesses are urged to educate themselves and others about the
measures necessary to prevent human-caused wildfires. Such measures include refraining from
constructing, igniting, or using wood, coal, charcoal, or briquette fires except in areas
designated for that purpose, such as authorized campgrounds or locations on private property
that are free of debris. Additionally, smoking materials and fireplace ashes must be disposed of
responsibly, tow chains should not be allowed to drag, and all mechanical activities—such as
welding, grinding, or cutting—should be conducted only in safe areas and on days when wind
speeds do not exceed 10 mph. These and other mitigation strategies are critical to protecting our
community from dangerous and costly wildfires.

Declaration of Emergency
WHEREAS, the Navajo County Board of Supervisors is authorized by ARS § 26-311 and to
declare an emergency due to wildfire which endangers life or property within the city, or the
unincorporated areas of the county; and
WHEREAS, the Navajo County Emergency Manager is recommending enacting Emergency Fire
Restrictions as set forth under Navajo County Ordinance 01-19 Section 6 which outlines
Emergency Fire Restrictions for Navajo County to begin on February 28, 2025 at 0800: and
WHEREAS, the threat of wildfire in Navajo County can have a catastrophic impact on the lives,
property and welfare of the citizens and visitors of Navajo County and as such Navajo County has
a legal and ethical obligation to protect the health, safety and welfare of the citizens and visitors
of Navajo County; and
WHEREAS, the people, lands, infrastructure and economic activities within the boundaries of
Navajo County are under imminent threat from wildfire due to strong winds, relatively high
temperatures and extremely dry conditions, and;
NOW, THEREFORE the Navajo County Board of Supervisors issues this Declaration of
Emergency in Navajo County and the Chairman is hereby authorized to take all further
actions as may be necessary or appropriate to implement this declaration pursuant to ARS
§ 26-311.
