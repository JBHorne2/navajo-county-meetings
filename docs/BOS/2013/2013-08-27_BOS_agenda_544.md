      CALL TO THE PUBLIC: Marc Silliman addressed the Board concerning the White Mountain Lakes County 
Recreation Improvement District. Mr. Silliman indicated he would like to address the Board  of Supervisors as 
the Directors of that District. He indicated the concern is regarding a rule that is in the rule book that the District 
publishes, and this rule  was revised in May of 2011;  which is  Rule 8.5  that is  under the Section 8.0 which 
concerns lake use eligibility. The rule reads: “The White Mountain Lake Recreation Improvement District relies 
solely on funding fro m member property tax assessments . A  members use privileges , including issuance of 
guest passes are subject to suspension if the members’ property tax payments are one or more year’s delinquent, 
not to include the current year due.” Mr. Silliman said his concern is not because he or  anyone else he knows 
has been affected  personally. He indicated  that when it came to his attention,  he thought it was inappropriate 
because he does not know  of any other district that  uses the criteria of having current tax payments paid, to 
receive the benefits of that district. Mr. Silliman indicated he thinks the school districts wouldn’t tell a parent 
that they cannot send their child because they haven’t paid their taxes or a fire department that  gets a call  and 
looks at the tax rolls before they  decide to dispatch the fire truck to go put out a fire, because that property tax 
was not current.  He reported last month he did take this issue to his advisory committee where he asked them to 
discontinue the administration and to strike the rule from the book. He reported they cited as they do at the start 
of the rule that the fact that the tax districts money comes from the assessment s and the levies being paid.  He 
indicated they likened it to a private enterprise, like a golf county club where a member of that club, one would 
not be permitted to play if they had not paid the  greens fees and are not current.  The committee also expressed 
that the district is an exclusive district and that it is there to serve the members of the district and not the general 
public. They stated there is an administration that looks at the tax rules and says if you are not a property owner 
you cannot use the lake without being a bon afide guest of a district member. Mr. Silliman did ask further if 
someone owned multiple properties; for instance why I would only need to be current on one property if I own 
3 properties. He felt that this sets up an inequity where a struggling family who hangs on to their one home 
cannot make a current tax payment in comparison to a wealthy owner  who only has to be current on one 
property to be able to use the lake.  He felt this was a discriminatory practice and indicated he felt all should be 
able to enjoy the privileges of the lake and there are a  number of things that cause him  concerns; perhaps 
someone could sue the district for this  discriminatory practice . Mr. Silliman then  asked the Board  of 
Supervisors to  consider putting this under review and if it is appropriate to give  direction to the advisory 
committee and particularly to the lake administration team to tell them that this is inappropriate and that this 
should not be in the rule book.  He asked the Board to review the rules, to confirm the legality of these rules and 
advise the district. He asked the Board to take away the sub jective nature of thi s. He indicated each property’s 
owner pays $42.00 a year for their  current assessment, including  residences or vacant lo ts. Mr. Silliman 
indicated it is not a large amount of money and he did not  fill the use of th e lake causes a direct expense which 
would cost the district more money, because he reported  the district’s expenses are flat . Mr. Silliman indicated 
this is just the denial of a tremendous enjoyment in their community. Chairman Nez indicated they will look 
into the matter. Brad Carlyon , County Attorney stated that they  could not  discuss this because it  is not an 
agenda item but the Board could give direction to staff. Supervisor Tenney indicated he felt Mr. Silliman gave
