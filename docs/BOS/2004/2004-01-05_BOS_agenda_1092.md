HOMELAND SECURITY TASK FORCE: Sheriff Gary Butler: Approval of Intergovernmental Agreement 
(IGA) (Equipment Acquired Grant Funds from Arizona Department of Emergency Management) with local 
“First Responder Agencies” for distribution of funding for equipment, training, etc.: Sheriff Butler reported 
to the Board that the Homeland Security Task Force recommends accepting and signing the IGA’s with 
local 1st responder agencies for equipment, training, etc.  The Board is the funding agent for these funds 
as sub-grantee of the State.  Navajo County has IGA’s with State and Federal funding sources and the 
local qualifying agencies are sub-grantees of the county.  This IGA (prepared by Lance Payette) follows 
State and Federal guidelines for the distribution of funding and equipment. All entities with the exception 
of the Navajo Nation have returned their IGA’s for signing by the Chairman today. These funds will be 
used for training, equipment, maintenance, etc. for all qualified first responder agencies. Mr. Deal stated 
that he will be in touch with the Navajo Nation to see why they haven’t returned their signed IGA.  Mr. 
Shumway made a motion to approve the IGA’s with local agencies as presented; motion seconded by 
Mr. Brownlow;vote unanimously carried.  
 
EMERGENCY SERV ICES: Commander Larry Dunagan:Update on Threat Advisory Code “Orange” 
(High Risk): Commander Dunagan provided the Board with information regarding the different Codes.  
On December 20, 2003 the Threat was raised to “Orange” due to the intelligence community receiving 
increased “terrorist chatter” that there could possibly be some type of activity during the 2003 holiday 
season.  There are no known specific targets.  The increased “chatter” has also indicated that there may 
be some type of activity in the rural southwestern United States.  The switch to rural targets may be due to 
the increased security at high profile targets.  These targets are not intended to cause mass casualties 
but to crate economic chaos and damage critical infrastructure including electrical plants, substations and 
transmission lines, natural gas lines and transportation corridors.  All first responder agencies have the 
available information and any possible targets in Navajo County have been addressed and property 
authority notified.
