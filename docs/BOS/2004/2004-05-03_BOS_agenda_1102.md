hunting & fishing in Navajo County: Mr. Cooley presented information regarding the economic 
importance of hunting and fishing on Navajo County. Wildlife is big business: sportsmen generate

2) Bioterrorism Program Staff introduction and update : Ms. Tyler introduced the Bioterrorism 
Program staff: Kevin Neill, Bioterrorism P rogram Manager; Ann Bailey, Public Information 
Officer/Training; Sherri Helton, Tribal Liaison; Tamica Serna, Clerk II. Ms. Tyler explained that the 
objective of the Program is to develop plans, including timetables and necessary activities, to 
ensure Navajo County Public Health Services District’s ability to respond to a public health 
emergency. The Program will build the Health District ’s infrastructure and training to include 
emergency communication equipment, risk communication, workforce preparedness, disease 
surveillance and the ability to conduct mass vaccinations. No action taken; informational only. 
 
3) Update re: West Nile Virus
: Ms. Tyler advised that on April 28, 2004, Governor Janet Napolitano 
announced an effort to increase public awareness about West Nile Virus. The Arizona Department 
of Health Services has issued an emergency order making West Nile Virus a reportable disease. 
Ms. Tyler presented several things the public needs to know: 1) Eliminate standing water outside 
the home where mosquitoes can breed; 2) Change the water in birdbaths, planters and animal 
water pans at least twice a week; 3) Repair leaky outside faucets and pipes; 4) Move air 
conditioner drain hoses frequently; 5) Wear insect repellent and lightweight clothing that covers 
arms and legs when going outside at night; 6) Horses should be vaccinated. Mr. Deal requested 
weekly updates on this issue.  No action taken; informational only. Mr. Thompson made a 
motion to return to regular session; motion seconded by Mr. Brownlow; vote unanimous approving 
the motion.

SPECIAL DISTRICTS: : Public Hearing: Resolution and order establishing and organizing the 
Scott’s Pine Tract A Improvement District, Lakeside area: Mr. Cox advised that approximately 
66% of the property owners have signed petitions to form the improvement district. The roads are 
currently primitive and are flat, without adequate drainage. Kent Delph, of Delph Engineering, noted 
that this area was platted in the 1960's, prior to established drainage, etc. and was not built as a 
subdivision. The developer is responsible for paving ? of the road adjacent to the subdivision (one 
lane) and the improvement district would be responsible for paving the other half (lane). The best 
way to accomplish this is to do both halves at the same time. At this time, public comment was 
requested. Karl Davis presented petitions requesting road and flood control improvements. John 
McFail stated he supports, and has no objection to paying for, an improvement district. Jennifer 
Malkop, Gayle Orth, Leslie Horne, David Skousen, Wendy Skousen & George Hamann spoke in 
opposition to the district. Their concerns included drainage and flooding issues, the “retention 
pond” which they believe is a flood control structure, and the fact that they believe the homeowners 
do not want an improvement district (the developers do) They also voiced concerns about having to 
pay for the developers improvements. They believe the county is responsible for the drainage and 
flooding issues and have not taken the responsibility and fixed the problems. Mr. Cox presented 
some history. The area was platted in 1966 and at that time, the Board of Supervisors had 
absolutely no control over subdivision. They did not control the installation of roads or flood control 
devices. The roads were primitive and never built to standards. The retention pond that 
homeowners refer to was owned by a private party. The county had no control over it as it was not 
public property. The county cannot improve the roads; it can only maintain them in the condition they 
were in when the county began maintenance. By law, the county cannot improve roads which are 
not declared county highways. When asked why permits are being issued to build in a flood plain, 
Mr. Cox explained that one can build in a flood plain, but certain restrictions exist on the design. 
Tom Hieb, 
Deputy Director of Flood Control, explained that 1993 had a very wet winter, and several 
areas were identified as needing improvement. Scotts Pine Meadows was prioritized as #5 of 8 
areas identified. The consultant’s report indicated that roadside drainage systems would help. The 
consultant then looked at the entire Wagon Wheel area. The “retention pond” was called a lake, and

qualify as a flood hazard area as defined by the state and the Federal Emergency Management
