5) Resolution Establishing the Navajo County Road Maintenance policy: Mr. Cox explained that the 
Level of Development road classification concept will provide a more objective and equitable process for 
prioritizing maintenance resources. It will eliminate the need to have a Primitive Road policy. The current 
policy is too restrictive, and with the county’s growth, does not allow for an adequate level of service. The 
new policy will provide the following benefits: 1) Combine the Book of Maintained Roads and the Book of 
Primitive Roads into the Navajo County Maintained Road System, where the maintenance provided will 
be based on the road function and average daily traffic; 2) The process will clearly define the level of 
service to be given for ea ch roadway classification; 3) We will be allowed to improve services to 
roadways as areas grow; 4) The process will be more in line with the state statutes; 5) The process will 
allow for more roadways to be included in our inventory; 6) the process will allow us to better manage our 
inventory. 
 
Deputy Director of Roads, Dusty Parsons, explained that the proposed Road Maintenance Policy 
includes Level of Development categories for county maintained roads. These levels of development 
include: A) Major collectors: These roads qualify for federal funding assistance and they serve thru traffic 
between arterial roads and other major collectors, or they link neighborhoods; B) Minor collectors (>400 
vehicles per day (vpd)): These roads serve thru traffic within local areas, provide connection to local roads 
and link major collectors and arterials; C) Subdivision roads: These are local roads built to county 
standards in accordance with the subdivision approval process; D) Minor collectors with <400 vpd. These 
roads serve thru traffic within local areas and may provide connection to local roads and streets. (This 
category is broken into several sub-categories: D-1: minor collectors with >250 vpd <400 vpd; D-2: >100 
vpd <250 vpd; D-3: <100 vpd); E) Local roads (>100 vpd < 250 vpd).  These roads serve as access to 
farm, residence, business or other abutting property. Thru traffic is local in nature and extent; F) Very low 
volume roads: These roads serve various functions but they carry very low traffic volumes of less than 100

vpd. They serve to access farms, public lands, ranches and/or residences.  
 
The County wants to provide the best possible service to the residents under the law, and the proposed 
Level of Development Road Maintenance Policy will help to accomplish that. The Public Works Director 
will be primarily responsible for determining the schedule of maintenance for primitive roads, according to 
Navajo County Policy/Procedure Guidelines. Roads that designated as Primitive Roads will receive 
maintenance, at a schedule not to exceed three times per calendar year. (Primarily fall and spring). All 
primitive roads will be maintained in the general condition they are received. No drainage or 
improvement projects will occur on these roads at County expense. All driveway culvert cleaning and 
installation will remain the homeowner’s responsibility. The County will continue to remove snow from 
Non-County maintained roads that are designated primitive roads, but they will not blade rough road 
conditions after storms except as part of the primitive road maintenance schedule. The County will not 
expend public funds on the purchase of material to improve primitive road, but they may use donated on-
site material to maintain roadway surfaces that are in need of repair. Drainage problems that occur 
during non-scheduled maintenance periods will not be repaired by the County.  No primitive road will be 
brought up to county standards using public funds or county forces.

6) Request from the Navajo Nation Department of Transportation for additional funds from available 
Special Road Funds for school bus routes within Navajo County (Pinon School District, Jeddito School 
District & Teesto School District): Gene Martinez, Environmental Specialist for the Navajo Nation DOT, 
explained that the Board provided funding in February, 2002 for environmental and archaeological 
studies on school bus routes on the Navajo Reservation within Navajo County. To date, 154 miles of 
school bus routes have been studied and environmental assessments completed. Mr. Martinez is here 
today to request funding for assessments on five additional school bus routes (91 miles total) Mr. Deal 
emphasized the importance of these projects, as many of the roads on the reservation are in such bad 
shape that schools have had to cancel classes due to “mud days”. He stated that he would like to fund up 
to $100,000 from Special Road Funds, but would like to revisit the issue at a later date to determine if the 
balance of the request ($52,502.48) could be funded through Navajo County Flood Control District funds. 
Mr. Deal made a motion to approve funding in the amount of $100,000 ($76,000 from District II Special 
Road Funds; and $24,000 from District I TEA-21 Funds) for school bus routes within Navajo County, as 
presented, with the understanding that this issue will be revisited at a later date; motion seconded by Mr. 
Thompson; vote unanimous approving the motion.

SKY-HI DOMESTIC WATER IMPROVEMENT DISTRICT: (Cont. from 3/29/94): Worksession with 
members of the Board of Directors of the Sky-Hi DWID, concerning legal, financial and administrative 
issues related to the operation of the District: Mr. Brian Patrick made a time-line presentation to the 
Board and stated that he feels they have followed the letter of the law in holding meetings and conducting 
business for the district. He said there is no time to have these battles; they need to concentrate on 
providing safe water to the district. He added that most of the residents of the district are content with the 
Board as it is, and one member, Mr. Klock, is not. Mr. Brownlow advised that he brought this matter to the 
Board of Supervisors (BOS) because if the Board of Directors (BOD) of the district fail to provide for the 
residents, it would fall to the BOS to run the District. He said he believes the responsibility should be with 
the BOD, not the BOS. Deputy County Attorney Lance Payette advised that he has been dealing with 
these issues for some time. He said this is basically a well run district, but there are philosophical 
differences. He has advised them to resolve these differences, or the Board of Supervisors would have to 
step in and run the district. The current BOD seems ready and willing to take care of business. Mr. 
Payette stated that while he would not say the actions of the BOD have been legal, they have been logical 
and business-like. Mr. Brownlow said he has faith in the BOD, and that they can work out these issues on 
their own. Mr. Shumway noted the BOS has a fiduciary responsibility if the BOD cannot resolve these 
issues. Mr. Patrick stated he believes they can work out the issues on their own, and get on with the 
business of running the district. Mr. Shumway made a motion to support the efforts of the Sky-Hi Water 
Improvement District, and to give them the opportunity to continue to operate the district as prescribed by 
law; motion seconded by Mr. Deal; vote unanimous approving the motion. (Mr. Patrick presented a 
petition containing the signatures of 64 persons who support the BOD)
