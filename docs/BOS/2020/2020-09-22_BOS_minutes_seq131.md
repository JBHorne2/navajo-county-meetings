BOARD OF SUPERVISORS:

a.

Presentation on the Timber Mesa Fire and Medical District Bond process and capital plan:   Chief Brian Savage gave a presentation.  He addressed the Board regarding the history of the Fire District and the communities they cover.  He advised that they currently have 93 full time employees, that they operate out of five staffed fire stations, one airport facility and the administration and support services facility.  He further advised that they have one training division in cooperation with the Pinetop Fire District. Chief Savage spoke about the financial history of the District.  He addressed the accomplishments of the fire district since 2014. He advised the Board of the current needs for the bond and addressed what the bond would be used for.  Supervisor Seymore inquired what the current PSPRS liability is.  Chief Savage advised that it is in excess of three million dollars and indicated that they do have a plan to pay it off.  Chief Savage went through each item that the bond will be used for and described how it will be used.  Supervisor Jack inquired if the training center is available to everyone around the region.  Chief Savage indicated that the training facilities are available to other entities.  Chief Savage addressed the bond repayment.  Supervisor Seymore indicated that he appreciates the presentation and explanation today and for the information provided to the public.  Supervisor Whitesinger inquired as to the response from the public and types of questions they have had.  Chief Savage addressed public response and questions that have been presented to them.  Supervisor Whitesinger thanked Chief Savage for the presentation.  Supervisor Thompson thanked Chief Savage for educating the community.  Chairman Whiting thanked Chief Savage for the presentation.  Chief Savage thanked the Board for their time.

REGULAR AGENDA:

1.

Update on COVID-19 :  Jeff Lee advised that we continue to be in a positive spot with COVID in Navajo County.  He further advised that as of this morning there are a total of 5751 case in Navajo County, which is a 222 increase in the last thirty days.  Mr. Lee indicated that is a positive number if you look back to June and July when they were seeing fifty to a hundred cases per day.  He advised that Navajo County is meeting all the benchmarks for schools and high-risk business to begin phased reopening's.  He further advised that they are working closely with those entities to provide support and education through their reopening process.  Mr. Lee thanked everyone in Navajo County, indicated that our actions and sacrifices are paying off and that we have slowed the spread.  He advised that we can't let up and that we really need to continue the precautionary measures, such as staying home if you are sick, wearing a mask in public and physically distancing.  He further advised one area we can improve on is staying home if you don't feel well.  Mr. Lee stated that the flu vaccine is available and that they are working with community partners to provide drive thru vaccination sites. He indicated that it is very critical to get a flu vaccine so that it does not add another stressor to the healthcare system and to avoid a flu/COVID co-infection.  He encouraged everyone to reach out to the Health Department or their healthcare provider to get their flu vaccination.  He advised that they will be posting the drive thru locations on their social media and COVID website. Supervisor Seymore inquired as to an update on the numbers for people hospitalized and for critical cases in our area.  Mr. Lee advised that he doesn't have information as to all cases but that currently they are seeing less severe reactions.  He indicated that the healthcare facilities in our area do have capacity which is positive.  Supervisor Seymore indicated that he appreciates Mr. Lee and his team and all they have done.  Supervisor Thompson addressed being cautious when going out and worrying about people who could be contagious but not showing systems.  He inquired if he is being overcautious.  Mr. Lee indicated that it is a good approach to have when going out.  Supervisor Thompson indicated that he does support abiding by the CDC guidelines and that they can feel more comfort with following the guidelines. Supervisor Jack thanked Mr. Lee for his good work and effort in keeping everyone informed.  He addressed the town hall meeting with President Nez and discussion that was held regarding reducing numbers.  He addressed the public schools reopening and the concerns with the kids going to school off the reservation.  He advised that one of the Delegates indicated that there has been a spike in Kayenta after a gathering and indicated that the gatherings should not be happening.  Chairman Whiting requested that Mr. Lee relay back to his team the appreciation from the Board.  He advised that he appreciates the efforts in partnering with the schools and the community to work together as things come up.  He thanked Mr. Lee and his team for their dedication.

Supervisor Thompson made a motion to return to regular session; motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

PUBLIC WORKS:

a.

Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 32-2020 , extinguishing two public roadway easements in the Linden area, detailed in Abandonment Record 20-01 over APN 409-21-020A:   John Osgood addressed the Board regarding the item.  He indicated that at one time there was an idea for a subdivision in this area but that there is not a plan for it in the future.  He advised that the easements are on private property and were reserved for public roadways which are no longer needed.  He further advised that as to the request, the applicants indicated there is no need for the public to travel on their property when there's no public destination.  He indicated that Engineering, Planning and Zoning and the County Attorney's Office have reviewed the request and determined that no parcels are land locked and that there is no public need for the easement.  He further indicated that they have notified the surrounding properties of the extinguishment request and that there is no know opposition to it.  He advised that there is no fiscal impact with the action and that after review staff supports the application.
Supervisor Seymore made a motion to adopt Resolution Number 32-2020, extinguishing two public roadway easements in the Linden area, detailed in Abandonment Record 20-01 over APN 409-21-020A; motion seconded by Supervisor Thompson .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE : Report from County Manager, County Attorney and Board members:
Glenn Kephart addressed how everyone is working hard to provide mandated services. He addressed the grants that were on todays agenda and thanked everyone for their work on those. He thanked Bryan Layton for his work and leadership with that team on the GFOA Award. Mr. Kephart thanked Supervisors for their work on the Census count and encouraged them to keep up the good efforts.
Supervisor Thompson thanked everyone for participating today. He reported on meeting with the communities and agencies in the Navajo Nation and the work he has done with those entities. He indicated that they are sharing the messages for continuing precautions for COVID. He addressed his concerns with the Census on the Navajo Nation and indicated that he is really bothered that the Federal Census Office moved the deadline to September knowing there is a crisis. He indicated that they are advising people on the Census, but people are cautious about going out. He advised that they have been on the radio advertising that the Census needs to be done.
Supervisor Whitesinger reported she has been working on Census for the tribal communities. She indicated when they initially started a month ago, they had 4% of the population that was accounted for. She further indicated that it is important to get the word out.  She has reached out to the tribal community, been on the radio, and has been mobilizing and doing daily work to get the Census word out. She advised that it is important as it allows for their trust rights and treaty rights to be accounted for. She indicated that one thing that continually comes up as a Native community member is that they account for only 2% of the nation and that it is very important for tribal community members to be counted and be represented at all levels.
Supervisor Seymore indicated that it is great to work with the Board and for the efforts and care that they show the community.; He addressed the importance of the Census and the responsibility of the people to fill out the Census and to vote. He addressed early ballot voting and the ballot boxes for drop off instead of mailing them. He indicated that we need to do our part and vote. He thanked Supervisor Whitesinger for her prayer. He expressed his appreciation for each Board member and all that they do. He indicated he is thankful that we live in a community where we care for one another.
Supervisor Jack thanked the Board members and indicated that he appreciates all they do for their communities and for their dedication, leadership and commitment. He thanked all of the Navajo County entities for their support and advocacy in making improvements for our county. He addressed the hard times and indicated we are looking forward to something better. He stated it has been a pleasure and honor to work with everyone and wished everyone the best in the future.
Chairman Whiting  indicated that he is grateful to serve with everyone. He wished Debra Chapman a Happy Birthday. He indicated that he appreciates everyone's participation at the County Supervisors Association and the leadership they each demonstrate and the unity there. He advised that they had a productive CSA meeting and that they had a chance to talk about what the upcoming Legislative session may look like. He indicated that they will have a virtual CSA Legislative Summit in October. He indicated that he would like to see partnership formed with the Sheriff's Office, the County Attorney Office, the Board, Cities/Towns and School Districts regarding cyber bullying in schools. He addressed the issue on different social media pages and the issues that it's causing with students not wanting to attend school.  He stated that we need to look for ways to act. He encouraged that students should not follow those pages on social media that encourage this behavior. He indicated that they have reached out to several youth in leadership positions and requested that they stand up and do something about this issue.  He reported on their response and reactions.  He indicated that we need to be aware and be the difference. He encouraged that we look for opportunities to serve and help those that stand in need.
