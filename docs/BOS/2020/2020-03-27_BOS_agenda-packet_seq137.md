Navajo County - For Official Use Only 
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE 
March 25, 2020 
 
Limited in-person access to County services 
 
 
Holbrook, AZ – In an effort to maximize social distancing and to protect the health of residents and 
staff, Navajo County Government facilities will close to the public beginning Thursday, March 26, 
and will remain in effect until further notice.    
Essential services will still be provided to residents and businesses as the County takes proactive 
measures to restrict the spread of COVID-19.  Although buildings will close to the public, County 
offices will remain staffed and residents can continue to conduct County business and get help 
by phone, email, mail, or on the County website www.navajocountyaz.gov. In-person services will 
be available by appointment only.   
“Navajo County Government is still open for business,” explained County Manager Glenn Kephart, 
“even as many in-person services will be modified to comply with protocols provided by our 
national, state, and local leaders.” 
Under the current direction of the Arizona Supreme Court, court services at the Superior Court 
and Justice Courts will remain open.  
Residents are encouraged to call 928-524-4000 or visit the County website with any additional 
questions.

March 12, 2020 
 
Mr. Troy Egan 
 
 
Dear Mr. Egan, 
 
It is our understanding that you might be interested in re-establishing the rail spur that existed 
here in the White Mountains of Arizona years ago.  This is a topic of great interest to this region 
not only from the standpoint of moving goods in and out of the region as it did during those 
years but also as a possible scenic tour of an area that would be of great interest to locals and 
visitors alike. 
 
The mountain terrain and scenic vistas would provide a fantastic backdrop for visitors during the 
summer months, as well as the travel through the golden aspens in the fall.  It could also serve as 
a means of transportation for skiers to one of the best ski resorts in the southwest during the 
winter. 
 
This possibility, along with the restoration of transportation of goods and services in and out of 
the region, would obviously provide an economic engine for a region that has otherwise 
struggled economically due to the reduction and elimination of existing industries, such as the 
paper mill, timber industry, coal fired power plants, etc. 
 
We would encourage you to continue your pursuit of this endeavor and would offer our support 
of your efforts to do so. 
 
 
Sincerely, 
 
 
 
Jason E. Whiting, Chairman 
Navajo County Board of Supervisors

operation of digital radio technology for improved communications. Different from the previous

When the backhaul is not sufficient, dispatch will not be able to communicate effectively with
those in the field and a drop in communications will occur. However, field personnel will still
hear each other while using the same repeater and may not realize dispatch can no longer hear or
record what is occurring in field. This causes confusion and a dangerous scenario that will be fixed
by utilizing Cellular One’s microwave system.
By utilizing Cellular One's microwave backhaul, Navajo County will have a dedicated space on
the largest and most reliable microwave system in Navajo and Apache county for critical
communication traffic. This will also allow us easily to expand to new sites once the current
system is stabilized and brought back to standard operations to any site shared by or near to
Cellular One. Additionally, in the event of a fault or failure, Cellular One will respond to correct
any issues within their microwave system and Navajo County will only need to be concerned
about connections to and from Cellular One.

Background and Specifications 
 
The purpose of this project is to purchase and install a position sensor for the Schoens 
Dam principle outlet gate.  Previous work has included research and specifications for 
various position sensor options, including magnetic non-contact and linear position 
sensors.  In our research, we found that submersible linear position sensors have limited 
ranges but would be the better option compared to magnetic sensors due to the high 
precision required for mounting of the non-contact sensors.  The longest-range submersible 
linear position sensor is manufactured by Positek (see the attached white paper for the 
LIPS S114/5 sensor series) – these have a standard maximum range of up to 
approximately 32 inches.  The Schoens Dam hydraulic actuator has a stroke of 
approximately 48 inches. 
 
Positek will manufacture a custom sensor solution that will provide approximately 50 inches 
of travel on their LIPS S115 sensor.  The model number for this custom sensor will be  
S115.1270HI50NUZ577 equipped with a 16/3 cable and submersible connector -  The 4-20 
mA output will be measured by a Campbell CR800 data logger equipped ALERT 
transmitter, and a display for viewing the gate position and reservoir pool elevation on site.  
This project will require approximately 500 feet of 16/3 signal cable with submersible 
connectors. 
 
Campbell equipment including the AL200, Cell210, CD295 display, RF320 Radio will 
provide the control aspects for the custom sensor solution.

Overview 
This scope of work presents the tasks ATKINS (Consultant) will complete to assist the County with 
determining water surface elevations and inundation limits associated with Rainbow Lake in the Show 
Low area of Navajo County, Arizona. The following work will be performed: 
 
 Perform research with the County, owner/operator, and FEMA for any information on the 
existing hydrology and hydraulics for the lake and lake operation data, if any. 
 Create a digital elevation model for the study area. It is our understanding that the County 
will supply elevation data including County-generated topography for the Lake, LiDAR from 
the Apache-Sitgreaves Forest, and survey shots for the spillway. Since the data is coming 
from various sources, work will be necessary to compile all information and resolve any 
differences in source datums. 
 Perform hydrology for the watershed. Prepare a HEC-HMS model f or the area draining to 
the lake, consisting of a maximum of five subbasins. The NRCS Curve Number loss 
method and Clark Unit Hydrograph methodology will be used. The HMS model can be used 
to develop an initial high water elevation for storage, if any, resulting from the lake. The 10-, 
25-, 50-, 100-, and 500-year return intervals will be modeled. 
 Determine initial conditions for lake, either assuming full to  the spillway elevation or some 
other elevation based on data collected and discussions. 
 Prepare a hydraulics model for the lake using HEC-RAS version 5.0.6 (1-dimensional flow 
routines). It is assumed that the model will extend far enough upstream and downstream to 
normalize boundary conditions and ensure no downstream conditions impact the water 
surface elevations. The bridge at SR 260 will not be included and it is assumed that 
backwater from this structure does not impact the lake. 
 Develop an inundation map and water surface elevations for all  return intervals for the 
County’s use. It is assumed that this is for local use only and will not be submitted to FEMA.  
 Summarize all work performed in a technical memorandum. 
 
It is assumed that the County will supply all necessary topogra phic data for the project. Included in 
the scope is general project management activities, quality control, and conference calls with Navajo 
County. No site visits or in-person meetings in Navajo County are included. Services will be provided
