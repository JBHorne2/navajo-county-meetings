Update on COVID-19 :  Jeff Lee stated that we do continue to see a steady rise of cases across Navajo County and as of this morning, we have 1,550 cases in Navajo County and there are 16,783 cases in Arizona.  He indicated that they do anticipate that case counts will continue to rise with the additional testing efforts that are taking place.  He stated that one effort is a coordinated effort with local health departments, the Arizona Department of Health Services and Medicaid funded long-term healthcare facilities with the goal being to test all of these long-term facilities within the next 2 weeks.  He indicated that they are working closely with facilities in Navajo County to ensure they have the resources to perform the test or respond to any positive test in the staff and residents.  He stated that as we continue to move forward and more businesses and services reopen, it is key we continue to take this seriously.  He indicated that the risk of exposure continues to remain high, and we need to continue to practice healthy measures to keep our community safe, including physical distancing, staying home if you feel ill, hand washing, coughing or sneezing into a tissue or bent elbow, disinfecting frequently touched surfaces and wearing a face mask covering the mouth and nose when in public.  Supervisor Thompson stated that he knows that there's a lot of blitz testing that's happening, and a lot of smaller communities on the Navajo Nation have been requesting such testing.  He asked if there is any way that Navajo County can provide or contribute to getting blitz testing done on the Reservation.  Jeff Lee stated that their focus is that all communities which need testing supplies get those supplies, and upwards of 35 added test machines are either in place throughout the Navajo Nation or on their way.  He indicated that he is happy to follow up and obtain the exact numbers and locations for those test machines.  Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Lee and his team for all their efforts during this time.

Business Outreach Update :   Paul Watson addressed the Board.  He recognized Stephanie Ray and Kathleen Smith for their efforts with the team to disseminate information to our businesses to help support them during this time.  He stated that they established an employerhelp@navajocountyaz.gov email address for individuals to ask questions, and those questions were disseminated to team members who could most appropriately answer those questions.  He spoke about the different E-Blast topics that have been disseminated from the beginning of April to last week.   He stated that they have received responses from surveys with regard to how and when we reopen our economy and have provided information regarding moving forward with reopening.  He shared the two surveys which were sent to local businesses and a summary of those responses.   He indicated that a significant number of our businesses did receive funding through the Paycheck Protection Program or the Economic Injury Disaster Loan Program.  He spoke about the significant concerns regarding unemployment and the number of applicants who have not received their benefits.

Stephanie Ray spoke about programs the Workforce Development Team will be focused on to help our businesses move forward.  She stated that those employees who were temporarily laid off will be getting a significant payment and will be hesitant to go back to work, so businesses will have a struggle ramping up their businesses because they're not able to get their prior staff back.  She spoke about the creativity that businesses implemented which will benefit them move forward.  She stated that they have the ability to work with an employer and help employees gain new skills that they now need.  She indicated that as companies pivot their business operations, some may be identifying a new component of their business that they want to implement, so they will work with Moonshot to develop a marketing campaign where these businesses can upload a pitch, and there is funding available through Moonshot.   She spoke about Local First Arizona as a new partner they're working with to develop a year-long contract where they will come in and help us identify what business owners need, work with those business owners with those needs and provide a feasibility study to determine how to support these businesses long term.

Mr. Watson stated that the business response team included our Chambers of Commerce, as well as the Small Business Development Center, and they have been working together to help support local businesses.  He spoke about the yellow and red banners encouraging citizens to shop local, as well as flags individual businesses can put up.  He stated that at various times they have had over 18 different individuals and organizations represented on the team.  Mr. Watson spoke about the amendment to his contract to work full time on assisting with this program through the end of April, and he was brought back to a part-time status beginning May 1st.  Supervisor Seymore expressed appreciation to Mr. Watson and Ms. Ray for their efforts in supporting our businesses.  Supervisor Whitesinger expressed appreciation for Mr. Watson and the team's efforts and spoke about the importance of our local businesses.  Supervisor Thompson thanked Mr. Watson for his efforts in Navajo County and his leadership with the NACOG Economic Development Committee.  He stated that he would like to see employment resources expanded into the area.  Mr. Watson stated that he will follow up.  Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Watson and Ms. Ray for continuing to look for ways to support our local businesses during this pandemic.

Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 16-2020 , adopting the Safe and Healthy Operations Plan to ensure the health and safety of county staff and the people we serve, through the COVID-19 Pandemic :   Glenn Kephart discussed the item.  He spoke about the need to change our way of doing business and our practices due to the COVID-19 pandemic in order to continue to provide high quality services while protecting the safety of Navajo County employees, our partners and the public we serve.  He further spoke about following the guidelines set forth by the CDC, the Governor's Executive orders and the Arizona Department of Health and Safety.  He stated that there has been great cooperation throughout Navajo County.  He gave a background of the Board of Supervisor's declaration and the modification of procedures.  He spoke about the Governor's declarations and executive orders throughout the pandemic.  He stated that our plan to return smarter has been developed collaboratively by County Administration, Elected Officials, Department Directors and the Navajo County team and is in compliance with the CDC, Arizona Department of Health and Safety and Governor Ducey's executive orders.

Bryan Layton stated that we're finding that balance between our stewardship and delivering services.  He spoke about the first step being to limit walk-in public access; deliver service by phone, email, the website; limit in-person visits to appointment only; limit internal circulation of our own staff; provide disinfectant cleaning tools for our department and working remotely and staggering shifts.  He spoke about being able to meet the needs of our departments by being creative and resourceful.  He stated that due to exposure in some of our work areas, those areas were temporarily closed, and there was a deep cleaning and disinfecting of those areas.  He indicated that the County Manager sent updates to all employees to keep them abreast of what happened and the appropriate measure which were taken and he continues to send updates.  He stated that Mr. Kephart and he have had several meetings with departments.  He spoke about custodial functions, including adjusting schedules and cleaning processes to keep staff safe.  He thanked Melissa Buckley and her team for practicing new measures in the mail room.

Mr. Layton stated that we expect to resume public access beginning June 1.  He indicated that the County has created a plan for the continuing safety and healthy operations and delivery of County services, which was collaboratively developed and is in compliance with current guidelines from the CDC, ADHS and the Governor's executive orders.  He stated that it is expected that this plan will continue to evolve.  He indicated that the main focus of the plan is to provide a safe workspace for employees to deliver services to residences by adhering to the following steps:

Promote appropriate physical distancing and physical safety measures, including encouraging everyone to maintain appropriate physical distancing, use physical barriers and protective equipment where needed and provide physical sanitation stations throughout County facilities

Cleaning and disinfecting of public spaces and collective workspaces to include daily cleanings of workspaces, provide cleaning supplies to the departments and expect employees to regularly provide additional cleaning of their personal workspace and equipment

Protections for walk-in services to include temperature checks of employees and the Public, use of self-screening questions and members of the public may be asked to reschedule a visit

Face covering suggestions - per CDC guidelines face coverings are encouraged but not required except in the Courts per the AOC

Continue to work remotely and conduct meetings remotely and avoid large gatherings.  The Board of Supervisors meetings will return to being conducted in the Board Chambers after June 1 with an option for remote participation.  Other public meetings shall follow similar guidelines and procedures.

Continue to monitor out-of-county travel requests and work with staff regarding CDC guidelines.

He stated that staff recommends approval of this resolution.  Chairman Whiting indicated that there was input from department directors and elected officials and expressed appreciation for the process so everyone has a voice.
Vice-Chairman Jack thanked the team for their efforts and made a motion to adopt Resolution Number 16-2020 , adopting the Safe and Healthy Operations Plan to ensure the health and safety of county, staff and the people we serve through the COVID-19 Pandemic; motion seconded by
Supervisor Seymore .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

WHITE MOUNTAIN LAKES COUNTY RECREATION IMPROVEMENT DISTRICT: Board of Directors Session :

a.

REGULAR AGENDA

1.

Consideration of entering into a Management Consultant contract with Remarkable Recreation Solutions :
Melissa Buckley discussed the item.  She spoke about the different options Navajo County has looked at to successfully manage the White Mountain Lake County Recreation Improvement District since 2017.   She further spoke about the virtual town hall which was held last week.  In response to one of the questions raised during Call to the Public, Ms. Buckley stated that White Mountain Lake is a private lake, and each member receives a booklet with lake rules, along with another booklet on boating safety.  She indicated that it is not up to the District employees to police the lake; but if there is a major infraction, they should call 9-1-1 and speak to the County Sheriff's Office.  She spoke about the proposed contract to provide management consultant services with Remarkable Recreation Solutions which is for one year and is eligible for four one-year renewals.  She spoke about the experience of Mr. Honberger.  She stated that Remarkable Recreation Solutions will continue to work with and manage the lake operations and that Mr. Honberger can be available for an emergency on any given day.  She thanked Pat Stevens for her work as the lake administrator and stated that Ms. Stevens will conclude working for the District on June 7th.  She indicated that the services that she has provided in the past will be taken over by the management company.   She stated that this contract was originally solicited through the Request for Proposal process and one bid was received which has been withdrawn due to pricing.  She indicated that approval was given to negotiate a contract with Remarkable Recreation Solutions under the sole source methodology pursuant to Section 13.7(b) of the Navajo County Fiscal Policy.  She recommended approval of the contract.

Glenn Kephart also thanked Ms. Stevens for her service.  He spoke about the selection criteria, the experience of Remarkable Recreation Solutions and contract negotiations.  He addressed the questions and comments which were posed during Call to the Public.  He indicated that staff does not get to approve the contract, they recommend approval.  He thanked the community and those who participated in the town hall and submitted questions and comments for sharing their voice.  He stated that this proposal will not change line items in the District's budget.  He stated that Mark Honberger will be available on Saturdays, although it would not be a regular workday.   He spoke about the 20% fee for grants that Mr. Honberger secures.  He indicated that writing grants is above and beyond the operation of the District, and it is up to the discretion of the Board of Directors whether we want to apply for a grant.   He stated that any grants would be a benefit to the District.  Mark Honberger, Remarkable Recreation Solutions, addressed the Board.  He spoke about the time involved in securing, writing and managing a grant.  He indicated that he has done grants throughout his career.  He stated that the standard is not a percentage of the grant, but comes in as an hourly rate of $30.00 an hour, and the fee would be most likely less than 10%.
Supervisor Seymore indicated that what he heard that although the contract says 20%, Mr. Honberger does not anticipate the amount would exceed more than 10%, and Mr. Honberger indicated that that would be the case, and he is not opposed to changing that in the contract.  Supervisor Seymore thanked Melissa Buckley for her work that she has done for the District, as well as Pat Stevens.  He thanked Chairman Whiting for the work that he has done on behalf of the District.  He stated that he is excited to have a professional like Mr. Honberger come on board.  He thanked the community for their input.  Supervisor Whitesinger expressed appreciation for the community and the time spent by staff to find the needed support.  Supervisor Thompson thanked Ms. Buckley for her efforts.  He expressed support for the item.  Chairman Whiting clarified with Mr. Honberger that he is looking to modify the proposed contract with an hourly rate with a cap not to exceed 10%, and Mr. Honberger indicated that that is correct.  He asked Mr. Honberger to address his availability on Saturdays.  Mr. Honberger stated that patrol staff will be there on Saturdays, and he is nearby and available for emergencies as needed.  Vice-Chairman Whiting thanked all those who worked on this.  He further thanked Ms. Stevens for her efforts and wished her well in this new season of her life.  He thanked the team for coming together and finding a solution.  Supervisor Seymore made a motion to approve entering into a Management Consultant contract with Remarkable Recreation Solutions with the modifications to the contract as discussed; motion seconded by Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
