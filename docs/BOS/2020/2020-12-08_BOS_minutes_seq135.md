1.

Consideration and possible approval of an Intergovernmental Agreement with ADHS, Contract Number IGA2021-03 EDC Water Testing for Lead in Arizona Public Charter Schools, to fund water testing in Public Charter Schools throughout Navajo County :  Tanya Baker addressed the Board regarding the item.  She indicated that they will be testing for lead in five different charter schools throughout Navajo County and that it will serve approximately five hundred students and their families.  She advised that ADHS will provide $2000 to cover the cost of water sampling and drinking water sources in those public charter schools and that it will be completed by June 2021.  She further advised that this would help ensure that water in the Charter Schools is safe to drink. She indicated the education will be provided to all schools throughout Navajo County by the end of this IGA.
Supervisor Thompson inquired once testing is completed and depending on results what are some of the actions that may be taken.
Ms. Baker advised that the information will be shared with the parents, families and teachers and the Environmental Protection Agency with Arizona Department of Health Services to intervene if needed.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion to approve Intergovernmental Agreement with ADHS, Contract Number IGA2021-03 EDC Water Testing for Lead in Arizona Public Charter Schools, to fund water testing in Public Charter Schools throughout Navajo County; motion seconded by Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

a.

Update on COVID-19 :  Jeff Lee advised that he doesn't have positive news to report on the spread of COVID.  He advised that last week the White House Corona Virus Task Force issued a report on Arizona and according to the report we are in a dangerous place due to current, extremely high COVID transmission levels and the limited hospital capacity.  The further advised that any additional surge will compromise patient care as well as medical care overall.  He indicated that all states and counties must work to flatten curve now in order to sustain the health system.  He addressed the precautions and risk for those over 65, those that have high risk health issues and those who have been in large groups outside of their immediate household.  He stated that as of this morning Navajo County has 9056 cases, which is an increase of 2175 cases in less than a month.  He indicated that the ADHS dashboard this morning shows that Arizona has 12,314 new cases today.  He addressed the precautions that we should still be using, such as, physical distancing, wearing a mask in public, staying home when possible and staying home when you feel unwell.  He advised that they are still linking a majority of the cases back to people being out when not being well.  He advised that the vaccine is close, and it will help slow down the spread but while we wait for the vaccine, we need to act now and we have to be better at following the CDC mitigation measures.  He addressed the continued meetings with partners regarding the vaccine and indicated when it is available they are ready to distribute and vaccinate the priority groups in a timely manner.
Supervisor Thompson stated that when things get difficult, we turn to hope and prayer.  He indicated that it is up to each one of us to abide by CDC guidelines.  He addressed the pain of losing family members and those close to us.  He thanked Mr. Lee.
Supervisor Seymore inquired if there is anything to do for the hospitals as they get close to capacity.  He inquired if they have sites available for additional needs and if there are places once vaccines are available to provide those.
Mr. Lee indicated that they meet with the hospital's multiple times a week and that they are in close coordination so they can submit resource request to a State level for any additional needs that they have.  He further indicated that recently that have been able to facilitate multiple resource orders for staffing to increase the capacity within the local healthcare facilities.  Mr. Lee indicated that ADHS had a process for onboarding establishments or organizations that want to be vaccinators for the COVID vaccine.  He advised that they are working with 17 organizations who have indicated that they would like to be a vaccinator.  He further advised that we are in a much better spot to be able to distribute the workload with the number of places willing to provide the vaccines.
Supervisor Jack inquired as to the process for the distribution of the vaccine.
Mr. Lee addressed distribution of vaccine and the different priority groups.
Supervisor Whitesinger indicated that she appreciates the Health Department and Mr. Lee for all that they are doing.  She addressed having public support for tracing and taking the right steps in caring and protecting ourselves.
Chairman Whiting indicated that he appreciates what Mr. Lee and his team for all they do.  He advised that Mr. Lee and his team have done a great job and that he continues to hear that from various partners.  He further advised that he knows they are working with school districts, with the different cities and towns and with the different healthcare providers.

Supervisor Thompson made a motion to return to regular session; motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

NORTHEASTERN ARIZONA INNOVATIVE WORKFORCE SOLUTIONS:

a.

Consideration of Memorandum of Understanding With the Northeastern Arizona Local Workforce Development Board and Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act Partners:   Stephanie Ray addressed the Board regarding the item. Ms. Ray advised that this Memorandum of Understand was previously in place but that this one took a little extra time to negotiate.  She stated that the Memorandum of Understand has 15 possible partners legislated through the Workforce Innovation Opportunity Act that the Workforce Board and the Board of Supervisors are required to enter into this agreement with.  She advised that there are twelve that provide services within the Northeastern Arizona area.  She further advised that this document aligns the partners, that it outlines the commitment to having accessible and quality work force services and that it outlines the details of how together they can reach the goals of the Workforce Development Plan.  She indicated that they have agreed that the businesses are their primary customers and that the job seeker is their most valuable resource and that by working closely with business and understanding their needs and preparing the job seekers, they can bridge the gap between open jobs and job seekers skills.  She further indicated that it also takes negotiations on shared costs, which is a big part of the document.
Supervisor Seymore stated that he appreciates Ms. Ray's knowledge of the Memorandum and thanked her for all of her efforts.
Supervisor Thompson thanked Ms. Ray for all that she has done.
Chairman Whiting thanked Ms. Ray for her presentation.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion to approve the Memorandum of Understanding With the Northeastern Arizona Local Workforce Development Board and Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act Partners; motion seconded by Supervisor Thompson .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

PUBLIC WORKS:

a.

Consideration of a temporary closure of Woodruff/Snowflake Road for bridge repairs:    John Osgood addressed the Board.  He indicated that the Arizona Department of Transportation preforms bridge inspections for Navajo County and the results of a recent routine annual inspection showed a broken steel member in the bridge.  He advised that Public Works has worked with it's bridge consultant to prepare plans and a bid packet for the repairs.  He stated that the repair work will require that the bridge and the road to the bridge be closed for the duration of the repair and that it is anticipated that the bridge will be closed for a maximum of three days, weather and schedule permitting.  He advised the they will place signs out informing the public of the road closure including the date of the closure for a minimum of twenty days prior to the start of the work.  He advised that staff recommends approval.
Supervisor Seymore inquired if this will solve the problem or if it is a quick fix.  Mr. Osgood advised that this would fix the problem.
Bryan Cook also stated that it will fix damage to bridge and not required additional repairs for this issue.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion to approve the temporary closure of Woodruff/Snowflake Road for bridge repairs; motion seconded by Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote un unanimous approving the motion.

b.

Consideration to award Contract B20-09-029 Little Colorado River Bridge Repairs located at Woodruff-Snowflake Road to Restruction Corporation :    John Osgood advised that sealed proposals were received by the Board of Supervisors and bids were publicly opened and read.  He further advised that the successful bid was reviewed by the county engineer, the project consultant and senior engineer and that they are all in concurrence that this contractor has sufficient experience to complete the project.  He stated that the fiscal impact of the project is $11,607.46, with the source of funding being the Highway User Revenue Fund.  He stated that staff recommends approval.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion to award Contract B20-09-029 Little Colorado River Bridge Repairs located at Woodruff-Snowflake Road to Restruction Corporation; motion seconded by Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

c.

Consideration of Task Agreement Number 21 to Memorandum of Agreement between the Bureau of Indian Affairs Department of Transportation- Navajo Region and Navajo County for the continued funding support of two Navajo County Road Maintenance Equipment Operators :  John Osgood indicated that approval of this item continues a successful partnership between Navajo County and the Bureau of Indian Affairs Department of Transportation.  He advised that it allow equipment works to provide the Navajo Nation with road maintenance assistance on local public roads and school routes.  He indicated that the current agreement is set to expire at end of this month, and the new agreement, if approved, will extend the partnership another year and allow the equipment operators to signifyingly assist in keeping the roads passable, especially through the snow and mud.  He advised that the source of funding is Highway User Revenue Fund, and the fiscal impact is $60,000.00 in fiscal year 2020 and $60000 in fiscal year 2021 to be split evenly between District I and District II.  He stated that staff recommends approval of the item.
Supervisor Thompson expressed his appreciation to Mr. Osgood and Miles Begay for their help in this.  He indicated that at the beginning it wasn't easy with all the policies and regulations to get to this point.  He further indicated that this started out as a request from the students asking for help with improvements on the reservation.  He stated that they have been able to do a lot together and thanked everyone involved in this partnership.
Supervisor Jack expressed his appreciation to Mr. Osgood and Miles Begay for all that they have done.

Supervisor Thompson made a motion to approve Task Agreement Number 21 to Memorandum of Agreement between the Bureau of Indian Affairs Department of Transportation- Navajo Region and Navajo County for the continued funding support of two Navajo County Road Maintenance Equipment Operators; motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Consideration of a contract with Atkins in the amount of $26,290 to study the Joseph City Wash and recommend next steps to mitigate flooding in the area around Joseph City :  Sandra Phillips addressed the item.  A presentation was shown.  She provided a background of the Joseph City wash, the previous flooding issues and the concerns.  She advised that some of the areas are private property without easements and/or State-owned property that the wash goes through. She further advised that there is community support for the study of the wash.  She stated that they have partnered with the railroad and that they have cleaned debris out from under their portion of the wash.  She addressed the proposal from Atkins and the area that they will studied.  She advised that currently they are ahead of schedule by approximately six months.  She stated that staff recommends approval.

Supervisor Thompson stated that since the most recent flooding the issue has been brought up to State Transportation Board.  He recommended that they be contacted regarding the progress on this item.  He stated that the flooding doesn't only impact the railroad but also, I-40.
Supervisor Whitesinger thanked everyone that has invested so much time on this.  She stated that she feels it is important to help and support our communities to ensure public safety.
