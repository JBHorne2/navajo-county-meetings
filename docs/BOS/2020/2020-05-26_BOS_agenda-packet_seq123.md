Title: ASLD: Public Easement Acceptance for Gateway Oasis IV LLC
Submitted For: Bill Bess, County Engineer/Assistant Public Works Director 
Submitted By: Bryan Cook, Sr. Engineering Project Manager II
Department: Public Works
Motion before the Board:
Public Right-of-Way acceptance packet from the Arizona State Land Department granting a
Public Right-of-Way which will then allow Gateway Oasis IV LLC to develop a permanent
access to their subdivision
Background:
Navajo County Public Works has been working with Gateway Oasis IV LLC to  transfer an
easement from Private to Public Right-of-Way through the State Land Department for the
developed of a road in the White Mountain Lakes Community. This road will be developed by
Gateway Oasis IV LLC as part of their proposed development currently under review by the
Planning and Zoning Department.
This acceptance packet with the State Land Department will grant a Public Right-of-Way which
will then allow Gateway Oasis IV LLC to develop a permanent access to their subdivision.
Gateway Oasis IV LLC has provided funding for all associated costs in partnership with Navajo
County to transition the easement to the Public. With the assistance of a public agency,  the
Arizona State Land Department was able to allow for the easement to be both dedicated to the
public and to be perpetual in granting.
Navajo County will not accept the road for maintenance until it has been built to County
Standards determined by the Planning and Zoning subdivision requirements.

Fiscal Impact:
No Fiscal Impact is anticipated. As part of the original agreement with Gateway Oasis IV LLC
approved by the BOS in December, all costs were to be paid by Gateway, the instigating and
benefiting party.

Title: Data Access/Exchange Agmt between ADOT and NCSO
Submitted For: David Clouse, Sheriff  Submitted By: Cheryl Powell, Deputy Chief 
Department: Sheriff 
Motion before the Board:
Data Access/Exchange Agreement between ADOT and NCSO allowing NCSO ability to submit
crash records electronically and retrieve crash data
Background:
The Sheriff's Office (NCSO) currently submits crash forms to the AZ Department Of
Transportation (ADOT) via email once the report is completed by deputies.  The process can take
up to two weeks or more.  NCSO has received a GOHS grant to purchase the hardware
compatible with the AZ TraCS program.  To utilize the program, this agreement with ADOT will
allow deputies to submit the crash data electronically and query, analyze and retrieve crash data as
needed.  Citations will also be submitted electronically.
Deputies will be decreasing their time spent on a traffic stop or collision investigation with
citations and crash reports submitted electronically instead of manually.
Either party may terminate the agreement for convenience or cause upon 30 days written notice.

Title: GOHS 2020-405c-013 AZ TraCS Implementation grant for $54,079
Submitted For: David Clouse, Sheriff  Submitted By: Cheryl Powell, Deputy Chief 
Department: Sheriff 
Motion before the Board:
Governor's Office of Highway Safety grant 2020-405c-013 for $54,079 to implement the AZ
TraCS program
Background:
The Sheriff's Office (NCSO) has been awarded a Governor's Office of Highway Safety (GOHS)
grant for $54,079 to purchase traffic records enforcement related materials and supplies (printers,
scanners and laptops).
Currently, NCSO manually submits traffic citations and crash reports to the Arizona Department
of Transportation (ADOT).  The preparation of crash reports takes up to two weeks to prepare,
submit for approval, and then forward to the records clerk for submission to ADOT.  With the AZ
TraCS software the deputies will be able to submit the AZ Crash Report to ADOT and citations to
the Justice Courts electronically thus freeing the deputies for other patrol functions.
The grant expenditures are reimbursed upon receipt of financial reports.  The award period is
10-01-19 to 09-30-20

National Public Works Week 
WHEREAS, public works professionals focus on infrastructure, facilities and services that are of vital importance to 
sustainable and resilient communities and to the public health, high quality of life and well-being of the people of Navajo 
County; and 
WHEREAS, these infrastructure projects, maintenance operations, emergency management coordination and essential 
services could not be provided without the dedicated efforts of public works professionals from s tate and local 
governments and the private sector; and 
Whereas these public works professionals are responsible for rebuilding, improving and protecting our nation’s 
transportation system; water supply; water treatment and solid waste systems; public buildings; and other structures and 
facilities essential for our citizens; and 
WHEREAS, the men and women of  the Navajo County Public Works, Facility Maintenance and Emergency Management 
teams excel in protecting our infrastructure and the health and safety of  residents, businesses, and visitors by expertly 
providing roadway maintenance and snow removal operations; administrative business and governmental finance 
services; facility maintenance and custodial operations; emergency management coordination; technical engineering and 
flood control services; building and zoning inspections; tribal transportation project and community cleanup coordination; 
planning and zoning services; and fleet maintenance operations;  and 
WHEREAS, many of our employees are emergency responders 24/7 during snow storms, fires, floods, traffic accidents and 
other public safety incidents; and 
WHEREAS, these dedicated employees strive to embody the Navajo County values of Teamwork, Accountability, Integrity, 
Excellence and Innovation every day while providing superior public service.  
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT PROCLAIMED, by the Board of Supervisors of Navajo County, that May 17-23, 2020 is 
designated as “National Public Works Week” in our community and urge all the people of Navajo County to join 
with representatives of the American Public Works Association to pay tribute to the men and women of Navajo 
County Public Works, Facility Maintenance, and Emergency Management for the outstanding contributions they 
make in protecting our health, safety, and quality of life.

I. Promote appropriate physical distancing and physical safety measures 
in accordance with CDC guidelines

• Modify workspaces as necessary and feasible  
• Physical sanitizing stations will be installed throughout County facilities  
 
II. Cleaning and disinfecting of public spaces and collective workplaces 
• Custodial staff perform daily cleanings of workspaces 
• Wiping down all public surfaces (doorknobs, light switches, etc.) 
• When an exposure has been identified, a deep cleaning of that area is done 
• Majority of departments have been issued sanitizer bottles and 
disinfectant for departmental use 
• Employees are expected to regularly provide additional cleaning of their personal 
workspace and equipment. With a focus on high traffic areas such as doorknobs , 
filing cabinets, etc.  
• Disinfectant cleaning supplies will be provided by the Facilities team  
 
III. Protections for Public and Staff interactions   
• Beginning June 1st Public access will be open for walk-in business-  
• Upon entry, Public will be asked to do a temperature check and if temperature 
exceeds 100 degrees F., admission will not be granted. They may also be asked,  
• “Have you come in contact with anybody who has tested positive or 
presumptive positive for COVID-19? “  
• Do you feel well? 
• Visitors will be required to maintain social distancing guidelines  
 
• Temperature checks for public access will be handled at each entrance  
 
IV. Face masks 
• A mask is an extra safeguard —using a mask doesn’t mean you can skip physical 
distancing or other precautions. Masks are not required but may be used as  one 
more tool to help prevent spread. If is important that masks be worn and handled 
properly. The following link is a short video on the basics: 
https://tinyurl.com/yb8xer4w

V. Temperature checks 
• Employees are required to take temperatures each day upon entry into a County 
facility 
• Infrared thermometers will be stationed at common entrances throughout the

• For employees who have a temperature over 100°, employees should 
contact their immediate supervisor 
• Members of the public entering the building will have their temperature checked 
upon entry, may be asked to reschedule if temperature is over 100°, and will be 
advised to consult with their medical professionals

• Continue to work remotely as much as possible  and as appropriate 
• Conduct meetings remotely, when feasible.  ZOOM and T EAMS are two good on-

•  In-person team meetings must adhere to proper physical distancing guidelines- 
Consider the size of room, number of people, and plan accordingly. Set up room 
in advance, if possible 
• Avoid large gatherings until further notice 
• Continue to find creative ways to facilitate collaboration and healthy teams 
• Inviting others to your remote team meetings that might not normally be 
expected to attend, to increase team collaboration

• Beginning after June 1st BOS meetings will return to being conducted in the Board 
Chambers with an option for remote participation 
• Social distancing as directed by CDC will be implemented 
• Hygiene Station will be positioned at the entry to the chambers 
• The number of people permitted in the chambers will be limited based on CDC 
guidelines. If needed, the Code Talkers Conference Room or other available 
conference space may be utilized for overflow or as a waiting room 
• Other public meetings, such as Planning and Zoning, shall follow similar guidelines 
and procedures

Submitted For: John Osgood, Public Works Director

Motion before the Board:
Contract with USGS to adjust two stream gages that are within the Joint Funding Agreement 
Background:
Navajo County Flood Control District has teamed with the U.S. Geological Survey (U.S.G.S.)
over many years to operate and maintain surface water gaging stations. These gages are primarily
located on the Little Colorado River or washes that flow into this river. 
There is no increase in funding from Navajo County for this work. 
Gages utilize technology to calculate the volume of flow based upon a surveyed cross section of
the channel at the gage location and the height of the water.  With the sandy washes in our
county, that cross section is constantly changing and in some cases the flow channel moves away
from the sensor.  Sediment is often picked up and carried downstream during large events
(degradation) or settles out when flow volumes decrease (aggradation). 
Two of the gages - Puerco River near Chamber (upstream of Holbrook) and the gage at the
Holbrook are particularly susceptible to this aggradation and degradation process.   This
causes false or inaccurate data to be reported.
In coordination with the USGS Staff, Flood Control District Staff is recommending that the
Puerco River gage be calibrated to register flows greater than 500 cubic feet per second (cfs) or
more and the Holbrook gage to flows greater than 50 cfs.  These flow volumes will give staff
notification to start watching the flows so that the public can be notified prior to flood levels.

The Joint Funding Agreement 18CMAZ06000 Amendment 1 between the U.S. Geological 
Survey (USGS) and the Navajo County ending September 30, 2021, work plan methodical 
approach is hereby amended as follows: 
1. The USGS is proposing changes to operations at two sites operated for Navajo
County. The first is at the Puerco River near Chambers (09396100), and the other is
at Little Colorado River at Holbrook (09397000). Historically, we operated the Puerco
River site with the objective of producing daily discharge values only when the flow
had a daily average value greater than 500ft3/s. For the last several years we have
been monitoring and measuring the complete range in flows. At lower flows the
sensor is vulnerable to sediment either moving along the bed, filling the channel (and
thus covering the sensor; compromising the accuracy of the data), and/or changing
the gage pool/control. For this site, operations will be modified to focus on
monitoring, measuring, and publishing flows when the instantaneous values (15-
minute data) are greater than 500ft3/s. A banner statement will be placed on the
USGS website informing viewers of this change and a threshold line at 500ft3/s will
be seen on the discharge hydrograph screening out any data below that value.
For the Little Colorado River at Holbrook we have always done our best to monitor, 
measure, and report the whole range in flows. Recently within the USGS agency, our 
product focus has been directed away from daily values to instantaneous values (15-
minute data). The challenge is that the sand channel moves regularly and the 
sensor, in this case a radar mounted to the bridge, often becomes isolated from the 
flow following elevated discharge. After a flow event when the channel moves away 
from a spot below the radar the data recorded is compromised because the sensor is 
hitting sediment and not a water surface. This gives false or inaccurate returns. At 
streamflow discharges above 50ft3/s the complete main channel is filled with water 
giving the radar an appropriate target. For this site operations will be modified to 
focus on monitoring, measuring, and publishing flows when the instantaneous values 
(15 minute data) are greater than 50ft3/s. A banner statement will be placed on the 
USGS website informing viewers of this change and a threshold line at 50ft3/s will be 
seen on the discharge hydrograph screening out any data below that value. In 
addition, at this site we will install an additional radar as a backup prior to the 2020 
Monsoon season. 
These changes will help ensure quality data for use by Navajo County and will not 
compromise our ability to monitor and report the timing and magnitude of high flow 
events. No additional costs are associated with these changes.
