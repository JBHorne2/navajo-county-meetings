d.

Presentation and overview of the 2020 Census :  Kimberley Robinson gave a presentation regarding the upcoming census. She spoke about the Census being 100-percent confidential.  She stated that March 12, 2020, is the first day people can respond to the Census online at 2020census.gov or via telephone.  She indicated that the U.S. Federal Government has set aside $675 billion for allocation to communities based on Census data.  She stated that 95 percent of households will get an invitation in the mail starting this week, and 5 percent of households will have a form delivered by a Census worker.  She spoke about the two tribal partnership specialists.  She indicated that a reminder letter will be sent March 26 through April 3rd to those who have not responded; and from April 8 to April 16, a letter and questionnaire will be delivered to those who have not responded.  She stated that if they do not hear from someone by April 30th, Census takers will begin knocking on doors.  She encouraged everyone to please count everyone living with you and indicated that children aged 0 to 5 are the most undercounted people, and they need to be counted.  She stated that they are looking for questionnaire assistance sites and indicated that the Navajo County libraries are all on board.  She indicated that their number one goal is to increase and encourage self reporting. She spoke about how to identify census workers by their badges, phones, tablets and census tote bags.  She stated that they are currently hiring in Navajo County, and people can go to 2020census.gov/jobs or call 1-855-job2020.  She indicated that Census day is April 1, 2020, which is the day you look at for counting those people in your home as of that date. She answered questions of the Board and spoke about community partners.
Supervisor Seymore thanked Ms. Robinson for her presentation and efforts and stated that he expects our Census numbers to be much more accurate because of the legwork of Ms. Robinson and her team.

NAVAJO COUNTY PUBLIC HEALTH SERVICES DISTRICT:  Board of Directors Session :  At 9:13 a.m., Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion to enter into the Navajo County Public Health Services District; motion seconded by Supervisor Seymore .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

a.

Presentation and update on Co-Vid-19 :  Jeff Lee, Health Director, addressed the Board regarding COVID-19 or Coronavirus. He indicated that they have implemented the following weekly communication and coordination plan:

Mondays and Tuesdays, county health director coordination calls, as well as statewide partner update with the Arizona Department of Health Services;

Wednesdays, meetings internally with Courts, County Administration, school superintendents and administration and NPC.  He stated that they also have a coordination call with all health care facilities on and off the reservation, and wrap up with an internal team planning meeting to get ready for the rest of the week;

Thursdays, push out information to our cities/town managers, school superintendents and administrators, department directors and elected officials.  He stated that they will also issue a press release on Thursday and push out information on social media.

He encouraged everyone to frequently check their dashboard which can be accessed by going to the Navajo County home page and following the link.  He stated that it will be updated at least once or twice a week, but if information needs to be sent out immediately, they will do that.

He spoke about what everyone should do to help prevent the spread of the Coronavirus as follows:

Avoid close contact with people who are sick.

Avoid touching your eyes, nose and mouth.

Avoid shaking hands.

Stay home when you are sick.

If you feel sick, utilize telemedicine if available or call the healthcare facility prior to arrival

Cover your cough or sneeze with a tissue, then throw the tissue in the trash.

Clean and disinfect frequently touched objects and surfaces using a regular household cleaning spray or wipe.

Wash your hands often with soap and water for at least 20 seconds throughout the day.

He thanked his staff for putting in long hours communicating and coordinating with our partners to make sure our residents have the most updated information and guidance to keep our friends, families and neighbors safe. Mr. Lee answered questions of the Board.  Supervisor Thompson spoke about the importance of education and thanked Mr. Lee and his staff.  Mr. Lee indicated that the Arizona State Lab is the only lab in Arizona currently that is allowed to run the tests at this time.  He stated that when an individual presents at a healthcare facility, they have to meet certain criteria for the Health Department to sign off on that individual to be tested.  He indicated that the medical  professionals can get the swabs from the patient onsite and then the swabs are sent to the State Lab, and it is about 24 hours to get the information back. Mr. Lee indicated that state was awarded $500,000 last week and the money will be allocated towards testing capabilities.

Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Lee and his team for the information that has been going out to all of our citizens and partners. He spoke about the importance of talking with our families and discussing preventative measures and being alert, aware and responsible.  He reiterated the things that we can do to help, including no handshakes, washing our hands for 20 seconds, stay home if you're sick, avoid close contact with those who are sick, cover your cough, clean and disinfect surfaces frequently.   Supervisor Jack thanked Mr. Lee for the information and inquired whether we have a smaller chance because we are so isolated, and Mr. Lee indicated that there is still a chance; however, we are more isolated.

Supervisor Thompson made a motion to return to regular session; motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
