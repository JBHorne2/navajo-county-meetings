REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

Update on COVID-19 :  Jeff Lee thanked our nurses and their teams for the outstanding work that they do for our community.  He stated that cases continue to rise across the county; and as of this morning, Navajo County has 1,017 cases.  He indicated that in Navajo County, we have completed 3,694 tests, and the numbers of cases will continue to increase with the increased testing efforts.  He stated that this past weekend, Summit participated in the Arizona blitz testing efforts.  He indicated that facilities have the ability to apply to be a blitz testing location through the Arizona Department of Health Services (ADHS).  He stated that they will continue to share the information from ADHS as to different blitz locations across Arizona.  He indicated that individuals will need to adhere to the requirements for each particular location.  He reminded everyone that the Navajo County Health Department does not test individuals for COVID.  He stated that based on the current testing algorithm and a quick triage of symptoms or situation, a health care facility/provider chooses whether a test is needed.  He indicated that the Navajo County Health Department's role in testing is to approve a specimen to be sent to the State laboratory.  He stated that yesterday, dine-in services for restaurants started, and more retail businesses have begun to reopen.  As we move forward, he encouraged everyone to continue practicing the precautionary measures:  physical distancing, staying home if we feel ill, hand washing, coughing or sneezing into a tissue, disinfect frequently used surfaces and wearing a face mask in public.

Mr. Lee indicated that fire season is quickly approaching, and they will start providing regular updates based on the recommendations of their group.  Supervisor Thompson inquired regarding the criteria for reopening or relaxing the restrictions.  Mr. Lee said one of the key indicators that ADHS and the Governor are using to lessen the restrictions or reopen some businesses is the total percent positive.  He indicated that currently, in Arizona, over 159,000 COVID tests have been taken, and 6.5% of those have come back positive; and as long as we see that percent positive trending in a downward trajectory, that gives the Governor an indicator to start looking at reopening things.  He stated that other things they're considering is hospitalization rates, utilization of key assets and personal protective equipment in those facilities.  Supervisor Jack inquired regarding the request for another test site alongside IHS as Kayenta is a hot spot, and Mr. Lee stated that he does not have an update on that request; but to be a testing site, a facility or organization has to apply through ADHS.  He indicated that additional testing supplies have been provided to the Navajo Nation.  Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Hill and his team for making sure public health is taken care of and getting good and accurate information out to the public.

Supervisor Thompson made a motion to return to regular session; motion seconded by Supervisor Seymore .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES :

a.

Presentation regarding the Census Operational Timeline and Updates :  Kimberley Robinson, Partnership Specialist, Northern Arizona U.S. Census Bureau, addressed the Board.  She spoke about the updated timeline for the 2020 Census due to COVID 19.  She stated that field activities will resume after June 1st.  She indicated that the Census Bureau will work with partners to determine their comfort level with regard to the timeline.   She stated that for those experiencing homelessness, they will work with partners to make sure they're counted.  She indicated that their non-response follow-up will begin August 11th through October 31st.  She stated that this does not include those who do not get home mailed delivery.  She stated that group quarters will start July 1st through September 3rd; transitory locations will start September 3rd through September 28th tentatively; and updating addresses and leaving questionnaires will begin June 13th through July 9th.  She indicated that Navajo County's numbers will stay low for self-response until the end of June due to the amount of areas who do not get mail.  She stated that the field office will tentatively open June 1st, but they will follow state guidelines.  She spoke about the option to complete the Census over the phone for those who have an identifying number.  She stated that they want to ensure Navajo County is accurately counted and are revamping materials for schools for when they reopen.  She stated that jobs are still available in Navajo County.  She spoke about what we can do now to encourage citizens to respond to the Census.  Chairman Whiting thanked Ms. Robinson for her presentation.  Supervisor Thompson thanked Ms. Robinson for her efforts and quick response.

b.

Consideration and approval of the Uni Source Franchise Agreement for the period of 25 years :  Jason Moore discussed the item.  He stated that Uni Source has to apply for a franchise to operate in Navajo County every 25 years.  He indicated that this agreement does not include the provision requiring Uni Source to pay the County a certain amount on an annual basis for the right to operate in the County, because that provision became unconstitutional.    Supervisor Whitesinger inquired how long we have had this in place prior to now, and Mr. Moore stated that the existing agreement was a 25-year agreement, and he does not know how long Uni Source had been operating in Navajo County prior to that time.  Vice-Chairman Jack made a motion to approve the Uni Source Franchise Agreement for the period of 25 years; motion seconded by Supervisor Seymore .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

PUBLIC WORKS :

a.

Consideration of approval of Intergovernmental Agreement between Navajo County and Pinon Chapter requesting road maintenance funds :  John Osgood discussed the item.  He stated that this request represents a unique partnership between Navajo County, Pinon Chapter, Pinon Unified School District and the Bureau of Indian Affairs to creatively leverage resources which help improve road maintenance, increase access to education to Chapter school children and reduce absenteeism.  He indicated that the funds will be used to reimburse the Chapter for the purchase of equipment used to maintain the public roads within the Chapter.  He stated that the fiscal impact is $100,000, which is authorized and budgeted in the special road project fund for District I, and the source of the funds is the Highway User Revenue Fund which can only be used for transportation-related activities on public roads.  He indicated that staff recommends approval.  Bessie Allen, Pinon Chapter President, thanked the Board for the opportunity to advocate on behalf of the school district and the community.  She stated that they continue to reach out to everyone to help with the roads to allow school children to safely get to and from school.   She requested that the Board approve this item.  Vice-Chairman Jack expressed appreciation for this intergovernmental agreement.  He indicated that they have been working on this for a couple of years.  He thanked Ms. Allen for her leadership.  Supervisor Thompson expressed appreciation for the work that has gone into this agreement.  Supervisor Thompson made a motion to approve the Intergovernmental Agreement between Navajo County and Pinon Chapter requesting road maintenance funds; motion seconded by Vice-Chairman Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
