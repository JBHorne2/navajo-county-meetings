REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

Update on COVID-19 :  Jeff Lee indicated that our numbers continue to rise, and our healthcare system partners are facing staffing and bed capacity issues.  He stated that risk of exposure to COVID is high, and we need to treat this virus as if it is everywhere we go.  He indicated that as of this morning, there are 105,094 cases in Arizona wth 1,927 deaths, 117 of which were reported this morning.  He stated that for Navajo County, we have 4,015 cases with 134 deaths.

At 9:17 a.m., Supervisor Thompson was present.

Mr. Lee stated that 49% of our cases came in the month of June.  He indicated that our behaviors are helping to spread the virus, and we each need to do our part to help slow the spread.  He stated there is not a way to prevent or stop the virus completely, but we can reduce the risk by using the tools we've been given.  He focused on staying home if you're sick.  He stated that the best tools to slow the spread are physical distancing and staying home as much as we can.  He recommended that you wear a mask if you cannot maintain a 6-foot distance between yourself and others.  He spoke about washing our hands, frequently disinfecting and coughing or sneezing into a bent elbow or tissue.  He stated that all these tools put together will slow the spread and lower our individual risk from getting COVID or passing it on to others.  He requested that our community do our part to slow the spread by following these recommendations.

Supervisor Jack thanked Mr. Lee for the report.  He stated that he has tried to encourage his constituents to wear masks all the time and follow the recommendations.  He indicated that when this started, there was talk about this virus would probably slow down when it warms up, and it seems like the opposite is happening.  Mr. Lee indicated that the most difficult thing about this virus is that we're learning as we go.  Supervisor Thompson indicated that he is doing his part to communicate with his community.  He stated that he is worried about school opening in the fall and expressed the desire for everyone to use masks.  Chairman Whiting stated that the public is demanding answers that are not there, and experts are trying to provide data prefacing that they do not know what the virus is going to do.  He requested that Mr. Lee explain how the CDC has changed its opinion with regard to masks.  Mr. Lee stated that the early recommendation by the CDC was that masks were not a recommended preventive measure; however, this virus is different than any others we've dealt with in recent history and likes close contact settings and adding another barrier, a face mask or covering, helps slow the spread.  Chairman Whiting asked Mr. Lee to clarify the point that the mask does not protect me, it protects you; and for it to work, it would require everybody to wear a mask.  Mr. Lee stated that by wearing a mask, you are protecting others; and in a group of 10, masks will only be effective if the vast majority are wearing masks which is due to the amount of asymptomatic people who test positive and are contagious.
Chairman Whiting inquired whether the mask is being asked to be worn if you can't socially distance, and Mr. Lee stated that our best tool is physical distancing; and when you have to break that barrier, wearing a mask will significantly reduce the risk of spreading and slowing the spread of the virus.  Supervisor Thompson stated that his message is to adhere to the guidelines of the CDC.  Supervisor Jack stated that he heard from some people that they took every precaution, and they still got the virus, but some people who did not take those precautions from the same family did not get the virus.  Mr. Lee indicated that the virus attacks our weakest point.  He spoke about the difference in immune systems.  He stated that the tools do not eliminate the risk, but they lower the risk.  Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Lee and his team for all that they are doing to help the community.

ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES :

a.

Consideration and adoption of the proposed Tentative Budget for Fiscal Year 2020-21 for Navajo County :  Bryan Layton spoke about the FY 2021 preliminary budget.  He indicated that a flat property tax levy plus new construction caused the property tax rate to lower.  He stated that we have strong financial reserves and are prepared to respond if needed.  He spoke about the budget foundational steps, including maintain services at current levels, ability to weather potential protracted loss in sales tax and capacity and infrastructure.  He shared the 2020 data of primary property tax rates and levies for counties throughout Arizona.  He indicated that for 2020, Navajo County has the 4th lowest property tax rate and low property values.  He spoke about the property tax allocation in Navajo County with the vast majority of each dollar going to schools and 8 cents to the county.  He indicated that state shared sales tax came in strong this year, and we are projected to collect $13,040,000 in 2020; and in 2021, we are projecting a 24% decrease.  For the county sales tax, he stated that 2020 came in higher than anticipated, and we are projecting a 30% decrease for 2021.  He stated that the total general fund budget for 2021 is $47,817,409, with increases being due to contingency funding from grants and savings to be ready to respond, state retirement systems, investment in new treasurer system, rising cost of doing business and labor costs.  He stated that 41% of our budget is for public safety, 6.8% is Arizona state costs and payments, 10.2% for mandated services, 19.88% county-wide costs and administration and 22.08% carry-forward contingency.  He indicated that 67% of the operating budget is for staff and employee-related expenses.  He spoke about special revenue funds, including the jail district, emergency management, health district grant funding, potential grants for workforce, potential grants for the County Attorney's Office, increase in capital outlay, funding for an election year and growth in health trust savings.  He stated that we have not increased employee premiums for 3 years for healthcare and have saved the County over $9 million compared to our prior plan.

Looking forward, Mr. Layton spoke about approaching criminal justice differently with the following objectives for both juveniles and adults with low-level/no-violent offenses:

Send fewer people to jail and lower recidivism rate

Address underlying issues of crime such as substance abuse, mental health and other social factors.

He stated that this is smart and compassionate and makes a great business plan.  He thanked Chief Probation Officer Jason Cash who has done a lot to develop those relationships and the details to make this happen.  He stated that staff recommends adoption of the tentative budget for FY2020-21.  Supervisor Seymore thanked Mr. Layton and his team and requested that he state the amount of the budget.  Mr. Layton stated the amount of the FY2021 general fund budget is $47,817,409, and the total consolidated budget is $147,160,909.  Supervisor Thompson thanked all who have been involved in preparing the budget.  He expressed pride in Navajo County for pulling through financial challenges.  He stated that because of what is going on with COVID-19, we have not been able to use all our flexible spending and requested that be carried over into the new year.  Mr. Layton stated that they will have that discussion.  Supervisor Whitesinger thanked Mr. Layton for the work and dedication that he put into this.  She spoke about the importance of transparency.  She stated that she is grateful for the work the judicial system is putting forth to help us be proactive in the future.  She indicated that there is gratitude in the work that the Board is doing in not reaching the levies and being cognizant in how the dollars are being utilized.  Chairman Whiting thanked Glenn Kephart, Bryan Layton, Paige Peterson, Melissa Buckley, members of the Board, department directors and elected officials for coming together to prepare this budget.  Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion to adopt the proposed Tentative Budget for Fiscal Year 2020-21 for Navajo County; motion seconded by Supervisor Thompson .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
