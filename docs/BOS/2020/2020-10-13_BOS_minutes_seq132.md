2.

Update on COVID-19:    Jeff Lee advised that as of this morning there are 5923 cases in Navajo County.  He indicated that over the past ten days there has been a slight rise in new cases.  He advised that these are the first cases in some time that are not linked back to a school exposure, indicating that there is still community spread within Navajo County.  He further advised that they have seen a significate drop in the numbers of tests performed over the past ten days, which will affect the percent positivity benchmark for schools and businesses.  He indicated that there is still testing capacity with the healthcare providers throughout the County, so if you feel sick and want to be tested there should be no challenges in finding a place to get tested.  He advised that they will be posting locations on their dashboard that will give the details for where, how and when someone can get tested.  He stated that they know there is Covid fatigue, but the challenge is to keep following the CDC and the ADHS recommendations to prevent another spike.  He advised that we need to focus on the recommendations for physically distancing, staying home when you feel ill, washing your hands and wearing a mask when you can't physically distance.  He further advised that this will also help with flu season, and that following these precautionary measures can help lessen the impact of the flu season to our community.  He indicated that the flu vaccine is available, that they are working with their community partners to provide drive thru locations throughout Navajo County.  He further indicated that they will continue to update social media and the Covid dashboards with the drive thru locations and dates.  He advised that this year it is crucial to get a flu vaccine to avoid another stressor on the healthcare system and to help avoid any flu/Covid co-infections.
Supervisor Whitesinger inquired if it is true that the virus is changing in a sense that it might not be as challenging to us as in the past, that it might be a milder form.
Mr. Lee advised that there is some evidence that the virus is changing, but that they don't have enough evidence to make solid predications.  He indicated that some of the things they are seeing is lessoned symptoms, meaning people are not having such a severe reaction from it.  He further indicated that the healthcare system is learning more about the virus and learning how to treat it better, so those who may have a little more severe reaction are being treated in a way that people are recovering from it much quicker and with less intensive care at the hospitals.  He advised that there are some good signs but that it's too early to say that it has changed to a weaker version of the virus.
Supervisor Whitesinger thanked Mr. Lee for the information.
Supervisor Thompson stated that he thinks no matter how the situation is improving as we move forward that we still need to continue to closely follow the CDC guidelines.  He thanked the health professionals and the people on the front line doing all that they do to ease the burden of the virus.  He stated that on the Navajo Nation they were hit hard, that things are getting  better, but that he believes in order to get over the virus  we need to continue to follow the CDC guidelines.  He advised that he is following those guidelines and the Executive Orders of the Navajo Nation President.
Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Lee for the updates he provides and for the active approach they are taking.  He indicated that he knows that his team is actively working with the communities and the schools.

Consideration of a Proclamation declaring our gratitude to Douglas A. Ducey, Governor of the State of Arizona, for the initiative he took in implementing Indigenous Peoples Day :  Supervisor Whitesinger advised that in early September the Governor passed a Proclamation supporting Indigenous Peoples' Day to be recognized on October 12, 2020.  She indicated that this Proclamation is a historic effort for the State of Arizona since this hasn't happened in the past and provides an opportunity for tribal communities to be empowered and to work towards not being the marginalized communities that they often are. She stated that they are grateful for the efforts of all who worked to make this day possible.  She advised that the Initiative Team which includes her son, who chaired the group, and Senator Peshlakai have worked very hard on this for years prior.  She addressed what it meant to her the day her son received the letter with the Proclamation from the Governor.  She advised that in Arizona there are twenty-two federally recognized tribes and indicated that taking the time to recognize those communities is a great accomplishment.  She read the Navajo County Proclamation.  Supervisor Whitesinger stated that one of the things that is important to understand in being able to recognize Indigenous People within our state and across the country is that it is an important effort but that it doesn't take away from the history of our culture or our nation. She indicated that it is important to be reminded that the great love of our country comes from the history and being able to tell a truer story of our history but not to take away from those efforts that came before.  She further indicated that we want to be able to recognize all efforts within the United States and all of those who have made our country what it is and that we recognize the Indigenous People as part of that great history.
Supervisor Thompson thanked Supervisor Whitesinger.  He indicated that we are all reflecting on the comments she made as we move towards Native American Heritage Month and Veterans Day.  He further indicated that we need to remind each other that we are here together and that we respect one another.  He stated that he appreciates the efforts made in working together with the legislators and with the Office of the Governor. He addressed the personal impact he had being appointed to the State Transportation Board and indicated that it is difficult to get things moving in the right direction for addressing rural and remote improvements. He stated that it will take a lot of work on our part to make changes.  He indicated that he is reminded of what the Veterans have done for the land, the people, the government and the constitution.  He further indicated that he is also reminded of what Thanksgiving is about and that it is very sacred to him to be reminded to respect others and learn from one another.
Supervisor Seymore thanked Supervisor Whitesinger and indicated her son, Dillon needs to be recognized for his great work, for taking this on as a youth and to be able to be successful.  He indicated that he appreciates her remarks about being a great nation and that it is the cultures and the history that keeps us strong and that it is those freedoms that we all fight for.
Supervisor Jack thanked the Supervisors for their remarks.  He indicated that he appreciates this Proclamation, that he thinks it is good and has been a long time coming.  He further indicated that it is something to look forward to in the future, it encourages better relationships and that there will be a better understanding.  He stated that he hopes there will be more opportunities for working together.  He further stated that we all come together when it's time to defend this country, no matter how diverse the country is.  He stated that in times like now, with the crisis, we all need to help each other.
Chairman Whiting indicated that he echoes what the other Supervisors have said.  He indicated that Supervisor Whitesinger , her son Dillon, and all of those involved have brought us all together, to unify us and help recognize an important part of our history and part of our culture in Arizona.  He indicated that he appreciates the time Supervisor Whitesinger has taken to put this Proclamation together, and her son's leadership in working with the Governor.

PUBLIC WORKS:

a.

Consideration and possible approval of license extension agreement with AZTEC Land and Cattle Co. LLC for the Woodruff Pit:   John Osgood addressed the item.  He indicated that in 2016 Navajo County and Aztec Land and Cattle entered into a license agreement that allows the County to remove materials from two ridge lines located within Aztec Land and Cattle Company's property, also known as the Woodruff Pit. He indicated that the license has expired and that both parties have agreed to an extension until 2024.  He advised that the materials removed from the pit are used by road maintenance crews for spot repairs on county-maintained gravel roads.  He further advised that as part of this agreement the County has contracted for an archeological survey of the pit boundaries. He indicated that they have spoken to the consultants and that the survey didn't find anything of cultural value or of concern to the continued gravel operations and to Native American culture in this specific area.  He advised that using this pit has been operationally efficient.  He further advised that funding is from HURF and that staff recommends approval.
Supervisor Seymore inquired how many cubic yards are taken out of there per year.
Mr. Osgood indicated that he didn't have the figure at this time but that he could provide that information after the meeting. He advised that he knows they don't use a larger amount of material from there every year.
Supervisor Seymore inquired as to what kind of material they use from the pit.
Mr. Osgood advised that it is a gravel material that is in rough shape, that they don't add anything to it, that they run it through a grizzly screen and that they use it just for spot repair.
Supervisor Thompson indicated that there is a law that was put in place by the United States Congress referred to as the Religious Freedom Restoration Act which has been set in place for projects that would impact a cultural sight.  He further indicated that there are very strict policies to follow if you are going to impact a cultural site.  He inquired if there are any concerns with the lawsuit regarding this project.
Mr. Osgood indicated that he is not aware of this site having anything to do with a lawsuit.
Glenn Kephart advised that in 2016 when they redid the agreement that this site is not part of the lawsuit, that the lawsuit is regarding the Butte to the north that was being mined.
Claire Brophy with Aztec indicated that she knows they are getting a survey of the site to determine if there are any cultural artifacts in this area that would be disturbed.
Supervisor Thompson thanked them for the clarifications.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion for approval of license extension agreement with AZTEC Land and Cattle Co. LLC for the Woodruff Pit; motion seconded by Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Consideration of the purchase of two 2020 140-15 AWD Caterpillar Motor Graders with a trade-in of two Motor Graders from Empire Machinery in the amount of $565,117.64:   John Osgood addressed the item and indicated that this continues the replacing of vehicles that are at the end of their useful life.  He advised that these are among the most utilized pieces of equipment in the County road maintenance fleet. He indicated that about 55% of the roads that the County maintains have gravel surfaces where these blades are used every day and that they are also used for snow removal operations. He indicated that this purchase will replace two graders that are 28 and 36 years old.  He further indicated that the new equipment uses approximately 42 percent less fuel per hour of equipment operation which keeps costs down. He advised that with the equipment trade-in's the fiscal impact will be $565,117.64 and the source of the funding is from the Highway User Revenue Fund.  Mr. Osgood advised that staff recommends approval.
Supervisor Thompson inquired as to what is done  with the trade-in's since they are no longer useful.
Mr. Osgood advised that some of the equipment will be repaired and that they may sell it to another user as a used piece of equipment.  He further advised that a lot of work is needed on both graders.
Supervisor Thompson inquired how he could obtain one of the graders.
Mr. Osgood indicated that if HURF money is going to be used and the graders would be used on HURF eligible roads, he could contact a representative at Empire and let them know of the interest.  He further indicated that if it was not HURF eligible roads and funding that he could still contact them.   Mr. Osgood advised that the parts are very hard to find for the equipment due to their age.
Supervisor Thompson addressed the roads to the schools on the Reservation that are maintained by the BIA and the Navajo Nation and indicated that it is difficult to get funding and equipment to maintain these roads. He indicated that he would like to discuss this further with Mr. Osgood.
Supervisor Thompson made a motion to approve the purchase of two 2020 140-15 AWD Caterpillar Motor Graders with a trade-in of two Motor Graders from Empire Machinery in the amount of $565,117.64; motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and possible adoption Resolution Number 38-2020 approving/denying a Special Use Permit to operate an already existing 198' meteorological tower on APN 110-02-004A, a portion of Township 15 North, Range 20 East, Section 14 of the Gila and Salt River Meridian:   Cody Cooper addressed the item and advised that this was previously approved in 2013 through Resolution 12-13.  He indicated that the applicant has requested that the permit have no expiration date, that the previous permit had conditions, one being that three years after it was approved there would have to be an application to extend the special use permit, that based on their records the permit did expire in 2016 but that all other conditions had been met.  He indicated that another condition was that the county could treat the tower as a nuisance following the expiration, but that there have been no complaints and that based on that staff felt that this item should come before the Board and be approved for a special use permit for the continued use.  He advised that through the Planning and Zoning Commission in July the only question raised was whether the Game and Fish Department would be comfortable having no expiration date for the towers. He indicated that they did contact Game & Fish and that they have noted that they don't have concerns with no expiration date, and they are in support.  He indicated that staff recommends approval subject to conditions, which are no expiration date, 90 days after the end of use it will be removed, and that all other conditions of Resolution 12-13 stay in place.
Chairman Whiting opened the meeting, there being no public comments, Chairman Whiting closed the public hearing.
Supervisor Thompson inquired if there have been any written comments or telephonic comments regarding the matter.
Melissa Buckley indicated that none have been received.
Mr. Cooper advised that they haven't received any public comments, that they did post and did the required outreach.

Supervisor Seymore   made a motion for adoption of Resolution Number 38-2020 approving a Special Use Permit to operate an already existing 198' meteorological tower on APN 110-02-004A, a portion of Township 15 North, Range 20 East, Section 14 of the Gila and Salt River Meridian; motion seconded by Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Consideration and approval of Navajo County's Community Rating System Annual Recertification Letter:   John Osgood addressed the Board regarding the item. He advised that the primary goals of the rating system are to reduce flood damage to insurable property, to strength and support the insurance aspects of the National Flood Insurance Program and to encourage a comprehensive approach to the flood claim management. He further advised in addition the rating system flood plain management activities provide enhanced public safety, reduced damage to property and public infrastructure and an avoidance of economic disruption and loss.   He indicated that the benefits for Navajo County residence and property owners is that through the County's participation in this program they have increased opportunities to learn about risks, evaluate their individual vulnerabilities and take actions to protect their homes and businesses.  He further indicated that property owners also receive a ten percent reduction in flood insurance premiums.  He advised that staff recommends approval.
Supervisor Seymore inquired if the discount is standard with all companies for the ten percent or if they have to submit paperwork to the county to get that discount.
Mr. Osgood indicated that he does not believe that there is any additional information required but that he will look into  that.
