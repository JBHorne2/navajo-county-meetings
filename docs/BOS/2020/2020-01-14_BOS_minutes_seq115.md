NAVAJO COUNTY BOARD OF SUPERVISORS' MINUTES
Tuesday, January 14, 2020

Present:
Lee Jack, Sr, Vice-Chairman; Jesse Thompson, Supervisor District II; Jason Whiting, Chairman; Daryl Seymore, Supervisor District IV; Dawnafe Whitesinger, Supervisor District V

Staff Attendance:
Glenn Kephart, County Manager; Brandt Clark, Deputy County Attorney; Melissa Buckley, Clerk of the Board

9:00 a.m . Call To Order: Invocation and Pledge of Allegiance was led by Supervisor Seymore.

BOARD OF SUPERVISORS :

a.

Election of Chairman and Vice-Chairman for 2020 :  Chairwoman Whitesinger opened the item.  Supervisor Thompson made a motion to elect Jason Whiting as Chairman and Lee Jack, Sr., as Vice-Chairman for 2020; motion seconded by Supervisor Seymore .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

b.

Presentation to Chairwoman Dawnafe Whitesinger for her 2019 service :
Chairman Whiting thanked the Supervisors for their support and stated that he hopes to continue to follow in the former Chairwoman's footsteps. He indicated that Chairwoman Whitesinger has been an outstanding leader for all of Navajo County.  He stated that she is calming, kind, unifies the group, strong, determined and very capable. He indicated that she did a great job leading through Prop 421, trying to reconnect with our employees this year, and she maintained partnerships with many of the cities and towns. He spoke about her service on the First Things First Board and White Mountain Regional Transportation Board.  The Board of Supervisors presented Supervisor Whitesinger with a small token of recognition for her service this past year.  Supervisor Thompson thanked Supervisor Whitesinger for her leadership during 2019 and indicated that her politeness and kindness was helpful to a lot of people. He stated that Supervisor Whitesinger motivated this board. Supervisor Seymore congratulated Chairman Whiting . He then thanked Supervisor Whitesinger for her great leadership and partnership with our cities and towns. Supervisor Jack also thanked Supervisor Whitesinger for her service and leadership to Navajo County. Supervisor Whitesinger thanked her fellow Board members. She thanked the organization for all of their help in serving our communities. She spoke about the love of family, love of community, love of tribal community and service.  She spoke about the challenging conversations which were had this past year regarding our financial crisis and expressed gratitude to those who were invested in the conversation.

CALL TO THE PUBLIC :  Buzz Essel, 1770 Edwina Place, Show Low, Arizona, concerned citizen of White Mountain Lakes, addressed the Board. He spoke about his background.  He spoke about the lack of attention that the White Mountain Lake community receives.  He indicated that there is a total disconnect. He stated that there are over 4,000 potential property owners, and the lake is the biggest issue.  He inquired as to what entity he would have to join to be heard as a citizen of Navajo County. He requested that someone come out and talk to them. He expressed concern about future development. He invited any member of the Board of Directors of White Mountain Lake County Recreation District to the community to address the residents.  He indicated that members of the Board were invited to previous meetings and have rejected those invitations.  Chairman Whiting directed Melissa Buckley to get with Mr. Essel. Supervisor Seymore stated that he would be happy to attend the meeting, as well.  Theresa Dwyer, 8315 Juniper Drive, Show Low, concerned taxpaying resident of White Mountain Lake Estates, addressed the Board.  She spoke about taxation without representation in regards to the White Mountain Lakes District.  She indicated that historically, the lake district met on a monthly basis and now they are having only a yearly meeting, which is not what the citizens want.  She stated that there has been a lack of communication between the Board and citizens within the District.  She spoke about the meeting on Sunday where it was mentioned that members of this Board think the citizens of White Mountain Lake are unorganized and unless they have a group representation, the Board does not want to meet with them. She stated that Walt Blackman attended the meeting, but the Board of Supervisors/Board of Directors did not.   She expressed concern regarding the intentions of a group who would like to take over the running the lake from the Board of Supervisors.  She also spoke about the additional road fee that she pays and stated that she believes there is mismanagement from the road district, as well.  She stated that they have had developers come in and fail, and the citizens are left to clean up the mess.  She spoke about being protected from developers like this. Penny Stegeman, 8323 Juniper Drive, White Mountain Lake Estates, inquired when the Board of Supervisors will be having a meeting for the residents of White Mountain Lake Recreation District.  She spoke about a group of concerned citizens of the community regarding the proposed RV development who indicated that the Board will only talk with a group like the community association.  She encouraged the Board to view the video of the meeting on Sunday.  She stated that the White Mountain Lake Community Association represents less than one percent of all the owners of the White Mountain Lake Community and spoke about concerns regarding this organization.  She indicated that there is another group sending out cards in the mail to all owners to sign a petition regarding taxation without representation.  She stated that they should not have to pay a fee of $20.00 to have their voices heard by the Board of Supervisors.  She indicated that there is very little information regarding where her taxes go and that they are one of the highest property tax rates in Arizona.  She expressed concern regarding the proposed RV development.  She requested a meeting with the Board for White Mountain Lake Recreation District.

Chairman Whiting encouraged those who spoke to please talk to Melissa Buckley and set up a time to be able to communicate. He indicated that there are some clear misunderstandings. He stated that the road district has a separate Board. He indicated that he would like to hear more about the fee they are paying to communicate. He then spoke about the budget. He indicated that there is a team that is hired and works for the District.  He stated that Pat Stevens is there day to day to take the complaints and recommendations and then after the season is over in the fall, Ms. Stevens comes to the Board of Supervisors and gives a report regarding what went well and where people want to see changes.  He indicated that from that point in time, they work on what can be done within the budget to fix or address the issues brought up.  He further stated that this is followed by a public meeting where the community is informed regarding where the money was spent, where the money will be spent the following year and where it's being suggested.  He indicated that there is then a formal meeting where the budget is voted on by the Board of Directors and people have the opportunity to come and speak. He stated that if there needs to be anything further, we can address that.

PUBLIC WORKS :

a.

Consideration and possible adoption of Resolution Number 04-2020 , approving/denying designating public roads within the Timberland Acres Special Road District #1 as primitive roads :  John Osgood discussed the item.  He stated that Items 6a and 6b will allow the County to continue partnering with Timberland Acres Special Road District #1 in compliance with updated Arizona Revised Statutes around primitive roads and maintenance of public streets and in compliance with Board of Supervisors approved road maintenance policy.  Supervisor Seymore thanked staff for their work on this item.  Supervisor Thompson inquired whether there are any ADT requirements, and Mr. Osgood stated that there are no requirements, other than if they had been laid out and opened before June 1990.  Supervisor Seymore made a motion to adopt Resolution Number 04-2020 , approving designating public roads within the Timberland Acres Special Road District #1 as primitive roads; motion seconded by Supervisor Thompson .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

b.

Consideration of the Intergovernmental Agreement between Navajo County and Timberland Acres Special Road District #1 regarding road maintenance :  John Osgood discussed the item.  He stated that this will facilitate continuing road maintenance partnership opportunities between the road district and Navajo County within the framework of the County's maintained road system policy and ARS statutes pertaining to primitive road maintenance.  He indicated that there is no fiscal impact associated with this item.  Supervisor Seymore made a motion to approve the Intergovernmental Agreement between Navajo County and Timberland Acres Special Road District #1 regarding road maintenance; motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

c.

Consideration of Approving Task Agreement No. 19 to Memorandum of Agreement between the Bureau of Indian Affairs Department of Transportation- Navajo Region and Navajo County for the continued funding support of two Navajo County Road Maintenance Equipment Operators :  John Osgood discussed the item.  He indicated that the Bureau of Indian Affairs Department of Transportation and Navajo County continue to enjoy a successful operational partnership.  He stated that the County provides the funding for and hiring of heavy equipment operators to the Navajo Nation within Districts I and II which supplements and assists the BIA with road maintenance, especially on school bus routes.  He indicated that this new agreement will continue to support the two equipment operators through 12/31/2020.  He stated that the fiscal impact is $60,000 for the year with $30,000 each from special road district project funds for Districts 1 and 2, and the source of the funding is the Highway User Revenue Fund, HURF, which is dedicated solely for transportation-related programs and projects.
Supervisor Thompson indicated this is a good project partnering with BIA, Navajo Nation Department of Transportation, as well as local communities, addressing and blading these school bus routes.  He stated that the Navajo Nation and the BIA do not have adequate funding to do everything they're required to do, so the County comes in and supports them.  Supervisor Thompsonmade a motion to approve Task Agreement No. 19 to Memorandum of Agreement between the Bureau of Indian Affairs Department of Transportation - Navajo Region and Navajo County for the continued funding support of two Navajo County Road Maintenance Equipment Operators; motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Supervisor Jack thanked Mr. Osgood and staff for working on this item and indicated that this is a good example of partnership to address the terrible roads on the Reservation.  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
