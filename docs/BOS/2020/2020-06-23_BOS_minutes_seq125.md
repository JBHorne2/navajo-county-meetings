REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

Update on COVID-19 :  Jeff Lee stated that we continue to trend in the wrong direction.  He indicated that as of this morning, Navajo County has 3,166 cases and 97 fatalities, which is an increase of 937 cases since the last Board meeting.  He stated that the risk of exposure remains extremely high, and we all need to do a better job of changing our behaviors, taking the precautionary measures seriously and making them part of daily routine.  He indicated that none of the precautionary measures will prevent the spread or eliminate the risk for COVID-19, but if we implement them into our daily routine, we will drastically reduce the risk and slow spread.

Staying home if you feel sick:  He stated that we are seeing many cases in Navajo County and across Arizona where someone felt a little off in the morning but went to work, and this is not the time to risk that.  He encouraged all to be critical about how you feel and stay home if you feel off.

Physically distancing:  Make sure that you keep at least six feet between you and another person.  This virus spreads very easily in close groups and close contacts.

Wearing a mask:  If you cannot guarantee that you can control physical distancing, it is highly recommended that you wear a facial covering.  Mr. Lee stated that this will not eliminate the spread, but it is a good tool for us to use to help lower the risk.  My mask helps protect you, and your mask helps protect me.

Frequent hand washing:  Changing up our daily routine to include frequent hand washing not just after we use the restroom or before we eat, but multiple times throughout the day.

Disinfecting frequently touched items:  Don't forget about cell phones, pens, keyboards, car keys, etc.

Coughing or sneezing into a tissue or bent elbow and avoid touching your face.

If we all follow these precautionary measures, we can slow the spread of the virus.  Supervisor Thompson stated that he is hearing that the increase is because of more testing, but it's actually behaviors.  Jeff Lee stated that there are many factors to why we are seeing more cases, and one of those factors is increased testing, but a large portion is attributable to the behaviors.  Chairman Whiting expressed appreciation to Mr. Lee and his department for all that they do.  He indicated that several mayors have come out recently with statements trying to educate and encourage people strongly to change their behavior so we can slow the virus down.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion to return to regular session; motion seconded by Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Consideration of the Public Safety Personnel Retirement System Pension Funding Policy pursuant to A.R.S. §38-386.01 :  Glenn Kephart discussed the item.  He stated that the fund has been increasingly underfunded.  He indicated that this has affected Navajo County similarly to other jurisdictions in the state.  He stated that Arizona Counties is working cooperatively with the County Supervisor's Association, the current Public Safety Personnel Retirement System (PSPRS) Board Chairman, the system's new actuary and the PSPRS new administrator regarding future options to address this in a fiscally responsible way, so we begin reducing the unfunded liability over a period of time.  He indicated that the current item would reduce that liability over a 20-year period.  Supervisor Seymore inquired regarding what the cost would be over the 20 years, and Mr. Kephart stated that they are looking at several options, and one is to pay down the debt as we have the ability to do so or issue debt to fund it.  Supervisor Seymore made a motion to approve the Public Safety Personnel Retirement System Pension Funding Policy pursuant to A.R.S. §38-386.01; motion seconded by Supervisor Thompson .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

NORTHEASTERN ARIZONA INNOVATIVE WORKFORCE SOLUTIONS :

a.

Presentation - Northeastern Arizona Local Workforce Development Board Updates :  Stephanie Ray spoke about the local board roles, including strategizing, managing and building the workforce system.  She discussed the responsibilities of the chief elected official.  She gave an overview of the Northeastern Arizona Workforce System.  She spoke about Reveille Foundation, which was awarded the contract as the one stop operator.  She discussed the budget for program year 2020/Fiscal Year 2021.  Ms. Ray spoke about the goals for the 2020-2024 local plan:

Goal 1: Promote a strong economy by building Northeastern Arizona's capacity to attract, retain, and grow thriving businesses.

Goal 2: Create a local workforce system that is relevant to business customers through dynamic partner alignment and integration, and by building on collaborative initiatives in each of the region's population centers.

Goal 3: Prepare job seekers to defend against property by matching job seekers to job creators for a successful career that provides amply.

Ms. Ray spoke about the priority projects, including a workforce quality initiative, entrepreneur training and support, entrepreneurship ecosystem development and integrated technology system.  She discussed the proposed empowerment centers.  Supervisor Whitesinger expressed appreciation for the thoughtfulness that went into developing the plan.  Supervisor Seymore thanked Ms. Ray for the presentation.  He indicated that he would like to see us incorporate the needs of Broadband.   Supervisor Thompson indicated that there is a shortage of agricultural workers and asked if there are any plans for people to take up that trade?  Ms. Ray stated that as we gather data, if there is a need, they can place priority on it and put job seekers into the right training to meet the needs.   Chairman Whiting thanked Ms. Ray for the update and what we are doing to support local communities.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 21-2020 approving/denying a Special Use Permit for AJ's Getaway RV Park, for construction and operation of a recreational vehicle (RV) park, consisting of 43 RV-camp sites and 30 spaces for RV and boat storage on 14.83 acres of land, located at the northeast corner of Highway 277 and Buckskin Road :  Berrin Nejad discussed the item.  AJ and Michelle James, the applicants, addressed the Board.  Michelle James spoke about the proposed campground and being good neighbors.  She indicated that light pollution is important to them.  She spoke about their security detail and stated that they will not have full-time residents.  She indicated that they are working on security measures and emergency evacuation plans which will be completed before they open.  She stated that the campground is special needs friendly.  Will Flake, engineer for the applicant, indicated that he is available to answer any questions.   Chairman Whiting opened the public hearing; there being no comments, closed the public hearing.  Supervisor Seymore inquired of the applicant whether the building onsite is where the applicants will live or part of the campground, and the applicants indicated that that is where they will live.  Supervisor Seymore inquired regarding the wall for garbage, and Mr. James stated that it will be a 3-sided enclosure 30 to 40 feet off Buckskin Road.  He stated that the storage spaces are for parking storage only.  Supervisor Seymore inquired regarding the timing of the sewer project and whether there is work the applicants can do in the meantime, and Mr. Flake stated that assuming this special use permit gets approved, they will move forward with the improvement plans and design for development.  He indicated that because of the amount of sewer flows that this project will produce, the project will have to be submitted to ADEQ for their approval which will be part of that design.  He stated that the applicants' plan is to get a grading at risk permit and begin grading the project and starting on the fence.  He indicated that they cannot do improvements until the County approves the improvement plans.  Supervisor Seymore made a motion to adopt Resolution Number 21-2020 approving a Special Use Permit for AJ's Getaway RV Park for construction and operation of a recreational vehicle (RV) park, consisting of 43 RV-camp sites and 30 spaces for RV and boat storage on 14.83 acres of land, located at the northeast corner of Highway 277 and Buckskin Road; motion seconded by Supervisor Whitesinger .  Supervisor Whitesinger thanked the property owners for working with the community and being good neighbors.  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
