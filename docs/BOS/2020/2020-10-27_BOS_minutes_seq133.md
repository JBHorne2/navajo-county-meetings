REGULAR AGENDA:

1.

Consideration of a Declaration of Emergency due to extreme fire danger and to enact Section 7 of County Ordinance 01-19 Emergency Fire Restrictions, to enter into Stage 1 fire restrictions :  Catrina Jenkins advised that last week the White Mountain Fire Restriction working group met and due to the extreme danger and threat of wildfire, recommended going into Stage One Fire Restrictions for the unincorporated areas of Navajo County.  She further advised that the cities of Show Low and Pinetop have also opted to go into Stage One Fire Restrictions.  She indicated that Apache Sitgreaves is already in Stage One fire restrictions, as are many of the other forests throughout Northern Arizona.  She further indicated that Apache County is also going into Stage One fire restrictions.  She advised that the ERC's are high, that last week they were at 95 percent, that we have been fortunate to get some moisture, but that they don't know if that will mitigate the threat because temperatures are expected to go up and it will dry out again.  She recommended that Navajo County move into Stage One Fire Restrictions for the time being.
Supervisor Seymore indicated that he appreciates the report and that he feels like it is what they need to do given the situation.
Supervisor Whitesinger indicated that she appreciates our partners who spend a great deal of time ensuring that we are being proactive.  She thanked Ms. Jenkins. She stated that she knows that it is important to our communities.
Supervisor Seymore made a motion for a Declaration of Emergency due to extreme fire danger and to enact Section 7 of County Ordinance 01-19 Emergency Fire Restrictions, to enter into Stage 1 fire restrictions; motion seconded by Supervisor Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

2.

Update on COVID-19:   Jeff Lee advised that as of this morning there are 6307 cases and 238 deaths in Navajo County.  He indicated that over the past couple of weeks they have continued to see a rise in Navajo County and that the healthcare facilities are starting to see an increase for both flu and COVID.  He advised that they continue to work closely with the healthcare facility partners ensuring that they have the staffing, equipment and PPE that they need to get through the fall and winter seasons.  He indicated that overall in Navajo County we have done a good job with following the CDC recommendations and guidelines, that it has helped slow the spread but that we have let our guard down a little bit and that now we need to do better in a few specific areas.  He advised that one is staying home when unwell, that they have linked many cases back to the workplace; physically distancing; and wearing a face covering when you are unable to physically distance.  He indicated that they are finding that people are not following these guidelines as closely as they have in the past and that making these recommendations part of our daily routine will help slow down the spread of COVID in our community again.  He addressed the importance of contact tracing and indicated that they are experiencing more situations where they have contacts or confirmed positive cases who are not willing to share the information to help break that chain of infection.  He requested that when the Public Health Nurses call that people answer the questions to help break that chain.  He addressed the COVID vaccine and indicated that they are working closely with the state, federal and local partners and once the vaccine is released that they can get as many people vaccinated that want to be vaccinated.
Supervisor Seymore inquired as to the supply capacity at this time.
Mr. Lee indicated that there is a shortage of gloves, that they do have a small supply of gloves to help their partners out and that they are seeking new vendors to try to buy more.
Supervisor Seymore i nquired as to testing capacities, if they can increase or maintain the testing capabilities.
Mr. Lee indicated that there is plenty of testing ability throughout the county.  He further indicated that they are working with the healthcare, schools and EMS agencies on the rapid testing capacity.  He advised that if anyone wants to be tested in Navajo County there are plenty of places.
Supervisor Jack inquired as to the vaccine and how soon it would be available.
Mr. Lee indicated that the timing is unknown right now, that it is possible it could be available by the end of the year, but that it depends on the trials.
Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Lee and his team.

Supervisor Jack made a motion to return to regular session; motion seconded by Supervisor Seymore.  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
