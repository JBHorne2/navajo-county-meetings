CALL TO THE PUBLIC :  Terry White, 8323 Juniper Drive, Show Low, addressed the Board regarding a possible RFP/RFQ for a management company for White Mountain Lake County Recreation Improvement District. He stated that at a meeting with county representatives a month ago at White Mountain Lakes, there was no discussion or presentation of a management company being brought forward.  He indicated that he wants to know how this came about, how any costs have been applied and why it was never discussed with the community. Theresa Dwyer, 8315 Juniper Drive, Show Low, addressed the Board regarding the notice of an RFQ which was being published for the management of the lake.  She stated that she is not against it, but she does not think they have enough information prior to posting the RFQ.  She indicated that she thinks there is a missing link when it comes to information being shared with the taxpayers. She stated that she has not seen much information on the White Mountain Lake County Recreation Improvement District on the Board of Supervisors' agendas. She indicated that it is required that the Board have quarterly reports presented at a White Mountain Lake County Recreation Improvement District Board of Directors meeting per resolution, and those have not been done.  She stated that they have found in their review of the quarterly reports and the budget, the numbers are inconsistent from one report to the next.  Penny Stegeman, 8323 Juniper Drive, White Mountain Lake Estates, addressed the Board.  She indicated that she has not seen mention of an RFQ for the lake management in any quarterly report or BOS agenda or meetings.  She stated that what is happening now is unacceptable.  She indicated that the administrator has stated that there is a private resident who is negotiating on behalf of the community and the County to get quotes on repairs of the docks.  She stated that prior to hiring a management company, shouldn't we try to correct what is wrong?  She inquired whether a cost analysis has been done.  She spoke about the bad information that is floating out on Facebook and on a website called whitemountainlakeboard.com.  She requested that the website which the community has paid for be the site on which information is posted for property owners.  She spoke about the voluntary community association meeting last week at which committees were formed for beautification of Carr Circle, bike trails, redesign of the lake and a beach area and dock repairs.  She stated that this group represents less than one percent of the property owners with four members who will monetarily prosper from the RV development coming in.  She indicated that every property owner should have a say on whether a management company is brought in, especially if it means their taxes are going up.  Vice-Chairman Jack directed staff to address those concerns noted today. Supervisor Seymore recommended staff hold a Study Session or more discussion on this matter to be more thoroughly brought up to speed of all the issues regarding the White Mountain Lake County Recreation District.

ASSESSOR :

a.

Presentation on the Valuation Process :  Cammy Darris gave a presentation on the valuation process.  Ms. Darris spoke about the Assessor's Office team and the great work that they do.   She stated that property taxes pay for local government, schools, law enforcement, libraries, community colleges and special districts.  She indicated that the three factors to the property tax calculation are value/assessment, which is done by the Assessor's Office; legal class ratio, which is established by the legislature; and tax rate/levy amount, which is established by the taxing jurisdiction.  She spoke about the valuation of bare land, improvement value and market factors.  She further spoke about jurisdiction budgets, property classifications and ratios.  She stated that valuations are used to spread the tax responsibility over all taxable properties, not generate tax dollars.   She spoke about Prop 117.  She stated that they do have property tax exemptions and property freezes for widows, widowers, disability, churches, some non-profit organizations and seniors.   She spoke about the appeal process.  Supervisor Seymore inquired whether the budget they're working on now is fiscal year 2019, and Ms. Darris said the notices of values they just sent out are for 2021 which are based off the '19 sales.   Supervisor Thompson inquired whether a reduction in the percentage for commercial properties is going through the legislature, and Ms. Darris indicated that there is talk of a bill which includes a request to lower it.

BOARD OF SUPERVISORS :

a.

Presentation: Northland Pioneer College 101 :  Maderia Ellison, Vice-President for Administrative Services for Navajo County Community College District (NPC), addressed the Board.  She spoke about the financial resources which help carry out the mission of the college and make available the faculty and administrative costs.  She gave a brief summary of NPC.  She stated NPC serves Navajo County primarily but also provides services to Apache County.  She indicated that they are audited yearly by the Arizona Auditor General.  She stated the majority of the funding comes from local taxpayers.  She spoke about the state appropriations they receive.  She stated that they set the property tax at the maximum, so they can protect the equalization aid.  She spoke about tuition and indicated that NPC is at the lowest rate in the community college system in the State of Arizona.    She spoke about declining enrollment.  She stated that their largest expense is compensation and benefits.  She indicated that they have four campuses and five centers distributed over two counties.  She spoke about the expenditure limit and indicated that the formula is outdated and needs to be amended.  She stated that the community college districts are working together to try make some statute amendments.  She spoke about available exclusions in statute.  She further spoke about the fund balance and how they have accumulated and saved money over the years.  She indicated that as of June 2019, they have $87.7 million, $20 million of which is set aside for construction at the Show Low campus.   She provided copies of the comprehensive annual financial report.

Supervisor Seymore inquired whether they can get a permanent base adjustment from their budget from the State, and Ms. Ellison stated that they can as it relates to the expenditure limit, and that is what they are trying to do.  Supervisor Whitesinger inquired regarding the decline in enrollment.  Mark Vest, President of NPC, addressed the Board regarding enrollment. He indicated that last year, there was a 3.5% enrollment decline. He indicated that Navajo County is an aging county. He spoke about how when unemployment is down, community college enrollment declines, as well.  He indicated that we do have some population drain in Navajo County, as well. Supervisor Seymore thanked Mr. Vest and Ms. Ellison for the work that they do to help our workforce. Supervisor Thompson inquired as to NPC's proposed plans for loss of jobs in the coal industry, and Mr. Vest indicated that they have met with APS and offered their services in job retraining. He further spoke about NPC's power plant program which has been repurposed into energy and industrial based programs, which is known as the skill center. Mr. Vest answered questions regarding the Dine' College and the Navajo Nation.

PUBLIC WORKS :

a.

Consideration of temporary road closure to facilitate deck repairs on Woodruff Bridge on Woodruff Road :  John Osgood discussed the temporary road closure to facilitate deck repairs on the Woodruff Bridge on Woodruff Road. He indicated that the project was first bid in 2018; however, due to some technical concerns, we went back out to bid in 2019 and have worked with the contractor on scheduling issues due to weather and material availability.  He stated that at this time, the project is scheduled to start on March 9th, weather permitting, and the contractor and Navajo County's engineering team both agree that a total closure of the bridge and road is necessary to obtain the best repair result.  He indicated that the two-week closure includes contingencies and worst-case scenario, but they expect the work to take less time than that.  He stated that signage will be done prior to the project, and our maintenance teams will make sure that the detour and surrounding roads are maintained as well as possible throughout the project. Mr. Osgood answered questions of the Board regarding full closure of the bridge.  Supervisor Thompson made a motion to approve a temporary road closure to facilitate deck repairs on Woodruff Bridge on Woodruff Road; motion seconded by Supervisor Seymore .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

b.

Consideration of award of Contract #B20-01-006 Supplying and Delivering Liquid Asphalt Products to Hawker & Evan Asphalt Co., Inc., Cactus Transport, Inc., and Western Emulsions, Inc. :  John Osgood discussed the award of the contract for supply and delivery of liquid asphalt products.  He indicated that this is an annual contract.  He stated that awarding to all three vendors gives us the flexibility to utilize a second contractor should the lowest bidder not be available to provide products we need.  He stated that the source of the funding for this item is the Highway User Revenue Fund dedicated solely for transportation-related work.  Supervisor Seymore inquired whether the contractors are willing to do partial work, and Mr. Osgood stated they are.  Supervisor Whitesinger made a motion to approve Contract #B20-01-006 Supplying and Delivering Liquid Asphalt Products to Hawker & Evan Asphalt Co., Inc., Cactus Transport, Inc., and Western Emulsions, Inc.; motion seconded by Supervisor Thompson .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
