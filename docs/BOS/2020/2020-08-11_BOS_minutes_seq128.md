2.

Update on COVID-19  : Jeff Lee stated that over the past couple of weeks they have seen a significant decline in cases across Navajo County and across Arizona.  He indicated that as of this morning there are 5391 cases and 202 deaths in Navajo County.  Mr. Lee indicated that continuing to follow the mitigation measures and personal precautionary measures is key in keeping the numbers trending in the right direction.  Mr. Lee stated that over the past six months they have been working with the schools and businesses.  Last week ADHS addressed the benchmarks for the schools to re-open and yesterday they announced benchmarks for the higher risk businesses.  Mr. Lee shared a presentation that addressed the benchmarks.  He indicated that the benchmarks are based on community spread and that the CDC defines community spread in three areas: minimal, moderate and substantial.  He stated that ADHS, all fifteen Health Directors, the State and many of the community partners have had input to identify the measures and the metrics that would best serve each community but also have universal metrics to follow across Arizona.  Mr. Lee indicated that they are looking for a two-week period of declining cases or cases below 100 per 100,000, that percent positivity they are looking for a mark of below 7%, which is 7% of the tests taken that came back positive.  He stated that COVID like illness is a BioSense Syndromic Surveillance Platform that hospitals report when people report to the ER, that it is a survey-based system, that the system is regionalized and that our region includes Apache, Coconino, Yavapai and Navajo County.  He indicated that the ADHS is recommending that for in person learning in schools that there be a two week decline in the number of cases or a two week period with new case rates of 100 per 100,000 and a two week period with less than 7% positivity.  He stated that for COVID like illness surveillance they are looking for two weeks of hospital visits for COVID like illness below 10%.  He stated that for Navajo County benchmarks, we are meeting the decline in two weeks of cases and meeting the two weeks of COVID like illness surveillance and that we are getting close, but we are still above the 7% range.  He indicated that looking at the timeline in Navajo County we have never been below the 7% but that for us to get back to where we are at now at 9.9 % is a huge positive and we are trending in the right direction.  He further indicated that for high risk businesses it is the same criteria, except for one difference on the cases for 100,000 rather than two weeks of declining cases they are looking for two weeks of less than 100 cases per 100,000.  He stated that the benchmarks must be meet for 14 days in a 12-day reporting period.  He indicated that for the high risk businesses such as gyms, movie theaters, water parks, tubing, bars and night clubs, during substantial spread all high risk businesses are recommended to be closed.  He stated when it gets to moderate things can start to re-open in a phased approach and once we get to minimal it will allow even more capacity for those entities to open up.  Mr. Lee stated that in Navajo County right now for these businesses we are in substantial spread in two of the categories.  We have not been below 100 cases per 100,000 for two consecutive weeks, we are right at the 10% mark for percent positivity and that we are in the moderate range for the COVID like illness surveillance.  He indicated that the numbers are trending in the right direction, that the actions and sacrifices we are taking to physical distance, stay home as much as possible and staying home when sick are all paying off and is going to allow more business to open and we will be able to phase back into what the new normal may look like.  Mr. Lee indicated that we have been working very closely with the school district superintendents and that they anticipate that by the end of the week they will hear from the districts about what their plans are for school re-openings.  Mr. Lee indicated he would answer any questions the board may have.  Supervisor Seymore indicated that he appreciates Mr. Lee sharing the information and stated that whatever they can do to get the information out on how close we are to re-opening our schools and some of the other businesses would be very helpful.  He thanked Mr. Lee and his team for all they have been doing.  Supervisor Jack requested that Mr. Lee email him the information presented today and indicated that he knows the COVID updates are very important.  Mr. Lee indicated that he would send the information.  Chairman Whiting requested Mr. Lee relay to team his appreciation and indicated they have been working hard.  He indicated that he was able to watch a portion of the live interview with some of the and that they spoke about some of their experiences and what they are going through. He further indicated that he has noticed a great level of communication.  Chairman Whiting stated that he has heard from different superintendents and other people that Mr. Lee continues to partner with in the communities and that they also have appreciated the updates and the information that is helping them make decisions at their level.

BOARD OF SUPERVISORS:

a.

Navajo County Fair Update:  Michael Sample, Fair Board President addressed the Board regarding the Navajo County Fair being cancelled.  He indicated that it was a hard decision to make, that there was much debate within the board meetings, but that they came to an agreement that it would be best for the safety of the public.  Mr. Sample advised that most of the other fairs have cancelled.  He indicated that our carnival had cancelled back in July and some of the other vendors had cancelled as well.  Mr. Sample stated that they have decided to move forward with the Jr. Livestock show, Horse Achievement Show and the Small Stock Show.  He advised that they decided to move forward with those events to give the kids an opportunity to complete their projects for this year.   Mr. Sample addressed the schedule for the shows that will still be held and indicated they are encouraging the public to come out and support these groups. He indicated that many of the kids have dedicated their lives growing up in 4H and FFA and for some of the kids this will be their last year to participate in these programs.  Mr. Sample indicated that the fair board is working with the Health Department, is coordinating with the City of Holbrook and they are also contacting other fairs throughout the state to see what they have done to make their Jr. Livestock Shows a success.  Mr. Sample advised that they have started a Buyers Club for the Jr. Livestock and addressed how the Buyers Club works.  He indicated that they appreciate all the support they have received from Navajo County.  Mr. Sample addressed the precautions that will be taken into consideration.  Supervisor Whitesinger thanked Mr. Sample and the Fair Board.  She indicated that she knows that all the kids work hard to raise their livestock and how this is an important time for them.  She stated that she appreciates the work that has been done to make it a successful year for them.   Supervisor Thompson thanked Mr. Sample and stated that he knows that the decision was not easy to make.  He indicated that everyone is very concerned about the safety of children as well as parents and all of the participants. He inquired if there are any participants from the Navajo Nation due to the lock down and curfew that has been extended.  Mr. Sample advised that there is a group from the Kayenta area.  He indicated that they are taking into consideration the curfew and that is the reason for the modified schedule.  Supervisor Thompson thanked Mr. Sample for the information.  Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Sample, the Board and everyone involved for their consideration and looking for ways to be able to partner with the youth in making sure that they are still successful.  He indicated that he appreciates the efforts and the amount of time and energy spent to find the best way to move forward.

TREASURER:

a.

Consideration and approval of the following items: Receipt of the Annual Report of Collections and Delinquencies in accordance with A.R.S. §42-18002; Settlement of Accounts; and Request for Authorization to Invest Funds Pursuant to A.R.S. §35-327(G):  Kari Lopez addressed the Board regarding consideration and approval of the Receipt of the Annual Report of Collections and Delinquencies; Settlement of Accounts and Request for Authorization to Invest Funds.  Mrs. Lopez shared a presentation.  Ms. Lopez indicated that as of June 30 the property tax collected is a little over 86 million which leaves around 2.6 million in uncollected taxes, after sending out delinquency notices.  She further indicated that they are still collecting some of the property taxes for 2019. Mrs. Lopez advised that the second part of the report is regarding county investments.  She indicated that she will utilize the same objectives moving forward when making investment decisions and stated that safety is her priority, then she looks at liquidity to make sure there is cash on hand and finally she looks for yields.  Mrs. Lopez indicated that as of June 30 the county has over 238 million invested in short term investments and government securities.  She further indicated that the interest received was a little over 4.7 million.  She advised that this has increased a little over 600,000 from the previous fiscal year.  Mrs. Lopez advised that the government securities did a little better this year, that we had a little higher yield on the government securities so the interest received was a little higher.  She addressed how the savings in the money market mutual fund started off really good towards the beginning of the year but since the economy has changed significantly in March, April, May and June those months were a little bit of a decline in the yield as to what we were receiving.  She indicated that we are still making more in our savings with the money market account than we had made when we had a regular savings account.  Mrs. Lopez advised that the final part of report is on the treasurers processing fund and according to ARS 11-495(C) the County Treasurer shall annually submit to the Board of Supervisors the amount of anticipated revenue under this section. She indicated that the anticipated fund revenue for fiscal year 2020-21 is estimated to be $10,000.00 based on the estimated sale of tax lien certificates of one million.  She advised in the past couple of years they have always made a little over a million with the certificates.  She indicated that in 2020 they sold 1193 which is less than the previous year but the certificates are higher so the total sold is 1.77 million in 2020.  Mrs. Lopez advised that she is requesting the Board to acknowledge and approve the annual treasurer's report and that she is also requesting authorization to invest and re-invest county, school and special districts funds as necessary. Supervisor Seymore made a motion to approve the treasurers report as presented and to allow the Treasurer to invest and re-invest the funds pursuant to A.R.S. §35-327(G); motion seconded by Supervisor Thompson .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Consideration of approval of modification No. 2 to Contract No. C012217 between the Navajo Nation and Navajo County related to road maintenance and improvements on tribal roads:   John Osgood discussed the item.  He indicated that the $350,000 grant that they are seeking modification on can be used to improve the economic, educational and social conditions of families living in the Bird Springs, Dilcon, Indian Wells, Jeddito, Teesto and Whitecone Chapters.  Mr. Osgood indicated that the reason for the contract modification is that the scope of work in the original MOA between the County and the Navajo Nation envisioned the purchase, delivery and placement of asphalt millings to build or improve bus turnouts and small segments of roadway throughout the six Chapters.  He further indicated that last year's contract modification extended the agreement from November 2019 to November 2023.  He stated that this modification is a slight change in scope from the original scope, and they are hoping that after hearing from several chapters about specific improvement needs as they relate to improving school bus routes and emergency services from remote areas and having the ability to utilize some of the funds purchase road maintenance equipment, which strengthens the chapters ability to respond to emergency road repairs as necessary.  Mr. Osgood addressed the partnerships that came together for funding to purchase a used motor grader from a cooperative contract that the County has with the City of Tucson.  Mr. Osgood indicated that the Navajo Hopi Land Commission unanimously approved a Resolution approving the County's request to re-allocate $58,103.00 of grant funding to purchase the grader for the Dilcon chapter.  He indicated that there is no county general funds or HURF funds that will be used to purchase the grader or road maintenance material.  Mr. Osgood addressed the three projects completed to date through the grant and indicated that over the next year they will be working with the chapters to address priorities.  Mr. Osgood stated that staff would recommend approval of the item. Supervisor Thompson asked for support on this item.  He indicated that it has been a really good partnership.  They have completed several projects,  primarily focuses on public roads particularly for bus turn arounds and that it not only benefits the Bureau of Indian Affairs the Navajo Nation Educational Programs but also benefits school districts.  He further indicated that it will be primarily purchased for the Dilcon community.  Supervisor Thompson made a motion to approve the modification of No. 2 Contract No. C012217 between the Navajo Nation and Navajo County related to road maintenance and improvements on tribal roads, motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.  Supervisor Thompson thanked all those involved in this project.
