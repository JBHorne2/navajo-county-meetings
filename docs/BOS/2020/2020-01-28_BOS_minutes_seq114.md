PUBLIC SAFETY :

a.

Presentation on the Navajo County Criminal Justice Behavioral Health Treatment/Diversion Program Proposal : Sheriff David Clouse spoke about a proposal for a behavioral health treatment center for all aspects of the criminal justice system.  He stated that there has been a rise in mental health issues in public safety, and, currently, 20 percent of those incarcerated have a mental health issue or concern which led to their incarceration.  He indicated that mental health crisis and intervention is becoming a part of academy training.  He spoke about changes in law enforcement where they're starting to develop some trainings in crisis intervention and mental health first aid, but there is a need for more resources and services.  Brad Carlyon, County Attorney, spoke about increasing case loads and decreasing attorneys. He stated that a way to reduce those caseloads is through adequate treatment for defendants. He stated that 85 percent of the cases are either mental health related or substance abuse related, and we do not have adequate treatment in Navajo County.  He further stated that our probation department does not have adequate funding to be able to give the necessary treatment to these individuals. He indicated that when a defendant receives treatment through ChangePoint or AHCCCS, their substance abuse issues are addressed, but not the criminology issues that they have.  He spoke about having more of a diversion program.  He stated that we have tried through our limited resources to have programs such as Drug Court and Veterans Court; however, those programs are limited to a few people. He indicated that Probation does not deal with misdemeanor defendants, so those folks come back through our felony system.  He stated that we need to develop a robust behavioral health system for Navajo County. Jason Cash, Chief Probation Officer, spoke about the genuine desire of some adult and juvenile probationers to improve their lives; however, most were unsuccessful due to the limited resources. He spoke about the proposed program and the interventions needed. He stated that the Probation Department's two main goals are to protect the community and rehabilitate the offender.  He spoke about the risk-needs-responsivity principle (RNR) and indicated that Navajo County Probation Department does not have those interventions.  He stated that the Probation Department has taken steps towards interventions; however, they only serve about 10% of the probationers. He indicated we need to increase our access to behavioral professionals and programming that targets those high risk offenders.  He stated that currently in Navajo County, there are about 500 high risk probationers and if we can properly apply the RNR principle, we can expect a 30-percent reduction in recidivism.

Mr. Carlyon indicated that we have a void in behavioral health, and we need to provide Navajo County's own behavioral health team. He stated that we can provide services at no cost to Navajo County for over 1200 people a year. He spoke about the deflection level, diversion program, services at the probation level, specialty courts and starting our own treatment centers which we have the facilities to do.  He stated that we can partner with NPC, WOIA and outside entities to assist with resumes and how to interview which saves lives and families and reduces the cost to the criminal justice system. He indicated that they want to get a behavioral health specialist to help develop a business model. Sheriff Clouse stated that they are looking for local options for treatment centers so when a deputy comes across a situation they will have another option besides incarceration, leaving. He spoke about the numbers in the jail and believes about 20% of those housed need mental health services. He spoke about waiting for beds to open elsewhere which then causes delays in the criminal justice system. He spoke about reaching individuals with treatment to reduce recidivism. Jason Cash spoke about the probationers not having the access to the programs that they need and sometimes they have to wait months to be sent out of county to residential treatment.  He stated that they would like to have behavioral health counselors in the probation offices working with probation officers on a custom treatment plan for each high risk probationer.  He indicated that we have about 750 cases being supervised in Navajo County, 500 of which are high risk. He stated that he believes this program would be successful and a big savings to Navajo County.  He indicated that with the diversion program Mr. Carlyon spoke about, he believes the 250 low risk individuals would never end up on probation as they are self-correctors.  Mr. Carlyon spoke about restoration to competency being done in-house with this program, which would save over $200,000.00 a year. He spoke about a presentation to Representative Blackman, who indicated he was going to try to get funding for this program. He stated that they want to go out for an RFP to develop the business plan and will ask at a future meeting for the authority to do it.  He indicated that he believes in their budgets that they  have the budget to pay for it.  He stated that the Administrative Office of Courts is excited, because they know this plan can be replicated.  He asked for the Board of Supervisors' support. He indicated that if we can qualify Native Americans for AHCCCS, AHCCCS will bill IHS so services can be provided to everybody in the county.

Supervisor Seymore thanked the team for their efforts and indicated that they have his support. Supervisor Whitesinger stated that she appreciates their innovation in thinking about how to better serve our communities.  She indicated that we at the county would support, as well as our families. She spoke about the education system and the consortium which was established for behavioral health services. She indicated that this program has her support. Supervisor Jack indicated that this program is needed and further spoke about a veteran he knows who has utilized a similar program and is doing well. He indicated his support. Chairman Whiting thanked the team for the innovation and for reaching out to Representative Blackman. Chairman Whiting stated that helooks forward to them coming back to the Board for action.

commercial-retail space, and open space on APN 207-04-006A and 207-04-007B located west of Heber in Township 12 North, Range 16 East, Section 15 :  Sandra Phillips discussed the item.  She stated that this project has Forest Service Road 504 that goes through it.  She indicated that if the master site plan is approved, then there will be a zone change which will be before the Board.  She stated that the applicant held a public meeting at Heber-Overgaard Fire Department which was well attended.  She indicated that this will be 450 homes on 309 acres, 4 acres of commercial and 1.9 acre multi-family.  She stated that before the Board today is a density and a concept.  She stated that the applicant will do buffers to the forest and provide a water system and waste water treatment plant.  She indicated that there has been a preliminary traffic study done.  She stated that one concern was the speed, and they've been working with Arizona Department of Transportation on the speeds.  She spoke about the concern about the impact on public safety and school and stated that if this develops within the next 20 years, additional revenue would give fire, Sheriff and schools additional funds.  She indicated that the applicant mentioned if they hit any ruins or remains, they would close off the area and notify the state.  She spoke about the Light Ordinance which is in place in Navajo County.  She stated that another concern was the use of the park and indicated that this would be a private park on private land.  She indicated that development would be in the next phases.  Supervisor Seymore inquired whether the manufactured homes would have their own area, and the applicant indicated that they are considering manufactured homes due to the labor shortage.  He spoke about the quality now of manufactured homes.  He stated that their intent is to have specific subdivisions that have certain sized lots or have an identity and would not allow manufactured homes in the lower density subdivisions.  Supervisor Seymore inquired regarding the placement of the water and the sewer, and the applicant stated that water is at the high end and wastewater is on the lower end, and their intent is to start on the east end and expand west.  Supervisor Whitesinger inquired about the feasibility of people investing and building property, and the applicant stated that they are estimating that this is a 13-year build-out with 30-35 homes per year.  He indicated they designed this so they could address the housing needs of more than those looking for a second home.  Supervisor Jack inquired regarding the labor shortage, and the applicant stated that there is a shortage of labor in the trades needed to build homes, and he sees this as an opportunity to bring more employment to the area.

Chairman Whiting opened the public hearing.  Jerry Call, 1980 Little Doe, Overgaard, spoke in favor of the project.  He stated that these are quality developers and believes they've done a great job in determining the demand.  Paul Bastedo, 995 Rock on Dam Tank Road, Chevelon, spoke in favor of the project and stated that the community needs this project to grow.  He indicated that there is not enough work in the community.  Roger Owens, 1670 Black Canyon Rd, Heber, spoke in favor of the project.  He indicated that any students who move in the area would be greatly appreciated.  He stated that there is no doubt their fire department can handle the increasing calls that come in from this.   Chairman Whiting closed the public hearing.  The applicant stated that the home building industry is in the process of moving into modular building, and the intent is that they have specifically said they do not want mobile homes there.  Ms. Phillips stated that this is recommended for approval by the Planning and Zoning Commission.  Supervisor Seymore made a motion to adopt Resolution Number 07-2020 approving request for approval of Master Site Plan for Wendover Pines, for up to 450 residential units, a commercial-retail space, and open space on APN 207-04-006A and 207-04-007B located west of Heber in Township 12 North, Range 16 East, Section 15; motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 09-2020 approving/denying request for approval of Site Plan Amendment for Rocky Rim Resort Cabins, for up to 50 residential units and open space on APN 206-01-033 and 206-01-034 located north of State Route 260 in Overgaard in Township 12 North, Range 17 East, Section 34 :  This item was taken up before the other Public Works items at 10:32 a.m.  Sandra Phillips discussed the item.  She indicated that this is a park model cabin project which is located on 10.61 acres of vacant land and is adjacent to Mogollon Airport to the north and west, a small 38-cabin rental properties on 10 acres to the east, and state Route 260 and Bison Ranch to the south. She stated that the applicant received approval from the fire chief on the layout and has worked with Navapache and has provided submitted ADEQ papers.  Ms. Phillips indicated that all emails received have been placed in the Board's packet. She spoke about some of the concerns, including loss of property value, water and sewer, property taxes, ATV traffic, and RV's. She indicated that the applicant initially wanted to do RV's in the interim as he developed his site and has since withdrawn that request. The applicant, Brett Ferguson, spoke about his plan and transition from an RV park to the park model project. He spoke about the rules and regulations regarding landscaping, cleanliness, noise, parking, etc.  He spoke about the splash pads.  He stated that he believes that this would be a benefit to the community and has plans to work with the local restaurants for promotions to encourage people to get out and spend their money in the community. He indicated that he emailed the surrounding HOA's and has attended one of the HOA Board's meeting. He indicated that he has addressed some issues and is willing to work with the neighbors on the rules and regulations. He stated that he has incorporated the issue of drones into the rules. With regard to the concerns about ATV's, he indicated that there will only be two vehicles allowed, and they cannot park on the street. He addressed concerns regarding the possibility of Airbnb, including a peer review system. He spoke about incorporating a quiet time consistent with Navajo County.  He further spoke about the concerns of squatters, and he indicated that this will be very strictly controlled. He spoke about the mandated specifications of the cabins.
Supervisor Seymore asked about all 50 units being sold or applicant owned, and the applicant indicated that the units would be sold individually in a build-out phase with seven models to choose from.  Supervisor Seymore inquired whether there will be some CC&R's, and the applicant stated that he has them.  Supervisor Seymore asked if this was going to be platted correctly where they can sell them individually and retain ownership, and Ms. Phillips stated that the application will own the land, and individuals can buy the unit on top of that.

Chairman Whiting opened the public hearing indicating that all who have indicated they want to speak will have 2 minutes, or a few designated spokespersons will be given 5 minutes.  Roger Owens, 1670 Black Canyon Rd, Heber, spoke in favor of the development. He indicated that he believes in property rights.  He spoke about increased revenue.  Jeff Davis, 2342 Lazy Eight Circle, Overgaard, spoke in opposition of this development indicating concerns about the impact in the area.  He stated that there is no enforcement criteria in the rules and regulations.  He expressed concern about noise, drone activity, change in the area because of the density of this project.  Brandon Arnow, 1800 Toadstool Road, Heber, spoke in favor of the development. He stated that he is grateful that this is a well-thought-out project and will be very attractive, and the tax revenue will benefit our schools. He indicated that he has an Airbnb and has been impressed with the people who have come to rent out his cabin.  Todd Nuttall, 2274 Spitfire Circle, Overgaard, spoke in favor of the project. He indicated that he looks forward to this project and indicated that he has seen diminishment in the area. He spoke about the millennial market who come to the area and what they are looking for. He spoke about the splash pads. He spoke about the opportunity of someone turning these into an income property.  He stated that we need our property values to go up again and believes this will do it. Jerry Call, 1980 Little Doe, in Overgaard, spoke in support of this project. He stated that he and his wife have been active members in the community. He spoke about the positive impact this project will bring into the community and the taxes that will bring. He indicated that he believes that the concerns have been addressed. Earl Leet, 2750 Columbia Lane, stated that he is not extremely happy about the project but is not against it. He spoke about his property that is adjacent to the project. He indicated that he knew that there would be something on that property, but not 50 cabins. Tim Scruggs, 2308 Kitfox Circle, Mogollon Air Park, spoke against the project. He stated that he and his wife moved out there for peace, quiet and clean air, and this project is not going to be quiet. He indicated that ATV's and airplanes do not mix. He stated that this density is absurd and asked who is going to enforce all those rules. He indicated that there will be dogs, ATV and noise.  He stated that this is not going to turn out well, and the Sheriff's Office will see an influx. Shane Siren, Lott 333 in Mogollon Estates, stated that he has not built on his lot. He stated that believes the proposed development is of major concern, and he is opposed to it. He mentioned that he believes this project will have a negative physical and monetary impact on present and future development of Mogollon Air Park and Mogollon Estates. He stated that Mogollon Air Park and Mogollon Estates have provided substantial property taxes for Navajo county. He spoke about the splash pad being a potentially dangerous traffic hazard. John Chackerian, 2641 Lodge Loop, Overgaard, asked the applicant if there was a replacement program being in place for these units. He stated that he also has questions regarding the rules and regulations and who will enforce those rules. He asked if they will allow residents to park their vehicles at their site even when they are not there; about structures and how the applicant would monitor the use of the splash pad; and if Mr. Ferguson sells the property, will the rules still apply. Bob Lloyd, 2827 Chandelle Lane, Overgaard, spoke in opposition indicating that traffic is going to be horrific. He stated that he has concern about the appearance and unsustainability of the density. He indicated that the construction is a big concern. He stated that this is essentially a trailer park at the entrances to one of the most beautiful areas. He asked about who will enforce the CC&R's and over what period of time and how is it going to be maintained as it degrades.  Ken Floberg, 2801 Air Park Drive, Overgaard, spoke against the project. He spoke against Airbnb's, which he believes are transient rentals. Ben Mayer, 2341 Quarter Horse Trail, stated he lives across the street at Bison Ranch and indicated that that his concerns have to do with the lighting, dumpsters and public safety.  Chairman Whiting closed the public hearing.

Sandra Phillips addressed some of the concerns as follows:

This was never intended as a single-family property

The traffic study did not require additional turn lanes

RV storage is not part of the proposal

The splash pad will be fenced and secured

It is not part of Planning and Zoning's purview that there will be an on-site manager

Airbnb is a state statute that limits what we can do as a County to limit people

Concerns about lighting are addressed in the County's light ordinance

Sufficient trash collection will be part of what Planning and Zoning will go through when they look at the improvement plans for the development of the site

They have met with the fire and sheriff, and the additional tax revenue will help with the budget of those services, and there has not been a request by either of them for additional satellite offices.
