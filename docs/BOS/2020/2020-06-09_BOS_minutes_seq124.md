REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

Update on COVID-19:  Update on COVID-19 :  Jeff Lee stated that as of this morning, there are 2,229 cases in Navajo County and 28,296 in the State of Arizona.  He indicated that over the past week, we have seen a significant increase in daily case counts.  He spoke about the hospital bed capacity throughout Arizona and, based on the trend, that may become an issue.  He stated that the number of hospital beds and ICU beds in use for COVID-19 have increased over the past couple of weeks and the trend for hospital COVID-like illness surveillance is going back up.  He stated that the tools we have is to go back to the precautionary measures:  physical distancing, staying home if you feel sick, avoid touching your face, wash your hands with soap and water, covering your cough and sneeze, disinfect frequently touched items and surfaces and wearing a face mask when in public.  Supervisor Thompson asked whether the increase in testing has created the upward trend, and Mr. Lee stated that more testing will result in increased case count; however, the trend can also be attributed to our behaviors in public.  He stated that if we do a better job following  the precautionary measures, we'll still see increases, but not as large.  Supervisor Thompson asked how well are we prepared for the fall and winter, and Mr. Lee stated that we do have capacity, and we are continuously planning and working with our partners to build up personal protective equipment capacity and staffing so that we can be as prepared as we can.   Supervisor Seymore expressed appreciation to the Health Department for all that they do.  Chairman Whiting asked about whether the data represents Navajo County facilities or facilities statewide, and Mr. Lee indicated that the day shows facilities statewide, but they can look specifically at healthcare facilities in Navajo County, and the trend is identical to the statewide trend.   Chairman Whiting asked if all facilities throughout Navajo County are seeing an increase, and Mr. Lee stated that the increase in case counts is all across Navajo County.   Supervisor Whitesinger stated that she and Supervisor Seymore were in discussion about the rising number of cases in the White Mountain Apache Tribe and have organized a COVID-19 Relief drive to provide support to those families who test positive to have resources they need, so they don't have to leave their home.   She indicated that within the past 8 days, they have lost 6 people in their community to COVID-19, and the rate of increase is at 5.1%.  Supervisor Seymore  stated thatthey will be collecting donations at Show Low City Hall and Blue Ridge High School on Thursday from 8 a.m. to 11 a.m.  He indicated that they are also working with Pinetop-Lakeside.  Supervisor Thompson stated that no matter where the community is, every community has people who have been impacted.  He thanked Supervisor Seymore and Supervisor Whitesinger for their efforts.  Chairman Whiting thanked Jeff Lee for keeping us informed and for all those in the Health Department for their efforts, including contract tracing.  He expressed pride regarding the level of communication being provided to the public by Navajo County.

At 9:50 a.m., Supervisor Jack was absent.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE: Report from County Manager, County Attorney and Board members :   Supervisor Seymore stated that it's great to be back and thanked Navajo County and its citizens.  He indicated that the National Association for the Advancement of Colored People and the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints met 2 years ago and started a process on how they were going to deal with racial indifferences, and they recently met again.  Supervisor Seymore  read a portion of their statement:  "We agree with Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr.'s statement, that 'hate cannot drive out hate.  Only love can do that.'  We call on people everywhere to join us in this journey of understanding and overcoming.  America is great because she is good.  It is paramount that we discover the goodness in each and every human heart.  In this spirit, there are things every one of us can do.  We invite all to pray to God that the people of this land will heed the Divine call  to abandon attitudes of prejudice against any group of God's children.  We also invite people of goodwill everywhere to look for ways to reach out to serve someone of a different background or race.  Everyone can do something.  Prejudice, hate and discrimination are learned.  Thus, we call on parents, family members, and teachers to be the first line of defense.  Teaching children to love all, and find the good in others, is more crucial than ever.  Oneness is not sameness in America.  We must all learn to value the differences.  We likewise call on government, businesses, and educational leaders at every level to review processes, laws, and organizational attitudes regarding racism and root them out once and for all.   It is past time for every one of us to elevate our conversations above divisive and polarizing rhetoric.  Treating others with respect matters.  Treating each other as sons and daughters of God matters.  We likewise remind everyone that we must renounce illegal acts such as looting, destruction, and defacement of public or private property.  Never has one wrong been corrected by a second wrong.  Evil has never been resolved by more evil.  Arm in arm and shoulder to shoulder, may we strive to lift our brothers and sisters everywhere, in every way we can.  We first linked arms as friends and have now locked arms in love and brotherhood.  The people of America can do the same." He spoke about trusting in each other to find the good that we have.  He expressed his hope and prayer that we can find the good in each other, choose not to judge and return good from bad.  Supervisor Whitesinger thanked Supervisor Seymore for those words.  She spoke about the partners involved in the COVID-19 relief drive:  City of Show Low, Town of Pinetop-Lakeside, The Community Fast and the Fort Apache Heritage Foundation.  She further spoke about the need for healing in our local communities and our nation.  She stated that she understands the plight that we are facing and can see contentions which exist in our own community and how important it is for each of us to reflect on our own behavior regarding our biases and how we can change those biases.  She indicated that it is each of our responsibility to love one another and serve our neighbors.  She expressed gratitude for serving alongside her colleagues and those serving within Navajo County.  Supervisor Thompson thanked each member of the Board of Supervisors for staying united in addressing the COVID-19 issue and doing as much as we can to reach those who need support.  He spoke about reflecting during the Pledge of Allegiance and what it means.  He indicated that the curfew on the Navajo Nation still continues.  Chairman Whiting spoke about the great leaders in Navajo County.  He thanked the County team for their efforts every day and how they are looking for ways to operate safely and continue to provide services.   He thanked our communities for doing a great job and how we can do better as it relates to COVID-19.  He spoke about the unity among the Board.  He shared a quote:  "Unitedly we declare that the answers to racism, prejudice, discrimination and hate will not come from government or law enforcement alone.  Solutions will come as we open our hearts to those whose lives are different form our own, as we work to build bonds of genuine friendship, and as we see other as the brothers and sisters we are – for we are all children of a loving God."  He indicated that we can be better and encouraged all to open their hearts and serve.
