existence of Federal funds.  For example, when State funds are appropriated for a stated purpose 
and Federal funds are awarded for that same purpose, the State replaces its State funds with 
Federal funds, thereby reducing the total amount available for the stated purpose.”
The following example is provided in the Federal Financial Guide to help clarify the difference 
between supplementing and supplanting: 
State funds are appropriated to hire 50 new police officers, and Federal funds are 
awarded for hiring 60 new police officers. At the end of the year, the State has 
hired 60 new police officers, and the Fe deral funds have be en exhausted. The 
State has not used its funds towards hiring new officers, but instead reduced its 
appropriation for that purpose and assigned or appropriated the funds to another 
purpose. In this case, the State has supp lanted its appropriation with the Federal 
funds. If supplanting had not occurred, 110 new officers would have been hired 
using Federal funds for 60 officers and State funds for 50 officers.

certify its understanding of and adherence to the prohibition against supplanting of State

Meeting Date: 10/27/2020  
Title: Declaration of Emergency due to extreme fire danger to enact Stage 1 Fire
Restrictions
Submitted By: Catrina Jenkins, EMP Director
Department: NC Public Health Services District Division: Emergency Management
Presented By: Catrina Jenkins
Motion before the Board:
Consideration of a Declaration of Emergency due to extreme fire danger and to enact Section 7 of
County Ordinance 01-19 Emergency Fire Restrictions, to enter into Stage 1 fire restrictions. 
Background:
The White Mountain Fire Coordinating Group met Monday October 19th to discuss the current
conditions and the possible need to enter into fire restrictions. The WMFCG is comprised of
Apache and Navajo County Emergency Management, Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest,
Arizona State Department of Forestry and Fire Management, Bureau of Land Management - Ft.
Apache Agency, Northeast Arizona Fire Chiefs Association and Northeast Arizona Police
Association.  The primary goal of the White Mountain Fire Coordinating Group is to create a
unified approach to fire restrictions as well as information and education.
As you may know, Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest is currently in Stage 1 restrictions as are
several of the national forests in Arizona.
 
 Traditionally, the various agencies try to enter into restrictions together in order to maintain
unified messaging and avoid confusion for the public. This year has been different in many ways
including the conditions of our forest.  This year we are seeing a drought that has impacted the
forests throughout the entire state with significant affects in many areas. Tonto National Forest
has had multiple fires with evacuations and lost property this year. Other areas states are seeing
devastating fires such as California, Washington, Oregon and Colorado with loss of life and
property.
 
 After heavy discussion of unfavorable weather forecasts, firefighting resource availability, and
current forest conditions (including the high energy release components values), it was voted
unanimously to enter Stage 1 fire restrictions.  According to the forest service, there are no
severity resources available, these are funds above and beyond what is budgeted for regular
funding levels. Additionally, manpower is reduced as they are deployed to other fires throughout
the country.  The current state of our land is extremely dry, as if we are in mid to late June with
relative humidity values in single digits during the days and little to no recovery values at night.
The weather forecast shows increased winds this weekend and only nominal chances of
precipitation but not until Monday. If sparks were to ignite a fire in an unincorporated area of our
county, the likelihood of our resources being able to catch it are slim to none.
