PUBLIC HEARING: FINAL 2020-21 BUDGET PURSUANT TO §42-17104:
Convene a Special Meeting of the Board of Supervisors for final budget adoption.
Consideration and possible adoption of Resolution Number 24-2020 , approving the proposed final budget for fiscal year 2020-21 for Navajo County.   Supervisor Seymore made a motion to convene a special meeting of the Board of Supervisors; motion seconded by Supervisor Thompson .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Bryan Layton addressed the Board regarding the budget.  Mr. Layton indicated that the Board approved the preliminary budget at the last meeting and that there are no changes.  Mr. Layton gave highlights of the budget.  The budget this year  focuses on very strong financial reserves, under the Boards direction the last several years the focus was keeping operational costs low and building financial reserves, so combined this year with the Cares Act Funding and the voter approved jail district this put the County on some positive financial footing as we face an uncertain economy moving forward.  Mr. Layton stated that as a county we have leaned out our operations the last couple of years and the focus was to maintain service deliveries.  We need the ability to weather a potential protracted loss in sales tax.  We need to have the infrastructure ready to respond to the continuing emergency that we are facing as well as any other emergencies.  Mr. Layton advised that we have the fourth lowest property tax rate in Arizona.  We are one of only four counties in Arizona that did not raise their property tax rate this year.  Mr. Layton indicated that staff recommends approval of the budget.
Chairman Whiting opened the public hearing, there being no comments, closed the public hearing.
Supervisor Seymore thanked staff for the wonderful job in preparing the budget and working through this during these economic times.  He indicated he appreciates that we are one of four counties that did not increase taxes.  He also thanked the departments.  Supervisor Whitesinger expressed gratitude for those who have worked on the budget not just today but building up to help present this type of budget over several years.  She indicated that we have had many challenges concerning the budget and have had to meet mandates expressed to us from the State.  Supervisor Whitesinger indicated we have worked to ensure the entities are able to provide support to our communities and to those who come to the county in need of support.  She further expressed that she is grateful to be able to work with such a wonderful team and all of those who have invested time. Supervisor Thompson indicated that he is very thankful for the collaboration and all those who contributed to the development of the budget.  Supervisor Thompson thanked Mr. Layton and his office.  He further thanked the citizens who have contributed towards economic growth.  Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Kephart, Mr. Layton, the team and Paige Peterson. Chairman Whiting further thanked Supervisor Jack for being able to meet with the different offices and departments and their leadership for being able to work together to come up with the budget.  He thanked Mr. Layton for the presentation.   Supervisor Jack extended his appreciation to Mr. Layton and everyone who worked on the budget. He indicated that he feels it is positive and is looking forward to working with everyone on the budget in the upcoming year.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion to adopt Resolution Number 24-2020 , approving the proposed final budget for fiscal year 2020-21 for Navajo County; motion seconded by Supervisor Thompson .  Vote unanimous approving the motion

REGULAR AGENDA:

1.

Update on COVID-19 : Jeff Lee thanked our partners from Summit Healthcare and Little Colorado Medical Center for joining him.  Mr. Lee advised that the risk of COVID-19 remains extremely high for Navajo County, currently we have 4877 cases and 162 fatalities.  He stated that over the past couple of days our numbers have flattened out and are looking to trend downward, but that there is a significant backlog on pending tests, so they anticipate the daily numbers will jump back up as those tests are processed.  Mr. Lee indicated the goal is slowing the spread and avoiding risk.  Staying home when possible, physically distancing, wearing face coverings when in public, washing hands, disinfecting surfaces and coughing or sneezing into a tissue or bent elbow.  Mr. Lee highlighted that staying home if you feel ill is key.  Mr. Lee turned the time over to our partners, Jack Dempsey and Christine Ross from Little Colorado Medical Center and Ron McArthur from Summit to give an update on their facilities.
Ms. Ross shared a presentation with the Board. She addressed Little Colorado Medical Center's procedures.  She indicated LCMC has been hit from the beginning of the crisis.  They have set up some procedures from the beginning that are helping them now.  They currently have two types of testing and three different ways to do it. Ms. Ross addressed in house testing for COVID which has been very beneficial but that they have a limited amount of supplies for that testing.  She indicated they are proud to be a partner with U of A, who is doing some antibody testing throughout the state. Ms. Ross indicated that LCMC has sent out 1400 tests and 15 % have been positive which is pretty consistent for Navajo County.  Ms. Ross addressed the numbers of patients and the COVID room set up.  Ms. Ross addressed numbers for their different facilities.  She indicated that they have seen that the average hospitalization is much longer.  She said they meet with community partners twice weekly to go through state and local stats, go over any new orders and any issues going on and how to work together.
Ron McArthur indicated that Summit didn't see the surge early.  In March/April/May average number of COVID patients was 1-2 and ER was 5.  They have seen a huge surge since June, and it continues to go up on a regular basis.  On average they have 30-40 in-patients daily for COVID or COVID rule out.  Summit has had to increase beds due to the increase.  Mr. McArthur stated that the biggest difficulty is staffing, they are having to get additional staff. Mr. Lee thanked Ms. Ross and Mr. McArthur for being present and indicated that he appreciates their partnerships.
Chairman Whiting thanked everyone the for presentations.  Supervisor Thompson inquired about the antibody testing.  He stated that working together brings good results.  Ms. Ross indicated that she will send the U of A testing information.  Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Lee, Ms. Ross and Mr. McArthur for their presentations.  He indicated that it is important to hear from our local providers in our health care system so our citizens have awareness as to what's actually happening, not at a national level, but it's important to understand what is happening at the local level to be able to make the best decisions moving forward.

Supervisor Seymore made a motion to return to regular session; motion seconded by Supervisor Thompson .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
