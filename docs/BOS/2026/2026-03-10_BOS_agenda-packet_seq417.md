CALL TO THE PUBLIC: 
Donna Shurwin addressed the Board, stating that she is an Arizona native and
has been a resident of Navajo County for 31 years.  She explained that she was
speaking on behalf of herself and many citizens who are seeking transparency
and proper stewardship of taxpayer resources.  Ms. Shurwin requested that the
Board place an item on a future meeting agenda to formally end all Navajo County
government participation in Real AZ Economic Development Group, Inc., and any
of its other  iterations.  This request included discontinuing the use of Navajo
County resources, staff time, branding, and accounts for promotional purposes and
confirming the organization’s dissolution and cessation of operations once
completed.  Ms. Shurwin stated that public records reflect Real AZ voluntarily
dissolved its Arizona Corporation Commission entity (No. 2370947) on October
17, 2024.  She cited Arizona Revised Statue 10-11405 and Real AZ’s bylaws,
noting that post-dissolution activities are limited to “winding up” affairs and do not
permit conducting new business or receiving new revenue.  However, she said
that Navajo County ledgers show Real AZ transactions continuing into 2025,
including the receipt of membership funds from developers such as Blue Earth
Renewables. Ms. Shurwin further stated that taxpayer funds have supported Real
AZ for years through $5000 annual membership dues, including what she
described as Navajo County’s pre-payment of fiscal year 2025/2026 dues on July
31, 2025, approximately 8 months after the reported dissolution. Ms. Shurwin
addressed Supervisor Whiting directly, requesting that, given his role as
President of Real AZ, per Arizona Corporation Commission filings, he recuses
himself from her proposed agenda item and from any discussion or vote on
projects developed by Real AZ from today forward in order to avoid the
appearance of a conflict of interest, per Arizona Revised Statue 38-503.  She
closed by reiterating that her statement was made on behalf of herself and others,
and she urged the Board to take the requested actions regarding Real AZ to
restore public trust.  
Chairman Seymore invited staff to investigate the matter if they see fit.

INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY:
a. Consideration and approval of new policies for Information Technology
for use in making decisions and for use in
State-sponsored audits. 
Tom Franklin greeted the Board and began his presentation. He
explained that the Information Technology policies are not only
considered best practices, but also ensure the county meets the state
audit requirements and demonstrates a strong commitment to
compliance and security. He noted that policies must evolve as
technology advances and new risks develop, adding that the last
Board-approved policy update occurred in June 2018.  Mr. Franklin
stated that an intentional and comprehensive review was conducted by
the Information Technology Security team in partnership with an
industry-recognized consultant.  As part of the process, policies were
consolidated and modernized to reflect current standards and practices.
He explained that the streamlined suite of 16 policies strengthens the
county’s overall security posture and reaffirms its commitment to
safeguarding public resources. He clarified that policies establish what
must be done, while procedures outline how those requirements are
implemented through clear, actionable steps that ensure consistency and
repeatability. Together, policies and procedures form a complete
framework; policies define the standards, and procedures make those
standards practical and auditable in day-to-day operations. Because
technology and cyber threats evolve continuously, he emphasized that
procedures must remain adaptable. Mr. Franklin further stated that
Navajo County relies heavily on technology to deliver essential services
such as public safety, health, finance, and community programs. These
systems store sensitive information that must be protected to maintain
public trust. He explained that the updated security policy suite serves as
the foundation for safeguarding those systems and data. He concluded
his presentation by outlining how the proposed policies work together to
protect county systems, information, and the citizens they serve, and
stated that staff recommend approval of the revised policy suite.
Supervisor Whiting recognized that technology consistently changes
and evolves and expressed appreciation to the Information Technology
team for protecting Navajo County.
Supervisor Peshlakai expressed support and appreciation for the
updated policies, stating that they appear to be well-thought-out and that
he is in support of the updated policy suite.
Chairman Seymore thanked the Information Technology team for
keeping Navajo County safe.
Vice Chairwoman Whitesinger made a motion to approve the new
policies for Information Technology that will replace the changes made in
June 2018, motion seconded by Supervisor Benally.  Vote unanimous
approving the motion.

d. Consideration of approval of an Intergovernmental Agreement with the
State of Arizona, Department of Transportation (ADOT) for the Shumway
Road @ Silver Creek Bridge Project.
A presentation was shown. Madhav Mundle greeted the Board and shared
a brief background and timeline for the project. Mr. Mundle explained that
the Silver Creek Bridge at Shumway Road was originally constructed in the
1900s and that it is classified as a minor collector according to the federal
roadway classification. It provides access between the unincorporated area
of Shumway and State Route 77.  He added that the bridge is in FEMA
Flood Zone AE.  He said that the bridge was selected for the project
because the guarders show signs of stress and deterioration, is only load
rated for 25 tons, and is a scour critical bridge with a low rating.  He
explained that, from Navajo County’s long-term transportation planning
perspective, replacement of the bridge is better than rehabilitation.  Mr.
Mundle stated that funding on the project will utilize a federal formula grant
coming through the state and that the total estimated cost of the project is

WHEREAS, Holbrook, established in 1882 with the construction of the Atlantic & Pacific 
Railway, served as a vital railhead for supplies headed to Fort Apache and other locations. Named

WHEREAS, the National Register of Historic Places is the official list of the Nation's historic 
places worthy of preservation. The County Courthouse was listed on July 31, 1978. Authorized by 
the National Historic Preservation Act of 1966, the National Park Service's National Register of 
Historic Places is part of a national program to coordinate and support public and private efforts 
to identify, evaluate, and protect America's historic and archeological resources; and

museum on June 13, 1983. With the continued support of Navajo County, the City of Holbrook, and 
countless private donations, the museum has become a unique experience for all who visit. The 
museum also provides office space for the Holbrook Chamber of Commerce and serves as an 
official Arizona Visitor’s Information Center; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Navajo County Historical Society Museum, housed in the historic courthouse, has 
been a work in progress since 1983. Spanning over 10,000 square feet, the museum is filled with an 
array of collections and artifacts, each piece donated by local families and history enthusiasts. 
Volunteers have carefully arranged exhibits that span from Prehistoric times and the Anasazi era to 
Native American, Pioneer, and Route 66 settings, utilizing every available space within the 
courthouse’s storied walls; and

and several additions constructed at later dates. The building is a two-story, brick, and wood-framed 
structure. The exterior multi-wythe brick walls have substantial cracks on all four sides of the 
structure. Based on a preliminary assessment, the current condition of the building requires 
designing adequate repairs and conducting a more detailed investigation to address structural 
concerns; and

for railroad official Edward F. Winslow. In 1972, the Eagles’ hit single “Take It Easy” featured
the lyric “standing on a corner in Winslow, Arizona,” placing our small town on the national
map. While our downtown proudly preserves its historic character, our community continues to
look toward the future—particularly in strengthening access to essential healthcare services.
LCMC is a 501(c)(3) not-for-profit Critical Access Hospital providing primary inpatient and
outpatient services to more than 32,000 residents across rural northeastern Arizona. LCMC
borders the Hopi Reservation and the Navajo Nation and works closely with the Winslow Indian
Health Care Center to ensure coordinated, quality care throughout the region.
LCMC delivers compassionate, patient-centered care while offering a challenging, professional,
and supportive work environment for its staff. The hospital currently provides comprehensive
services including inpatient and outpatient care, emergency services, a multi-specialty clinic,
laboratory and imaging services, pharmacy, general surgery, obstetrics, medical-surgical care,
and cardiology.
Beyond clinical services, LCMC staff are deeply engaged in the community—hosting the annual
cancer walk, supporting local food drives, participating in the Rotary Club, and organizing
educational events to help residents better understand their insurance benefits. The hospital
plays a vital role in delivering lifesaving healthcare services not only to Winslow, but to
surrounding rural communities as well.
The requested funding will allow LCMC to expand its radiology services through the acquisition
of a permanent MRI machine. Currently, patients must travel more than an hour to obtain MRI

(OIVP), administers funding from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) for the Overdose 
Data to Action (OD2A) Cooperative Agreement. The primary objective of the Overdose Data to Action in 
States (OD2A-S) initiative is to  enhance ADHS’s capacity to track and prevent both nonfatal and fatal 
overdoses while also identifying emerging drug threats. 
1.2. OD2A-S focuses on implementing surveillance strategies and promoting evidence- based interventions 
designed to immediately reduce overdose morbidity and mortality. The initiative targets opioids, stimulants, 
and polysubstance use when these substances are used in combination. Central to OD2A -S is a data-to-
action framework that emphasizes the utilization of surveillance and other data to inform prevention efforts 
and policy decisions, with a strong commitment to addressing health equity and disparities. 
1.3. ADHS is dedicated to supporting County health departments in the execution of data- driven prevention 
programs. As part of the OD2A -S priorities, ADHS provides necessary support to participating counties, 
which shall engage in activities aligned with the following prevention strategies: 
● Strategy Seven (7): Public Safety Partnerships/Interventions 
 
● Strategy Eight (8): Harm Reduction 
 
● Strategy Nine (9): Community-Based Linkages to Care (LtC) 
 
1.4. The abuse and addiction to opioids represents a significant and complex public health challenge at both 
national and State levels. Over the past two (2) decades, drug overdose deaths have escalated, becoming 
the leading cause of injury-related death in the United States. According to CDC data, there were 92,452 
overdose deaths reported in 2020, a thirty percent (30%) increase from 71,130 in 2019. Of these deaths, 
opioids were implicated in 69,031 cases, accounting for seventy -five percent (75%) of all drug overdose 
fatalities. 
 
1.5. Historically, the opioid epidemic was largely driven by prescription medications. Data from Arizona’s 
Controlled Substances Prescription Monitoring Program (CSPMP) indicates that in 2019, there were 
approximately 4.1 million Class II-IV prescriptions written and over 240 million pills dispensed in the State—
equating to around thirty -four (34) controlled substance pills for every Arizona resident. However, r ecent 
statistics reveal that Arizona ranks twenty-eighth (28
th) in opioid prescribing, with 44.1 prescriptions per 100 
people, indicating that prescription practices are no longer the primary cause of overdose deaths. 
 
1.6. Currently, the predominant factor in the opioid crisis is the emergence of fentanyl. In 2019, synthetic opioids 
were responsible for more than 36,000 deaths in the U.S., representing seventy-three percent (73%) of all 
opioid-related fatalities that year. The majority of these deaths were linked to illicitly manufactured fentanyl, 
commonly found in counterfeit pills or mixed with other drugs, such as heroin. In Arizona, the presence of 
fentanyl in overdose cases rose dramatically from nine percent (9%) in 2017 to fifty percent (50%) in 2021. 
 
1.7. Beyond the tragic human toll, the financial implications of opioid misuse are substantial. In 2019 alone, 
there were 56,623 hospital visits in Arizona related to opioid use, with an average cost of $11,942.00 per 
visit, resulting in an estimated $676 million in healthcare costs associated with opioids. 
 
1.8. The escalating addiction rates and overdose deaths, driven by both prescription and illicit opioids, 
underscore a growing national crisis. Overdose fatalities now rank as the leading cause of preventable 
injury deaths in the United States, necessitating urgent and effective interventions through initiatives like 
OD2A-S.

2. OBJECTIVE 
 
2.1. The objective of this Agreement aims to address the escalating opioid crisis in Arizona by enhancing the 
capacity of County health departments to implement effective prevention strategies, improve access to 
overdose prevention tools and treatment options, and strengthen community linkages for individuals with 
Opioid Use Disorder (OUD). The expected outcomes include reduced opioid misuse, increased retention 
in care, and improved overall community health and safety. 
 
3. SCOPE OF SERVICE 
 
3.1 The purpose of this Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA), led by the Navajo County Public Health 
Services District (NCPHSD), is to strengthen public safety partnerships, expand harm reduction 
access, and build community-based linkages to care to prevent opioid overdose and improve health 
outcomes. 
 
3.2 To enhance collaboration with public safety, NCPHSD will maintain law enforcement representation 
on the Navajo County Overdose Fatality Review (OFR) team and implement a new protocol 
encouraging public safety agencies to conduct and submit case reviews prior to Registered Nurse 
(RN) led reviews. This process will help strengthen multi -sector involvement and ensure more 
comprehensive understanding of overdose cases. Additionally, NCPHSD will train public safety 
agencies on naloxone use and expand participation in Naloxone Leave Behind programs. 
 
3.3 In support of harm reduction, NCPHSD will increase naloxone access by creating at least two (2) 
sub-HUBs for community -based distribution and conducting naloxone trainings for at -risk 
populations and community members. Naloxone distribution and usage will be tracked in 
coordination with the Arizona Department of Health Services (ADHS). Media and educati onal 
materials will support broader community awareness about overdose prevention, safer drug use, 
and available resources. 
 
3.4 To further community -based linkages to care, NCPHSD will onboard a certified peer support 
specialist or case manager to provide navigation support for individuals impacted by substance 
use. This position will serve as a connection point between individuals and treatment, behavioral 
health, housing, and support services. NCPHSD will also work closely with correctional facilities, 
schools, and businesses to expand community engagement and connect more individuals to care. 
 
3.5 Through these activities, NCPHSD aims to reduce overdose deaths, expand equitable access to 
harm reduction services, and strengthen systems of care across Navajo County.

Motion before the Board:
Approval of a rental agreement between Randox Laboratories-US Limited and Navajo County Public

Background:
Navajo County Medical Examiner currently relies on external and state laboratory toxicology testing,
which carries no direct fee at the state lab but often takes up to 28 days and requires strict sample
handling, limited pickups, and significant staff coordination, delaying cause and manner determinations,
case closure, and family notification. To address these delays and reduce reliance on costly autopsies
where timely toxicology would be sufficient, staff propose renting an in house toxicology analyzer on a
monthly basis, with a fixed rental charge, per test cartridge fees, and a one time shipping and setup cost.
The analyzer would provide same day results within minutes, allowing earlier decisions on jurisdiction,
the need for autopsy, and release of remains, which improves investigative confidence, shortens death
certification timelines, and enhances service to families. Financially, the proposal is structured so that
avoiding a modest number of autopsies each year—well within the number of cases staff believe could
reasonably be handled with toxicology screening—would offset the analyzer’s annual cost, while also
reducing dependency on external lab logistics and potentially creating a platform to bill partner agencies
for testing.
Fiscal Impact
Fiscal Year: 26/27

Fiscal Impact:
The fiscal impact of renting an in house toxicology analyzer is expected to be cost neutral or modestly
positive, based on current spending and realistic autopsy avoidance. Year one costs total about $24,190
(rental, cartridges, and shipping/setup), with ongoing annual costs of roughly $21,690 thereafter. Because
each autopsy costs about $3,500 or more, avoiding approximately seven autopsies in the first year—and
six to seven in subsequent years—would fully offset the analyzer’s annual expense, a threshold staff
believe is achievable given an estimated 10 autopsies in 2025 that likely could have been avoided with
timely toxicology results.

Project Overview 
The Woodruff community in Navajo County (County) experiences frequent nuisance flooding, mostly along 
1st Street and County Road, due to the lack of drainage infrastructure. Currently, runoff flows along roadway 
alignments in undersized roadside ditches or  adjacent properties, and ponds at intersections. Runoff 
generally flows south to north through the community. These conditions create impacts to resident vehicular 
ingress/egress and parking. The County would like to develop construction documents based o n the 
drainage evaluation completed by Kimley -Horn in March 2025. This feasibility study developed drainage 
project alternatives with phasing and cost analyses. The County would like to develop construction 
documents for the selected alternative, and for b oth phases per the finalized evaluation  memo dated 
3/12/25.  
Project Understanding and Approach 
Plans, special provisions, and an engineer’s estimate of probably cost will be developed for the Phase 1 
and 2 improvements identified in the drainage evaluation. The final design effort will include field survey to 
support design, utility locating services, and utility coordination. Services not included in this scope of work 
include public involvement, geotechnical evaluations, and FEMA map revisions for work in the Little 
Colorado River floodplain. 
Scope of Services 
Kimley-Horn (KH) will provide the services specifically set forth below. 
Task 9 Driveway Design Services 
Kimley-Horn will complete designs for up to seventeen (17) driveways in the Woodruff Community. Profiles 
will be prepared for each driveway and included in the final plan set. 
 
Kimley-Horn will prepare one (1) driveway profile sheet showing the centerline profile for each driveway. 
 
Driveway construction notes and callouts will be presented on the drainage plan and profile sheets. 
 
Services Not Included 
Any other services, including but not limited to the following, are not included in this Agreement:   
• Potholing 
• New hydrology calculations that were not part of the drainage evaluation previously completed 
• Design of utility relocations 
• Public meetings attendance 
• Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) map revisions 
• Construction administration 
• As-built survey 
• Any other services not specifically set forth in the above scope of services 
 
Additional Services

performed at our then current hourly rates.
