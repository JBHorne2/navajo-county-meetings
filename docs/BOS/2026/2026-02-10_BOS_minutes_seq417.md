CALL TO THE PUBLIC :
Donna Shurwin addressed the Board, stating that she is an Arizona native and has been a resident of Navajo County for 31 years.  She explained that she was speaking on behalf of herself and many citizens who are seeking transparency and proper stewardship of taxpayer resources.  Ms. Shurwin requested that the Board place an item on a future meeting agenda to formally end all Navajo County government participation in Real AZ Economic Development Group, Inc., and any of its other  iterations.  This request included discontinuing the use of Navajo County resources, staff time, branding, and accounts for promotional purposes and confirming the organization's dissolution and cessation of operations once completed.  Ms. Shurwin stated that public records reflect Real AZ voluntarily dissolved its Arizona Corporation Commission entity (No. 2370947) on October 17, 2024.  She cited Arizona Revised Statue 10-11405 and Real AZ's bylaws, noting that post-dissolution activities are limited to "winding up" affairs and do not permit conducting new business or receiving new revenue.  However, she said that Navajo County ledgers show Real AZ transactions continuing into 2025, including the receipt of membership funds from developers such as Blue Earth Renewables. Ms. Shurwin further stated that taxpayer funds have supported Real AZ for years through $5000 annual membership dues, including what she described as Navajo County's pre-payment of fiscal year 2025/2026 dues on July 31, 2025, approximately 8 months after the reported dissolution. Ms. Shurwin addressed Supervisor Whiting directly, requesting that, given his role as President of Real AZ, per Arizona Corporation Commission filings, he recuses himself from her proposed agenda item and from any discussion or vote on projects developed by Real AZ from today forward in order to avoid the appearance of a conflict of interest, per Arizona Revised Statue 38-503.  She closed by reiterating that her statement was made on behalf of herself and others, and she urged the Board to take the requested actions regarding Real AZ to restore public trust.
Chairman Seymore invited staff to investigate the matter if they see fit.

Presentation on the Giving Machine Initiative.
Supervisor Whiting introduced Rikki Johnson, expressing his gratitude for the dedicated efforts she and her husband, Cory, put forth year-round to organize the Giving Machine.
Rikki Johnson provided information regarding the Giving Machine program. She explained that the Giving Machine was established in 2017 by the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints to provide an opportunity for people to give in a Christ-like manner during the holiday season.  Ms. Johnson described the Giving Machine as a vending machine-style platform that offers items benefitting local, statewide, and international charities, which individuals can purchase as donations.  She emphasized that no administrative fees are deducted and that 100 percent of donations go directly to participating charities.  She provided a brief history of the program, noting that the first Giving Machine was launched in Salt Lake City in 2017 and raised approximately $500,000. The initiative expanded to 61 machines by 2023, at which time Snowflake first hosted the Giving Machine.  By 2025, she stated, the program had grown to 126 cities across more than 21 countries and had raised over $50 million by the end of the 2024 season.   Ms. Johnson also noted that the Giving Machine is inclusive, featuring charities of various faiths and backgrounds. She shared that during the 2025 season, a Holbrook-based charity was included.  She invited members of the Board to recommend a Winslow or other community-based charity for inclusion in the upcoming season. Ms. Johnson concluded by thanking Navajo County for its support of the Giving Machine, specifically acknowledging Bryan Layton and Rochelle Lacapa for their assistance in raising awareness about the program.

Additionally, Representative Blackman shared that he is reviewing two pieces of legislation introduced by Representative Tsosie concerning the Navajo Nation to ensure they align with the intent of the law. INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY:

a.

Consideration and approval of new policies for Information Technology for use in making decisions and for use in
State-sponsored audits .
Tom Franklin greeted the Board and began his presentation. He explained that the Information Technology policies are not only considered best practices, but also ensure the county meets the state audit requirements and demonstrates a strong commitment to compliance and security. He noted that policies must evolve as technology advances and new risks develop, adding that the last Board-approved policy update occurred in June 2018.  Mr. Franklin stated that an intentional and comprehensive review was conducted by the Information Technology Security team in partnership with an industry-recognized consultant.  As part of the process, policies were consolidated and modernized to reflect current standards and practices. He explained that the streamlined suite of 16 policies strengthens the county's overall security posture and reaffirms its commitment to safeguarding public resources. He clarified that policies establish what must be done, while procedures outline how those requirements are implemented through clear, actionable steps that ensure consistency and repeatability. Together, policies and procedures form a complete framework; policies define the standards, and procedures make those standards practical and auditable in day-to-day operations. Because technology and cyber threats evolve continuously, he emphasized that procedures must remain adaptable. Mr. Franklin further stated that Navajo County relies heavily on technology to deliver essential services such as public safety, health, finance, and community programs. These systems store sensitive information that must be protected to maintain public trust. He explained that the updated security policy suite serves as the foundation for safeguarding those systems and data. He concluded his presentation by outlining how the proposed policies work together to protect county systems, information, and the citizens they serve, and stated that staff recommend approval of the revised policy suite.
Supervisor Whiting recognized that technology consistently changes and evolves and expressed appreciation to the Information Technology team for protecting Navajo County.
Supervisor Peshlakai expressed support and appreciation for the updated policies, stating that they appear to be well-thought-out and that he is in support of the updated policy suite.
Chairman Seymore thanked the Information Technology team for keeping Navajo County safe.
Vice Chairwoman Whitesinger made a motion  to approve the new policies for Information Technology that will replace the changes made in June 2018, motion seconded by  Supervisor Benally .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

d.

Consideration of approval of an Intergovernmental Agreement with the State of Arizona, Department of Transportation (ADOT) for the Shumway Road @ Silver Creek Bridge Project.
A presentation was shown. Madhav Mundle greeted the Board and shared a brief background and timeline for the project. Mr. Mundle explained that the Silver Creek Bridge at Shumway Road was originally constructed in the 1900s and that it is classified as a minor collector according to the federal roadway classification. It provides access between the unincorporated area of Shumway and State Route 77.  He added that the bridge is in FEMA Flood Zone AE.  He said that the bridge was selected for the project because the guarders show signs of stress and deterioration, is only load rated for 25 tons, and is a scour critical bridge with a low rating.  He explained that, from Navajo County's long-term transportation planning perspective, replacement of the bridge is better than rehabilitation.  Mr. Mundle stated that funding on the project will utilize a federal formula grant coming through the state and that the total estimated cost of the project is $6,165.000 with $6,131,085 being provided by Federal grant funds. He explained that the new bridge will be over 130 feet long and will be designed to withstand a 25-year storm event with one foot of overboard, allowing for the passage of water.
He added that ADOT will administer the project and that through the on-call process they have selected Stanley Consultant and that the county will be engaged with ADOT throughout the project.  Mr. Mundle added that along with the bridge replacement, there will be a realignment of a portion of the road approaching the bridge, a FEMA map revision, and possible utility relocation.  He closed by saying that construction will be during fiscal year 2029 and that staff recommends approval.
Supervisor Whiting said that the project is in District 3, and that he had an opportunity to go to the Shumway area to view the proposed project and that the residents in the community would be happy about the improvements.  He thanked the team for their time and efforts put into the project.
Supervisor Whiting made a motion  to approve an Intergovernmental Agreement with the State of Arizona, Department of Transportation (ADOT) for the Shumway Road at Silver Creek Bridge Project as presented, motion seconded by  Vice Chairwoman Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
