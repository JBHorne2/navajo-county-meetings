CALL TO THE PUBLIC :
Chairman Seymore greeted the Board and explained that the Board received a letter from Kelly Meixler with comments pertaining to the meeting and that each Board member had received a copy.
Celia Laughlin stated that Secretary Fontes' letter on May 23, 2024, states that ARS 16-602 requires only a limited post-election hand count audit, not full manual tabulation and that ARS 16-621C provides a clear exception that hand tabulation is permitted when electronic tabulation becomes impractical. Ms. Laughlin claimed that that threshold had been reached in Navajo County.  She claimed that 117.4% of voter registration rates in Excel logs have been hidden from public view, refusal of the citizen funded forensic audit offered in spring of 2024, unverifiable logic and accuracy tests, observers witnessing printers operating feet from central computers with no modem checks, denied machine inspections, broken seals, unlocked ballot handoffs, observer exclusions, and unfulfilled public records requests are systemic failures that render the ESNS machines impractical for secure tabulation.  Ms. Laughlin requested that the Board declare electronic tabulation impractical for 2026 and to direct the immediate planning of precinct level hand counts or hand counts at every voting center. Ms. Laughlin informed the Board that her group is collecting signatures for a 2026 citizen initiative to make precinct hand counts the law of Arizona.
Donna Wastchak-Shurwin greeted the Board and stated that she has lived in Navajo County for 31 years and has been involved in the Election Integrity Network Group for 2 years.  Ms. Shurwin asked the Board why Navajo County does not publicly advertise the need for poll workers, stating that she feels that it's an important opportunity that every Navajo County citizen should have made available to them.  Ms. Shurwin recognized that there were a lot of young people in the audience and stressed the importance of them being able to participate in elections. Ms. Shurwin stated that in 2024 she offered to help the County find poll workers when Christen Haddox told her that she is challenged at each election to find poll workers.  She explained that her efforts have been shunned and undermined because most people she had referred, were ignored.  Ms. Shurwin expressed disappointment in Navajo County at how they spent grant money from the Center for Tech and Civic Life to build a new Elections Warehouse without installing quality video cameras for monitoring elections, and instead, installed low-resolution fisheye cameras without audio recording equipment.
Steve Carvajal greeted the Board stating that he is from Show Low. He said he wanted to talk to the Board about wind farms and special use permits.  He stated his opinion that the County does not have sufficient paperwork requirements in the permitting process.  Mr. Carvajal said that 3 entities work together, the developer, the constructor, and the operating company.  Mr. Carvajal claimed that the operating company, the one who will be paying the taxes, has not been brought to the table yet.  Mr. Carvajal shared his belief that there are 15-17 of these operations scheduled and that he does not believe that 17 multi-billion-dollar companies will come in to buy the operations.  He shared his opinion that the permitting process with planning and development is inadequate because it does not require the operating company to be part of the process.  He claimed that if an operator does not purchase the development projects, then there will be unused monstrosities ruining the landscape.  Mr. Carvajal implored the Board to change the requirements for permits to include an operating company and a 10-year minimum bond.
Belinda Quog greeted the Board and stated that as the 2026 election cycle approaches, she would like to remind the Board of the County's perceived negligence in the 2024 election cycle.  Ms. Quog expressed her belief that the 2024 primary should not have been legally certified as the margins of error and lack of due diligence were shameful. She urged the Board to put forth stronger efforts to successfully perform a lawfully processed election for 2026.  Ms. Quog shared what she perceived as errors with the previous elections, such as; recorded broken chain of custody events, ballot box camera malfunctions, zip drives and vote information left in kiosk machines delivered to precincts, antiquated logic and accuracy tests, ballot and voting observers made to sit on opposite side of the rooms and unable to observe, incorrect number of party representatives at precincts, precincts with zero voters, hundreds of voters turned away, election workers confirming that provisional votes aren't counted, boxes of open ballots, deputies picking up precinct reports that were insufficiently filled or not filled out, using uncertified machines, refusal of a highly qualified professional to audit the machines, Rayleen Richards, Christen Haddox, and a minor adjudicating ballots on a Sunday with no witnesses, unethically asking poll workers for their affiliation and selectively ousting mostly republican people, and refusal of qualified individuals to work on the reservation.  Ms. Quog asked the Board to do their due diligence for 2026 and make sure that none of the issues she brought up occur.
Chairman Seymore asked staff to look into the items that had been brought up in the call to the public, specifically the items on development agreements, elections, and poll workers.

Economic Development and REAL AZ Update
Bryan Layton stated that over the last few board meetings, there have been some questions about Real AZ about how it functions and how it works.  Mr. Layton then introduced Economic Development Director Chris Pasterz, who he stated would present on Real AZ and how it benefits the Northern Arizona region.
A presentation was shown.
Chris Pasterz greeted the Board and introduced himself, stating that he is employed as Navajo County's Economic Development Director and that one of his duties is serving as Executive Director for Real AZ. Mr. Pasterz stated that his presentation would answer some questions that have been recently brought up about Real AZ.  He started the presentation by saying that the Navajo County area is open for business and looking for growth.  Mr. Pasterz shared that Real AZ is an informal regional economic development organization led by community leaders committed to marketing, promoting, and strengthening the business environment across Navajo and Apache Counties to support long-term economic growth. He explained that over the last 15 years, Real AZ has had some different marketing skins, including Real AZ Corridor.  Mr. Pasterz explained that 15 years ago the regional economic development group was started so that it could promote the region to developers outside of Apache and Navajo Counties and provide technical assistance to businesses locating or expanding into the region. Additionally, they wanted to provide industry attractions, site readiness support, and partner networking support.  Mr. Pasterz referenced the recent updates to the website and the promotional videos, saying that industry leaders are impressed and that they haven't seen a region that is as cohesive and can answer their questions.  He then explained that approximately 3 years ago, when the Cholla power plant was planning to stop burning coal and the Navajo County Board of Supervisor was allocated American Rescue Plan Act dollars, they invested in the County's infrastructure and into the broadband middle mile.  They also invested approximately $400,000 into hiring an Economic Development Director for the County to help drive growth and to help lead Real AZ, adding that at the same time, APS invested $600,000 into the region's economic growth.  Mr. Pasterz referenced the Building Resilient Economies in Coal Communities (BRECC) program where Navajo, Apache, and Coconino Counties had been collaborating to retool the economies.  He explained that he became employed with Navajo County in September 2024 and in October 2024 it was decided to stay highly organized and to welcome all members into Real AZ willing to invest their time, talent, and treasure into achieving their mission through an informal economic development group, explaining that Real AZ functions as a program under Navajo County Economic Development with no statutory authority. He explained that the group started moving towards the BRECC areas that he'd mentioned previously and shared some marketing flyers with the Board.  Mr. Pasterz referenced an event they held, a National Federal Funding Summit, where 12 different federal funding agencies were brought to Joseph City and St. Johns and community partners were invited to present their projects to the funders. Mr. Pasterz then explained that the acronym R.E.A.L. stands for resources, energy, access, and lifestyle.  Mr. Pasterz then moved onto explaining that the market and utility companies pick the types of energy they want to use and that now, renewable energies are filling the gap, explaining that it is important that the leaders in the communities create pathways where economic benefit is retained.  Mr. Pasterz referenced an article written by NACo, himself, and the Navajo County Attorney talking about how counties can maintain control. He explained that the County does not have the authority to tell private landowners that they cannot do legal business on their property, but that they can make sure that things are mitigated for.  Mr. Pasterz recapped his presentation by reiterating that Real AZ is an informal advisory group, operating through regional collaboration and shared resources, that they have in-person, bi-monthly lunch meetings and that operational activities are funded through membership dues. He explained that operational activities consist of the website, brochures, and promotional videos. He further recapped by stating that, as Economic Development Director at Navajo County, part of his job duties is coordinating the activities of Real AZ and that there are no staff of Real AZ and that Real AZ is the promotional skin used to promote and lift the entire region. Next, Mr. Pasterz went over the priorities of Real AZ, created by the Executive Advisory Committee and the members.  The top 3 priorities are Industry Recruitment, Energy Community & Infrastructure Development, and Workforce Development.  He further explained that another of his duties is participating on the Northeastern Arizona Workforce Development Board as an Executive Committee member, which is another way to help promote economic growth in the region.  Mr. Pasterz then gave an example of bringing industry to the area, referring to how Real AZ was able to help ZenniHome decide to invest in the area by organizing meetings with local municipal partners and workforce developers.  He also shared some examples of how Real AZ marketing highlights the lifestyle of the communities in the region.  Mr. Pasterz then shared the story of a small business who was not a member of Real AZ, Carbon Utility, who the group helped find a suitable site in Holbrook for their operations. Next, Mr. Pasterz explained how the funding origins came from APS, the American Rescue Act, Membership Dues, the Office of Resiliency, and interest activity and that those fundings pay for the Navajo County Economic Development Director and the Real AZ operations. He then went over some of the positives that have come about from Real AZ activities, such as back taxes being paid on the paper mill property, the new Coal Community Development Manager that is paid through a partnership grant, the Route 66 Brownfields grant that is being utilized by the County and other partners, a housing manufacturer, solar battery farms, new technology and recreation businesses investing in the region, and 38 other new companies that have invested into Real AZ since he started. Mr. Pasterz explained that Real AZ is made up of 15 public companies, 22 private companies, 6 academic institutions, and 7 non-profits and that new members are always welcome. He then explained that the executive committee members of Real AZ are volunteers.  He shared with the Board some letters of support he'd received from some members of Real AZ. Mr. Pasterz concluded his presentation by sharing a slide of the members and partners of Real AZ and welcomed questions from the Board.

SUPERINTENDENT OF SCHOOLS:

a.

Consideration and approval of the recommended distribution of Secure Rural Schools (SRS) Title I funds to school districts.
A presentation was shown.
Rachael Lewis, Superintendent of Schools, presented information regarding the Secure Rural Schools (SRS) Title I, II, and III funds. Ms. Lewis stated that she wanted to provide background and education on the purpose and history of the Secure Rural Schools program. Ms. Lewis explained that in 1908, Congress committed to sharing 25% of federal timber harvest revenues with local schools and rural governments. She stated that this system functioned until the mid-1990s, when endangered species protections significantly reduced timber harvests, leaving schools without sufficient revenue. Congress then enacted the Secure Rural Schools and County Self-Determination Act to compensate for declining timber receipts. She noted that timber harvests have not recovered and that in years when SRS was not reauthorized, Navajo County received only approximately $55,000 to distribute among roads, Firewise communities, and schools. She described the challenges caused by inconsistent federal reauthorization, explaining that schools struggle to plan and hire long-term staff when funding is uncertain. She shared that superintendents use SRS funds for transportation needs, including bus purchases and repairs, as well as after-school tutoring programs, teacher stipends, and advanced coursework opportunities. She emphasized the importance of classroom aides, particularly in early grades, and noted that these funds help districts maintain essential support positions. Ms. Lewis reported that through lobbying efforts by elected officials, including members of the Board, Secure Rural Schools was reauthorized and backdated for fiscal years 2024, 2025, and 2026, though the authorization expires again in September. She reviewed the current allocation, noting that the County received $754,432, with $25,000 designated for roads, a portion for Firewise communities, and the majority for schools. She explained that $50,000 is distributed to the Navajo County Accommodation District, and the remaining funds are allocated to districts based on average daily membership and the number of eligible forest acres. She emphasized that districts use these funds responsibly and carefully, especially given ongoing uncertainty. She stated that FY25 funds are expected in mid-April with a similar allocation and will be submitted through the Consent Agenda, and FY26 funds are anticipated in mid-April 2027. Ms. Lewis concluded by thanking the Board for the opportunity to present and for their support, stating that she appreciates the chance to educate the public and has gained a deeper understanding of school needs through this process.
Vice Chairwoman Whitesinger made a motion  to approve the recommended distribution of Secure Rural Schools (SRS) Title I funds to school districts, motion seconded by  Supervisor Whiting .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

b.

Report to the Board regarding investigation of constituent complaints about lighting at West Camp Wind Farm.
Cody Cooper provided an update regarding complaints received by the Board of Supervisors concerning the blinking obstruction lights on the newly constructed West Camp Wind Farm. Mr. Cooper explained that in January 2026, the Board directed Planning and Development Services to contact the parent company for an update on the Aircraft Detection Lighting System (ADLS) required under the original Special Use Permit, contingent upon FAA approval. Mr. Cooper reported that the wind farm developer confirmed the ADLS hardware has been fully installed, which staff also observed during construction. However, the system cannot be activated until all FAA requirements are completed. These include full hardware installation, onsite flight testing, coverage mapping, and completion of the Obstruction Evaluation Airport Airspace Analysis. He noted that these steps can only occur once the wind farm is fully constructed and commercially operational. Mr. Cooper reported that although construction is complete, the West Camp Wind Farm is not yet in commercial operation. The company is currently anticipating to be operational no later than June 2026 and is attempting to expedite the timeline. Mr. Cooper emphasized that FAA requirements cannot be overridden by the County and that activation of the ADLS is dependent on federal processes and grid-connection timelines. Once FAA testing and approvals are complete, the ADLS will be activated, and the obstruction lights will illuminate only when aircraft are detected within the system's parameters.
Chairman Seymore thanked staff for looking into the matter.
Vice Chairwoman Whitesinger asked for clarification, confirming that the obstruction lighting is currently operating continuously. She further inquired that, upon the anticipated June activation of the Aircraft Detection Lighting System, the lights would only illuminate when an aircraft is within the required detection radius and asked how many miles that coverage area includes.
Mr. Cooper responded that the obstruction lighting is currently operating continuously because the Aircraft Detection Lighting System has not yet been authorized for full commissioning. Staff explained that the FAA still requires the lights to remain active for pilot safety while the towers are within navigable airspace and until all FAA testing and approvals are complete. He further explained that once the new system is activated, it will include a horizontal detection buffer of three nautical miles in all directions around the wind farm. If an aircraft is detected within that three-mile radius and at an altitude of 200 feet above ground level or higher, the obstruction lights will illuminate.
