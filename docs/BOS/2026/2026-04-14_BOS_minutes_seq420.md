CALL TO THE PUBLIC :
Annette Tenijieth greeted the Board and introduced herself as a Councilwoman for the White Mountain Apache Tribe, representing District 3. She expressed her support for David Marshall for the position of Navajo County Recorder. Ms. Tenijieth emphasized that the Recorder's role carries a responsibility that reaches every corner of the County and requires precision, consistency, and public confidence. She stated that David Marshall understands these responsibilities and would approach the position with a steady, service-minded perspective and a clear focus on getting the fundamentals right.
She noted that Mr. Marshall recognizes the importance of serving all communities across Navajo County with fairness and respect, including tribal communities. She added that he would bring a practical approach to the office, prioritizing accountability, transparency, and ensuring the Recorder's Office functions effectively every day. Ms. Tenijieth asked the Board for their consideration and support in appointing David Marshall as Navajo County Recorder.
Jerome Kasey III introduced himself as Chairman of the White Mountain Apache Tribe and recommended that the Board appoint David Marshall as Navajo County Recorder.
Mr. Kasey stated that he is a lifelong resident of Navajo County and has lived on reservation lands that serve as key tourism areas within the County. He emphasized that the decision before the Board is significant to local communities and that the leadership chosen for the Recorder's Office carries meaningful weight for tribal communities. He explained that he comes before the Board as a fellow Navajo County resident, a tribal leader, and a representative of a sovereign nation whose people live, work, and participate fully in the civic life of the County. Mr. Kasey expressed strong support for the appointment of David Marshall as Navajo County Recorder, stating that the Recorder's Office serves as a foundation of integrity and carries responsibilities that demand discipline, accountability, and trust. He noted that David Marshall would bring a steady hand, a clear sense of duty, and a commitment to serving all Navajo County residents with professionalism and fairness.
Donna Shurwin addressed the Board and stated that she would like to assist the Elections Office in recruiting poll workers. She referred to the student who sang at the start of the meeting and noted the importance of engaging students in civic participation. Ms. Shurwin shared that her research indicates there are 7,967 high school students enrolled within the County's schools, estimating that nearly half are 16 years of age or older. She then referred to ARS 16-531, sections F–H, which outline the qualifications for minors to serve as clerks on an election board. Ms. Shurwin stated that involving the youth in this capacity would be beneficial and would help teach the value of service. She proposed that the Elections Office and the Superintendent of Schools Office collaborate to encourage high school students to serve as poll workers. Ms. Shurwin concluded by thanking the Recorder's Office for advertising poll worker opportunities on the new voter registration card she recently received in the mail.
Chairman Seymore asked staff to continue the dialogue with Ms. Shurwin about how the County can increase their poll workers.
Celia Laughlin addressed the Board and expressed support for Ms. Shurwin's comments. She then stated that she wished to speak regarding hand counts and the need for a corrective action plan. Ms. Laughlin referenced her previous remarks during Call to the Public and clarified that the voter registration figure she cited, 117.4%, was a statistic from Apache County. She also referred to correspondence she has had with County Manager Bryan Layton and stated that there is a video dated July 28, 2025, showing Rayleen Richards, Christen Haddox, and a minor entering the Elections building and marking and processing ballots through tabulators without bipartisan observers or witnesses present. She further referenced a contract with ESNS that she said limits access to machines to only county approved employees. Ms. Laughlin stated that she does not wish to re-litigate the 2020 or 2024 elections but wants to ensure protection for the 2026 election. She claimed that for three years she has presented evidence of a broken chain of custody, including 36 defective official ballot reports, the absence of retained machine logs, and missing drobox videos, which she stated violate HAVA's 22 month retention requirement. Ms. Laughlin noted that Mr. Layton's email referred to future training and checklists, but she questioned whether improvements would occur without a mandatory corrective action plan. She then proposed that the Board adopt her corrective action plan, stating that a CAP is not optional but is a professional standard used in regulated industries, providing ownership, a 90-day timeline, and enforceability. She also referenced a parallel lawsuit pending in Maricopa County seeking the same corrective action plan she is requesting the Board's support, stating that the issues are statewide and nationwide.

Ivan Walker introduced himself as a member of the White Mountain Apache Tribe and expressed his support for David Marshall being appointed as Navajo County Recorder. Mr. Walker concluded by stating that David Marshall has the integrity, vision, and work ethic to serve Navajo County well, and he encouraged the Board to support Mr. Marshall's appointment as Navajo County Recorder.
RaNae Rowles greeted the Board and stated that she was grateful for the opportunity to speak. She provided the Board with a handout. Ms. Rowles stated that all elected officials and employed officers of the state are required to take an oath of office affirming that they will support the laws of the state. She said that on October 30, 2025, she met with Jose Lerma and Recorder Jordan to notify them of 41 voters on the Navajo County voter roll who had out-of-state mailing addresses and active voter registrations in other states. She stated that one of those individuals appeared to have voted by mail in Arizona in the same election in which he reportedly voted in person in South Carolina, which she said constitutes a Class 5 felony under ARS 16-1016. Ms. Rowles stated that she requested an investigation into the alleged double voting and asked that the Recorder's Office follow Arizona law by confirming the out-of-state registrations with the appropriate county clerks so they could be canceled, preventing further election mail from being sent. She referenced ARS 16-165(B), which requires a county recorder who receives credible information that a person has registered to vote in a different county to confirm the registration with that county and, upon confirmation, cancel the voter's registration. She then cited ARS 16-1009, which states that a public officer who knowingly fails or refuses to perform a duty prescribed by law is guilty of a Class 3 misdemeanor. Ms. Rowles said she assumed the Recorder's Office was complying with state law and subsequently emailed Mr. Lerma and Recorder Jordan documentation regarding an additional 26 out-of-state registrations in November and 129 more in January and February 2026. Ms. Rowles stated that a review of the County's voter roll dated April 8, 2026, showed that none of the registrations had been confirmed or cancelled as required by law. She added that more than 1,700 voters in Navajo County receive a permanent mail ballot at an address that indicates they are no longer in residence. She referenced ARS 16-1017, which states that it is a Class 2 misdemeanor to knowingly vote in a county where one no longer resides. She expressed concern that sending ballots and election information to voters whose mailing addresses do not indicate residency in the County could lead individuals to believe they remain eligible to vote. Ms. Rowles stated that continuing to send election mail to addresses that cannot be confirmed is a problem that could impact the results of County and local elections. She asked the Board to select a Recorder who is willing to use existing resources to ensure County elections remain secure by confirming that only residents of the County are voting and by verifying whether a voter's registration remains accurate when mailing addresses are updated within or outside the County.

BUDGET WORK SESSION :
The work session convened at 1:38 p.m.
Jayson Vowell reported that staff had completed one-on-one budget meetings with departments and elected officials and have consolidated their submissions into a summary. He stated that, after removing contingencies and one-time items, recurring 2026 expenditures and revenues were both about $48 million, while preliminary 2027 recurring expenditures are projected to rise to roughly $49.3 million and recurring revenues to about $51.8 million, leaving approximately $2.4 million in ongoing capacity. He attributed about half of the 2.7% recurring expenditure increase to salary-related items, including sheriff step increases, shifting one GIS-related full-time employee from HURF to the general fund, and bringing the court commissioner's pay up to the statutory level, with the remainder driven by inflationary cost increases such as utilities. He indicated staff would feel comfortable recommending a total 5% employee pay increase, structured as a combination of across-the-board and performance-based adjustments. He indicated that about $450,000 is needed for salary market adjustments to keep key job classes competitive, especially sheriff's deputies, attorneys, some executive roles, and court clerks. He noted a specific goal of bringing Clerk of the Court and Superior Court clerk positions to a more consistent pay level, as they have historically been misaligned. He reviewed departmental requests such as vehicle tracking software for the Assessor's Office, an Active Directory system analysis, possible migration of software services to the cloud, public website improvements, expanded wireless VPN for the IT Department and dedicated funding to explore countywide artificial intelligence applications.  Bryan Layton stated that while there is no specific artificial intelligence proposal yet, they want to set aside resources so the county can proactively and deliberately explore AI, either by hiring a consultant or forming an internal team. He emphasized that the goal is to use AI not just for simple tasks like email support, but to meaningfully improve county processes and workforce efficiency.  Mr. Vowell outlined requests for additional staffing and related costs, including a Public Fiduciary secretary, two positions in the County Attorney's Office, an entry level support position and a third civil deputy, and AI-based case redaction software. He also described the Superior Court's request for certification pay and 0.5 full-time employees each in Winslow and Show Low, as well as school district requests for about $50,000 to run a new literacy program and for the County to potentially absorb the Consortium, noting that a reliable cost estimate for that absorption is not yet available.   Supervisor Peshlakai spoke about potential improvements for the Kayenta Justice Court, including access to the existing Starlink Wi-Fi, additional security cameras on the east parking lot, fencing and reinforcement of the building's exterior, and an assessment of needed exterior repairs. He also noted that Public Health staff providing birth and death certificate services there should have secure Wi-Fi access when handling sensitive health information.  Supervisor Whiting requested clarification regarding the Consortium request, specifically why the schools no longer want to handle it and what the consortium is trying to accomplish, indicating he did not fully understand the request being made of the county.  A discussion was held regarding the school consortium request for the county to assume responsibility for its roughly 20–25 employees' health and liability insurance.  A discussion was held regarding how to structure a proposed 5% employee compensation increase, weighing across-the-board COLA versus performance pay. Mr. Layton indicated that staff are comfortable with a 5% total adjustment and that the board can decide how much should be guaranteed base pay versus merit-based performance pay.  He described how performance funds are distributed by directors and elected officials.  Mr. Vowell reviewed some of the one-time requests, such as funding for furniture for the schools and a metal records storage building and workstations for the Clerk of the Court.  A discussion was held regarding the proposed school literacy program funding request and seeing if there is grant money available to support the program.  Mr. Vowell reviewed the county's cash position, noting a beginning general fund balance of about $31.6 million projected to grow by roughly $2 million to $33.6 million, with past declines tied only to deliberate transfers for pension and capital projects. He explained that after reserving roughly $11 million for stability, about $22.5 million remains available, and even if all currently identified one-time items were fully funded, the county would still end the year near $28 million in cash, a level he characterized as financially comfortable while the county prepares for future impacts from Cholla coming off the tax rolls.  Mr. Vowell reviewed the capital budget, noting about $1.3 million in previously budgeted but unspent capital items such as the treasurer's system and a fire alarm system for the main building. He highlighted a major new item, an estimated $2 million to replace aging county fuel sites and tanks, and explained that, alongside these, the county maintains roughly $900,000 in recurring capital for items like fairgrounds building needs, the computer replacement program, and regular copier and vehicle replacements. He added that IT capital will be moved into the IT department's operating budget to improve accountability and transparency for technology spending.   Chairman Seymore  inquired whether the building improvements line item referred only to adding space or features to facilities, or if it also covered general building repairs.  Mr. Vowell explained that the listed building improvements are specific project upgrades, such as reconfiguring the Silver Creek facility and security/counter work in the Holbrook building, while routine repairs are handled in the Facilities budget. He also noted Planning and Zoning's request to digitize records and mentioned that a proposal to buy roll?offs and a truck for code enforcement cleanups is still being evaluated against using private waste haulers.  Mr. Vowell stated that the Benefit Trust Fund budget includes a new benefit lead analyst position to address increasing benefits-related workload. He also outlined key Public Works funding, including special road funds by district, major county road and bridge projects, significant equipment replacement, and building improvements for existing Public Works facilities.   Supervisor Peshlakai  inquired if the projects are all county-maintained roads, outside city limits.  Mr. Layton advised that they are all county-maintained roads.   Supervisor Benally  spoke about work needed on North Park Drive.  Mr. Vowell reviewed the Health District budget requests, including funding for two additional vehicles, more drug and medical supplies, about $30,000 for computer monitors and copiers, and higher utility costs tied to the Taylor facility. He added that there are no significant changes proposed in the Library District or Jail District budgets compared to the prior year.   Chairman Seymore  inquired if there is funding to cover the increase in gas.  Mr. Vowell advised that they will revisit that with the departments.  A discussion was held regarding road and bridge projects and the limited space and need for improvements at the Kayenta Justice Court.
The work session ended at 2:29 p.m.
