PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration of a Liquor License from Theodore Van Gaines III (Agent) for Love's Travel Stop #278 located at 4703 Main Street Joseph City, AZ 86032.
Amanda Silverhorn explained that a 010 Beer and Wine Store liquor license application has been received from Theodore Van Gaines, an agent for Love's Travel Stop #278.  She stated that the Sheriff's Office, Treasurer's Office, Health Department, and Planning and Development have all responded that the application meets the requirements of their departments, the 21-day posting has been completed, and that staff recommends approval.
Chairman Seymore opened the meeting for public comments.
Karliyn Bushman provided copies of a petition to the Board. Ms. Bushman explained that she has been a resident of Joseph City for 50 plus years and is speaking as a representative of many of Joseph City’s citizens, explaining that the petition had gained over 200 signatures in about 60 hours over a holiday weekend.  Ms. Bushman stated that liquor has never been sold in the community and that the community members wish for it to continue not being sold there. Ms. Bushman said that to her knowledge this is the third time that the community has opposed a liquor license for Love’s Travel Stop.  She then shared that Love’s does not participate in community activities, events, and is not a member of the Chamber of Commerce.  Ms. Bushman acknowledged that proper regulations have been followed, but that the establishment has made no effort to gather community input.  Ms. Bushman then relayed a message from another community member that drinking is already a problem that will be compounded by the selling of package liquor and expressed concern over the lack of effective notice to the community and asked that the item be tabled until the community has adequate time to express their opinion.
Chairman Seymore allowed Ms. Bushman to have one more minute to speak.
Ms. Bushman asked if the license passes, what will be done to keep the community safe, stating that the response time of the Sheriff’s Office to the area has been observed as over 30 minutes.
Jenny Fish Oldham stated that she has lived in Joseph City since she was 9 years old and that it has been a dry community and relatively safe area.  She stated that community members have previously explained that they would always oppose a liquor license application for the establishment, explaining that liquor can be bought in the nearby areas of Jackrabbit and Holbrook and that the community members of Joseph City do not want to be like other communities.  Ms. Oldham asked the Board to either deny the application or table the item to give the community time to express their concerns.
Chairman Seymore asked if a representative of Love’s was present. Being that there was not, he closed the public comment portion of the meeting.
Chairman Seymore explained that the Board of Supervisors' role is to determine if the facility requesting the liquor license meets the guidelines such as distance from schools.  He stated that citizens should reach out to the applicant of the State Board of Liquor Licensing with their concerns.
Supervisor Peshlakai asked staff to clarify if the posting was done in compliance.
The Clerk replied that the posting is only required to be done at the location of the establishment requesting the license.
Chairman Seymore asked if anyone knew what the distance from the establishment to the local schools was?
Ms. Oldham stated that the location is less than a mile from the High School.
Jason Moore, appearing virtually, from the County Attorney’s Office, noted that there appeared to be some confusion from the audience regarding the noticing requirements associated with the item under discussion. He clarified that certain land use matters, such as Special Use Permits, require mailed notice to property owners within a specified distance of the proposed activity. However, he explained that the legal requirements for liquor license postings are different. He stated that for a liquor license application, the only required notice is the physical posting at the business location applying for the license. Mr. Moore added that he did not know the exact distance between the Love’s Truck Stop and the high school or the school district boundaries. Based on staff comments, Mr. Moore indicated that the legal noticing requirements appeared to have been satisfied. He concluded that if compliance with the statutory posting requirements has occurred, there is limited action the Board of Supervisors can take regarding the notice concerns raised.
Chairman Seymore asked if staff could verify whether the establishment was within a mile of the school.
Ms. Bushman stated that the regulations are that the establishment must be 300 feet from a school.
Supervisor Whiting acknowledged and thanked the individuals who came to speak, noting that it can be intimidating and sometimes frustrating when the public does not fully understand the processes involved. He stated that it appeared the speakers did have some understanding of the issue and expressed appreciation for Mr. Moore’s explanation regarding the legal requirements the Board must follow.
He clarified that the posting requirements were completed as required by law, which is why the notice was placed where it was. Supervisor Whiting also appreciated the Chairman’s explanation that the final decision-making authority regarding liquor licenses rests with the State, not the County. He emphasized that the Board’s role at the local level is limited to reviewing specific factors and providing feedback to the State, not deciding whether the license should or should not be approved. Supervisor Whiting asked if the packet containing the public comments could be forwarded along with the Board’s response so that the State could see the concerns expressed by the community. He noted that members of the public could make their voices heard when the State considers the matter. He stated that although he understands and shares some of the community’s concerns, those issues are not what the Board is voting on. The Board is limited to determining whether the application meets the required criteria, and staff’s review indicates that it does. He thanked the speakers for attending and for ensuring their voices were heard, expressing hope that the information provided will help guide their advocacy to the appropriate decision-making authority.
Vice Chairwoman Whitesinger asked Mr. Moore if the Board were to not approve the application what would the next steps be?
Mr. Moore explained that if the applicant has satisfied the statutory requirements for the liquor license and the State is otherwise willing to approve it, the Board of Supervisors has very little discretion in the matter. He noted that subjective opinions about alcohol, whether from members of the public or from the Board, do not factor into the legal standard the Board must apply. He reiterated that if the applicant has met the State’s requirements and County staff have conducted their review and are recommending approval, the Board’s legal authority to deny the application is extremely limited. He added that denying the request without a legal basis could expose the County to potential legal action from the applicant. Although he could not predict the outcome of such a case, he stated that, based on the information available, he did not see a legal basis for the Board to deny approval.
Supervisor Peshlakai asked if there was a prior application from the establishment and if it was approved or denied by the Board of Supervisors.
Ms. Bushman stated that there have been prior applications denied by the Board but that she did not have time to research the date.
Supervisor Peshlakai asked if staff could confirm.
Chairman Seymore explained that staff would need time to research and suggested the item be tabled.
Supervisor Peshlakai asked if staff could do additional research before a vote is made.
The Clerk confirmed that the deadline to return the application to the state is June 20, 2026 and Chairman Seymore said that the item can be tabled to the next meeting, giving staff direction to look into prior applications.
Supervisor Whiting stated that he would like some clarity regarding previous applications from the establishment.
Chairman Seymore made a motion to table the item and gave staff direction to research prior applications for Love's Travel Stop #278 and to contact the Arizona State Department of Liquor Licensing regarding prior applications, motion seconded by Supervisor Benally .  Vote 4-0 approving the motion with Supervisor Peshlakai not voting.

SUPERINTENDENT OF SCHOOLS:

a.

Presentation on Navajo County Superintendent of Schools Office & Education Service Agency Service Programs Report
A presentation was shown.
Rachael Lewis explained that the Special Services Consortium consists of school-level specialists who work directly with students requiring services through their Individualized Education Programs (IEPs). The consortium contracts with districts throughout Navajo and Apache Counties to provide these services, offering a cost-sharing model that reduces expenses for districts while ensuring students receive needed support. Services range from education specialists to occupational therapists. She noted that 1,152 students are currently receiving services. She reported that the number of virtual therapists remains steady. While virtual services are not preferred, they remain a viable and successful option in rural areas.
Ms. Lewis stated that as of March 31, the program budget is approximately $1.5 million, which includes salaries and operating expenses. Additional expenditures are expected through June 30.   Ms. Lewis noted continued Alternative School District designation, a successful Dropout Recovery Program in partnership with Legacy Learning Community, and 16 graduates this year compared to three last year. She highlighted strong progress in literacy initiatives, including LETRS training with a 97 percent teacher pass rate. Additional updates included science fair participation, new partnerships with NPC and the Navajo County Library District, and ongoing efforts to unite local school leaders. She announced an upcoming teacher conference scheduled for July 27–28 in partnership with Apache County.
Chairman Seymore thanked Ms. Lewis for the report and expressed his appreciation for her efforts to improve the schools.
Supervisor Peshlakai expressed appreciation to Ms. Lewis for her engagement with the schools in district two, noting that her attendance at the Native Education Summit was appreciated.

Consideration of an Agreement between Northeast Arizona Training Center (NATC) and Navajo County for the Shooting Range Berm Improvement Project.
Chelle Ewald presented an agreement with the Northeast Arizona Training Center (NATC) for improvements to the firearms training range located at 1840 West Paper Mill Road in Taylor. She explained that the range is utilized by the Navajo County Sheriff’s Office for training and certification. Ms. Ewald further explained that NATC requested assistance to raise and improve the existing berm to enhance safety for range users and the public who use nearby recreational trails. He expressed appreciation for the Board’s advocacy and CSA’s support in working with state legislators. She also shared that last month she attended the Hopi Code Talkers Memorial event at Kykotsmovi where she had the opportunity to meet veterans and current members of the Armed Forces, enjoy dinner with them, and socialize. He expressed appreciation to staff and department heads, as well as the Vice Chair, for their work during the budget process, which included multiple meetings reviewing the County’s financial position and comparing it with prior years. He stated that the County remains committed to maintaining a level tax levy, placing Navajo County among the lowest tax assessments in Arizona. Chairman Seymore shared that he recently traveled to Utah for a graduation and a family wedding and visited Glen Canyon Dam. Chairman Seymore expressed gratitude for the recent rainfall in the area and recognized it as a welcome blessing. He concluded by thanking citizens for their engagement and attendance.
