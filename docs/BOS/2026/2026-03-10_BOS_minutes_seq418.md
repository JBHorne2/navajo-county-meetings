CALL TO THE PUBLIC :
Chairman Seymore explained that the Call to the Public format has been revised and will now be reserved for general comments; individuals wishing to speak on a specific agenda item will be invited to do so when that item is presented.
Donna Shurwin greeted the board, stating that she lives in Pinetop, Arizona. She noted that she had spoken at the February 10, 2026, meeting to request that an agenda item regarding Real AZ be added and expressed disappointment that her request had not been honored. Ms. Shurwin stated that concerns are increasing among Navajo County constituents regarding the county's involvement with Real AZ, and that many citizens question why Navajo County continues to engage with an entity they believe to be dissolved. She further questioned whether it is legal for the private entity known as Real AZ to operate as Navajo County's formal economic development department with shared employees and office space within the government complex.  Ms. Shurwin expressed her view that Real AZ only pursues the green energy industry, which she believes does not foster balanced economic growth and instead negatively affects the County's longstanding tourism industry. She questioned the fairness of Real AZ's practices, stating that they appear to benefit paying members rather than serve the interests of Navajo County residents. Ms. Shurwin stated her belief that Real AZ aligns itself with entities seeking green energy subsidies and tax incentives, claiming that Navajo County citizens will bear the costs through taxes, higher energy bills, and diminished economic opportunities.  She further stated that the existing solar farms and wind turbines have consumed large tracts of land, disrupted sacred sites, affected wildlife habitats, and eradicated agricultural industry.  She expressed the view that green energy projects provide temporary jobs, reduce property values, deflate legacies, contribute to unemployment and do not address rising living costs or provide lasting benefits.  Ms. Shurwin concluded by requesting that the Board place her proposed agenda items on the March 24, 2026, agenda: to impose an immediate moratorium on all Navajo County wind, solar, and battery storage projects to allow time for further evaluation of their impacts, and to disassociate from and dissolve Real AZ in order to restore fairness to the County's energy policy.
Chairman Seymore stated that an item will be placed on a future agenda to address Ms. Shurwin's concerns.
Kelly Meixler thanked the Board for allowing her to speak and introduced herself as a local business owner committed to safeguarding Navajo County's future.  She stated that she is also asking the Board to place Ms. Shurwin's suggested items on a future agenda.  Ms. Meixler said she'd build on the moratorium that Ms. Shurwin asked for, outlining several actionable steps that she believes will protect the grid, property values, and the people of Navajo County. Ms. Meixler said the first step she suggests is to rescind Resolution 17-2025, stating that the measure influenced by Real AZ has enabled unchecked renewable expansion without protection, leading to grid instability and lower property values. She explained that studies show homes near industrial solar sites lose 10-20% in value, explaining that rescinding Resolution 17-2025 will clear the way for policies centered on reliability while addressing privacy concerns regarding flock cameras that SB111 and Representative Marshall's bill curb to aim.  She expressed her view that the County should fight for a small nuclear reactor at the Cholla Power Plant, claiming that its existing infrastructure, transmission lines, water access, and workforce base make it ideal for a thorium-based SNR offering advancements in safe, efficient nuclear technology with minimal waste and enhanced safety features, which could spark a boom in Arizona mining, revitalizing thorium extraction and creating a new industrial hub.  She expressed her view that an SNR would generate hundreds of permanent jobs for locals, would boost the tax base and align with Federal Department of Energy initiatives, potentially securing 225 to 625 million dollars in modernization funds and ensuring safe, secure power without foreign dependencies. Ms. Meixler reaffirmed that a comprehensive plan would include robust ordinances, zoning laws, mandatory environmental and community impact assessments, two-mile setbacks from homes, requiring voter approval for large scale projects, implementing grid safeguards mandating 99% reliability, and creating a local energy fund from developer fees to support worker transitions and offset rate increases.  Ms. Meixler concluded by stating that the Board should enact a moratorium on renewables, dissolve Real AZ, rescind resolution 17-2025, fight for an SNR at Cholla, and enact protective ordinances. She then presented the Board with some articles that supported her suggestions.
Joan Lewis addressed the Board, stating that she is from Snowflake and a longtime resident of Navajo County. She asked the Board to revisit Resolution 17?2025 and the June 2025 zoning updates, which she stated weakened turbine setbacks and eased approvals for renewable energy projects. Ms. Lewis noted that while some conditions were added, she believes the changes prioritized intermittent energy projects over rural character and community needs. Ms. Lewis stated that residents requesting stronger protections, such as larger buffers, were denied, and she expressed the view that the final vote was rushed with limited outreach. She referenced the Arizona Corporation Commission's recent approval of gas conversions at the Springerville and Coronado plants, stating that these conversions will provide reliable baseload power, reduce emissions without requiring batteries, prevent blackouts, and preserve jobs. She shared her belief that transmission lines should carry firm power, gas now and nuclear power in the future. Ms. Lewis urged the Board to impose an immediate moratorium on all renewable energy projects, including solar, wind, and battery storage, to halt what she described as ongoing damage and to assess the true costs. She referenced SB111 and Representative Marshall's bill and stated that converting the Cholla plant to gas would result in less disruption and faster power delivery. Ms. Lewis also requested larger buffers, suggesting 10 miles. She concluded by asking the Board to cut ties with Real AZ, pullback Resolution 17-2025 and to prioritize people over permits.

b.

Budget presentation for fiscal year 2026-2027 General Fund revenue projections.
A presentation was shown
Jayson Vowell addressed the Board and stated that he would be presenting the first step in preparing the Fiscal Year 2027 budget, focusing on revenues, forecasting, and projections. He explained that the general fund functions as a "melting pot" from which many services are provided. Mr. Vowell reported that revenues remain strong and robust, noting that they have exceeded projections over the past several years. For the current fiscal year, his team is projecting a positive variance of approximately $2.4 million. Mr. Vowell further explained that, historically, about 79% of the revenue in the general fund is collected from taxes.  He explained that the 5-year average increase in state sales tax revenue has been 7.3% and the 5-year average increase in county sales tax revenue has been about 7.5%. He explained that his team uses conservative projections to safeguard against potential recessionary impacts and statewide economic pressures, including inflation, housing affordability, savings trends, and fluctuations in retail spending, particularly from remote purchasing. Mr. Vowell then said that the County is proposing to keep the current property tax rate in fiscal year 2027.  Mr. Vowell summarized that the increase from fiscal year 26 to 27 is an increase of approximately 6.7% which is subject to change. However, his team is not expecting any major impacts.
Vice Chairwoman Whitesinger expressed gratitude for the work done by the finance department to maintain a balanced budget.  She then stated that as a property owner she is appreciative of the flat property tax rate year after year and that she appreciates the department's efforts in helping to support community members by continuing to project the flat property tax rate.
Chairman Seymore stated that many issues arise at the state level that supervisors must work to keep within state budgets rather than shifting to county budgets. He noted that the state has still not addressed the future of the "Big Beautiful Bill" or how it will impact the state budget this year. Chairman Seymore expressed his appreciation for the finance team's efforts in staying informed about developments at the national, state, and county levels when preparing financial projections.

COUNTY ATTORNEY:

a.

Presentation by the Family Advocacy Center
A presentation was shown
Catherine Doyle introduced herself as the Director of the Navajo County Family Advocacy Center, a 501c3 that operates under the Navajo County Attorney's Office.  Ms. Doyle explained that the center is designed to help children and their families, that the center was created by the County Attorney's office to fill a gap of services in the community, and that it is a non-profit organization based off donations from the community.  Ms. Doyle said that her presentation uses statistics from Navajo and Apache counties explaining that every 36 hours a child crime is committed and that 99% of the victims know their offender and that year over year there is a continued increase in victims. Ms. Doyle shared that the Family Advocacy Center's mission statement is that the "Navajo County Family Advocacy Center's goal is to reduce the stress that families, especially children, may face as victims of abuse, neglect, domestic violence, or sexual assault during the investigation and prosecution of a crime against them."  Ms. Doyle stated that services have continually been added over the years since the center was created in 2011 and that they now have two locations, one in Show Low and one in Holbrook.  She then outlined the services offered at the center, including temporary housing, care closets, forensic interviews and examinations, counseling, legal service navigation, and a crisis hotline.
Vice Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked Ms. Doyle for the work that her team does and for the presentation, stating that it was a good opportunity to understand what services are provided and communicate that to the public.
Chairman Seymore thanked the County Attorney's office for starting the program.
County Attorney Brad Carlyon said that his office started the program to help minimize trauma for children who become victims in the community. He said that local law enforcement officers too often did not have the proper training to interview the victims, and they would be re-traumatized.  Mr. Carlyon stated that the center can interview victims in a more comfortable child-friendly setting.  He said that the center initially started to conduct forensic interviews and minimize re-trauma, and that services have progressed, extending to helping parents as well.  He also explained that the center has expanded educational outreach and has brought community programs together to serve children in one place, and he thanked the County for participating and providing the building space. Mr. Carlyon concluded by stating that the center also serves the surrounding communities of Apache County and that the FBI, BIA, and tribal police can utilize the services as well.
Chairman Seymore expressed appreciation to County Attorney Carlyon for developing the Family Advocacy Center and to Ms. Doyle for the presentation. He said that he'd had the opportunity to tour the facility in Show Low and that it was fantastic.

BOARD OF SUPERVISORS:

a.

Consideration and approval of a Proclamation for Joseph City Sesquicentennial Commemoration.
Supervisor Peshlakai greeted the Board and invited members of the Joseph City Chamber of Commerce to join him. He explained that the community of Joseph City will be holding its sesquicentennial celebration, marking its 150th anniversary. He noted that the community was settled on March 24, 1876, and was originally known as Allen's Camp before being renamed St. Joseph in 1878. In the 1920s, the name was changed to Joseph City to avoid confusion with St. Joseph, Missouri. Supervisor Peshlakai then read the proclamation.  He then mentioned that the community of Joseph City will be holding their Founder's Day events on March 20–21, 2026.
Cat Hansen thanked the Board for recognizing Joseph City and invited everyone to join them for the festivities on March 20–21, 2026.  She mentioned that on March 20th they will congregate at the old pioneer dam for a campfire circle, then on Saturday, the 21st they will start the day at 6:00 a.m. with an anvil blast, pancake breakfast, 5k and 10k runs, a parade at 10:00 a.m., followed by a barbeque, auction, activities on the local farm, a boutique, walk through history, basketball tournament, rifle shoot, food trucks, and a family dance.
Supervisor Peshlakai made a motion to approve a Proclamation for the Joseph City Sesquicentennial Commemoration, motion seconded by  Vice Chairwoman Whitesinger .  Vote 3-0 approving the motion with Supervisor Whiting not voting and Supervisor Benally absent.
