SHERIFF'S OFFICE:
a. Celebration of the retirement of K-9 Sejn.
Lt. Daniel Deets recognized Detention K-9 Sejn for his service and retirement.
He reported that K-9 Sejn began his career with the Arizona Department of
Public Safety and was retired at six years old due to policy changes. DPS
contacted the Sheriff’s Office after Sejn’s handler reported difficulty keeping him
at home following retirement due to boredom. The Sheriff’s Office evaluated
Sejn and determined he would be an excellent fit for the Detention program. K-9
Sejn played a significant role in maintaining the safety and security of the jail
facilities. His presence served as a deterrent to the introduction of contraband,
and he assisted on numerous callouts with Lt. Deets and Deputy Ostertag. Sejn
was known for his high energy and work ethic, earning the nickname “Benjamin
Button.” During his service with the Navajo County Sheriff’s Office, K-9 Sejn
assisted in the seizure of approximately 138.7 pounds of methamphetamine, 28
pounds of marijuana, 18,573 fentanyl pills, eight PCP blotters, and 1.5 ounces of
crack cocaine. These seizures represent significant contributions to public safety
within Navajo County.
Chairman Seymore expressed his appreciation to K-9 Sejn and wished him
and Mr. Neff good luck.

a. PUBLIC HEARING: TRUTH IN TAXATION pursuant to A.R.S. §42-17104
(primary) and §48-254 (secondary): Consideration and possible approval of
property tax levy with no change to property tax rates from the prior year. 
A presentation was shown. 
Jayson Vowell presented the Navajo County Truth in Taxation Hearing for Fiscal
Year 2026-2027. He explained that state statute prescribes the required wording
for the public notices, which must be issued whenever there is an increase in
property taxes. He reported that four notices were published: one for the County’s
primary property tax and three for secondary districts; Flood Control, Public
Health Services, and the Library District. Mr. Vowell reported that the primary
property tax rate will remain unchanged at 0.8114. For Fiscal Year 2027, the
maximum allowable levy is $9.8 million, with a recommended levy of $9.2 million.
He stated that although the rate is unchanged, property tax revenues will
increase slightly because of higher assessed valuations. The levy increases from
the prior year (from $8.8 million to $9.3 million) totals approximately $409,000,
with $209,000 attributable to new construction and $200,000 due to valuation
changes. This valuation-driven increase triggers the requirement for a Truth in
Taxation hearing. Mr. Vowell reviewed the calculation used to determine both the
maximum levy and the minimum levy rate, noting that anything above the
calculated minimum requires this hearing. He also presented a historical
comparison of allowable and actual levies. He explained that for FY 2027, the
County’s recommended levy is approximately $571,000 below the allowable levy,
representing tax savings for residents. He compared counties statewide,
reporting that Navajo County had the fourth-lowest property tax rate in FY 2026
and is expected to remain fourth lowest for FY 2027. He emphasized that this
reflects responsible taxation, noting that about two-thirds of Arizona counties
have a rate at least double that of Navajo County. Mr. Vowell outlined the
services supported, directly or indirectly, by property taxes, including public
safety, transportation infrastructure, health and welfare functions, land use
planning, and state-mandated services such as the Assessor, Elections,
Recorder, School Superintendent, and Treasurer. The $200,000 valuation driven
increase helps offset inflationary impacts on these services. He reported that no
rate changes are recommended for the three secondary property tax districts
(Health District, Flood Control District, and Library District), which will remain the
same for FY 2027. Mr. Vowell explained that, on average, the County retains
about 14% of each property tax dollar; 8% from the primary rate and 6%
combined from the secondary districts, with the remainder distributed to other
taxing entities. He provided an example of a Navajo County home assessed at
$288,000 (Zillow estimate of $554,000). For this property, the annual increase
would be approximately $5.05 for the primary tax and $4.70 for the secondary
districts, totaling $9.75 per year.
Supervisor Whiting expressed appreciation for passing on the tax savings to
residents.
Chairman Seymore opened the meeting for public comment, being no
comments, he then closed the public hearing. 
Vice Chairwoman Whitesinger made a motion to approve the property tax levy
with no change to property tax rates from the prior year, motion seconded by
Supervisor Benally. Leah Thomas polled the Board, with the vote being 5-0
approving the motion.

Pension Funding Policy – Fiscal Year 2026-2027 
 
 
This pension funding policy is done in accordance of ARS § 38-386.01. The following terms 
used throughout this policy are defined as:  
 
Unfunded Actuarial Accrued Liability (UAAL) – Is the difference between trust assets 
and the estimated future cost of pensions earned by employees.  
 
Annual Required Contribution (ARC) – Is the annual amount required to pay into the 
pension funds, as determined through annual actuarial valuations. It is comprised of 
two primary components: normal pension cost – which is the estimated cost of pension 
benefits earned by employees in the current year; and, amortization of UAAL – which 
is the cost needed to cover the unfunded portion of pensions earned by employees in 
previous years. The UAAL is collected over a period of time referred to as the 
amortization period. The ARC is a percentage of the current payroll.  
 
Funded Ratio – Is a ratio of fund assets to actuarial accrued liability.

In this section, I develop an opinion of value via the sales comparison approach.  The cost and income approaches are not considered applicable as valuation techniques and are not developed.  Sales Comparison Approach  The sales comparison approach to value produces an estimate of value for real estate by comparing recent transactions involving similar properties within the surrounding or competing market area of the subject property. Inherent in this approach is the principle of substitution, which holds that when a property is replaceable within the market, its value tends to be set at the cost of acquiring an equally desirable substitute property, assuming that no costly delay is encountered in making the substitution.  By analyzing comparables involving arms-length transactions between willing, knowledgeable buyers and sellers with reasonable market exposure, price trends can be identified from which value parameters may be extracted. Comparability in terms of physical, locational, and economic characteristics is an important criterion in evaluating the comparables with respect to the subject property. The basic steps involved in the application of this approach are as follows:  I. Researching recent relevant property sales and current offerings throughout the competitive area.  II. Selecting properties which are considered to be most similar to the subject, and then analyzing the selected comparables, giving consideration to the time of sale and any change in economic conditions which may have occurred in the intervening time up to the date of value. Other relevant factors of a physical, functional, or locational nature are also considered.  III. Reducing the sales price to common units of comparison (i.e., the price per square foot of gross building area, etc.).  IV. Making appropriate adjustments between the comparable properties and the property being appraised.  V. Interpreting the adjusted sales data and drawing a valid conclusion.  The specific unit of comparison used in this analysis is the sales price per square foot of site area, calculated by dividing the sales or offering price by the gross site area.
