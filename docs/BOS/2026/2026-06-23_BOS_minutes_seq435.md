SHERIFF'S OFFICE:

a.

Celebration of the retirement of K-9 Sejn.

Lt. Daniel Deets recognized Detention K-9 Sejn for his service and retirement. He reported that K-9 Sejn began his career with the Arizona Department of Public Safety and was retired at six years old due to policy changes. DPS contacted the Sheriff’s Office after Sejn’s handler reported difficulty keeping him at home following retirement due to boredom. The Sheriff’s Office evaluated Sejn and determined he would be an excellent fit for the Detention program. K-9 Sejn played a significant role in maintaining the safety and security of the jail facilities. His presence served as a deterrent to the introduction of contraband, and he assisted on numerous callouts with Lt. Deets and Deputy Ostertag. Sejn was known for his high energy and work ethic, earning the nickname “Benjamin Button.” During his service with the Navajo County Sheriff’s Office, K-9 Sejn assisted in the seizure of approximately 138.7 pounds of methamphetamine, 28 pounds of marijuana, 18,573 fentanyl pills, eight PCP blotters, and 1.5 ounces of crack cocaine. These seizures represent significant contributions to public safety within Navajo County.
Chairman Seymore expressed his appreciation to K-9 Sejn and wished him and Mr. Neff good luck.

b.

Celebration of the retirement of K-9 Zolton.

Lt. Deets recognized the retirement of K-9 Zoltan. He reported that Zoltan joined the Navajo County Sheriff’s Office in 2017 and was originally assigned to Deputy Keith before being assigned to Lt. Deets. During his nearly nine-year career, K-9 Zoltan established a strong record of service, including five criminal apprehensions with no bites. Zoltan’s seizure record includes: 89.4 pounds of cocaine; 40.3 pounds of fentanyl powder; 757,330 fentanyl pills; 18 pounds of heroin; 1,819.6 pounds of marijuana; 1,316 THC vape pens; 600 pounds of methamphetamine; 600 ecstasy pills; 54 LSD blotters; and $220,339 in illegal currency. Deets noted that while most canines are primarily motivated by play, K-9 Zoltan was motivated by his work. Throughout his service, he demonstrated consistent dedication to detection and protection.

He stated that every Supervisor in the state serves on the CSA board. He highlighted CSA’s work facilitating connections with lawmakers, agency leadership, policy professionals, and statewide committees and connecting with professional staff to share information and support. Mr. Sullivan reported on the expansion of CSA’s data and analytics capacity, including the redesign of the CSA website and the development of six online data tools: the County Encyclopedia, State-County Revenue Comparison, County Profiles, Probation Dashboard, Revenue Dashboard, and related policy resources. Mr. Sullivan also reviewed CSA’s research partnerships with the Arizona Board of Regents and universities, recent podcast efforts, and communications tools designed to enhance public understanding of county government. A legislative update was provided, noting that the Legislature adjourned sine die two weeks prior. Mr. Sullivan summarized CSA’s engagement, including more than 100 direct visits with lawmakers, 49 committee testimonies, 29 stakeholder meetings, and efforts that resulted in favorable amendments to 18 bills and the defeat of 34 bills. He discussed the FY 2025-2026 state budget, including concerns related to tax conformity impacts, county budget pass-through protections, long-term care funding methodology corrections, and items affecting probation funding. Additional updates included changes to transportation funding affecting HURF-related programs, sheriff-related budget items, CSA-run bills such as House Bill 2270, reactive advocacy on zoning, energy deployment, public records, and pension legislation, newly enacted laws regarding public noticing and state affordability infrastructure districts, and ballot referrals impacting county operations, including onsite ballot tabulation requirements. Mr. Sullivan noted that CSA is preparing post-session reports and building resources for county professional staff, and that planning is underway for the upcoming policy summit in Coconino County. He closed by expressing appreciation for Navajo County’s involvement in CSA and affirmed CSA’s ongoing support for county leadership and staff.

PUBLIC HEARING: TRUTH IN TAXATION  pursuant to A.R.S. §42-17104 (primary) and §48-254 (secondary): Consideration and possible approval of property tax levy with no change to property tax rates from the prior year.

A presentation was shown.
Jayson Vowell presented the Navajo County Truth in Taxation Hearing for Fiscal Year 2026-2027. He explained that state statute prescribes the required wording for the public notices, which must be issued whenever there is an increase in property taxes. He reported that four notices were published: one for the County’s primary property tax and three for secondary districts; Flood Control, Public Health Services, and the Library District. Mr. Vowell reported that the primary property tax rate will remain unchanged at 0.8114. For Fiscal Year 2027, the maximum allowable levy is $9.8 million, with a recommended levy of $9.2 million. He stated that although the rate is unchanged, property tax revenues will increase slightly because of higher assessed valuations. The levy increases from the prior year (from $8.8 million to $9.3 million) totals approximately $409,000, with $209,000 attributable to new construction and $200,000 due to valuation changes. This valuation-driven increase triggers the requirement for a Truth in Taxation hearing. Mr. Vowell reviewed the calculation used to determine both the maximum levy and the minimum levy rate, noting that anything above the calculated minimum requires this hearing. He also presented a historical comparison of allowable and actual levies. He explained that for FY 2027, the County’s recommended levy is approximately $571,000 below the allowable levy, representing tax savings for residents. He compared counties statewide, reporting that Navajo County had the fourth-lowest property tax rate in FY 2026 and is expected to remain fourth lowest for FY 2027. He emphasized that this reflects responsible taxation, noting that about two-thirds of Arizona counties have a rate at least double that of Navajo County. Mr. Vowell outlined the services supported, directly or indirectly, by property taxes, including public safety, transportation infrastructure, health and welfare functions, land use planning, and state-mandated services such as the Assessor, Elections, Recorder, School Superintendent, and Treasurer. The $200,000 valuation driven increase helps offset inflationary impacts on these services. He reported that no rate changes are recommended for the three secondary property tax districts (Health District, Flood Control District, and Library District), which will remain the same for FY 2027. Mr. Vowell explained that, on average, the County retains about 14% of each property tax dollar; 8% from the primary rate and 6% combined from the secondary districts, with the remainder distributed to other taxing entities. He provided an example of a Navajo County home assessed at $288,000 (Zillow estimate of $554,000). For this property, the annual increase would be approximately $5.05 for the primary tax and $4.70 for the secondary districts, totaling $9.75 per year.
Supervisor Whiting expressed appreciation for passing on the tax savings to residents.
Chairman Seymore opened the meeting for public comment, being no comments, he then closed the public hearing.
Vice Chairwoman Whitesinger made a motion to approve the property tax levy with no change to property tax rates from the prior year, motion seconded by Supervisor Benally . Leah Thomas polled the Board, with the vote being 5-0 approving the motion.

Supervisor Peshlakai made a motion to Convene a Special Meeting of the Navajo County Board of Supervisors, motion seconded by Vice Chairwoman Whitesinger .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Jayson Vowell provided an overview of the Adopted Budget for Fiscal Year 2026-2027, noting no changes from the previously approved tentative budget. Key highlights include funding for roadway and bridge improvements, equipment and vehicle replacements, building upgrades, and technology enhancements. The budget also adds several new positions across the General Fund, Public Health, and Benefits Trust Fund. Special Revenue Funds total $150.2 million, supporting restricted programs such as Public Works, grants, health programs, and the Jail District. Mr. Vowell explained that Debt Service totals just under $2.3 million, reflecting continued savings from 2021 refinancing efforts. The Capital Projects budget is a little over $33 million, largely driven by the Winslow Levee project. Mr. Vowell requested approval of the total adopted budget of $258.2 million for Fiscal Year 2027.
