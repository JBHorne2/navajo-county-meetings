INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY:
a. Consideration of hiring Convergint as the Integrator for the Navajo County
Door and Camera project. They are currently under State Contract and
can be used to fully plan, purchase, install, and support an integrated
Door and Camera system.  
Sam Vanderbeek greeted the board and introduced himself, saying that
he currently works with Navajo County in the IT Department under Ken
Dewitt.  He said that he would be outlining the proposal to establish
Convergint Technology as the systems integration partner for surveillance,
cameras, and door access systems. Mr. Vanderbeek explained that the
county needs an integration partner because there are multiple different
systems, aging hardware, and limited centralized support. He said that in
order to move forward responsibly, the county needs a partner who can
help modernize, standardize, and maintain these critical systems. He
explained that Convergint is a nationally recognized leader in public
sector security integration that brings extensive experience in detention,
justice, and governmental environments and that they understand the
operational realities of the county. Mr. Vanderbeek explained that if
approved, Convergint will begin a comprehensive discovery and
assessment phase during the first 100 days, including a full inventory of
the existing surveillance and access control systems, a health and
vulnerability assessment of current hardware and software, a review of
network storage and retention requirements, direct engagement with
elected officials, department directors, and IT leadership to prioritize
upgrades, replacements, and risk mitigation. He also said one of the
major advantages of working with Convergint is that they can help identify
funding opportunities including federal public safety grants, emergency
preparedness grants, and technology modernization programs to help
reduce the financial burden and accelerate improvements using external

ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES: 
a. Presentation on the Holbrook Civic Center Concept
Bryan Layton greeted the Board and provided a brief update on the idea
of a Civic Center in Holbrook, which is envisioned as a partnership
between Holbrook Unified School District, the City of Holbrook, Northland
Pioneer College, and Navajo County.  He explained that it would likely be
out of reach for one entity to build but that it may be possible with multiple
entities in partnership.  He said that the Civic Center is envisioned as a
modern building with a large gym space and retractable seating to be
used for large events to be held in a flexible space with a regulation sized
pool, possible conference room and flexible office space as well.  Mr.
Layton explained that the Civic Center concept is in initial discussion and
that no financial commitment has been made and that the majority
stakeholders would be Holbrook School District, the City of Holbrook, and
NPC. He explained that the proposed project is in the discussion phase
and more research will need to be done before any formal presentation
will be brought before the Board.
Supervisor Whiting said that it is an intriguing idea and that he likes the
partnership that is being talked about, if it is within their role, authority,
and budget.
