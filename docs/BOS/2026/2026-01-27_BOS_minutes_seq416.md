Anna Parascando, appearing on Zoom greeted the Board, stating that she is a resident of Snowflake and will be addressing the tentative settlement with SBA Communications regarding the proposed cell tower at White Antelope and Concho Highway. She explained that her concern is about the wellbeing of the vulnerable individuals who have built a supportive haven in the area, reflecting that FCC guidelines have failed to address the proven effects of RF radiation that worsens electromagnetic hypersensitivity symptoms. She said that forcing the cell tower on the community would expose residents to constant RF radiation, potentially rendering homes uninhabitable and slashing property values. She asked the Board to let their vote reaffirm the county's commitment to its residents over corporate interests and reject the settlement.
Nina Beety, appearing on Zoom, greeted the Board and urged them to reject the settlement offer and let the court finish its expedited review. She said that she and other members of the public have researched telecommunications law and that there are limited grounds for a lawsuit by an applicant who disagrees with the county's decision. She referred to case law that she had previously sent to the Board that courts, including the Supreme Court, have denied damages and legal fees other than what is stipulated in telecommunications law. She stated that the community is protected by the Americans with Disabilities Act and the Fair Housing Amendments Act as well as Arizona Civil Rights laws and asked the Board to reject the settlement offer and let the court finish its expedited review.

Deb Schmeltzer thanked the Board for their support thus far regarding the SBA cell tower and strongly requested that the Board uphold their previous standings and turn down the settlement offer with SBA Communications to protect the vulnerable medically and legally disabled Multiple Chemical Sensitive (MCS) and Electromagnetic Hypersensitivity (EHS) individuals. She stated that she is one of the MCS-EHS individuals living in the community and that the proposed location of the cell tower would require everyone in the community to drive very close to the cell tower to access their mailboxes and the services in Snowflake. She stated that the radio frequency emitted by the cell tower would induce for her; impaired hearing, blurred vision, chest pain with heart palpitations, intestinal malfunction with nausea, vomiting, and migraines.  She explained that the community evolved by people building around each other to protect everyone from exposure to chemicals, herbicides, pesticides, and wireless technology and that their homes are constructed using non-toxic materials that shield from wireless technology. She explained that the community has been able to remain self-sufficient and functional. She cited that Navajo County regulations for cell tower facilities state that considering the public health, safety and welfare be considered in the sighting of wireless communication facilities and explained that this proposed location does not consider the welfare of the MCS-EHS community and should be turned down.
Elizabeth Kelley, appearing on Zoom, greeted the Board, explaining that she is the Director of the Electromagnetic Safety Alliance and has been working with the community for a few years. INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY:

a.

Consideration of hiring Convergint as the Integrator for the Navajo County Door and Camera project. They are currently under State Contract and can be used to fully plan, purchase, install, and support an integrated Door and Camera system.
Sam Vanderbeek greeted the board and introduced himself, saying that he currently works with Navajo County in the IT Department under Ken Dewitt.  He said that he would be outlining the proposal to establish Convergint Technology as the systems integration partner for surveillance, cameras, and door access systems. Mr. Vanderbeek explained that the county needs an integration partner because there are multiple different systems, aging hardware, and limited centralized support. He said that in order to move forward responsibly, the county needs a partner who can help modernize, standardize, and maintain these critical systems. He explained that Convergint is a nationally recognized leader in public sector security integration that brings extensive experience in detention, justice, and governmental environments and that they understand the operational realities of the county. Mr. Vanderbeek explained that if approved, Convergint will begin a comprehensive discovery and assessment phase during the first 100 days, including a full inventory of the existing surveillance and access control systems, a health and vulnerability assessment of current hardware and software, a review of network storage and retention requirements, direct engagement with elected officials, department directors, and IT leadership to prioritize upgrades, replacements, and risk mitigation. He also said one of the major advantages of working with Convergint is that they can help identify funding opportunities including federal public safety grants, emergency preparedness grants, and technology modernization programs to help reduce the financial burden and accelerate improvements using external funding sources.
Mr. Vanderbeek explained that the priority would be to focus on the two high-risk, high-impact areas of adult detention and juvenile probation first. He explained that Convergint has a support first philosophy, and before they recommend any new hardware, they will attempt to support legacy systems and maximize the value of what is already in place. Mr. Vanderbeek closed by saying that the long-term goal is a unified countywide security ecosystem that is centralized, scalable, better aligned with operational needs, and easier to maintain and that a partnership with Convergint gives a path towards a modern, reliable and sustainable security infrastructure across all county facilities.
Chairman Seymore asked what amount of the cost would be available through grants or other funding.
Mr. Vanderbeek said that it is unknown at this time.
Chairman Seymore asked if the project would be county wide.
Mr. Vanderbeek said that it would be a multiphase project that would last several fiscal years.
Supervisor Whiting made a motion  to approve hiring Convergint as the Integrator for the Navajo County Door and Camera project, motion seconded by  Supervisor Peshlakai.   Vote unanimous approving the motion.

PLANNING & DEVELOPMENT SERVICES:

a.

Consideration and adoption of  Resolution Number 01-2026 , approving/denying a Special Use Permit for the construction and operation of one meteorological monitoring tower (MET tower) on approximately .7479 acres of vacant land southwest of Joseph City.
A presentation was shown. Katherine Wright greeted the Board and presented SUP 25-004 for Winding Canyon Wind Met Tower.  She explained that the request is for a special use permit for the construction and operation of one meteorological monitoring tower on .7479 acres of vacant land 20 miles southwest of Joseph City at APN #111-07-003A Township 16N Range 17E, Sections 05, 07,09,17 of the Gila and Salt River meridian.  She said that the owner of the land is Aztec Land and Cattle Limited and that the agent is Kevin Gully.  She further explained that the project is on a 2761.22-acre parcel but will occupy .7479 acres that is zoned as A-General and that the tower will collect meteorological data for a future potential wind energy generation facility. Ms. Wright said that the tower will be 272 feet which includes a 1–2-meter lighting rod and is expected to operate up to 7 years.  She pointed out that the setbacks far exceed the one-to-one nonresidential setback and that the smallest is 1667.97 feet. She then showed a slide demonstrating examples of what the tower may look like.  She closed her presentation by stating that Navajo County Planning and Development recommends approval of the special use permit for Winding Canyon Wind Met Tower, subject to the 17 conditions listed in the staff report, adding that staff did receive an FFA determination of no hazard to air navigation.  She also added that there have been 25 emails against the SUP and 2 in support, as well as 10 voicemails against.
Supervisor Whiting asked how many of these towers have come in over the years and how many have moved forward.
Cody Cooper replied that over 20 years, approximately 20 towers have come into the county to explore and the only wind farms that currently exist in Navajo County are the Dry Lake windfarm north of Snowflake, a small portion of Chevelon Canyon windfarm which is primarily in Coconino County, and the West Camp windfarm which is in final construction, so only 3 have moved forward and in their research they have found that not quite half have been decommission by landowners or companies due to finding that the wind energy was not viable.
Supervisor Whiting reiterated that while it is part of the process to put up the MET towers, a significant number have not resulted in producing a windfarm.
Cody Cooper confirmed and added that one of the goals of Navajo County's Comprehensive Plan is to make sure that if renewable energy is to come in, that it is well researched before a project comes in and these towers provide the tools to find if there is true viability for one of these types of facilities.
Chairman Seymore asked if the fence would go around the guide wires as well as the tower and asked if it would be a chain link fence.
Katherine Wright confirmed that the fence will encompass the guide wires and that it will be a chain link fence.
Chairman Seymore asked if a condition could be added that the fence be cleaned on a regular basis so that debris does not gather.
Katherine Wright confirmed that she could add that as a condition.
Chairman Seymore recognized that there were a few comments on the matter and explained that speakers will have 3 minutes.
Kelly McCauley greeted the Board and said that he is one of the adjacent landowners, that his family has been there since the early 1900s and that they have no complaints about the tower being adjacent to their property.
Steven Brophy greeted the Board and stated that he is the President of Aztec Land and Cattle and that the proposed tower is on their land. He confirmed that if there is adequate wind as demonstrated by the data collected, it will be in furtherance of a project that will be presented to the Board if it achieves commercial viability and that it will involve Aztec's and McCauley's land. He stated that the project is in an area of a known wind resource and the project has the potential to give ranching families an intergenerational cash flow which will allow families to continue ranching beneath towers that may be built.
Francis Perkins greeted the Board on behalf of the McCauley Ranch and their Land and Cattle Company. She said that, along with her brothers and cousins, they are in support of the proposed Winding Canyon Wind Project, that there is no public land involved. They have had these lands since the early 1900s, and they have worked hard to take care of the land and will continue to do so, that they have thought long and hard and have decided to support the project that will hopefully continue to a profitable wind project that will bring good resources to the county and their family.
Chairman Seymore said that there were some well-written comments in support of and against the tower project and that many say give the land back to the cattle people and leave it like it is, he acknowledged that it is the cattle people who have come today in support of the project because they want to be good stewards, and so they can continue to keep their ranches.  He continued to reflect that the Earth was given to us, and we are accountable for it, that this generation has the ability to irretrievably change the land and is under tremendous pressure to unleash technology and reinvent our surroundings, growth and change are coming and that we are finding a balance of technologies with growth and that is being good stewards.
