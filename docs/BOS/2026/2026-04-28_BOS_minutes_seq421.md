CALL TO THE PUBLIC

Celia Laughlin addressed the Board and shared that for two years the citizens of Navajo County have shared their concerns regarding election administration and economic development. She stated that citizens have submitted affidavits and public records over the past two years outlining what they believe are significant issues, including chain of custody problems, unauthorized ballot processing, observer access concerns, and the denial of a citizen funded audit. She also cited a recent voter registration confirmation notice as evidence of inaccurate voter rolls in violation of ARS 16-1605 and 16-1606. She asked that the 7-step Corrective Action Plan provided to the Board be addressed on a future agenda. She additionally raised concerns about Real AZ, which she stated continued collecting funds after dissolution. She requested that the County support training of local students to serve as clerks who can help count votes and that County Attorney Brad Carlyon be placed on a future agenda to address questions regarding prosecution practices and election related legal matters. Ms. Laughlin concluded by stating that citizens are prepared to assist with lawful corrective actions.

Mike Montandon greeted the Board and stated that he serves as the Navajo County Assessor as well as a Precinct Committeeman in Navajo County. He explained that he participated in the process of selecting nominees to fill the vacancy created by the departure of David Marshall. Mr. Montandon noted that all three nominees are qualified and capable but expressed his support for Sylvia Allen. He further stated that the budgets were released the previous day and that budget discussions begin today. He emphasized that Ms. Allen is experienced and prepared to represent Navajo County immediately.

Cepand Alizadeh introduced himself as a representative of the Arizona Technology Council, located at 2800 North Central Avenue in Phoenix, Arizona. He expressed his support for Item 10a, the Development Agreement for the West Camp II Wind Farm. Mr. Alizadeh stated that a vote in favor of the agreement represents support for increased tax revenue, job creation, and economic progress in Navajo County. He concluded by noting that his organization hopes the Board will move forward with the development agreement.

Susan Clark thanked the Board for the opportunity to speak and stated that she and her family are opposed to Item 10a, the West Camp II development agreement. She explained that when they purchased their home on Hodges Lane in 2016, the nearest wind turbine was approximately five miles from their property. She stated that turbines are now located about two miles away, and if the proposed agreement is approved, the nearest turbine would be approximately one mile from their home. Ms. Clark stated that her family moved to the area for its desert views and pristine surroundings and that they accepted the presence of turbines at a five-mile distance. She expressed concern that the number and proximity of turbines have increased significantly and that they are now clearly visible both during the day and at night due to the red blinking lights. She also referenced concerns about vibrations and electromagnetic fields emitted from the turbines. She stated that at a meeting she attended, she was told these were not issues. However, she later received an email inviting her to become a partner in the project, offering monetary compensation in exchange for easements and a release of liability related to vibrations, electromagnetic fields, and land movement. Ms. Clark noted that these easements would remain in effect for the life of the project, up to 25 years, even if the property were sold.

Donna Wastchak provided the Board with a copy of the proposed corrective action plan referenced earlier by Celia Laughlin, along with a flyer regarding hand counting and related training. She stated that she has completed two classes and participated in a hand count at the state level during the Arizona GOP annual meeting. Ms. Wastchak noted that hand counting is a easy process and expressed her willingness to volunteer her time to assist anyone in the County who wishes to learn how to conduct a hand count. She further stated that she has spoken with Superintendent of Schools Rachael Lewis and reported that a donor has provided thousands of copies of the Arizona Constitution, the U.S. Constitution, and the Declaration of Independence for use by 4th and 12th grade students. She added that opportunities will be offered for 16-year-old students to work at the polls. Ms. Wastchak thanked the Board for their time and invited them to contact her with any questions.

Brian Reidhead introduced himself and greeted the Board. He stated that he owns a fixer upper property located at 122 Ranch Road in Taylor, Arizona. Mr. Reidhead reported that approximately $11,000 worth of materials had been stolen from the property. He stated that he contacted the Snowflake-Taylor Police Department and that the case was subsequently forwarded to the Navajo County Attorney's Office with a request for prosecution. Mr. Reidhead explained that he recently received a victim notification letter from the Navajo County Attorney's Office informing him that the office had declined to proceed with prosecution "in the interest of justice." He quoted the letter as stating, "although you may find this upsetting, it is important to remember that you have provided essential assistance in the prosecution of this matter; without the involvement of victims and witnesses the criminal justice system could not function." Mr. Reidhead asked what the criminal justice system is doing for him as a victim and urged those present to take action to help him obtain justice.

Chairman Seymore stated that the Board is working on some of the items mentioned in the Call to the Public and asked Mr. Reidhead to follow up on his issue with the County Attorney.

BOARD OF SUPERVISORS:

a.

Discussion and possible action regarding a Development Agreement with West Camp Wind Farm II, LLC for the development and operation of an up-to 500-megawatt wind energy facility in Navajo County.  Pursuant to A.R.S. §38-431.03(A)(3) the Board may convene in Executive Session  to receive legal advice from its attorney.
A presentation was shown

Bryan Layton greeted the Board and said that he would be sharing a proposed agreement for wind energy, stating the development proposal meets or exceeds the requirements of the ordinance, that it achieves meaningful performance to the Board's preferred criteria, provides partnership to mitigate community impacts, and although not popular it is a legal and lawful use of private property and that it is within the established zoning. Mr. Layton shared that Navajo County has abundant wind and solar resources, existing transmission infrastructure, interest from utilities, participating landowners, favorable topography, and that the state provides an 80% reduction in property tax for this type of project. Mr. Layton referred to the Comprehensive Plan, which is a vision statement and provides broad policy guidance on zoning and corridor plans, site plans, building plans, and certificates of occupancy. He shared that the Comprehensive Plan was recently updated following the state statues and a review of the zoning ordinance was completed with input from outreach and public hearings. As a result of public outreach, the new preferred criteria were adopted by the Board. Mr. Layton outlined the Proposed Development Agreement Framework; compliance with ordinance, preferred criteria, community benefit, and execute and repeat.

Cody Cooper reviewed the technical review of the project to ensure the proposed agreement met or exceeded all the standards of the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance. He noted that he project is for an up to 500 megawatt generation and 500 megawatt storage facility with an eight hour duration on batteries for the wind project and would utilize either a 25 mile gen-tie line to the existing Sitgreaves substation or a 10 mile gen-tie line within the project to the Cholla Sugarloaf Coronado transmission line via on an on-site switchyard and that both gen-tie lines were approved by the Arizona Corporation Commission as part of their Certificate of Environmental Compatibility. He stated that the project would encompass approximately 52,444 acres with the nearest part being located approximately 5 miles SW of Holbrook and would contain up to 144 wind turbine generators that could be between 633 and 774 feet. Mr. Cooper reported that the project's design meets or exceeds all applicable ordinance standards. He stated that required signage complies with the ordinance and that fencing will be installed around the substation, switching station, and potentially the operations and maintenance building. He noted that, pending FAA approval, the developer has agreed to install an aircraft detection lighting system along with down shielded, motion activated lighting. Mr. Cooper further reported that all outside agency reviews have been completed. The project has received a Determination of No Hazard to Air Navigation from the FAA and clearance from the Department of Defense Clearinghouse with no Notice of Presumed Risk. Road use and setback requirements have been reviewed, wildlife and environmental reviews are complete, and noise studies comply with ordinance standards and sound guidelines. He stated that reclamation and restoration assurances meet County requirements, including a bond to be renewed and updated every five years. Upon decommissioning, the developer has agreed to remove project components to a depth of 36 inches below grade. Mr. Cooper stated that for step one, through staff review Planning and Development has verified that the development agreement meets or exceeds the requirements of the Navajo County Zoning Ordinance. Mr. Cooper then explained step 2, performance criteria. He presented slides showing visual simulations of the before and after views from key observation points adding that the developer has agreed to increase the distance of the turbines from legal residences to one mile or greater rather than the required half mile. Mr. Cooper stated that the land has multiple existing uses, including ranching, hunting, and recreation, and that all of these uses would remain compatible with the proposed project. He explained that fencing would be installed only where necessary for human safety, and that the corridors will allow for the movement of migratory animals. He further noted that the developer has agreed to phase construction activities in coordination with the Arizona Game and Fish Department to reduce disturbance to fawning pronghorn and general wildlife movement. Mr. Cooper reported that the developer has received a letter of recommended conditions from the Arizona Game and Fish Department and has agreed to incorporate those conditions as applicable and feasible. Mr. Coper then went over the performance criteria for water, stating that the water will mostly be used for constructing the foundations of the wind turbines and that the applicant is proposing using an aggregate based road which does not require large amounts of water and that after construction water use will be minimal at the O&M building for sanitary uses. Mr. Cooper said that staff have verified that the development agreement achieves meaningful performance in multiple preferred criteria.

Chairman Seymore confirmed that there would be minimal water used and asked if the County could require them to use the aggregate based road material to help with dust control

Cody Cooper confirmed that water usage would be minimal and stated that he believed that the County could require that, however the developer doesn't plan on utilizing dust control, it was only included in the proposal in the event that the County requires dust control.

Supervisor Whiting asked for clarification on how values are determined for bonding.

Cody Cooper said that values are determined by a professional engineer licensed in the state of Arizona and they determine the total cost that would be anticipated upon decommissioning and subtract the value of any scrap components and then every 5 years the bond is updated as costs could increase and values could decrease.

Supervisor Whiting clarified that a 3rd party professional determines those figures every 5 years.

Cody Cooper confirmed and explained that the bond must be in place before shovel breaks ground.

Supervisor Whiting confirmed that the developer has agreed to one-mile setbacks.

Cody Cooper confirmed that there will be one-mile setbacks from legal residences and the developer has shown that with the turbine array corridors.

Chairman Seymore clarified that it would be for legal residences, which are permitted.

Cody Cooper said yes, permitted residences and stated that the Planning and Development Department had analyzed the area to find all permitted residences and passed that information on to the developer.

Vice Chairwoman Whitesinger asked if there was a quantity of reclaimed water or if it was a factor in the project.

Cody Cooper sated that there has been no reclaimed water proposed as part of the project, just the construction utilized water up front and then minimal to no water in the long term, stating that the applicant plans to use a private well that is on site and already drilled which is outside the authority of the County being that it is a private well permitted through the Arizona Department of Water Resources.

Bryan Layton explained the process, saying that the utility companies released RFP asking for the companies to come in, they design the project, meet with perspective landowners and then meet with County staff to perform a technical review which is what has been presented today. Mr. Layton then explained that a Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) must be in place before ground can be broken, additionally there must be a large generator interconnection agreement as well as the security bond. Mr. Layton then highlighted the tax that the project would generate and where the tax dollars would be spent, with a majority of it going to local school districts, he also pointed out that most tax revenue would come in at the start of the project and revenues would decrease over time due to depreciation. He also explained that the community impacts would be an increase in temporary workers which increases consumerism but places a strain on housing, code enforcement, public safety, infrastructure, and County finances. He explained that to combat that, the developer has proposed a partnership and will provide a one-time financial contribution of a $3.9 million community benefit and that the Board would determine how it is used. Mr. Layton then stated that the parcels of the project are not located within a fire district, but that the community benefit payment could aid the nearest fire districts. He then confirmed that most of the renewable projects in Navajo County are owner-operated and that the ordinances are geared more towards long term agreements and an owner-operated model. Mr. Layton concluded by stating that the security bond for the project would remain contractually obligated to the project even if it is sold. He further explained that the agreement meets obligations required by the ordinance and provides meaningful partnership to mitigate community impacts and concerns.
