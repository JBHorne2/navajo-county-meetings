CALL TO THE PUBLIC :
Michael Pitts addressed the Board and requested that the County impose an immediate moratorium on all new and pending utility-scale wind, solar, battery storage, and data-center projects under A.R.S. 11-833. He stated that the purpose of the moratorium would be to allow time to develop stronger and more protective regulations related to setbacks, structure heights, noise limits, visual impacts, decommissioning, road impacts, fire protection, environmental effects, and protections for neighboring landowners. Mr. Pitts expressed concern that recent Board actions, including Resolution 01-2025 and Resolution 17-2025, have weakened protections. He urged the Board to pause approvals, rescind those resolutions, restore prior standards, and strengthen them as needed before additional projects proceed. He emphasized that once such infrastructure is constructed, the impacts are long-term, and residents deserve assurance that regulations prioritize local communities.
Liz Olmstead from Show Low, addressed the Board and provided examples of other Arizona counties that have imposed moratoriums, bans, or restrictive zoning on large-scale renewable energy and data-center projects without being overturned in court. She referenced actions in Yavapai, Mohave, Cochise, Pima, and the City of Tucson involving restrictions on large-scale wind and solar development, data-center regulations, and potable-water use limitations. Ms. Olmstead urged the Board to stop encouraging such developers to locate projects in the region, stating that these developments negatively impact the local environment. She asked the Board to support the priorities of local constituents and consider the concerns being raised by residents.
Donna Wastchak addressed the Board and encouraged the County leadership to engage with the community to work together to explore alternatives to large-scale wind, solar, and data-center development. She emphasized the potential for ecotourism, dark-sky opportunities, and other economic options that highlight the area’s natural beauty and benefit a broader portion of the community. Ms. Wastchak expressed concerns about water use associated with proposed developments and noted the challenges faced by local cattle ranchers. She referenced A.R.S. 11 1101, stating her belief that it provides the Board with authority to ensure development aligns with the County’s comprehensive plan and protects community interests. She encouraged the Board to consider a round-table discussion to evaluate alternative economic pathways and noted that recent proposals appear to benefit only a limited number of landowners.

Presentation on A Home of My Own program
The County Website, Planning and Development Services page was shown.
Mischa Larish presented the Home of My Own program, created to support workforce housing in Navajo County. He explained that the County purchased six fully designed single-family home plans, which are now available at no cost for anyone building within the County’s jurisdiction. He said that the State mandate applies only to municipalities, but the County initiated the program too, due to local housing needs. Mr. Larish noted that the plans, which range from 576 to 1,613 square feet, include multiple elevation options and garage or carport choices. All plans have been reviewed and stamped by the County, and truss engineering has been started by Ballard Truss. This results in a minimum savings of $2,275 for applicants, as architectural plan and review fees are eliminated. Permits can be issued quickly once an applicant submits a simple option sheet. He added that the homes may also be used as accessory dwelling units under the County’s ADU ordinance. The program aligns with similar efforts statewide and was originally initiated through legislative work supported by Senator Sinema. Architect Mark Rogers provided the designs, allowing the County to make them freely available for public use.
Vice Chairwoman Whitesinger asked for clarification on where the program can be found on the website.
Mr. Larish provided the information.
Chairman Seymore asked if someone living in a municipality could use the plans.
Mr. Larish explained that the architect is currently working with every municipality in Navajo County.  He pointed out that the municipality of Snowflake may not have their program ready by the July 1st deadline, but that Navajo County Planning and Development Services has agreed to let them utilize the County’s plans until their program is established.

BOARD OF SUPERVISORS:

a.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and either a recommendation to the Department of Liquor Licenses & Control for approval or disapproval of the pending Liquor License Application from Theodore Van Gaines III (Agent) for Love’s Travel Stop #278 located at 4703 Main Street, Joseph City, AZ 86032, for a 010 Beer and Wine Store liquor license.
Amanda Silverhorn reported that a Series 010 Beer and Wine Store License application had been received from Theodore Von Gaines, agent for Love’s Travel Stop #278 located at 4703 Main Street in Joseph City. She stated that the Sheriff’s Office, Treasurer’s Office, Health Department, and Planning and Development had all responded that the application met their respective requirements and that the required 21-day posting period had also been completed. Ms. Silverhorn explained that staff conducted research into the history of the location. The truck stop was constructed in the 1997-1998 timeframe. Staff reviewed all Board of Supervisors agendas and minutes dating back to 1995 and found no record of any previous liquor license applications for this location.
She further stated that staff contacted the Arizona Department of Liquor Licenses and Control, which confirmed that it has no record of any prior applications for Love’s in Joseph City other than the current pending application. The Department noted that records for denied or surrendered licenses are retained for only ten years. Staff recommended that the Board consider the information presented when making its recommendation to the Arizona Department of Liquor Licenses and Control.
Chairman Seymore opened the meeting for public comments.
Karilyn Bushman provided a copy of a petition to the Clerk and addressed the Board. She stated that, upon further research, it came to her attention that Love’s #278 did not submit a completed liquor-license application in past years. She noted that the objections raised by the community in 1997-1998, and again at a later date, did not proceed because the applications were never completed.
Ms. Bushman stated that there had been visible public notice postings during prior attempts, and the community organized opposition to the proposed liquor license on those occasions. She further shared that Jean Varney has researched safety concerns related to the addition of a liquor license in the community and would be addressing those findings to the Board.  Ms. Bushman noted that a representative from the Joseph City School District was unable to attend, but that the district has concerns as the campus remains open, with the baseball field located less than a mile from the Love’s location and no sidewalks in the area. She explained that the school hosts summer sports programs and camps, including football and baseball, and one of the reasons outside groups choose this location is the absence of liquor sales in the community, which provides greater control and safety for visiting students. Ms. Bushman stated that multiple community members support maintaining a community without liquor sales and expressed opposition to the proposed liquor license. She also referenced the petition provided to the Board and stated that it includes concerns regarding the lack of 24/7 law enforcement presence in the area. Ms. Bushman concluded by urging the Board not to approve a liquor license in the community.
Jean Varney provided printed information to the Board.
Ms. Varney addressed the Board and stated she has lived in Joseph City for 10 years and had been a visitor to the community for 45 years prior to living there. She spoke regarding safety concerns related to the proposed liquor license. Ms. Varney reported that an average of approximately 3,225 vehicles use Exit 277, where Love’s is located, each day, totaling roughly 1.18 million vehicles annually. She noted that this represents many individuals who would have access to alcohol if it were made available at that location. She stated she initially believed the application was for a beer and wine bar, and while alcohol cannot be consumed inside the store, the parking lot is not considered part of the premises, creating an opportunity for consumption despite it being illegal.
She explained that nearly half of the vehicles are semi-trucks, with 58 parking spaces at Love’s that are fully occupied each night. Due to limited parking, many trucks park along roadsides, on-ramps, and off ramps as well. She expressed concern that travelers purchasing closed containers would be more likely to consume alcohol immediately rather than waiting hours to reach another destination. Ms. Varney stated she reviewed the Painted Desert Tribune’s archives for Sheriff’s Office reports from May 2011 through June 3, 2026, and identified 15 incidents directly related to alcohol, including DUIs, open container violations, and underage drinking. She added that many other incidents likely involved alcohol as a contributing factor. She further identified 21 violent crime incidents reported during the same period.
Ms. Varney allotted speaking time concluded.
Dan Bushman agreed to let Ms. Varney use some of his allotted time.
Ms. Varney referenced a Sheriff’s Office report for emergency and urgent priority calls in 2025, noting that 168 calls were recorded, with response times ranging from approximately 12 to 20 minutes. She stated that research shows legalizing on-premises alcohol sales in previously dry or restricted rural areas leads to a 10% to 25% increase in violent crime, with a single liquor license typically resulting in an additional 3.6 to 5 violent crimes per year. She expressed concern that this would increase demands on the Sheriff’s Office and heighten risk for residents.
Ms. Varney directed the Board to photos in the materials provided showing the proximity of the store to the community and the school, noting the absence of sidewalks and the frequency of pedestrian traffic. She stated that increased traffic and the availability of alcohol would create greater opportunity for accidents and unsafe situations along Main Street. She concluded by stating that the community would prefer not to have an alcohol license at all.
Dan Bushman addressed the Board and thanked them for the opportunity to speak. Mr. Bushman noted that he works for the school district and is heavily involved in his church. He reported that during his 10 years with the school district, and through his involvement in school sports, there have been multiple occasions where school staff have had to escort intoxicated individuals off school property. He commended the County Sheriff’s Office for its increased presence in recent years and noted their use of school facilities for training exercises. However, he stated he is concerned about response times.  Mr. Bushman further stated that law enforcement has also been needed to remove intoxicated individuals from other public spaces, including church buildings. He expressed concern about the pattern of intoxication related to these incidents and stated that, in his view, there is a clear correlation. He concluded by respectfully requesting that the Board deny the liquor license in Joseph City for the safety of the community’s children.
Dillard Atwell addressed the Board and stated that he is the General Manager of the Love’s Travel Center in Joseph City and was speaking on behalf of the company. He explained that Love’s is pursuing a liquor license at this time because, as the company continues to grow, it is expanding into states where it sees opportunities for business development. He noted that the Joseph City location is currently undergoing major renovation, including new concepts and improvements to the store and surrounding area. Mr. Atwell stated that Love’s has not had a liquor-license violation at any of its Arizona locations in more than 15 years. He shared that he previously managed a Love’s store with a liquor license and that they did not experience issues with alcohol-related incidents in the store or parking lot, and that that store was also near an open-campus school as well. He explained that Love’s conducts extensive training for employees on recognizing intoxication, underage sales, and restricted sales. He affirmed that staff are trained not to sell to individuals who appear intoxicated. He also noted that a nearby location, Jackrabbit, located four to five miles away, already sells alcohol, and drivers can easily stop there as well. Mr. Atwell stated that, based on his understanding, the store is more than 300 yards from any school activity area and therefore compliant with the distance requirements under Arizona law. He acknowledged concerns about extracurricular activities bringing students into the area but stated that if public intoxication already occurs, he does not believe selling beer at Love’s would contribute to issues involving individuals who may already be bringing alcohol from elsewhere. He concluded by stating that this represents Love’s position on the liquor license request.
Andrew Bushman addressed the Board and stated that, while he believes Love’s would comply with all laws and requirements regarding a liquor license, his concerns are directed at alcohol itself rather than at the business. He acknowledged that Jackrabbit, located approximately five miles away, already sells alcohol, but noted that the distance involved in traveling to and from that location creates a meaningful difference for residents compared to alcohol being available in their community. Mr. Bushman stated that he has no personal issues with individuals who choose to drink and noted that many of his friends do so. He described a recent incident while irrigating near the elementary school around 1:00 a.m., during which two vehicles were parked nearby with headlights on. He explained that the situation made him feel unsafe and considered whether he needed to be armed. He stated that the availability of additional alcohol in the community would not make him feel safer in such situations. He also explained that he owns 16 rental properties in Joseph City, including one located near the area of the proposed liquor license. He reported that the tenants of that property asked whether a fence could be installed if the license is approved, due to concerns that individuals could enter the unfenced backyard to seek shade or rest if intoxicated. Mr. Bushman stated that while he believes businesses have the right to operate as they choose, he is concerned about the additional burden that alcohol sales may place on Joseph City residents. He noted that the community has historically been dry, dating back before statehood, and has never faced the impacts of local alcohol sales. He also referenced concerns about property values and the safety of his renters. He concluded by thanking the Board for their time.
Chairman Seymore closed the Public Hearing.
Chairman Seymore explained that the Board does not approve or deny liquor licenses but makes a recommendation for approval or denial to Arizona Department of Liquor Licenses and Control.  He said that the Board’s responsibility is to look at what is presented and make their recommendation, stating that he is appreciative of the research that has been done and the presentations given.
Supervisor Whiting asked Mr. Carlyon if the Board’s responsibility was to evaluate whether the pending application meets the law.
Supervisor Peshlakai was offline at 10:16 a.m.
Supervisor Peshlakai was online at 10:19 a.m.
Brad Carlyon advised the Board that their role in the liquor-license process is to evaluate the potential impact on the community and make a recommendation to the State Liquor Board, which is the actual decision-making body. He stated that public opposition alone typically carries limited weight with the Liquor Board; however, objective facts demonstrating an adverse impact on the community may influence the Board’s determination. Mr. Carlyon stated that he was impressed with the members of the public at this hearing and at the prior hearing held two weeks earlier, noting that they presented objective data the Board could rely upon should it choose to recommend denial. He referred to a past example involving alcohol sales in the Sanders/Chambers area, where the community compiled data related to law-enforcement issues such as public intoxication and assaults, which helped support the denial of a license transfer. He explained that the Board may elevate community concerns by submitting the information received, including the petition provided, law-enforcement records referenced by one of the speakers, and any data gathered by the Sheriff’s Office, to the State Liquor Board along with its recommendation. He stated that if the Board’s majority believes a recommendation of denial is appropriate, it would be beneficial to include an analysis of the objective data to emphasize why the Board has those concerns.
Supervisor Whiting thanked Mr. Carlyon for the clarification and stated that it is important for the community to understand who ultimately makes the decision regarding the liquor-license application. He noted that regardless of the outcome of the Board’s discussion today, the community’s voice will need to continue before the State Liquor Board. He stated that he, personally, can assist in elevating those concerns to the appropriate body.
Vice Chairwoman Whitesinger stated that if the Board were to recommend denial of the liquor-license application to the Arizona State Liquor Board, one argument the Board could elevate is the community’s historical status as a dry town. She remarked that Joseph City may be one of the only dry communities in Arizona, which she described as historically significant. Vice Chairwoman Whitesinger stated that protecting that long-standing historical characteristic, spanning approximately 150 years, may be an important consideration. She emphasized that community identity and the values of the people who live there should be recognized as part of the discussion if the Board chooses to move in that direction.
Supervisor Peshlakai thanked the community members who submitted petitions and provided public comment regarding the liquor-license application. He stated that he appreciated the advocacy efforts demonstrated by residents. He then outlined additional concerns he believed were not previously addressed. Supervisor Peshlakai noted that Joseph City is connected to the southern portion of the Navajo Nation by a 13-mile county road (Territorial Road). He explained that the Navajo Nation prohibits alcohol sales, except for limited allowances at casinos located on fee lands. He stated that many tribal communities experience ongoing issues with alcohol-related harm, including DUI incidents and traffic accidents. He expressed concern that, due to the lack of law-enforcement presence on the Territorial Road, the availability of alcohol at the proposed location could create an unintended gateway for individuals transporting alcohol into the Navajo Nation, including potential bootlegging operations. He contrasted this with the high visibility of law enforcement along State Route 77, Navajo Route 6, and other monitored highways. Supervisor Peshlakai further noted that the proposed liquor outlet is located fewer than 15 miles from the Navajo Nation boundary, and that communities within that distance have long-faced disproportionate alcohol-related impacts. He stated that additional data, including tribal statistics, should be considered alongside information provided by the Sheriff’s Office and the Arizona Department of Public Safety. He reiterated his respect for the concerns raised by Joseph City residents and encouraged them to utilize their right under state statute to file a formal protest with the Arizona Department of Liquor Licenses and Control. He concluded by thanking the community for its participation and information.
Supervisor Benally stated that, after reviewing the photos, she noted the absence of sidewalks in the area, which means pedestrians must walk along the roadway. She commented that the proposed location appears to be very close to the school and expressed concern about public safety. She further noted that law enforcement staffing is limited, particularly in reservation communities, and agreed with Supervisor Peshlakai’s concerns regarding potential impacts on Navajo Nation residents. She stated that allowing alcohol sales at the proposed location could create an additional avenue for individuals from the reservation, where alcohol is prohibited, to obtain liquor. Supervisor Benally stated that liquor sales are generally pursued for profit and revenue but emphasized that if the local community does not want alcohol sold among them, it may not be appropriate to impose it upon them. She explained that while she has supported liquor-license approvals in the past, this was not because she favors widespread issuance, but because those proposals were for businesses managing their own operations. In this case, however, she stated that community members do not want alcohol in their area and should not be placed in a situation that increases unsafe conditions or crime. She expressed concern that some individuals may consume alcohol immediately upon exiting the store and entering their vehicles, despite laws prohibiting such behavior. She added that the absence of sidewalks further compounds safety risks, particularly for pedestrians. She concluded by stating that, for these reasons, she does not believe approval of the license would be a good idea.

Supervisor Whiting made a motion to decline the recommendation for approval of the liquor license application and directed staff to provide the documentation presented by the Joseph City community members to the Arizona Department of Liquor Licenses and Control. The motion further directed staff to include information regarding the historical nature of Joseph City as a dry community when submitting the materials to the Department and to provide Ms. Bushman with the contact information for the liquor board, motion seconded by  Supervisor Benally .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Jayson Vowell presented the Tentative Navajo County Budget for Fiscal Year 2026-27. He stated that significant work had been completed by staff, elected officials, and department directors through numerous meetings to review departmental needs and evaluate how to support operations from a fiscal perspective. He said this collaborative process resulted in the document before the Board.
Mr. Vowell highlighted the County’s budget priorities, including maintaining adequate financial reserves, forecasting healthy revenue streams, responsible stewardship of taxpayer resources, and presenting a structurally balanced budget where recurring revenues meet or exceed recurring expenditures. He noted that the Tentative Budget recommends no increase to primary or secondary property-tax rates. He also described efforts to align financial resources with operational needs across departments and to support investments in employees, infrastructure, and equipment. Mr. Vowell outlined several proposed capital improvements and investments, including roadway and bridge work, vehicle and equipment purchases, building upgrades, AI redaction software, vehicle-tracking software, and continued IT modernization efforts such as cloud migration and ADA-compliant website updates. He detailed new recommended positions for FY 27, including part-time positions in the Justice Courts, positions within the County Attorney’s Office and the Public Fiduciary, three and a half grant-funded positions in the Health District, and one position in the Benefits Trust Fund. He also outlined recommended employee pay adjustments, including a 3% cost-of-living adjustment, 2% performance pay, various market adjustments in specific job titles to stay competitive, and reinstatement of a $500 one-time payment in November. Mr. Vowell reviewed the summary of the County’s major fund types, noting total prior-year appropriations and the proposed FY 27 total budget of approximately $246.2 million. He highlighted property-tax components, secondary district levies, other key revenue sources, and the County’s maintained financial-stability reserve of approximately $11.3 million. He presented historical general-fund cash and fund-balance trends, noting improved financial health since FY 21 and increased alignment between cash and fund-balance levels. He also reviewed major general-fund revenue sources, including state-shared sales tax, county sales tax, and property tax, and discussed expenditure allocations by function such as public safety, criminal justice, mandated services, countywide services, administration, and capital projects. Mr. Vowell also reviewed budgets for the Health District, Flood Control District, Library District, Jail District, and Public Works, highlighting each fund’s revenue sources, expenditures, major projects, and grant activity. He noted that the Jail District continues to benefit from designated sales tax revenues and transfers required by statute, and he described expenditure categories such as personnel, medical services, supplies, and contingency. He reviewed the County’s outstanding debt, including the pension-obligation bonds issued in 2022 and refinancing bonds related to the jail upgrade and Public Works building. He explained that the pension-bond structure is projected to save the County approximately $11 million through FY 2038 compared to paying pension obligations entirely through payroll rates. Mr. Vowell then outlined major capital-project investments, including the Winslow Levee Project with a budget of approximately $31.1 million, fuel-system and fuel-tank upgrades, and a security-modernization project for County facilities and the jail. He concluded by noting that the public hearing for the Adopted Budget and the Truth-in-Taxation hearing will take place at the next Board meeting on Tuesday, June 23. He stated that he was available for Board questions or comments.

Presentation on Economic Development in Navajo County and the use of REAL AZ branding.
Item 7c. was presented before items 7a. and 7b.
A presentation was shown.
Bryan Layton presented an overview regarding the structure, authority, and current operations of the County’s regional economic development partnership. He explained that Arizona counties are authorized under A.R.S. 11 254 to use public resources for economic development purposes, including sharing information about local advantages such as natural resources, infrastructure, and workforce. He stated that collaboration among regional entities helps strengthen economic opportunities and attract businesses. Mr. Layton reviewed the history of the regional partnership, explaining that past leaders recognized the challenges of economic development when undertaken by a single entity. Over time, the partnership evolved into a nonprofit organization known first as the Real AZ. Corridor and later as Real AZ. He noted that while using an external nonprofit was at times advantageous, the governing body ultimately voted to dissolve the nonprofit. Following dissolution, participating entities, including Navajo County, returned to a regional partnership model operated within county government. He outlined that Navajo County has since handled all financial transactions for economic development as part of its regular operations and continued using the Real AZ branding due to its established recognition. He stated that the Board had received questions regarding the former nonprofit and the current economic development structure, prompting staff to complete an internal assessment as directed by the Board. Mr. Layton explained that the assessment identified three areas for clarification and improvement: First, the regional partnership is operating without a formal intergovernmental agreement. Staff will begin developing a formal agreement outlining roles, responsibilities, and governance among participating entities. Second, some meeting practices still reflect the former nonprofit model. Staff will discontinue those remaining elements and fully transition to a regional partnership format. Third, the Real AZ brand does not clearly differentiate the current partnership from the former nonprofit. Branding will be updated to clarify that the partnership is now an operational function of participating governments, not a separate legal entity. He emphasized that the regional partnership is not a public body, is not a regulatory authority, and has no independent legal status. Activities are carried out by county staff in their official capacities. Other participating entities may choose different staffing structures. Mr. Layton stated that the partnership supports economic opportunities in manufacturing, transportation, agriculture, forestry, tourism, healthcare, technology, traditional energy, and small business development. He clarified that the economic development department does not make land-use decisions, zoning determinations, permit approvals, or development agreements, as those remain within the jurisdiction of the appropriate local entity. He concluded by noting that the regional approach supports job creation, workforce development, infrastructure investment, business recruitment and retention, site readiness work, and regional marketing efforts, and that these outcomes would be more difficult for individual entities to achieve independently.
