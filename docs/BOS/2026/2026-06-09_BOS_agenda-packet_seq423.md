SUPERINTENDENT OF SCHOOLS: 
a. Presentation on Navajo County Superintendent of Schools Office & Education
Service Agency Service Programs Report
A presentation was shown.
Rachael Lewis explained that the Special Services Consortium consists of
school-level specialists who work directly with students requiring services
through their Individualized Education Programs (IEPs). The consortium
contracts with districts throughout Navajo and Apache Counties to provide these
services, offering a cost-sharing model that reduces expenses for districts while
ensuring students receive needed support. Services range from education
specialists to occupational therapists. She noted that 1,152 students are currently
receiving services. She reported that the number of virtual therapists remains
steady. While virtual services are not preferred, they remain a viable and
successful option in rural areas.
Ms. Lewis stated that as of March 31, the program budget is approximately $1.5
million, which includes salaries and operating expenses. Additional expenditures
are expected through June 30.   Ms. Lewis noted continued Alternative School
District designation, a successful Dropout Recovery Program in partnership with
Legacy Learning Community, and 16 graduates this year compared to three last
year. She highlighted strong progress in literacy initiatives, including LETRS
training with a 97 percent teacher pass rate. Additional updates included science
fair participation, new partnerships with NPC and the Navajo County Library
District, and ongoing efforts to unite local school leaders. She announced an
upcoming teacher conference scheduled for July 27–28 in partnership with
Apache County.
Chairman Seymore thanked Ms. Lewis for the report and expressed his
appreciation for her efforts to improve the schools.
Supervisor Peshlakai expressed appreciation to Ms. Lewis for her engagement
with the schools in district two, noting that her attendance at the Native Education
Summit was appreciated.

not to exceed $10,000 and that staff recommends approval.

1. DEFINITIONS  
1.1. Engaging Families and Young Adults Program: Will train, and coordinate placement of Family and Young 
Adult Advisors within designated County activities.  
1.2. Family Advisor: A Family Member, parent, grandparent, foster parent, aunt, uncle, adult sibling, adult cousin, or 
other adults, who are considered family by a child who has first -hand, lived experience with systems of care, for 
the purpose of having direct and meaningful input into the systems, policies, programs, and/or practices that 
impact care, health, well -being, and the lives of children, youth, and families, who are recruited, trained, and 
managed through the Engaging Families and Young Adults Program (EFYAP).   
1.3. Young Adult Advisor: Individuals ages eighteen to twenty -six (18-26), including those who have disabilities, 
ongoing healthcare considerations, and a variety of diversities, who are recruited, trained, and managed through 
the Engaging Families and Young Adults Program (EFYAP). 
2. BACKGROUND 
 
2.1. The vision of the Arizona Department of Health Services (ADHS) is "Health and Wellness for all Arizonans." The 
ADHS conducts a five (5) year statewide needs assessment, the Arizona State Health Assessment (SHA), to 6-
examine key health indicators and provide a comprehensive overview of the health of Arizonans. Annual updates 
to the SHA are also published. ADHS published the Arizona State Health Assessment: 2022 Annual Update
, 
which utilizes an evidence-based public health approach to improve the health and wellness of Arizona residents. 
Findings from the SHA inform the priorities outlined in the 2021-2025 Arizona Health Improvement Plan (AzHlP)  
and guide programming within ADHS, including that under the Title V Maternal and Child Health Services Block 
Grant (hereafter Title V MCH Block Grant) and the Preventive Health and Health Services Block Grant. The AzHlP 
creates a roadmap to improve the health of Arizonans over the next five (5) years through the development of 
partnerships and resources to work collectively on shared health improvement goals and strategies. The Title V 
MCH Block Grant places a high emphasis on engaging with families, young adults, and communities in MCH 
programs funded by Title V Funds. This includes building capacity between counties, family/young adult advisors, 
and local communities to partner in decision- making regarding Title V Programs to help achieve the identified 
MCH priorities. 
 
2.2. The mission of the Bureau of Women’s and Children’s Health (BWCH) is to “strengthen the family and community 
by promoting and improving the health status of women, infants, and children.” The BWCH administers the 
federally funded Title V MCH Block Grant and Preventive Health and Health Services Block Grant, in addition to 
other federally funded, private, and state-supported programs. 
2.3. This IGA was initiated in July 2020 and originally only included strategies and activities funded through the Title 
V MCH Block Grant. In July 2022, strategies and activities funded through the Preventive Health and Health

Board of Supervisors Regular 7. c.        
Meeting Date: 06/09/2026  
Title: Presentation on Economic Development
Submitted By: Leah Thomas, Clerk of the Board of Supervisors
Department: Administrative Services
Presented By: Bryan Layton
Subject:
Presentation on Economic Development in Navajo County and the use of REAL AZ branding.
Background: 
Navajo County advances economic development through a regional partnership in northeastern Arizona
with other local government entities.  This collaboration was originally established as the “REAL AZ
Corridor—Resources, Energy, Access, & Lifestyle.” Navajo County served as the fiscal agent and
primary driver of the partnership by providing funding, staffing, office space, and other administrative
support.
The partnership later created a nonprofit structure to expand flexibility, with the name shortened to
“REAL AZ.” This nonprofit was ultimately discontinued. Navajo County and the other local government
