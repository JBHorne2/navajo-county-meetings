Title: Renewal: Special Use Permit LAK101663 for the Pinedale Cemetery
Submitted For: Bill Bess, County Engineer/Assistant Public Works Director 
Submitted By: Bryan Cook, Sr. Engineering Project Manager II
Department: Public Works
Motion before the Board:
Renewal of Special Use Permit LAK101663 for the continued authorized use and occupation of
the National Forest System lands in the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest of 2.5 acres for the
Pinedale Community Cemetery, access road, and parking area for the next 20 years
Background:
Pinedale Cemetery is located within the Apache Sitgreaves National Forest and has been allowed
to be used as a cemetery. To continue to allow this use, it was necessary that Navajo County, a
Public Agency, work with the National Forest and be in control of the leased land and through
Navajo County extend agreements to Pinedale Cemetery for the continued use. 
Navajo County is in the process of transitioning the land from the Forest Service to Pinedale
Cemetery through a program known as the Townsite Act. This renewal will allow for more time
for the Townsite Act process to continue and allow the Pinedale Cemetery to continue to operate
unaffected under a Special Use Permit.

Fiscal Year: 2019

Fiscal Impact:
No fiscal impact is expected. Apache-Sitgreaves Permit is issuing the Special Use Permit
LAK101663 with no associated fee.

ORDINANCE NO. __-19 
 
NAVAJO COUNTY FIREWORKS ORDINANCE 
An ordinance of the Board of Supervisors of Navajo County, Arizona, adopting ordinance 
No. __-19 (Navajo County Fireworks Ordinance); prohibiting the use of “permissible consumer 
fireworks” in the unincorporated areas of Navajo County during times when there is a reasonable 
risk of wildfires; establishing a permitting process for public fireworks displays utilizing “display 
fireworks”; and specifying criminal penalties for the violation of this ordinance. 
Upon the Adoption of this Ordinance, Ordinance 01-17 is hereby repealed. 
1.)  TITLE 
This ordinance shall be known as the Navajo County Fireworks Ordinance. 
2.)  EFFECTIVE AREA 
 This ordinance shall be effective in the unincorporated areas of Navajo County, 
 excluding areas under the jurisdiction of the United States, the State of Arizona, or a 
 federally recognized Indian tribe. 
3.)  AUTHORITY/PURPOSE 
This ordinance is established in accordance with A.R.S. § 36 -1606 which authorizes the 
county to regulate the use of “permissible consumer fireworks” within the unincorporated 
areas of the county during times when there is a reasonable risk of wildfire s in the 
immediate county.  Additionally, the County is required by A.R.S. § 36-1603 to implement 
a permitting process for “display fireworks” utilized in public fireworks displays. 
 The purpose of this statute is to reduce and minimize  the risk of fire da ngers, 
 including structural or wildland fires,  associated with the use of fireworks in Navajo 
 County, and to help ensure the safety of Navajo County residents attending public 
 fireworks displays in unincorporated areas of the County.

4.)  DEFINITIONS 
 For the purposes of this ordinance, the terms "consumer firework," "display firework," 
 "fireworks," and "permissible consumer fireworks" shall have the same meanings as set 
 forth in A.R.S. § 36-1601. 
5.)  AUTHORITY OF EMERGENCY MANAGEMENT DIRECTOR 
 The Navajo County Emergency Management Director has the authority under Navajo 
County’s current “Outdoor Fire Ordinance” to establish restrictions on outdoor fires and 
emergency fire restrictions.  Under this ordinance, the Emergency  Management Director 
will additionally have the authority to restrict the use of "permissible consumer fireworks.”   
6.)  PROHIBITION ON USE OF FIREWORKS 
  Except as specifically allowed in this ordinance, it shall be unlawful for any person to use 
 fireworks, including consumer fireworks or display fireworks, in the unincorporated areas 
 of Navajo County. 
7.)  USE OF PERMISSIBLE CONSUMER FIREWORKS 
 The use of permissible consumer fireworks shall be permitted within the unincorporated 
 areas of Navajo County except for such time s and in such areas where the Emergency 
 Management Director has established restrictions on outdoor fires or emergency fire 
 restrictions pursuant to current Outdoor Fire Ordinance. 
 The term "permissible consumer fireworks" is specificall y limited to the definition set 
 forth in A.R.S. § 36-1601(5), and includes: 
  A.)  Ground and handheld sparkling devices; 
  B.)  Cylindrical fountains; 
  C.)  Cone fountains; 
  D.)  Illuminating torches; 
  E.)  Wheels; 
  F.)  Ground spinners; 
  G.)  Flitter sparklers; 
  H.)  Toy smoke devices; 
  I.)  Wire sparklers or dipped sticks; 
  J.)  Multiple tube fireworks devices and pyrotechnic articles. 
 
 Permissible consumer fireworks do not include anything that is designed or intended to 
 rise into the air and  explode or to detonate in the air or to fly above the ground, 
 including, for example, firework items commonly known as bottle rockets, sky rockets, 
 missile-type rockets, helicopters, torpedoes, roman candles and jumping sticks.

8.)   DISPLAY FIREWORKS 
Any person, entity, or organization may apply to the Navajo County Board of 
Supervisors for a permit for the use of  display fireworks and other fireworks as part of a 
public fireworks display.  Any use of display fireworks  or fireworks not included within 
the definition of permissible consumer fireworks without a permit is prohibited. 
 A.)  Applications 
Applications to conduct a public display of fireworks using display fireworks shall 
be submitted to the Clerk of the Board of Supervisors  on a form prescribed by the Board 
of Supervisors not more than one-hundred twenty days (120), but not less than sixty (6 0) 
days, prior to the date of the proposed display.   
 B.)  Bond 
The Application submitted to the Clerk of the Board  must be accompanied by a 
statement that the Applicant is willing to provide a bond in an amount not less than $500.00, 
or in such other amount as deemed appropriate by the Board of Supervisors not to exceed 
one-million ($1,000,000) dollars.  Alternatively, an applicant may provide proof of liability 
insurance in a similar amount along with a letter from the issuing insurance company 
indicating that the applicant's insurance will cover the applicant  for liability connected to 
this event.  Failure of an applicant to submit a bond or proof of insurance to the Clerk of 
the Board of Supervisors prior to the time of the event shall result in the  automatic 
nullification of the permit. 
 C.)  Application Requirements 
  Applications must be accompanied by a map of the display area, which designates 
 the proposed seating area for spectators, nearby structures, parking areas, public roads, 
 the fallout zone, overhead lines, and fire department access to the area. 
 D.)  Review of Applications 
After receipt of the Application by the Clerk of the Board of Supervisors,  all 
applications for public fireworks displays shall be reviewed by the Navajo  County Sheriff 
pursuant to A.R.S.36-1603, who shall visit and inspect the display area. The Sheriff (or his 
designee) shall meet with a Fireworks Coordination Group comprised of the following: 
Sheriff, local fire chief, county emergency manager, facility/property owner, a local 
pyrotechnic technician, a local business owner and Chamber of Commerce president (or 
their designee), to obtain input suggestion on the application.

 The Sheriff shall either recommend that the Board of Supervisors  approve or 
disapprove the application.  The Sheriff may recommend approval if it is determined that: 
  a.)  This display will be supervised by a competent pyrotechnic operator.  In  
  determining whether that qualification has been met, the Sheriff shall consider the 
  operator's qualifications, experience, training, and/or certification; 
  b.)  It appears that the fireworks display will be conducted in a safe location that  
  does not create an unreasonable risk of fire danger; 
  c.)  That the display will be discharged and fired so that it will not be hazardous to 
  property or persons, and that adequate fallout zones have been designated that are  
  a sufficient distance from the viewing area; 
  d.)  If the display is not within the boundaries of an approved fire district, the  
  evidence of the availability of services from an adjacent fire district or department 
  has been provided.  It shall be the responsibility of the Applicant to obtain a letter  
  of approval from the fire district or department. 
  e.)  The manner and storage of the fireworks appears to be safe. 
  f.)  The organization has provided a sufficient bond or proof of liability   
  insurance. 
For purposes of conducting reviews of applications, the Sheriff's Office and/or the fire 
district shall retain at  least one person on staff who has received training i n pyrotechnic 
displays and is familiar with the requirements and standards for public fireworks displays 
set forth by the National Fire Protection Association, including Section 1123, Code for 
Fireworks Displays. 
 E.)  Decision on Application 
The Sheriff s hall complete the  investigation within 30 days of receipt of  the 
application.  Upon completion of the investigation, the Sheriff shall submit a written 
recommendation to the Board of Supervisors that either 1) recommends that the Board of 
Supervisors grant the Application and approve the plan as submitted by the Applicant, 2 ) 
recommends that the Board of Supervisors deny the Application, or 3 ) recommends that 
the Board of Supervisors approve the Application if the Applicant will agree to 
modifications of the plan or special conditions upon the permit. If the Board of Supervisor 
approves the application and places special conditions upon the permit, the applicant shall 
comply with all special conditions placed upon the permit at least forty-eight hours before 
the scheduled public fireworks display. If every special condition placed upon the permit 
is not met before the forty-eight (48) hour deadline, the permit is null and void.

All permits for public fireworks display issued by the Board of Supervisors shall

c.)  That the Applicant has provided the County proof of a bond or insurance in the

 G.)  Transfer/Assignability 
After a permit is granted, the sale, possession, use and distribution of fireworks for 
the dis play shall be lawful for that purpose only.  Permits issued by the Board of 
Supervisors pursuant to this Ordinance shall not be transferrable or assignable. 
H.)   Validity of Permit 
Permits issued pursuant to this section and prior to any fire restrictions being 
enacted, remain valid pursuant to the following provisions. If any emergency fire 
restriction are ordered or in effect, pursuant to the Navajo County Outdoor Fire Ordinance, 
the Sheriff (or his designee) shall meet with the Fireworks Coordination Group to  obtain 
their input and expertise, to address any public safety concerns  regarding the public 
fireworks display. The Sheriff (or his designee) may withdraw his approval of the permit

believes the public firework display could constitute a hazard to property or endanger any 
person. If the Sheriff (or his designee) withdraws his approval of the permit and 
application, pursuant to A.R.S. § 36-1603, the permit is revoked.   
 The Navajo County Board of Supervisors reserves the right to revoke any permit

Motion before the Board:
Consideration of approving the acceptance of School House Lane into Navajo County 
Maintained Road System.
Background:
Navajo County Public Works has worked with the Linden Elementary School and residents along
School House Lane to obtain the required public roadway easements which allows a roadway that
is in good condition to enter our maintenance system.  Now that the easement has been obtaine,
Public Works can provide road maintenance services in support of safe school operations   in the
form of maintenance and snow removal activities adjacent to the school and bus stops.
Now that School House Lane has sufficient easement, public necessity, and is already in a
condition that does not require the county to make improvements, the road qualifies for County
Maintenance.
School House Lane will enter our system as two segments. The first segment is paved for
approximately 0.1 miles and is identified as the portion of School House Road adjacent to Linden
Elementary School (between State Route 260 and the cattle guard) on School House Road. The
second segment will be graded for 0.38 miles commencing from the cattle guard to the north.
Recommendation: 
Staff recommends the approval to accept the described section of School House Lane into the
County Road Maintenance System.
