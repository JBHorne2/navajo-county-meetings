ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES :

a.

Annual Update from Northland Pioneer College :  Betsy Wilson, Executive Director of Northland Pioneer College (NPC) Friends and Family, stated that NPC is launching a new initiative to come to community entities and provide regular updates.  She reviewed a PowerPoint presentation.  She stated that NPC is a regionally accredited, publicly supported, comprehensive community college.  She spoke about the importance of being an accredited institution.  She stated that the Navajo County Community College District is the governing body.  She indicated that NPC is the second largest community college district in the nation and was established in 1972.  She spoke about each of the four campuses and five Center locations.  She stated that NPC serves 7,000 students annually, and anyone can take classes at NPC.  She spoke about dual enrollment and Northeastern Vocational Institute of Technology (NAVIT), which allow students to take classes both towards a high school diploma and college degree while still in high school.  She further spoke about Technology to Advance Learning Outcomes at Northland (TALON), which allows them to put an NPC instructor into any number of high school classes throughout the district without them having to travel to that high school.   She stated that 50% of high school students who take at least 7 college credits are more likely to complete a college program.  She spoke about those credits which are transferrable to the three Arizona state universities.   She stated that they also offer courses for GED and college prep.  She further spoke about STEM, STEAM, non-credit classes, online classes and teacher certification classes.  She stated that NPC is a great choice because community college instructors are there to teach.

Mark Best, President of NPC, spoke about his background.  He stated that he began working at the college in 2001 and has been president for about 8 months.  He spoke about capital projects in which the college has been engaged for about 20 years.   He further spoke about the funding for community colleges in Arizona.  He indicated that there is no capital funding for ongoing building maintenance or technology and they are therefore expected to shift money from the operational budget.  He stated that in the early 2000's, the District Governing Board did not want to subject taxpayers to a secondary tax in the future, so, if the college was going to do any construction of facilities, they would only do it if money was available in the building fund.  He indicated that the last major piece of the ongoing capital plan is a number of projects at the Show Low campus, which includes reconstruction and stabilization long term of the learning center; expanding the Science and Allied Health building; and move the automotive, welding and metal arts onto the campus.  He stated that they have a waiting list for students in the welding and nursing programs.  He indicated that the Board has authorized $20 million in the building fund, to apply to these three projects.  He stated that they would like to come back in the summer for an annual report.  Supervisor Thompson inquired as to who the current board members are.  Mr. Best stated that the current governing board members are Derrick Leslie, Daniel Peaches, Frank Lucero, Elias Jouen and James Matteson.  Supervisor Williams thanked Mr. Best and Ms. Wilson for speaking today.  He inquired regarding the time table for the projects at the Show Low campus.  Mr. Best stated that they are not sure yet, but they will break the project into discreet chunks over a 3- to 5-year timeline.  He indicated that he does not know if the project will take the entire $20 million amount.

b.

Budget Presentations

a.
County Attorney's Office - Brad Carlyon :  Bryan Layton stated that at the next meeting on March 12th, we will have a public hearing regarding the implementation of a Jail District.  Brad Carlyon gave a budget presentation.  He stated that we are in a crisis and will still be in a crisis even if the Jail District passes.  He indicated that the County Attorney's office is down to 42 employees from 54 employees in 2009.  He spoke about Victim Services and indicated that it has gone from 2 employees in 2009 to 9 employees through grant funding.  He stated that they are now able to have an advocate in every courtroom, including for misdemeanors.  He indicated that they also have 2 crisis advocates who respond 24/7 to crime scenes for major felonies and a volunteer coordinator who is developing a volunteer base of advocates.  He stated that they have developed a Domestic Violence Sexual Assault Coalition and they coordinate a Child Sexual Assault and Abuse multiple discipline team to go over cases and see how they can do better.  He indicated that they are developing an Adult Sexual Assault Response Team and are developing law enforcement protocols.  He stated that in the past calendar year, they provided 9500 victim services.  He indicated that ony the salary of the Director and half of 2 victim advocates comes out of the general fund, and the rest is grant funding.

He stated that in 2009, his office had 17 prosecutors with 1,300 felonies and 2,001 misdemeanors; but in 2019, his office has 14 budgeted prosecutors, but 12 actual prosecutors handling 1,565 felonies and 2,823 misdemeanors.   He indicated that that equals a 41% decrease in prosecutors, including vacancies, with felony cases going up 20% and misdemeanors increasing by 41%.   He spoke about case load distribution.  He stated that it takes 30% of a trial attorney's time to cover Justice Court.  He stated that his 9 trial attorneys have between 80-90 open felony cases, not including those 3 attorneys with misdemeanors.  He indicated that one attorney has resigned, and his cases had to be spread out, so those same attorneys are now covering 90-100 cases while the state average of felony case loads is 50.   He stated that attorney salaries should increase with experience.   He spoke about the disparity between the starting salary for an attorney with Navajo County and those starting with Greenlee County, La Paz County, Coconino County and Gila County and indicated that attorneys in these other counties do not have the 90-100 felony case loads.  He further spoke about the disparity between the pay of deputy public/legal defenders with little experience who make more than prosecutors with more criminal experience.  He stated that the criminal defense contract pays $80,000, plus those attorneys can take private pay clients.  He spoke about the difficulty of filling vacancies due to the pay and case load.  He spoke about what would happen if he loses another prosecutor.  He stated that he is trying to reduce case loads by offering more diversions and declining more cases.  He indicated that we are not being paid the actual cost for municipal misdemeanor contracts, and he does not have the resources to do them anymore.

He stated that for the FY20 Budget, he will give up one of the current vacancies; and for FY21, if there is no jail district, he will have to let go of 2 more attorneys and 1 legal assistant.  He indicated that the legal assistants are more paralegals, and the window clerk at the Clerk of the Court gets paid more than his legal assistants.  He stated that we will have to cancel municipal misdemeanor contracts, discontinue Drug Court and Veteran's Court.  He indicated that he will not be able to charge county misdemeanors, including DUI and domestic violence cases nor many Class 4, 5, and 6 felonies.  He stated that the civil attorneys will have to assist with criminal cases.  He indicated that passing the Jail District does not avert the crisis that is occurring in prosecution and criminal justice because we will still not be able to replace anyone who leaves due to the overwhelming case loads.   He stated that what is needed is competitive salaries for prosecutors, public defenders and law enforcement and additional prosecutors and public defenders to reduce case loads.

Supervisor Thompson stated that municipalities could step up to cover the cost of prosecuting misdemeanors, and Mr. Carlyon stated that even if they were paid our actual cost, he does not have the people to cover them.  He indicated that he has taken on 50 felony cases, all of the juvenile cases, financial fraud cases and Holbrook Justice Court to reduce the burden on his prosecutors.
Supervisor Williams thanked Mr. Carlyon for the presentation.  He inquired regarding the average salary of criminal prosecutors and civil attorneys in his office, and Mr. Carlyon provided
Supervisor Williams with a document showing that average.
Supervisor Williams inquired how Navajo County's average salary compares to other counties', and Mr. Carlyon stated that every year AACO does a salary and benefit survey, and it's hard to do an exact comparison because it's hard to tell within classifications how much experience they have.   Mr. Carlyon stated that he has no issue with the salaries the deputy public/legal defenders are receiving, but the prosecutors need to be brought up to that, and he looks forward to continuing the conversation.
Supervisor Thompson inquired how increasing case loads and less prosecutors will that impact the public, and Mr. Carlyon stated that so far, it hasn't.  He stated that the prosecutors love the community, love their work and are doing all they can to get justice; but with the last attorney leaving, he is not sure how it's going to impact.  He indicated that the focus will have to be on really serious cases.
Supervisor Thompson thanked Mr. Carlyon for the presentation.
Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked Mr. Carlyon for his presentation.  She spoke about the impacts to the public and stated that she can see that there will be significant impacts.   Mr. Carlyon stated that it is not sustainable for the attorneys to have ever-increasing case loads.  He indicated that part of the philosophy of the criminal justice system has been changed to treatment; and if we are not charging lower level felonies, we are not getting the treatment to these people, and crime will escalate.
