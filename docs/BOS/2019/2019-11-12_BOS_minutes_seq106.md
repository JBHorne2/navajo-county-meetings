PUBLIC WORKS :

a.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 28-19 approving/denying a Special Use Permit allowing for the construction of an approximately 3,000 acre, 400 MW solar energy generation and storage facility on parcels that are zoned RU-20 or are State Land, APN 107-01-023, 107-05-031C & D, & 110-15-001D, portions of Township 18 North, Range 18 East, Section 36; Township 18 North, Range 19 East, Sections 30, 31, & 32; and Township 17 North, Range 19 East, Section 5 of the Gila and Salt River Meridian in the Joseph City area :  Sandra Phillips discussed the item. She indicated that part of the project is State land.  She stated that the property is currently vacant and is about 3,000 acres, about 300 megawatts, has panel heights of 10 feet, and anticipation of a potential storage site which will tie into the existing grid. She showed a brief Power Point presentation of what the project could look like. She indicated that there is a concern about the drought that we have been through, the sensitivity of the ground and dust factors which have been worked out with the applicant.  She indicated that there are a lot of stipulations to which the applicant has reviewed and agreed.  She stated that Planning and Zoning Commission recommended approval to the Board.  Quinten Stewart, an analyst at Invenergy, addressed the Board regarding the project. He spoke about the project in relationship to Joseph City and Holbrook. He stated that landowners for this project are Aztec Land Company and State of Arizona and point of interconnection would be to the Cholla Power Plant. He indicated that there will be 200 to 400 jobs created during construction, as well as local economic activity, and there will be 3-5 full time jobs once the project is complete.  He stated that this will add new property tax revenue for Navajo County and that the project will have minimal impact on local infrastructure and services.  He indicated that during construction, they will make sure the roads are maintained and have worked with Rusty Despain, as well as the County, to determine the best way to incorporate this project into the Joseph City Fire District.  He stated they are submitting their storm water pollution prevention plan soon to start to submit their revegetation restoration plan.  He indicated that they have completed all of their environment due diligence on this site and that they are committed to responsible development. He indicated that they are currently submitting a bid to Arizona Public Service (APS) for this project and if selected, their estimated commercial operations date would be the end of 2021.

Chairwoman Whitesinger opened the public hearing.  Former Supervisor JR DeSpain addressed the Board, indicating that he leases his land from Aztec and will lose some farming property. He stated that this will reduce his herd by a third which will reduce his income. He requested that this company's feet be held to the fire, so that they live up to what they are asked to do and leave this land the way they found it.  He stated that he will reduce his herds and work with the company to make sure the cattle do not disturb the operation.  He indicated that they have been good stewards of the land. He thanked the Board for what they do. Supervisor Williams asked about the size of the land Mr. Despain's family is using, and Mr. DeSpain indicated that they have about 15,000 acres, some of which is Aztec's and some of which is APS's. Supervisor Thompson thanked Mr. DeSpain for his heartfelt comments. Supervisor Jack expressed his appreciation to Mr. DeSpain for stating his concerns regarding the land and his family. Vice-Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. DeSpain for his service and for his partnership with the applicant and working with staff towards an agreement that everyone can work with. Mr. DeSpain thanked County staff. Chairwoman Whitesinger closed the public hearing.  Supervisor Williams asked about the roads and what happens if the project is no longer, and Ms. Phillips stated that they have worked with the applicant and landowners regarding a decommissioning plan to make sure the land is restored and there are safeguards and bonds in place.  Susan Eness with Invenergy indicated that they do have a requirement to hold a decommissioning bond that will be equal to the cost to remove the equipment from the property.  Vice-Chairman Whiting made a motion to approve a Special Use Permit allowing for the construction of an approximately 3,000 acre, 400 MW solar energy generation and storage facility on parcels that are zoned RU-20 or are State Land, APN 107-01-023, 107-05-031C & D, & 110-15-001D, portions of Township 18 North, Range 18 East, Section 36; Township 18 North, Range 19 East, Sections 30, 31, & 32; and Township 17 North, Range 19 East, Section 5 of the Gila and Salt River Meridian in the Joseph City area;  motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Presentation of a Medal of Valor to three Navajo County Sheriffs Office deputies : Sheriff Clouse expressed gratitude for the opportunity to recognize two deputies from the Navajo County Sheriff's Department and a deputy from the U.S. Marshals Service who responded to a call in Winslow on October 10th and put their lives in jeopardy to save the lives of others. He introduced David Gonzales, U.S. Marshal from the District of Arizona; Deputy Van Bailiff from the U.S. Marshals; and Mike Cifu, U.S. Marshals Area Supervisor out of Flagstaff.  He further introduced Deputy Traistaru, U.S. Marshals; Lt. Scott Peterson, Navajo County Sheriff's Office; and Sgt. Wayne Lesperance, Navajo County Sheriff's Office and spoke about each of the officers. He spoke about the wanted fugitive in question, the concerns regarding the fugitive's erratic and potentially dangerous behaviors and Lt. Peterson's desire to find a safe way to bring this fugitive into custody.  He indicated that there was information that this fugitive was located at a residence in Winslow, and Lt. Peterson reached out to the U.S. Marshals for assistance.  He spoke about the danger in apprehending the fugitive and how these three officers helped three children and their babysitter to safety while the suspect who was armed and dangerous was gaining entrance into the residence.  He stated that these officers went above and beyond for the citizens they serve while putting themselves in danger and presented each of them with a Medal of Valor.  David Gonzales addressed the Board.  The Board of Supervisors thanked the deputies for the work that they do.
