ONE OR MORE SUPERVISORS MAY ATTEND VIA VIDEO AND/OR TELEPHONICALLY 
The Navajo County Board of Supervisors will hold a meeting open to the public on Tuesday, April 23, 2019, in the Supervisors’
Chamber in the Navajo County Governmental Complex, Holbrook, Arizona. The Board may vote to meet in a closed executive
session to discuss certain matters as indicated on the following agenda. The Board may meet in executive session pursuant to
A.R.S. § 38-431.03(A) (3) for legal advice on any item.
Persons with disabilities who need accommodation to attend or participate in the meeting may contact the Clerk of the Board’s office
at (928) 524-4053; at least 48 hours prior to the meeting so an accommodation can be arranged.
Items on the agenda may be considered out of order at the Chairman’s discretion. A copy of the agenda background material
provided to the Board, with the exception of items to be considered in executive session, is available for public inspection at Navajo
County website, www.navajocountyaz.gov

Dear Chairman Burns, Commissioners Tobin, Dunn, Kennedy and Olson, 
 
Since the 2002 Rodeo Chediski fire burned over half a million acres on the Mogollon Rim and 
destroyed almost 400 homes and structures, Navajo County has remained at the epicenter of the 
risk of complete communities’ destruction in catastrophic landscape scale forest fire in Northern 
Arizona. 
 
It is not lost on the residents of the County that the town of Paradise CA that was incinerated in 
the recent Camp Fire looked strikingly similar to the town of Pinetop-Lakeside, and it is but a 
further reminder that at any time forested communities in Navajo County can suffer the same 
devastation visited upon Paradise CA by the Camp Fire, which took 88 lives, destroyed 18,804 
structures and cost $16.5 billion. 
 
The risks of catastrophic landscape scale forest fires continue to increase throughout the 
Southwest. The costs from these events are likely to affect all ratepayers regardless of whether 
they live in forested counties or not, and the liabilities can have major fiscal implications for 
regulated utilities such as PG&E.   
 
Conversely, thanks to the operation of the sole bioelectricity generation plant in Arizona, over 
the last five years the White Mountain industry has completed the restoration treatment of 50,378 
acres of Ponderosa Pine forests and watershed in the White Mountains of northeastern Arizona. 
Over 1.3 million green tons of small diameter logs were processed to fund forest restoration, and 
over 1 million green tons of biomass hazardous fuel were removed and processed to generate 
electricity. This supported 190 jobs, resulting in over 3 million hours of high wage labor that 
earned over $61 million in direct salary and contributed over $221 million to the local rural 
economy as of this writing.  
 
Quite simply, bioelectricity works, and it allows the immediate large-scale forest restoration that 
our communities need without further delay. The success of forest restoration in general, and the

<!-- page 108 -->
Four Forest Restoration Initiative (4FRI) in particular is critically important to the communities, 
economies, and the environment of northern Arizona.  
 
As the Arizona Public Service Company (APS) is engaged in a 60 day study to evaluate the 
feasibility of converting one coal unit at the Cholla plant to burn biomass, Navajo County 
supports whichever company and technology that provide the solution best adapted and readily 
executable for the responsible disposal of up to 1.5 million green tons of restoration biomass 
annually in Northern Arizona, with the lowest ratepayers’ impact. 
 
Whether the most adapted and readily executable technical solution is the conversion of a coal-
fired unit at Cholla consuming the same amount of biomass as a 60 MW purpose-built plant 
would, or the construction of a new purpose-built 60 MW bioelectricity generating plant, in the 
current state of technology it is likely that a large portion of the up to 1.5 million green tons of 
restoration biomass that need to be disposed of annually will need to be burnt to generate 
electricity.  
 
Navajo County certainly supports the mechanisms in the Commission Policy Statement for 
waivers to be issued for the production of alternative biogas, biofuel or other bio-energies as 
technology may allow in the future and provided they consume the same amount of Arizona 
restoration biomass, but suffice it to say that, factually, in the current state of technology none of 
these alternatives are immediately scalable to the million tons of biomass level required in 
northern Arizona. In its race against catastrophic landscape scale forest fire, Arizona does not 
have the time to serve as technology incubator for the bioenergy technologies of tomorrow. 
Arizona needs it forests restored today, using proven technology. 
 
Further, whether APS convert a coal-fired unit at Cholla or finish their RFP process for the 
procurement of 60 MW of biomass electricity, the Commission’s 60 MW carve-out only 
provides a solution to the role of bioenergy in Arizona if it is implemented in parallel with the 
continued operation of the existing 30 MW capacity in Snowflake. This is why an individual rate 
case for APS could not substitute for a revision of the REST Rule. The overall objective remains 
a total of 90 MW biomass capacity in Arizona to enable the implementation of the annual 
objective of 50,000 acres of forest restoration in northern Arizona. 
 
The Forest Service has been diligently working to issue soon a new RFP for the landscape scale 
implementation of 50,000 acres of forest restoration per year in northern Arizona for the next 20 
years. Restoration logging biomass (tree tops, branches, non-merchantable material, etc.) 
represents approximately half of the fuel removed during forest restoration treatments. The 
responsible disposal of this biomass through the production of bioenergy is an absolute 
imperative for the landscape scale implementation of 4FRI. 
 
Without a revised REST Rule that includes a 60 MW carve-out for biomass energy, there is no 
clear market path for the disposal of up to 1.5 million green tons of restoration biomass annually 
in Arizona. This is likely to greatly compromise the effectiveness of the Forest Service RFP 
designed to attract private investments in forest restoration in Arizona. 
 
Without a revised REST Rule that includes a 60 MW carve-out for biomass energy, there is no 
mandate for APS to either convert a coal-fired unit at Cholla, or to finish their RFP process for 
the procurement of 60 MW of biomass electricity.

WHAT IS  THE E NE RGY RE LE AS E  
COMP ONE NT (E RC)?
The Energy Release Component (ERC) is based on the estimated potential 
available energy released per unit area in the flaming front of a fire. The day-
to-day variations of the ERC are caused by changes in the moisture 
contents of the various fuel classes, including the 1,000 hour timelag
class. The ERC is derived from predictions of (1) the rate of heat release 
per unit area during flaming combustion and (2) the duration of flaming.
The ERC graphs are only one component used in determining the fire 
danger in various areas. We use this data as well as other factors to 
determine if and when we should enter into fire restrictions.

WHITE MOUNTAIN FIRE RESTRICTIONS WORKING GROUP
The White Mountains Fire Restrictions Working Group is a collaborative group 
that works together in the interest of public safety. The WMFRWG comes 
together annually at the beginning of fire season to discuss the seasonal 
outlooks, potential issues or concerns and strategies for sharing information to 
the public. Soon after the group meets weekly through conference calls. The 
following criteria determine fire restrictions.
• Weather
• Forest conditions
• Available resources
• Area events and activities
While each agency is responsible for their separate jurisdiction, partnering 
agencies coordinate the date to go into, move up or out of restrictions 
according to the data.

An ordinance of the Board of Supervisors of Navajo County, Arizona, repealing 
Ordinance No. 01-17 (Outdoor Fire Ordinance); establishing fire zones in the 
unincorporated area; establishing restrictions on certain outdoor fires; providing 
for the implementation of emergency fire restrictions; and specifying civil and 
criminal penalties for violations.  
 
Preface 
The purpose of fire restrictions is to reduce the risk of human-caused fires during periods of unusually high 
fire danger and/or burning conditions. Fire restrictions impose many limitations on the public, and therefore 
should be implemented only after all other prevention measures have been taken. These measures include, 
but are not limited to: increasing the number of prevention signs, public contacts, media campaigns, 
increased enforcement of restrictions, etc. Fire restrictions should be considered when high to extreme fire 
danger is predicted to persist. Other considerations are the level of human-caused fire occurrences being 
experienced. Firefighting resources available, high human-caused risk potential, live fuel moisture is at or 
approaching historic thresholds, no significant relief in fire weather potential in the immediate forecast, large 
fire activity occurring on a unit (within the county) or within the Region, and fire preparedness levels so 
indicate. An appropriate level of preparedness to meet wildland fire management objectives is based upon 
an assessment of vegetation, climate, and topography utilizing the National Fire Danger Rating System 
(NFDRS).

NAVAJO COUNTY FIREWORKS ORDINANCE 
An ordinance of the Board of Supervisors of Navajo County, Arizona, adopting ordinance 
No. __-19 (Navajo County Fireworks Ordinance); prohibiting the use of “permissible consumer 
fireworks” in the unincorporated areas of Navajo County during times when there is a reasonable 
risk of wildfires; establishing a permitting process for public fireworks displays utilizing “display 
fireworks”; and specifying criminal penalties for the violation of this ordinance. 
Upon the Adoption of this Ordinance, Ordinance 01-17 is hereby repealed. 
1.)  TITLE 
This ordinance shall be known as the Navajo County Fireworks Ordinance. 
2.)  EFFECTIVE AREA 
 This ordinance shall be effective in the unincorporated areas of Navajo County, 
 excluding areas under the jurisdiction of the United States, the State of Arizona, or a 
 federally recognized Indian tribe. 
3.)  AUTHORITY/PURPOSE 
This ordinance is established in accordance with A.R.S. § 36 -1606 which authorizes the 
county to regulate the use of “permissible consumer fireworks” within the unincorporated 
areas of the county during times when there is a reasonable risk o f wildfires in the 
immediate county.  Additionally, the County is required by A.R.S. § 36-1603 to implement 
a permitting process for “display fireworks” utilized in public fireworks displays. 
 The purpose of this statute is to reduce and minimize  the risk of fire dangers , 
 including structural or wildland fires,  associated with the use of fireworks in Navajo 
 County, and to help ensure the safety of Navajo County residents attending public 
 fireworks displays in unincorporated areas of the County.

4.)  DEFINITIONS 
 For the purposes of this ordinance, the terms "consumer firework," "display firework," 
 "fireworks," and "permissible consumer fireworks" shall have the same meanings as set 
 forth in A.R.S. § 36-1601. 
5.)  AUTHORITY OF EMERGENCY MANAGEMENT DIRECTOR 
 The Navajo County Emergency Management Director has the authority under Navajo 
County’s current “Outdoor Fire Ordinance” to establish restrictions on outdoor fires and 
emergency fire restrictions.  Under this ordinance, the Emergency  Management Director 
will additionally have the authority to restrict the use of "permissible consumer fireworks.”   
6.)  PROHIBITION ON USE OF FIREWORKS 
  Except as specifically allowed in this ordinance, it shall be unlawful for any person to use 
 fireworks, including consumer fireworks or display fireworks, in the unincorporated areas 
 of Navajo County. 
7.)  USE OF PERMISSIBLE CONSUMER FIREWORKS 
 The use of permissible consumer fireworks shall be permitted within the unincorporated 
 areas of Navajo County except for such time s and in such areas where the Emergency 
 Management Director has established restrictions on outdoor fires or emergency fire 
 restrictions pursuant to current Outdoor Fire Ordinance. 
 The term "permissible consumer fireworks" is specificall y limited to the definition set 
 forth in A.R.S. § 36-1601(5), and includes: 
  A.)  Ground and handheld sparkling devices; 
  B.)  Cylindrical fountains; 
  C.)  Cone fountains; 
  D.)  Illuminating torches; 
  E.)  Wheels; 
  F.)  Ground spinners; 
  G.)  Flitter sparklers; 
  H.)  Toy smoke devices; 
  I.)  Wire sparklers or dipped sticks; 
  J.)  Multiple tube fireworks devices and pyrotechnic articles. 
 
 Permissible consumer fireworks do not include anything that is designed or intended to 
 rise into the air and  explode or to detonate in the air or to fly above the ground, 
 including, for example, firework items commonly known as bottle rockets, sky rockets, 
 missile-type rockets, helicopters, torpedoes, roman candles and jumping sticks.

Supervisors for a permit for the use of  display fireworks and other fireworks as part of a 
public fireworks display.  Any use of display fireworks  or fireworks not included within 
the definition of permissible consumer fireworks without a permit is prohibited. 
 A.)  Applications 
Applications to conduct a public display of fireworks using display fireworks shall 
be submitted to the Clerk of the Board of Supervisors on a form prescribed by the Board 
of Supervisors not more than one-hundred twenty days (120), but not less than sixty (60) 
days, prior to the date of the proposed display.   
 B.)  Bond 
The Application submitted to the Clerk of the Board  must be accompanied by a 
statement that the Applicant is willing to provide a bond in an amount not less than $500.00, 
or in such other amount as deemed appropriate by the Board of Supervisors not to exceed 
one-million ($1,000,000) dollars.  Alternatively, an applicant may provide proof of liability 
insurance in a similar amount along with a letter from the issuing insurance company 
indicating that the applicant's insurance will cover the applicant  for liability connected to 
this event.  Failure of an applicant to submit a bond or proof of insurance  to the Clerk of 
the Board of Super visors prior to the time of the event shall result in the  automatic 
nullification of the permit. 
 C.)  Application Requirements 
  Applications must be accompanied by a map of the display area, which designates 
 the proposed seating area for spectators, nearby structures, parking areas, public roads, 
 the fallout zone, overhead lines, and fire department access to the area. 
 D.)  Review of Applications 
After receipt of the Application by the Clerk of the Board of Supervisors,  all 
applications for public fireworks displays shall be reviewed by the Navajo County Sheriff 
pursuant to A.R.S.36-1603, who shall visit and inspect the display area. The Sheriff (or his 
designee) shall meet with a Fireworks Coordination Group comprised of the following: 
Sheriff, local fire chief, county emergency manager, facility/property owner, a local 
pyrotechnic technician, a local business owner and Chamber of Commerce president (or 
their designee), to obtain input suggestion on the application.

 The Sheriff shall either recommend that the Board of Supervisors approve or 
disapprove the application.  The Sheriff may recommend approval if it is determined that: 
  a.)  This display will be supervised by a competent pyrotechnic operator.  In  
  determining whether that qualification has been met, the Sheriff shall consider the 
  operator's qualifications, experience, training, and/or certification; 
  b.)  It appears that the fireworks display will be conducted in a safe location that

  c.)  That the display will be discharged and fired so that it will not be hazardous to

  e.)  The manner and storage of the fireworks appears to be safe. 
  f.)  The organization has provided a sufficient bond or proof of liability

For purposes of conducting reviews of applications, the Sheriff's Office and/or the fire

displays and is familiar with the requirements and standards for public fireworks displays

Fireworks Displays. 
 E.)  Decision on Application 
The Sheriff shall complete the  investigation within 30 days  of receipt of  the 
application.  Upon completion of the investigation, the Sheriff shall submit a written 
recommendation to the Board of Supervisors that either 1) recommends that the Board of

the Board of Supervisors approve the Application if the Applicant will agree to 
modifications of the plan or special conditions upon the permit. If the Board of Supervisor 
approves the application and places special conditions upon the permit, the applicant shall 
comply with all special conditions placed upon the permit at least forty-eight hours before 
the scheduled public fireworks display. If every special condition placed upon the permit 
is not met before the forty-eight (48) hour deadline, the permit is null and void.

been improved for the use for which it was conditionally approved, the board after notification by 
certified mail to the owner and applicant who requested the rezoning shall schedule a public hearing 
to grant an extension, determine compliance with the schedule for development or cause the 
property to revert to its former zoning classification.

remain that required timely compliance.  Typically, once effectuated, Zone Changes are permanent.

which states the following: 
 
I. The board may approve a change of zone conditioned on a schedule for development of the specific 
use or uses for which rezoning was requested.  If at the expiration of this period the property has not 
been improved for the use for which it was conditionally approved, the board after notification by 
certified mail to the owner and applicant who requested the rezoning shall schedule a public hearing 
to grant an extension, determine compliance with the schedule for development or cause the 
property to revert to its former zoning classification.

The purpose of the Special Development Zone (SD) as set forth in Section 1701 is to “provide for the

commercial centers, off-street parking areas, open space areas for recreation, and to preserve a rural 
atmosphere.”  To achieve this, this section also allows for the “Deviations from design standards for the specific

favor this type of development. In short, the purpose of the SD zone is to allow for mixed-use (retail, office and 
housing) and mixed-housing developments and subdivisions.

AZ EARNED SICK LEAVE POLICIES  
 
Arizona Earned Paid Sick Time (Less than 15 Employees) 
 
White Mountain Lakes County Recreation Improvement District provides paid sick time in 
accordance with Arizona’s Fair Wages and Healthy Families Act effective as of July 1, 2017. All 
District employees may accrue a maximum of twenty-four (24) hours of earned paid sick time 
(“PST”) per calendar year.   
 
Eligibility  
 
All employees are eligible for paid sick time.  
 
Employees who are exempt from the federal Fair Labor Standards Act’s overtime requirements 
(e.g., salaried exempt managers, professionals, administrative employees, or outside 
salespeople) are presumed to work 40 hours per week for accrual purposes, except for weeks in 
which they work less than 40 hours, in which case their paid sick time accrues based on the 
actual number of hours worked. 
 
Accrual and Usage  
 
At the commencement of employment, employees will accrue one hour of paid sick time for 
every 30 hours worked, up to 24 hours. PST is paid at the employee’s regular rate of pay and 
may be used in minimum increments of 1 hour.  
 
Employees may only accrue or use 24 hours of paid sick time per year. Employees may use 
accrued paid sick time as soon as it is accrued.  Employees are limited to using no more than 24 
hours per year. Unused PST will be cashed out at the end of the season which is the end of the 
pay period after Labor Day.  
 
Unused PST will not be compensated at the time of separation. An individual that has been 
terminated will not have their PST cashed out at the end of the season or end of employment.    
 
Covered Usage: PST may be used for time off for yourself or for your care of a family member 
for preventative medical care; having a mental or physical illness, injury, or health condition; or 
for obtaining diagnosis, care, or treatment for the same. If you or your family member is the 
victim of domestic violence, sexual violence, abuse, or stalking, PST may be used to obtain 
medical attention, services, counseling, relocation, legal services, or to participate in a civil or 
criminal proceeding. 
 
PST may be used when the Company or your child’s school or child care provider is closed due to 
a public health emergency, or to care for yourself or for a family member if it is determined by 
health authorities or a health care provider that presence in the community may jeopardize the 
health of others due to exposure to a communicable disease.  
 
Family members include your spouse or registered domestic partner, children (regardless of 
age), parents, grandparents, grandchildren, and siblings. Family members include those family

<!-- page 298 -->
relations resulting from adoption, foster care, step-relationships, legal guardianships, domestic 
partnerships, through marriage, and loco parentis status. Family members also include any other 
individual related by blood or affinity whose close association with you is the equivalent of a 
family relationship.  
Please provide reasonable advance notice to your manager of your intent to use PST, and if 
your absence is unforeseeable, you must contact your manager as soon as possible, preferably 
no later than one (1) hour before your scheduled start time. When possible, your notification 
should include the expected duration of the absence. 
 
If you are seeking to use three (3) or more consecutive workdays of PST, you may be required 
to provide documentation, such as a doctor’s note, to verify the need for your absence and 
qualification for PST. You are not required to provide any details concerning the health 
condition or domestic violence incident for yourself or your family member. Any information 
you elect to provide will be kept confidential. 
 
PST does not count towards the calculation of overtime.  
 
Retaliation  
White Mountain Lakes County Recreation Improvement District will not retaliate against an 
employee requesting or using paid sick leave in accordance with this policy. Employees will not 
be disciplined for the lawful use of PST, but once PST has been exhausted, the normal rules for 
unexcused absences will apply. 
 
Additional Information  
For additional information about this policy, please contact your Administrator.
