Personnel Service Awards : Congratulations to the following employees for their continued service to Navajo County: Angela Keene  (Superior Court) 20 years; Susie Nelson (Kayenta Justice Court) 20 years; Shirley Patterson (Show Low Justice Court) 10 years; Jason Moore (County Attorney) 10 years; Sandra Bachman (Jail Operations) 5 years and Emily Font (Clerk of Superior Court) 5 years:  Eric Scott recognized those employees for their continued service to Navajo County.  Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked those employees for their service.

b.

Presentation: We Are Your Neighbor Award to Ty Stirling and the Public Works Team :  Chairwoman Whitesinger stated that during our winter storms, 27 employees handled snow response duty, including blading equipment, plowing and spreading cinders, with approximately 450 response hours and approximately 1,720 tons of cinders spread on county roads.  She indicated that in addition to road opertions, those who work within the team, County Engineer, Bill Bess; Highway Manager, Rick Denton; and Fleet Manager, Russ McCray, spent over 15 hours each plowing snow and spreading cinders.  She expressed appreciation to those who are in leadership being an example and working among those who are working on our roadways and serving the public.  She spoke about New Year's Eve night when Ty Stirling helped blade a road and guided an expectant couple to the hospital in time to have their baby which is a great example of neighbors helping neighbors.  Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked Mr. Stirling for his exceptional work serving our communities.

c.

Follow Up to the Financial Update and FY2020 Budget Development Plan :  Bryan Layton gave a recap on the upcoming financial challenges of a $2.6 million to $5.2 million deficit or uncertain revenues.  He stated that because we have had a strong state economy over the last couple of years, we are taking a two-year approach and developing reduction proposals for the Board's review. He indicated that the Administration reduction package will save us nearly $300,000, and we will start implementing that now.  He stated that at the direction of the Board, we are investigating the feasibility of taking the issue of a jail district to the voters and develop a possible timeline.  He indicated that they received an opinion from the County Attorney's Office that the County could reintroduce that initiative to the voters in 2019, and he believes that August is feasible and reasonable, and they will provide details of the timeline to the Board shortly.  He indicated that they investigated the possibility of using different ballot language for the voters, and that is still under review, and they will have more to report at an upcoming meeting.  He stated that we have spoken to our Cities and Towns regarding an increase of jail fees and sharing the medical costs. He indicated that the city and town managers are very concerned about how to budget for those unexpected medical costs. He stated that he will have continued updates for the Board in future meetings.

Vice-Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Layton for the updates.  He stated that it was communicated clearly to our citizens on how services would look and feel differently, and that is starting to show.  He indicated that the citizens need to be aware that it is not good moving forward and would like to hear in a future report what each department is looking to do and how their services will change.  He spoke about realtors having an expectation of being able to close in 24 hours, which is not a reality anymore.  He spoke about the services provided for the School Districts which will be impacted.  He stated that real changes are coming, and how we do business will look different moving forward. Chairwoman Whitesinger expressed appreciation to Mr. Layton for keeping the Board informed and educating the Board on this process.  She spoke about the implications of these challenges.  She stated in previous years every department and every elected official has provided services in a way where community members did not feel the impact; however, this time our community members will feel this impact.  She indicated that we are still working through the processes, and we have asked the departments and offices to come to the Board and have conversations moving forward regarding what those deficiencies look like.

d.

PUBLIC HEARING :  Prioritize proposed Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) projects to be funded in 2019, and possible adoption of Resolution Number 02-19, authorizing the submission of an application for FY19 State Community Development Block Grant funds, certifying said application meets the community's previously identified housing and community development needs and the requirements of the state CDBG program, and authorizing all actions necessary to implement and complete the activities outlined in said application :  Isabel Rollins, Northern Arizona Council of Governments, discussed the item.  She stated that Navajo County will be receiving funds in 2019 in the amount of approximately $400,000.  She stated that at a public hearing, six projects were proposed and one was dropped, which is the County Courthouse renovation, because it would be very hard to qualify for the funds, which leaves five eligible projects to choose from, and the Board can only pick three of them for funding.  She suggested that the Board pick a back-up project just in case any of the three chosen do not go through.

Remodel and Upgrade to building used to house 30+ homeless individuals and families:  Pastor Sherman from the White Mountain Coalition Against Homelessness stated that they are a coalition of numerous churches and organizations who work to eliminate chronic homelessness in the White Mountains.  He stated that they are not just a shelter but a program to help people break that cycle of homelessness.  He indicated that they insist that people work and give them an unlimited amount of time to find a job.  He stated that they are all volunteers.  He indicated that they are asking for up to $175,000.  He spoke about trying to build a situation where they have a training center and also house families and individuals, and the funding will go a long way towards construction and fire suppression. Supervisor Williams thanked Pastor Sherman for being here and his support for the homeless. He asked about whether the building in the application has been sold. Pastor Sherman indicated that the owner has two buildings, one that is optimal and the other is a little larger and would require more work. Supervisor Williams asked Ms. Rollins if the location has to be figured out ahead of time, and Ms. Rollins indicated that the location does not need to be figured out ahead of time.

Sexual Assault and Domestic Violence Support Services:  Theresa Warren, Director of Alice's Place in Winslow, stated that they are asking for $36,000 to hire a sexual assault victim advocate.  She stated that Alice's place has been around for about 20 years and has a lot of experience and a good track record with victim advocacy.  She indicated that they are looking to expand their program to add on sexual assault specific services.  She stated that they are located in Winslow, but they serve the entire county, including Navajo Nation and Hopi Reservation.  She indicated that their area of service and mobile advocacy spans about 40,000 square miles and beyond that.

Sidewalk and Drainage Improvements (Hall Street Project):  Lisa Robinson stated that the application is for a sidewalk project and subdrainage improvements.  She indicated that this project is located within the Department of Housing's designated redevelopment area in downtown Show Low.   She stated that they have used CDBG monies in the past for a lot of improvements in the redevelopment area.  Supervisor Williams asked how long ago was it that Show Low received CDBG monies for this, and she responded that the last time they received County CDBG monies was in 2010.  She stated that the City of Show Low will put in a match for whatever it takes to complete the project.

Navajo County Historic Museum Improvements:  Glenn Kephart stated that this was submitted by County staff.  He stated the County museum is a valuable and historic building that we need to continue to maintain and preserve in partnership with the City of Holbrook.  He spoke about the needed roof repair, but indicated that the challenge with this project is that it's difficult to define the population served by the museum.  He stated that staff recommends ranking this project low due to the likelihood of meeting all of the requirements.  He indicated that County Staff, along with Holbrook and several people in the community who are interested in the museum are looking to form a task force to determine funding options to get the roof done.

Installation of 8" water line with fire protection:  Glenn Kephart stated that a representative from Snowflake was unable to be here, but they have expressed the importance of this project which extends an 8-inch water line to a population that needs domestic water and extends the fire protection.   He indicated that it does serve  moderate to low income families and is a good project from staff'sperspective. Vice-Chairman Whiting indicated that according to the application, the intent of Snowflake is to match $125,000.  Supervisor Jack asked what the total amount being requested for the six projects. Chairwoman Whitesinger stated that the Board makes a recommendation of three projects and selects a backup project and we could potentially receive $400,000, but at this moment, the distribution amount has not been determined.

Domestic Violence Shelter:  Ms. Rollins stated that she does not have more information than what is in the packet.  She indicated that it was put forward by Nathan Updike with White Mountain Safe House, and they are asking for $50,000 for staff salaries for advocates who provide guidance, vocational training, group counseling services, help in court cases, transportation and otherwise care for victims of domestic violence and sexual assault.  She stated they also provide prevention training in a community outreach and build community support for victims.  She indicated that the White Mountain Safe House serves a lot of individuals but were having financial problems and turnover, and ChangePoint Integrated Health took over managing the organization.   Supervisor Williams asked about the prioritization and the amount given to each project. Ms. Rollins indicated that those requesting funds could use more funds so that we do not have to give funding back.

Chairwoman Whitesinger opened the public hearing.  Tom Brown stated that he represents a supporting nonprofit for homeless housing. He spoke about houses they rent in Show Low which evolved into leasing a hotel in Pinetop to better administer to the homeless people. He stated they are a program and not a shelter, and he would like to see the program help those who can recover and get a job and a place to live and help the chronically homeless and rescue mission. He stated that they do not have the resources or the building to do that.  He indicated that their success rate is 78% helping people become a functional part of society, and they would like to do more. He stated that the White Mountain Coalition Against Homeless has a balance in their account, people dedicated to giving and a good coverage of support, and they are asking the commissioners to seriously consider the White Mountain Coalition Against Homelessness.  William Bujlowski stated that he supports the White Mountain Coalition Against Homelessness and indicated that they overlap and provide help for the abused and the family violence and requested that the Board recommend them for the grant.  Maria Stokes, a member of the White Mountain Coalition Against Homelessness coalition and the salvation army, she stated that the motel system is not financially efficient and not a better way to help people get back on their feet.  She indicated that it takes a lot more than putting people in a room.  She stated they serve the whole community and would like to see a bigger building where they can serve the community in a greater capacity and provide the tools to get the success rate bumped up.

Supervisor Williams asked Ms. Rollins what would happen if the three projects which are picked add up to more than $400,000, and Ms. Rollins stated that once they know the exact amount of the grant, then the projects would be decreased proportionately.  Chairwoman Whitesinger asked whether any of these applications had prior funding.  Ms. Rollins stated that Alice's Place was funded a long time ago, Show Low has used other CDBG funding and Snowflake has received other CDBG funding but not for this particular project.  Mr. Kephart requested that Ms. Rollins review the timeline requirements in order to be awarded the money. Ms. Rollins stated that we are supposed to submit a letter of intent to the Department of Housing by February 1 listing the projects, full applications for those projects are due by June 1, the State then will review the applications and ask follow-up questions, and funds will be available by the end of the year.

Vice-Chairman Whiting spoke about the two projects inside of his District.  He stated that due to the Navajo County Historical Museum Improvement project being a difficult one to get approved, he would suggest ranking that one below the backup, but he does believe the water line in Snowflake is a viable project.  Supervisor Williams stated that he believes the Historical Museum should be ranked sixth.  He indicated that part of the challenge is that they are all great projects and benefit people, but we want to approve projects where we can use the full amount.  He stated that the homelessness is a regional issue and it makes a lot of sense to support that.  He indicated that he understands the comments with regard to Snowflake, and there is a match.  He stated that Show Low is a similar project, and the County has not sent CDBG money that way in nine years.  He stated that Alice's Place is a great project, but he is concerned about losing available money. He indicated that the safehouse is a blessing to a lot of people, but there is a commitment from ChangePoint already for funding.  There being no further public comments, Chairwoman Whitesinger closed the public hearing.  She stated that she agrees with the comments which have been made and believes they are sound priorities.  She expressed appreciation to the townships in working in partnership with these organizations, as well as being able to provide a matching fund for their application.  She stated that she feels supporting those who help our homeless population is very important.  She indicated that the priorities are the sidewalk and draining improvement, the installation of water line with fire protection and remodel and upgrade to a building used to house 30 homeless individuals with Alice's Place to be the backup. Vice-Chairman Whiting made a motion that the priority order be the remodel and upgrade to the homeless shelter for $175,000, address to be determined; the installation of an 8" water line for fire protection in Snowflake for $125,000, as well as the sidewalk and drainage improvements for $175,000 and then the sexual assault and domestic violence support service for $36,620 at Alice's Place as the backup; motion seconded by Supervisor Williams . Vote unanimous approving the motion.  Vice-Chairman Whiting made a motion to adopt Resolution Number 02-19 , authorizing the submission of an application for FY19 State Community Development Block Grant funds, certifying said application meets the community's previously identified housing and community development needs and the requirements of the state CDBG program, and authorizing all actions necessary to implement and complete the activities outlined in said application; motion seconded by Supervisor Williams .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

COUNTY ATTORNEY :

a.

Consideration of retaining the law firm Keller Rohrback, L.L.P. to represent Navajo County in potential litigation against opioid manufacturers/distributors in multi-district litigation pending in federal court in the State of Ohio, and authorizing the County Attorney to sign and approve a formal retainer agreement with the firm : Jason Moore discussed the item and spoke about the local opioid problem.  He stated that many of the drug problems start with prescription opioid pain killers.  He indicated that opioids have been mismarketed and used on a long-term basis with patients, and the risks of long-term addiction have been seriously overlooked and underestimated.  He indicated that a number of governmental organizations have initiated litigation against the manufacturers for some of the costs they have incurred.  He stated that a lot of costs for Navajo County are in the court system and law enforcement.  He spoke about the increase in the burden on the Courts, law enforcement and the County Attorney's Office.  He stated that they solicited bids from firms and received responses from 3 different law firms which were evaluated by a committee, and it was determined that the Law Firm of Keller Rohrback, L.L.P. was most aligned with what we were looking for and also had the most cost effective fee arrangement.  He introduced Ron Kilgard from Keller Rohrback, L.L.P.  Mr. Kilgard introduced himself and spoke about his firm's approach to the opioid crisis and litigation.  He stated that there are over 1,000 governmental entities and risk retention pools in this litigation.  He spoke about how the cases are organized.  He stated that this is not a class action case, and Navajo County will be represented for only Navajo County's interest.  He indicated that the cost to the county is zero, and  it is a contingency fee case with a 15% fee.  Mr. Moore stated that NPR was discussing the fact that these days adults are more likely to die from an opioid overdose than a car accident.
Supervisor Williams made a motion to retain the Law Firm of Keller Rohrback, L.L.P. to represent Navajo County in potential litigation against opioid manufacturers/distributors in multi-district litigation pending in federal court in the State of Ohio, and authorizing the County Attorney or a designee from his office to sign and approve a formal retainer agreement with the firm under the terms that were presented; motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
