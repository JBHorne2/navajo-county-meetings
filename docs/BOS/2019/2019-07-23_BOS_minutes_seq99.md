Presentation of the activities of the County Supervisors Association, including a discussion of the recent legislative session :  Craig Sullivan introduced Vanessa Fielder, a research analyst on the CSA staff.  He thanked the Board of Supervisors for their involvement in CSA and Vice-Chairman Whiting for serving on the Legislative Policy Committee and executive committee.  He spoke about what the County Supervisors Association is and the mission statement.  He further spoke about the different speakers who have presented at CSA Board meetings.  He stated that they also work regularly with professional staff of the counties through affiliate groups.  He spoke about the data that is collected which is relevant to the counties and helps with lobbying documents and is available to the counties.   Brad Carlyon inquired how the CSA database compares to what AACo has for their database, and Mr. Sullivan indicated that the CSA database is deeper and more Arizona specific.  Mr. Sullivan stated that for the 2019 session, 1318 bills were introduced; 331 passed, 210 of which were county relevant; 320 were signed; and 11 were vetoed.

He spoke about the FY2020 County Budget Priorities.  He stated that they were successful in obtaining the goals that were set and will need to still work on the Department of Juvenile Corrections issue.  He indicated that for Navajo County, relief in the State Budget is approximately $429,000, and $594,800 in the 2-year plan.  He stated that the State secured 4.4 million in presidential preference election primary resources in the next fiscal year and that the CORP increase was secured.  He spoke about Arizona Long-Term Care System (ALTCS) program, which is an indigent services program.  He indicated that this program has grown by 266% since 2000, and, statutorily, counties must provide substantial resources to the program.  He stated that the legislature put out a budget which was $313,000 for Navajo County for ALTCS, which is an 11% increase.  He spoke about infrastructure spending.  He stated that the legislature provided a state mechanism for online sales taxes to be captured just like brick and mortar sales, which shores up our existing sales tax base.

He stated that most of the bills CSA ran were for budget messaging.  He indicated that, overwhelmingly, the bills with which the counties had concerns were addressed favorably or defeated.  He expressed appreciation for the excellent partnership this Board has with their legislative delegation.  He spoke about a new groundwater committee formed under the auspices of the Governor's water augmentation committee which will look at rural groundwater issues.  He stated that they have pursued equity related to vehicle license taxes for gas and electric vehicles,  He indicated that they also repealed the $32 public safety fee which was implemented last year, and when that fee goes away, it will create exposure for the legislature to use HURF money to fund DPS.  He stated that it is anticipated that PTSD issues and workers' comp-related items will come back next year.  He indicated that 60 bills were introduced related to elections, most notably taking the responsibility for deploying emergency voting centers from the Recorder and moving it to the Board of Supervisors.  He stated that Senator Allen, Representative Blackman and Representative Thorpe were hands on in terms of securing these financial resources and protecting Navajo County's interests.  He expressed appreciation for their efforts.  He spoke about information and development and ongoing priorities and issues.

Supervisor Williams thanked Mr. Sullivan and Ms. Fielder for taking the time to come up and for all they do.  He spoke about the good communication between supervisors state wide.  Supervisor Jack thanked Mr. Sullivan for all the great work that they do for the counties.  Mr. Carlyon inquired regarding Title 36, and Mr. Sullivan stated that the legislature does not have its own plan with regard to Title 36, but there are interest groups in the Maricopa area.  He indicated that the Court piece is less of a threat, and there has been an ongoing discussion of changing the definitions in Title 36 to bring substance abuse related people in.  Mr. Carlyon stated that the Supreme Court Committee on Mental Health's interim report goes out in September, and they are changing the definition of mental illness to include substance abuse.  He indicated that he will provide a copy of the interim report.  Mr. Carlyon inquired where Mr. Sullivan sees criminal justice going next year.  Mr. Sullivan stated that the conversation is elevated and there was a meeting at the house yesterday, and there is a lot of interest among the supervisors in whether there is a cheaper way to get good results in the criminal justice system.  Mr. Carlyon stated that we need funding for treatment and diversion programs in Navajo County.  He indicated that county attorneys as a whole have been trying to educate the legislature that they are doing more with diversion even without resources.  He stated that they are working on making their own criminal justice reform bill which will be done piecemeal.  He inquired regarding the Governor's office priorities for next year, and Mr. Sullivan stated that he has a meeting next week and will be asking that question.  Mr. Kephart thanked Mr. Sullivan and his staff for their efforts in working with our administrative team on a regular basis.  Mr. Carylon stated that another legislative change which needs to happen is removing the requirement that counties cannot charge more than it actually costs for diversion, and Mr. Sullivan stated that they will take a look at that.  Supervisor Thompson thanked Mr. Sullivan for everything they do to serve the counties.  He indicated that they soon will be bringing Navajo legislators up here to talk about specific bills to the Native American communities and will need guidance.  Vice-Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Sullivan and his team for their partnership, efforts and the good, constant flow of communication.  He stated that we appreciate CSA's perspective and their servant's heart.  He presented Mr. Sullivan with a pen and holder.
