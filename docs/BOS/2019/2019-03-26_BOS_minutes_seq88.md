ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES :

a.

Budget Presentations :  Bryan Layton spoke about the upcoming schedule of budget presentations in April; in May, they will be finalizing decisions, balancing funds and any final discussions that need to happen; and in June, Paige Peterson will present the proposed budget.  He stated that we are taking a two-year approach to restructure our budget.  He indicated that our goal this year is to reduce $1.5 million from the budget.

a. Recorder's Office - Doris Clark, Recorder, spoke about her budget indicating that in 2017, they did not fill a vacant voter outreach position and got through the elections with a temporary.  She stated that in the recording part of the office, they were able to deduct 6.3% and in Voter Registration, they were able to deduct 8%. She indicated that she is nervous for next year's budget because she does not know where she would possibly cut. She stated that they have a new voter registration system that will need to be purchased, and Voter Registration has a vehicle that is 12 years old, and they really need a new vehicle.  She indicated that in next year's budget, if she has to reduce, it would be personnel, and they only have two people right now in Voter Registration; and with close to 70,000 active/inactive voters, one person cannot handle Voter Registration. She spoke about the option of hiring temps and stated that you have to be careful because it is confidential records.  She stated that there are two ladies out front doing recordings, a chief deputy and the recorder. She indicated that they are sharing one voter outreach person with Elections, and they will have to have a temp or two to cover the three reservations.  She stated that due to these reductions, we have witnessed customers who are getting frustrated because they do not think things are being done promptly enough; and if staff is reduced, it will take longer to record documents.
Supervisor Thompson indicated that we are going to have to ask the public to be patient with us during these budget cuts. He inquired what would happen if the vehicle with over 200,000 miles breaks down. He acknowledged the Recorder's situation.
Vice-Chairman Whiting stated that currently, due to the cuts that have gone on, customers are upset because of turn-around time; and if cuts are made, it will be longer. Ms. Clark indicated that we no longer offer services in Show Low.
Vice-Chairwoman Whiting stated thatservices are going to look a lot different due to the cuts that have already taken place and will take place.  He expressed appreciation to Ms. Clark for her efforts.  Ms. Clark spoke of the federal mandates regarding bilingual workers.
Supervisor Williams and
Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked Ms. Clark for her presentation.

b. Treasurer's Office - Debra Kester stated that her office has two different areas of responsibility:  property taxes and banking and reviewed the duties of each of those.  She reviewed the property tax calendar and stated that over 90,000-plus notices went out. She indicated that tax payments come in year-round. She stated that in 2001, her office has 8 employees, and they have lost 2 positions due to budget cuts. She indicated that the workload for each staff member has continued to increase.  She stated that requests to increase the staff have been unsuccessful.  She spoke about currently being unable to provide the level of service that she would like due to being understaffed. She stated that since 2004, the Treasurer has been working diligently to streamline all operations within the Treasurer's Office.  She stated that we currently have one of the largest parcels per employee ratio in Arizona. She indicated that sometimes there is a delay in posting revenue due to the current workload.  She stated that her office received over 21,000 phone calls in 2018, and her current staff cannot keep up with the phone calls.  She indicated that if she lost another employee, the parcel per employee count would be over 22,000 which will result in a significant delay in posting payments which will impact property taxpayers, school districts, fire, road and sewer districts and result in an estimated loss of $200,000 of interest revenue. She stated that if her staff is reduced by one member and if the remaining data input specialist suffers a major illness or requires extended time off, that could add 2 to 6 weeks' delay in posting revenue.  She spoke about what impact the delay in posting revenues would be to property taxpayers and county districts, including school districts, fire districts, etc.  She stated that districts may be required to and will have to open lines of credit to continue to operate.  She indicted that school districts' line of credit are required to keep a certain fund amount with a positive cash balance as required by state statute and that these lines of credit result in a loss of revenue to the interest charge and that will increase due to the increased days to pay the loan.  She stated that there will be times that they will max out their line of credit which means they will not have the funds to pay teachers.  She indicated that if her staff is reduced, she will not have the staff member to complete the deed to state process which will reduce revenue $2- to $300,000. She stated that tax collection rates will decline year after year. She spoke about the impact to County banking which includes investments, pay bonds, timely and accurate reports, audit requests, line of credit, interest apportionment, reconciliation and excess proceeds. She stated that if she loses one employee, the impact will be rippled through the whole county, and the deed to state process would have to be eliminated.  She indicated that she currently cannot provide the level of service that taxpayers and county districts deserve.  She spoke about how taxpayers closing escrow will be affected.

Vice-Chairman Whiting indicated that with over 90,000 transactions and 21,000 phone calls over a year, a delay in payments would energize more phone calls. He expressed appreciation for the information and indicated that it will help him in his communication at the State level and our citizens.  Ms. Kester indicated that most taxpayers pay in installments; therefore, the number of transactions is much higher than 90,000.
Supervisor Williams and
Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked Ms. Kester for her presentation.

c. Assessor's Office - Cammy Darris, Assessor, stated that the Assessor's database is what feeds the collection of the taxes.  She presented a picture of her fellow employees and showcased each of them.  She spoke about cross training in her office. She stated that they have initiated a program where each department in her office has to do a presentation to the rest of the office so everyone in the office knows a little bit about what each department does.  She stated the Assessor's Office budget in 2019 was $1,346,413, and the 2008 budget was just under $1.6 million, which is a 15% cut.  She indicated that the budget cuts that they are facing for 2020, they have cut two positions the first year and are looking at one position next year, which is approximately a third of her staff. She stated that 80% of her budget is personnel costs, 8.66% is daily operations and 10.1% is software costs. She indicated that in 2008, her office had 23 staff members and as of today, they have 17 members, a decrease of over 25% in the last 10 years. She stated that the workload of those cut then goes to the remaining employees. She spoke about the people in her office and their duties and responsibilities. She spoke about the cuts in each department. She further spoke about home visits
to help those who cannot get out with their
exemptions which would need to be cut. She spoke about all the departments and offices with which her office works for information in house. She indicated that her office previously had 10 field appraisers and now has 7 fields appraisers.  She stated that one appraiser values 86,000 parcels of land. She spoke about the certification requirements to be an appraiser and the costs associated with that.  She stated that Coconino County has 3,000 parcels per appraiser, and her office has 8,600 parcels per appraiser.  She spoke about the duties of the appraisers.  She further spoke about staffing the Show Low and Heber Office to ensure customer service in all areas of Navajo County. She further spoke about the importance of accuracy and the duties of the quality control department.  She spoke about her administration department of one and the duties of that team member.  She stated that her office supplies every jurisdiction with their assessed values.  She spoke about her budget that consists of office supplies, fuel, software, postage, other services which includes printing of notices, and travel budget. She stated that appraisers receive a $5 cell phone stipend. She indicated that her office does not attend any out-of-state conferences. She indicated that her office does train the trainer, so any employee who goes to a conference comes back and trains others in their department. She spoke about the Assessor database and how different departments within Navajo County request information from that database. She indicated that the Assessor's Office has one budget and is the only elected office that does not have a secondary fund. She spoke about what the loss of staff will do to her office:  impact the processing of permits which could result in a delay of new construction being placed on the tax rolls, delay in property boundary changes, delay in 911 addressing, property transfers could be delayed and what that would look like, inform voters by elections and special districts, delay in response to property owner requests, increase in fee-based services.  She stated that going back 10 years, 6 people have been cut from the Assessor's Office.

She indicated that her biggest concern is inconsistency within our own walls.  She stated that there is about an 8% difference in starting salary between a building inspector and an appraiser.  She indicated that there is a data entry person in her office with 11 years experience and greater responsibility making 8% less than a data entry person with 5-1/2 years in another department.  She spoke about the salary difference between drafters versus cartographers.
Supervisor Thompson indicated that he is with her and staff that they definitely need more help in running the department.  He stated that he understands and respects the pride that she has in talking about her office. He spoke about the Board dealing with this in a united way.  Ms. Darris stated they want to be part of the solution but that every department needs to be a part of the solution.
Vice-Chairman Whiting thanked Ms. Darris for her detailed report.  He commented on her services and the quality of workers in the Assessor's Office. He indicated that this presentation was very informative, and he would like to talk about the inequalities we can address between our walls. He thanked her and her office for what they continue to do.
Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked Ms. Darris for her presentation and made comments about quality services to the public.  She stated that the challenge ahead is not an easy one, and she values all those who work very hard on behalf of our community members.
