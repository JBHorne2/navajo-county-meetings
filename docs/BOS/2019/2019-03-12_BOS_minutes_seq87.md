10:00 a.m. PUBLIC HEARING:  Consideration and discussion of the proposed Jail District, reaffirming Resolution Number 13-19 , establishing a Jail District in Navajo County for the purpose of providing a dedicated revenue stream to support the existing jail and relieve the general fund of the burden of supporting the entire cost of the jail :   Bryan Layton reviewed the background of the fiscal challenge we are about to encounter, the options that we've discussed moving forward, how a jail district works and the impact on our cities and towns.  He spoke about the impact of the recession and how the County adapted and evolved which resulted in a very lean organization.  He stated that the projected annual lost revenues due to coal power generation disappearing is $2.5 million, in addition to an increase in State Retirement Plans.  He indicated that one of the options which has been discussed is cutting additional jobs.  He spoke about the County Attorney's presentation at the last Board of Supervisors meeting where he spoke about how additional cuts would impact prosecution services in the County.  He stated that staff did a revenue analysis at the direction of the Board of Supervisors which showed that user fees and fines could not be a solution for us.  He indicated that they also looked at property tax and stated that as a county, we can increase property taxes 2% a year which we did not do. He stated that a raise in property taxes would not be sufficient to make up for $2.5 million lost revenues and is not the right tool for the job.  He spoke about the option of a Jail District, which is a special taxing district approved by the voters to operate the jail. He stated that this is a one-third of a cent sales tax that all visitors, travelers, and community members would pay. He indicated that this would provide a dedicated revenue stream to pay for the daily operation of the jail; therefore, the general fund monies now being used to operate the jail can be used to cover expenses in other areas where we have shortfalls.  He stated that this is not designed to be a financial windfall for Navajo County but is designed to fill the gap.  He stated that with the Jail District, cities and towns would have an annual savings; but with it, they would have to keep paying the jail fees, sharing the costs of prosecuting misdemeanors and medical costs.
