PUBLIC WORKS :

a.

PUBLIC HEARING: Consideration and possible adoption of Resolution Number 33-19 approving/denying a Zone Change Request from the existing S.D. (Special Development) Single Family zoning on a 127.2 acre parcel known as "Silver Creek Home Sites" within the White Mountain Lakes Conceptual Master Plan to 125.8 acres of R-3 (Multiple-Family Residential) to allow them to develop an RV subdivision and 1.34 acres of C-R (Commercial-Residential) zoning on parcel APN 304-11-045 and 304-78-001B, a portion of Township 11 North, Range 22 East, Sections 9 and 10 in the White Mountain Lake area :  Sandra Phillips discussed the item. She indicated that this is a request for a zone change. She stated that the project has a new owner who wants to do something different than what is on the master plan. She indicated that they are requesting a zone change R-3 on 125.8 acres and C-R on 1.34 acres. She stated that this item was recommended to the Board for approval by the Planning and Zoning Commission. She indicated that some concerns that were addressed at the Planning and Zoning Commission were not zoning related but will come later. Will Flake, Painted Sky Engineering and Survey, representing the applicant, addressed the Board.

Chairwoman Whitesinger opened the public hearing.   Penny Stegman, 8323 Juniper Drive, White Mountain Lake, addressed the Board regarding her concerns.  She stated that the density of 1.5 to 2.36 is a significant increase for the small area as they are close to doubling the usage of the lake year over year, and residents are complaining regarding the lack of parking.  She indicated that she's not been involved with the developer coming forward to speak to the residents regarding concerns.  She stated that White Mountain Lake will get a bad rap, because those who utilize the RV park may not be able to get on the lake.  She inquired how this is not a conflict of interest as the Board of Supervisors is also the Board of Directors of the White Mountain Lake County Recreation Improvement District.  She requested that the Board add stipulations to this before moving forward on approval and a new rule book indicating how this volume will be handled.  Pat Stevens, 8750 Silver Creek Drive, White Mountain Lake, addressed the Board in opposition to this item indicating that this property would have lake access rights. She stated that the White Mountain Lake County Recreation Improvement District (WMLCRID) was formed with the properties included to be single-family residential in nature with the exception of Juniper Ridge RV Resort and Waters Edge Condos.  She indicated that the WMLCRID rules and regulations do not include RV camping.  She stated that the current number of owned parcels who have lake access is approximately 3,363.  She indicated that to date, she has given out 2,076 placards.  She stated that those staying at the proposed RV park will be there specifically to recreate, which may add 400 additional families.  She indicated that there are 26 parking spaces for people to use this lake and 12 boat trailer parking spaces.  She gave information on the current usage of the lake and expressed concern about the safety, usability and liability of the lake. She requested that a capacity study be done before this goes forward. Buz Essel, 1770 Edwina Place, Show Low, addressed the Board.  He indicated that he is a fairly new resident. He stated that the Board needs to realize that the lake in this community is the binding force to all that live there. He stated that when some come forward, they represent many more who cannot be here. He indicated that he wants a lake capacity study done before the Board even passes the zoning. He indicated that Supervisor Whiting indicated that he needed to meet with the residents, but he has not done that.  He requested that this item be pulled until further research can be done on factors which are not zoning related. Jim Cutler, 1763 Bourdon Ranch Road, White Mountain Lake, addressed the Board.  He stated that there have been two developments in White Mountain Lake which have never reached completion.   Regarding the proposed development, he expressed concern regarding how far the sewage disposal area is from Bourdon Ranch Road, and is the sewage disposal area a continuation of the existing sewage treatment plant in White Mountain Lake.  He further questioned who owns Parcel Number 304-11-022, the site of the sewage treatment disposal area.   An individual from the audience indicated that the developer owns that parcel.  He inquired regarding the intended use of the parcels to be zoned R3 and can the current road system handle the additional road traffic. He asked what provisions are being made for water, sewer, power and whether the development will be constructed in phases, what is the timeframe for construction, what type of financing and what assurances is the developer willing to provide that the construction will be completed in a timely manner. There being no further comments, the Chairwoman closed the public hearing.

Supervisor Williams requested that Ms. Phillips address the concerns. Ms. Phillips indicated that one of the stipulations with this zoning is that they do a traffic impact study, and water and sewer capacity is part of the plat process that will be addressed.  She stated that the concerns on the lake is not within Planning and Zoning's purview, and it is her understanding that only a portion of the project has access to the lake, and that will have to be worked out with the recreation district.  She stated that part of the 2006 master plan that has not been developed was planned for an RV resort, mobile home, so it was part of the master plan to have this kind of use within this area.  Chairwoman Whitesinger inquired regarding the response when notification went out to the community, and Ms. Phillips indicated that they have received some phone calls; it was posted a couple of times, and the postings disappeared; and residents showed up at the Planning and Zoning meeting with the same concerns. Supervisor Williams indicated that just the zoning issue is before the Board today.  Supervisor Thompson inquired whetherthere are any additional stipulations that need to happen, and Ms. Phillips stated that this is a rezone item, so the traffic impact study is a requirement.  She indicated that lake access will need to be done with the Rec District and is not a Planning and Zoning issue. Supervisor Williams thanked those who came today and stated that their concerns will need to be vetted if the project were to progress.  Supervisor Williams made a motion to adopt Resolution Number 33-19 , approving a Zone Change Request from the existing S.D. (Special Development) Single Family zoning on a 127.2 acre parcel known as "Silver Creek Home Sites" within the White Mountain Lakes Conceptual Master Plan to 125.8 acres of R-3 (Multiple-Family Residential) to allow them to develop an RV subdivision and 1.34 acres of C-R (Commercial-Residential) zoning on parcel APN 304-11-045 and 304-78-001B, a portion of Township 11 North, Range 22 East, Sections 9 and 10 in the White Mountain Lake area; motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

b.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 34-19 approving/denying a Special Use Permit allowing for the construction of approximately 3,650 acre, 477 MW wind energy generation and storage facility on parcels that are zoned A-General or RU-1, APN 110-01-004, & 110-01-005, portions of Township 15 North, Range 15 East, Section 22, 26, 27, 34, 35 and 36 in the Chevelon Canyon area :  Sandra Phillips discussed the item and indicated that this is a wind turbine project located south of Winslow on the county line.  She stated that the portion within Navajo County is approximately six sections of land.  She indicated that the turbines are planned quite a distance from existing residences and individual parcel owners.  She stated that the applicant has done significant studies for bird life, view sheds, impact to the community, artifacts and sound.  She spoke about planned access routes.  She indicated that approximately 477 megawatts could be generated from site.  She stated that the maximum height of the turbines could be 755 feet.  She spoke about public meetings in Winslow and the Hopi Tribe.  She indicated that it was recommended for approval by the Planning and Zoning Commission by a vote of 3-2.  The applicant, Terrance Unrein, S Power, addressed the Board.  He spoke about the background of S Power.  He stated that they are typically the long-term owner and operator of these assets, so they have a vested interest in developing these projects right.   He further spoke about the project indicating that they are planning on 477 MW AC, so this will be the largest wind farm in Arizona powered by 164 turbines, 8 of which will be located in Navajo County.  He indicated that their point of connection is an existing APS transmission line coming from the Cholla Power Plant.  He stated that this design is a result of nearly a year of robust environmental, natural resource and cultural surveys. He spoke about the certificate of environmental compatibility and the conditional use permit. He indicated that the Planning and Zoning Commission unanimously approved this in Coconino County last Wednesday. He spoke about the project benefits, including cost competitive and reliable and renewable energy, energy can be generated by using no water and producing no air emissions, will not preclude ranching, will increase the turbine payments to Arizona State Lands which directly benefit public education and university, positive local economic impact, and the project is in a remote location. He spoke about mitigation of visual impact. Supervisor Thompson spokeabout the company making contacts even when not being asked to do so. He asked about the responses the company has received from the Hopi Tribe. The applicant indicated that they sent notices and letters to 11 potentially affected tribal entities and have been speaking with the Hopi Tribe all of 2019 and spent many hours with them on July 15. He spoke about their cultural resources concerns. He indicated that those conversations have evolved, and he stated that in the beginning they were in opposition to this project, but he believes now that they may have changed their mind and are in a neutral position. He stated that they are working with the Hopi Tribe on other things in the region, and he doesn't think they would be having those conversations if they did not have their trust.

Chairwoman Whitesinger opened the public hearing.  Kim O'Haco McReynolds spoke in support of this item. She indicated that she is here on behalf of her family and indicated that this project represents the ongoing shift towards more renewable resources and creates a new opportunity for her and her family to sustain their ranching operation. She stated that after careful consideration and deliberation, her family decided that this project is closely aligned with their ranch philosophy of strong environmental stewardship. She indicated that the wind turbines can coexist with their operation and provide additional sources of revenue to help maintain the ranch, as well as continue their land operation.  She stated that she has been extremely impressed with S Power through their representatives. Clifford Qotsaquahu, Hopi Tribal Council, Hopi Energy and Water Committee and Hopi Land Commission, spoke in support of this project.  He spoke about the cultural concerns and requested that S Power give this presentation to the Hopi Tribal Council.  He indicated that they do have a study on the eagles, and the flight patterns are obvious. He stated that they requested that an archeologist be present during the surveys and the walkthrough to make sure that the sites which will be disturbed would clearly meet the elements of DAPRA and the Archeological Site Protection Act of 1979 and also with Hopi Tribe's own cultural preservation requirements.  He indicated that wind turbines are an eyesore, but it is beautiful that they create energy.  Dale Sinquah, member of the Hopi Tribe, said that the flow of communication was not there.  He spoke about the importance of the eagles and asked that the company speak to the Hopi Tribe which has the expertise. He indicated that the two-way communication on this project did not manifest itself. He indicated that they are not totally against it, but do not totally agree with it due to the fact that they were not fully consulted.  Bob Schlesinger, Vice-mayor of City of Winslow, indicated that the Winslow City Council is very excited about this project.  Chairwoman Whitesinger closed the public hearing .

Supervisor Thompson asked if the applicant will continue discussions with the Hopi Tribe, and the applicant indicated that they have continued to send correspondence and informational packets to the Chairman. He indicated the Hopi Renewable Energy Office will be receiving an email and mail package, and they are fully prepared to speak with the Hopi Tribe. The applicant spoke about the eagle flight plan surveys.

Supervisor Williams made a motion to adopt  Resolution Number 34-19 approving a Special Use Permit allowing for the construction of approximately 3,650 acre, 477 MW wind energy generation and storage facility on parcels that are zoned A-General or RU-1, APN 110-01-004, & 110-01-005, portions of Township 15 North, Range 15 East, Section 22, 26, 27, 34, 35 and 36 in the Chevelon Canyon area; motion seconded by Supervisor Jack . Vote unanimous approving the motion.
