duties; term B.  If the number of persons who file nominating petitions for an election to fill
precinct committeeman positions is less than or equal to the number of precinct committeeman
positions, the county board of supervisors may cancel the election for those positions not sooner
than seventy-five days before the election and appoint the person who filed the nominating
petition to fill the position. If no person has filed a nominating petition to fill a position, the
position is deemed vacant and shall be filled as otherwise provided by law. A precinct
committeeman who is appointed pursuant to this subsection after filing a nominating petition shall
be deemed an elected precinct committeeman. F. The term of office of a precinct committeeman is
two years and begins on October 1 after the primary election at which the precinct committeeman
was a candidate and continues until October 1 after the following primary election at which a
precinct committeeman is elected.

Department: Board of Supervisors
Motion before the Board:
Amendment to the Intergovernmental Agreement for Court Collection and Criminal Justice
Services
Background:
Navajo County has existing intergovernmental agreements with Winslow, Holbrook,
Snowflake/Taylor, Show Low, and Pinetop-Lakeside for court collections and criminal justice
services for criminal misdemeanors.  The intergovernmental agreement began on July 1, 2011,
and is part of a larger initiative to benefit the public by leveraging municipal and County resources
together to provide justice services.  The proposed amendment includes the following changes: 
extends the agreement one (1) year beginning on July 1, 2019; includes an annual assessment of
$5,430 to account for expenses related to prosecution services for costs such as case management,
computers, software, and supplies; and amends the Withdrawal / Termination section of
agreement from 120 days written notice to 60 days written notice.

AMENDMENT TO 
INTERGOVERNMENTAL AGREEMENT FOR COURT 
COLLECTION AND CRIMINAL JUSTICE SERVICES 
 
Between Town of Pinetop -Lakeside and Navajo County 
 
THIS AMENDMENT IS MADE THIS _______ DAY OF ______, 2019 between the Town of 
Pinetop-Lakeside, an Arizona Municipal Corporation, (“City”), and Navajo County, a political 
subdivision of the State of Arizona, (“County”). 
WHEREAS, the City and the County previously entered into an Intergovernmental Agreement 
for Court Collection and Criminal Justice Services on July 1, 2011 for court collections and 
criminal justice services for criminal misdemeanors; and 
WHEREAS, the City and County desire to extend the term of the Intergovernmental Agreement 
for Court Collection and Criminal Justice Services for an additional one (1) year term. 
NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the mutual promises contained herein, the parties agree 
as follows: 
1. Section 2; TERM / EFFECTIVE DATE is amended as follows: 
 
The term of this IGA shall be for one (1) year effective July 1, 2019. 
 
2. Section 4; RESPONSIBILITIES OF EACH MUNICIPALITY is hereby amended by adding 
Section 4(A)(4)(d), which shall provide as follows: 
 
d.  The Municipality’s Fee shall include an annual assessment of $5,430 to account for 
expenses related to prosecution services including, but not limited to:  case 
management software, IT equipment, software, supplies, printing, and postage. This 
assessment is due on or before August 1 st, 2019. 
 
3. Section 5; WITHDRAWAL / TERMINATION is amended by adding subsections (A) and (B) 
as set forth below: 
 
A.) The Navajo County Attorney’s Office has the right to withdraw from the 
Agreement's provision that it provide prosecution services for any criminal case 
arising within the municipal limits upon giving the municipality sixty (6 0) days 
prior notice. Upon termination of the Navajo County Attorney's Office obligation 
to prosecute under this Agreement, the municipality will be responsible for 
prosecuting all cr iminal cases that occur within their municipal boundaries. Upon

12 North, Range 17 East, Section 29, in the Overgaard area. 
Background:
The applicant is requesting a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction of a recreation
vehicle and boat storage facility where the subject parcel is located immediately south of
Highway 260.  The Heber-Overgaard Area Plan encourages vegetative buffer zones, architectural
design to maintain rural setting and to keep as much as possible of the natural environment.  The
applicant has included a 15ft landscape buffer along Hwy 260 which they intend to plant trees, and
agreed to 7 ft fencing that will be in alignment with the natural and rustic setting of the area.  As
the property sits directly adjacent to the highway, commercial uses are allowed
and encouraged.  Storage uses should be screened from view from rights-of-way. 
The proposed project fits within the recommendations of the Navajo County Comprehensive
Plan.  The proposed use would be permitted in the C-R zoning district by approval of a Special
Use Permit.  The storage facility will create a slight increase in traffic and applicant is working
with ADOT on improving existing driveway.  Planning & Zoning Commission recommended
approval to the Board by a vote of 6 yeas and 0 nays.

2.

 Legal Desc: Township 12 North, 17 East, Section 29 of the Gila and Salt River Meridian in 
  the Overgaard area. 
 
 District: IV

 Parcel Size: Approximately 4.58 & 4.60 acres respectively 
 
STATED REASON FOR REQUEST:  
Mark Dahl is requesting a Special Use Permit to allow for the building and operation of a storage 
facility accommodating RVs and boats. 
 
GENERAL CHARACTER OF NEIGHBORHOOD:  
 
The subject properties are zoned C-R for the front portions of the property that extend from 300’ from 
the centerline of adjacent State Route 260.  The remainder of the properties, to the west, are zoned 
R1-10.  To the south of the properties is Tall Timbers Park.  Toward the northwest and across State 
Route 260 are more properties that are zoned C-R for the first 300’ from State Route 260’s centerline 
with R1-10 zoning extending on the rest of the property.  Behind this property, to the west, are some 
single-family residential properties, zoned A -General and R1-10.

<!-- page 115 -->
PROJECT DESCRIPTION:  
 
The applicant has provided the following information to Staff regarding the intent of their project (see 
site sketch): 
 
1. The proposed project will allow for RV and boat storage, including: 
a. Travel Trailers 
b. Class A Motorhomes  
c. Toy Haulers 
d. Fishing Boats 
 
The applicant has indicated that only their family will be operating this property, at times through 
remote management by computer.  It is their intent for this facility to be available for use 24 hours a 
day, 7 days a week.  The plans given to Staff show that the parking at this facility will be uncovered. 
 
Within the site plan, there is an indicated landscaping buffer of 15 feet between the facility and State 
Route 260, which adjoins the property.

<!-- page 119 -->
ZONING DISTRICTS: 
ARTICLE 15 – (C-R) COMMERCIAL-RESIDENTIAL ZONING DISTRICT 
 
Section 1501 – Purpose  
 
The principle purpose of this zoning district is to provide for shops and services in convenient 
locations to meet the daily needs of families in the immediate residential neighborhoods and 
wholesaling or distribution activities in locations where there is adequate access to major streets or 
highways.  Principal uses permitted in this zoning district include retail and wholesale commerce, 
commercial entertainment, and some uses of a light manufacturing nature. 
 
ZONING ORDINANCE: 
Article 20 – Special Uses and Planned Unit Developments 
 
Section 2001 – Special Uses  
32. Storage of manufactured homes, travel trailers, boats and aircraft. 
 
COMPREHENSIVE PLAN/AREA PLAN:  
Comprehensive Plan 
4.0 Land Use Element 
4.7 Goals and Policies 
Goal 5: Improve the overall appearance of the County. 
 
Policy 5-A: Maintain natural scenic qualities of the County by identifying and protecting 
cultural resources; protecting wildlife habitat; natural plant communities and riparian areas; 
and encouraging protection of scenic vistas. 
- From the submitted site plan, it appears that it is the applicant’s intent to keep as many 
existing trees on the property as possible, as well as provide a landscape buffer area 
between the facility and the corridor along State Route 260. 
 
Goal 6: Maintain compatible land use patterns while encouraging free market development. 
 
Policy 6-B: Consider, if needed, providing for buffers between different land uses including, 
but not limited to, setbacks, walls or fences, and landscaping. 
- The applicant’s submittal indicates that there will be a landscaped buffer between the 
facility and the roadway and a fence on the rear of the proposed site to buffer the use from 
any adjacent residential uses behind the property. 
 
Policy 6-C: Require paved or other appropriately surfaced access to commercial and 
industrial uses.

<!-- page 120 -->
- Access to the property comes off the main paved thoroughfare in Heber-Overgaard, State 
Route 260. 
 
Goal 7: Facilitate commercial and industrial development, including business and technology 
uses, where appropriate to increase the sales tax and employment opportunities. 
  
Policy 7-A: Encourage strategic placement of commercial development so as to attract and 
locate shopping and employment uses that have paved or other appropriately surfaced access 
and are easily accessible, particularly in or near established communities and cities, and in 
communities with air and/or rail access. 
- As noted prior, this property has an appropriately paved and sized point of access for its 
operation through State Route 260. 
 
5.0 Circulation Element 
 
5.4 Goals and Policies 
 
Goal 1: Provide a range of circulation options that are safe and efficient and that complement 
local communities and the natural environment. 
  
Policy 1-B: Coordinate land use and circulation planning to encourage comprehensive and 
efficient development and growth patterns that support adjacent land uses, complement the 
character of adjacent communities and neighborhoods, and mitigate impacts on the natural 
environment. 
- As this property sits directly adjacent, the usage as an RV and boat storage facility is 
appropriate given that this corridor is mainly intended and used for commercial and storage 
purposes.  
 
Heber-Overgaard Area Plan 
 
The subject parcel is within the Heber-Overgaard Planning Area and, as such, the Heber-Overgaard 
Area Plan must be consulted. 
 
Land Use Element  
 
Goal: Encourage retail, wholesale, service, and office uses with limited manufacturing 
capabilities.  
 
Objective 1: Provide a vegetative buffer zone if a commercial area is to be located adjacent to 
residential area. 
- The applicant will be providing fencing on the rear of the site to buffer for residential 
properties, which, regardless, are not directly adjacent to this site. 
 
Objective 3: Architectural design of buildings to be controlled to maintain a rural setting. 
- The plans submitted for this project show that is intended to provide a natural and rustic 
theme/setting so as to fit in with the character of the Heber-Overgaard area. 
 
Objective 4: Keep as much as possible of the natural environment. 
- The applicant intends to keep all existing trees on the property that are possible and would 
not be required to be removed in order to set up the facility.

<!-- page 121 -->
Objective 5: Locate commercial areas for services and retail businesses convenient to 
residential areas and locate on principal arterial, minor arterial, or collector roadways. 
- This commercial business is located along the principal arterial road for the area, State 
Route 260. 
 
Objective 6: Land area to encompass up to 5 acres in commercial developments. 
- The footprint of this commercial development is approximately 2.3 acres in size, well 
under the recommended allowance of 5 acres for commercial developments. 
 
Analysis:  
 
The applicant has provided plans for an RV and boat storage facility, containing 51 spaces and 
encompassing an area of approximately 2.3 acres as indicated by the applicant.  
 
This property is located within the Heber-Overgaard Planning Area, meaning that both this Area Plan 
and the Navajo County Comprehensive Plan must be used to assess the suitability of this project.   
 
The proposed project meets goals and policies laid out in the Comprehensive Plan as it is of a suitable 
size for a commercial development, is looking to screen  itself and maintain the views of a major 
county corridor, fits in with nearby uses, and has the proper access and location type for a commercial 
business. 
 
This project also meets the goals and objectives of the Heber-Overgaard Area Plan as it will be 
buffered from any nearby residential lots, is looking to maintain a rural appearance, and is 
maintaining/enhancing the existing natural environment on the property. 
 
As of July 1 st, no calls have been received regarding this proposed Special Use Permit. 
 
During the June 27th, 2019 Planning and Zoning Commission meeting, Planning and Zoning staff 
recommended that the Navajo County Planning and Zoning Commission make the recommendation 
of approval of this Special Use Permit, subject to stipulations.  In that meeting, the Commission did 
unanimously make the recommendation of approval of this Special Use Permit to the Board of 
Supervisors, subject to the stipulations laid out within this Staff Report.

1. The item has been properly noticed and posted in compliance with Arizona Revised Statutes 
and Article 29.

11th, 2019.  The property was then posted with public notices on the same day.  This item was also 
posted in the Holbrook Tribune on June 12th, 2019.   
 
2. The proposal is in compliance with the Comprehensive Plan. 
The proposal meets the goals and policies of both the Navajo County Comprehensive Plan and the 
Heber-Overgaard Area Plan, which is relevant given its location. 
 
3. The proposed use is compatible with the permissible uses in the zoning district in which the 
property is located. 
The portion of the properties for which the Special Use Permit is being applied are zoned

4. The proposed use is compatible with the current and likely future uses of properties in the 
vicinity of the proposed use.

future uses of these properties.

Given the screening and access improvements committed to by the applicant, as well as the

minimal adverse effects on the public health, safety, and general welfare of properties in the 
vicinity.

A RESOLUTION OF THE NAVAJO COUNTY BOARD OF SUPERVISORS 
APPROVING A SPECIAL USE PERMIT FOR OLD WEST RV (MARK DAHL) 
 
WHEREAS, an application for a Special Use Permit was duly filed on May 31, 2019   by Mark 
Dahl requesting a Special Use  Permit on the current C -R zoned parcel to allow for the 
construction of a recreational vehicle and boat storage facility south of Highway 260; and 
 
WHEREAS the application concerns the following real property in Navajo County: 
APN: 206-15-010A & 206 -15-025 a portion of T12N, R17E, Section 29, in the Overgaard area ; 
and 
 
WHEREAS the application was considered by the Navajo County Planning & Zoning 
Commission at a duly noticed public hearing on June 27, 2019; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Navajo County Board of Supervis ors after considering the testimony and other 
evidence presented at the hearing, as well as the recommendations of staff, found that the Special 
Use Permit , is consistent with the public health, safety and gener al welfare and should be 
approved subject to the conditions set forth herein; and 
 
NOW, THEREFORE BE IT R ESOLVED the Board of Supervisors  hereby approves the 
Special use permit, subject to the following conditions: 
 
1. The applicant shall obtain any required Navajo County permits, including, but not 
limited to, building, sewer connection, grading, drainage, and sign permits. 
2. All lighting on the property shall face inward, shielded, be motion-activated, and be in 
conformance with the Navajo County Lighting Ordinance. 
3. The permitted Special Use shall be allowed to occur per the attached/approved site 
plan.  Any expansion or change of the Special Use shall require an amendment to this 
Permit. 
4. A 7’ fence shall be required surrounding the special use.  This fence shall be required 
to be a solid, opaque fence along State Route 260 and the southwestern side of the 
fencing enclosure, which is adjacent to residential lots.  On the northwestern and 
southeastern sides of the fencing enclosure, the fencing shall be opaque. 
5. Landscaping between the facility and State Route 260 shall be installed and irrigation 
provided to establish. 
6. No RV’s, trailers, or boats shall be placed on site until all site improvements are 
complete and approved by the County. 
7. Placement of the storage facility shall comply with all setback distances for the 
Commercial-Residential Zoning District.

8. The applicant shall proceed with a lot line adjustment to put the entire project on one 
lot prior to opening for business. 
9. Ingress and egress easement shall be dedicated over the shared driveway indicated in 
the site plan. 
10. If the permitted Special Use is not utilized within a twelve (12) month period, from the 
date of Board approval, this permit shall become null and void. 
11. This Special Use Permit shall run with the land pending compliance with all County, 
State, and Federal requirements.

Dear Members of the Navajo County Board of Adjustment:
We are requesting a special use permit and a zoning change for our property at 2883 Little Hawk 
Trail in Overgaard, AZ. We purchased this property in November of 2012. At that time the 
buildings included the main house, a garage, a shed, and a game room which has come into 
question as a livable space and, thus, is labeled as a “guest house.” We have documentation of 
building permits for the property from before we purchased it. In 2015 we applied for and 
received a permit for a metal carport beside the garage, and we currently have a permit pending 
for another metal building. This permit has been put on hold until the current situation becomes 
resolved. (See attached document for additional information.)
When we purchased the property, the “guest house” had a kitchen and a bathroom and because 
of this, is now apparently incorrectly zoned due to a change in zoning in recent years. We were 
not aware of this until it was brought to our attention in March of this year by Sandra Phillips, 
Assistant County Engineer/Planning Manager. We have been in contact with Sandra since then, 
and she came to our property to do a walk-through of the “guest house.” At that time she 
recommended in order for us to bring our property into compliance with current zoning 
regulations that we apply for a special use permit and a change of zoning from Rl-10 to RU-1.
We are high school teachers in Phoenix and have been using this property as a summer and 
weekend home. We do not plan to use the property any differently going forward. We are 
asking for the special use permit and change in zoning in order to be in full compliance with 
Navajo County.
We thank you for your consideration of our applications and look forward to getting this issue 
resolved.
Daniel M. Miller and Sheryl L. Filliater

STATED REASON FOR REQUEST:  
 
Daniel Miller & Sheryl Filliater are requesting a Zone Change from R1-10 (Single-Family Residential 
– 10,000 square feet minimum) to RU-1 (Rural – 1 acre minimum) to be able to bring themselves into 
compliance with Navajo County’s Zoning Ordinance.  Should the Commission approve this Zone 
Change, the applicant would then have to apply for a Use Permit to bring their already existing Guest 
House into compliance with the Zoning Ordinance.

ANALYSIS: 
 
The applicant’s property is currently zoned R1-10 and has a guest house, which contains a kitchen and 
bathroom.  According to Navajo County’s Zoning Ordinance, guest homes are not allowed within the 
R1-10 zoning category. 
 
Servant and caretaker quarters are allowed within RU -1 zoning, subject to obtaining a Use Permit.  
The applicant has indicated that the ability to obtain a Use Permit for the existing guest house on their 
property in order to bring their property into full compliance with Navajo County Building and 
Zoning standards is their reasoning for applying for their Zone Change from R1-10 to RU-1. 
 
The existing guest house on the property was permitted in Navajo County in 1997, despite not being 
an allowed use within the property’s R1-10 zoning.  In February of 2019, a permit was applied for to 
add another carport to the subject property.   However, as there is an illegally nonconforming structure 
on the piece of property, no further structures can be placed on the property prior to bringing these 
structures into conformance.  This is indicated within the Zoning Ordinance under Section 2603, 1, 
which states: 
  
A non-conforming use of land, building or structure shall not be enlarged, expanded, 
reconstructed or structurally altered unless such enlargement, extension, reconstruction or 
structural alteration, and further use of such property conform with the regulations of this 
Ordinance for the zoning district in which such property is located 
 
The Ordinance goes on to note under Section 2603, 2: 
 
 2. For the purposes of this section “expansion” means the following: 
  a. The construction of new buildings.  
 
As the subject parcel is completely surrounded by a neighborhood of R1-10 zoning and a change of 
zoning would solely benefit the landowner, while also providing privileges not available to adjacent 
landowners, an approval of this Zone Change would raise concerns over spot zoning. 
 
As of July 1, numerous calls have been received regarding this request.  All but two of these calls 
have been neighbors inquiring, with no objections given over the request.  Two calls have been 
received that voiced their support for this request. 
 
During the June 27th, 2019 Planning and Zoning Commission meeting, Planning and Zoning staff 
recommended that the Navajo County Planning and Zoning Commission make the recommendation 
of denial of this Zone Change, due to concerns over spot zoning.  In that meeting, the Commission 
made the unanimous decision to make the recommendation of approval of this Zone Change to the 
Board of Supervisors.
