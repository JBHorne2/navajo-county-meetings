ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES :

a.

Financial Update and FY2020 Budget Development Plan:   Bryan Layton gave a Financial Update and FY2020 Budget Development Plan.  He spoke about the financial threats, including the loss of sales and property tax from Kayenta Mine and Navajo Generating Station, an increased cost for the State Retirement System and the impact of minimum wage increases and wage compression. He further spoke about the uncertain revenues, including inmate revenues, flood control through flexibility language and a one-time relief from the state. He presented a 5-year forecast which shows that if we do nothing for five years, we would show a negative $14 million.  He indicated that because of a strong State economy which allows us some flexibility, they are proposing taking a 2-year approach to this and indicated that there needs to be collaboration. He stated that we will be losing a lot of current resources and will not be able to provide services as we currently do.  He spoke about some suggestions which have come forth, including advocacy at the State Legislature, more regional partnerships, jail fees and inmate medical costs, alternative work schedules, how to manage capital assets, support for local initiatives and special projects, stipends and retirement incentives. He spoke about the proposals in development, such as Administration would reduce 4 positions, would shift roles and responsibilities to existing staff, and there would be loss of some functions. He stated that there is an opportunity to build upon the successes that they've had in wellness and our health plan and assign more duties in the wellness area to our HR Tech and split fund that salary.  He indicated that the duties of one of the Board of Supervisors secretary deals a lot with HURF-related items, and there's an opportunity to responsibly split fund that salary.  He indicated that facilities would reduce 2 vacant positions, which would be a total of 5 positions that they've reduced over the past 3 years.  He stated that Facilities Management would focus on preventative maintenance and custodial duties; and if contract work is needed, we would have to go to an outside contractor.  He indicated that for Indigent Defense, they are proposing to eliminate the Legal Defender's Office, leverage vacancies and transition our contract attorneys to an annual basis.  He stated that Sheriff and his staff are currently developing recommendations, but they've indicated that jail fees and inmate medical costs will be looked at, and they will discontinue prisoner transport to the Justice Courts. He stated that the County Attorney and staff are currently developing recommendation.  He indicated that the County Attorney has informed the Board of Supervisors that he will not be able to continue the current arrangement regarding the Magistrate Courts with our partner cities and towns. Vice-Chairman Whiting asked if the cities and towns have been made aware of the County Attorney's position, and Mr. Kephart indicated that he is not aware that the letter was sent to the cities and towns.
Supervisor Thompson asked about the responsibility to reach out to the public and the cities and the towns regarding how this is going to impact them, and Mr. Layton indicated that we will need to sit down with each city and town individually, including the police chiefs, and talk these items through.

Brad Carlyon is present at 9:25 a.m.

Mr. Layton stated that as to the Treasurer, Assessor, Recorder, Health, Administration, Human Resources, Clerk of the Board and Superintendent of Schools, we will identify some natural connections, including having a consolidated front desk operation and establish a one-stop approach at the Holbrook Complex.  Mr. Layton stated that the plan is to come to the Board frequently and update the Board on proposals.  He provided the Board with a revenue analysis.  He spoke about possible revenue increases, including  jail fees and inmate medical costs, annual adoption of maximum property tax levy and some fines and fees.  He stated that none of these are an answer, but they are a tool set that we have.  He spoke about the next steps being to continue to develop reduction proposals, offices and departments to develop 2-year budgets and presentation of 2-year budgets by elected officials and department directors to the Board.

Vice-Chairman Whiting asked whether the correspondence which Mr. Carlyon sent to the Board regarding the magistrate courts has gone to the cities and towns, and Mr. Carlyon indicated it has not. Supervisor Thompson spoke about this being a similar experience to what we went through several years ago, and employees helped out by reducing their salary by 2%.  He stated that we had relied on creating a jail district which failed, and we will not be able to provide the same services.  He asked about the idea of going back out again for a jail district.  Mr. Layton indicated that there is time for us to go back out to the public regarding the formation of a jail district. Mr. Carlyon stated that the Board can take that to the public at any time, and he believes that if the Navajo County Board of Supervisors puts it before the public sometime in 2019, we will still be seeing the benefit of the maintenance of effort language currently in the statute.  Supervisor Thompson asked Mr. Layton to continue to look into moving forward with creating a jail district,  and Mr. Layton stated that we can look into that and come to the Board next meeting with a timeline and detail of what that might look like. Supervisor Jack asked how close we were to getting the jail district passed, and Mr. Kephart indicated that we fell about 162 votes short of passing. Vice-Chairman Whiting stated that hewould be interested in hearing more about the conversation and what the outreach would be for a second time to educate the public. He indicated that he would also like to hear about the partnerships with the cities and towns. He stated that he repeatedly had people tell him that the ballot language led them to believe that we were looking to build a new jail and inquired whether we could adjust that language. Supervisor Thompson stated that he is concerned with how are we going to approach the amount of funding that is  going to be reduced annually which will reduce the amount of services we provide.  He indicated that he would look at it as giving the public/stakeholders an opportunity to look at this again.  He stated that he wants to have these conversations again with our partnerships. Chairwoman Whitesinger spoke about the effect of our budget is far reaching into our communities, including the Navajo Nation, the White Mountain Apache Tribe and our cities and towns.  She stated that she appreciates the communication and the efforts to keep the Board informed and informing the public about how difficult this is to our county and the future of our county.

NORTHEASTERN ARIZONA INNOVATIVE WORKFORCE SOLUTIONS :

a.

Consideration of formation of the Northeastern Arizona Community Resource Network as a 501(c)3 Nonprofit that will be operated through Navajo County:   Stephanie Ray discussed Items A, B, and C. She spoke about the plan to incorporate Northeastern Arizona Community Resource Network (NACRN) as a 501(c)3 Nonprofit, develop a web portal, create multiple community access points and establish a community resource center.  She stated that a coordination of efforts is a quantifiable solution.  She spoke about establishing brick and mortar community resource centers where a variety of services could be offered.  She stated that the focal point of the resource centers would be the Arizona@Work Job Center and the entrepreneurial program.   She indicated that they are working with Moonshot at NACET with regard to entrepreneurship.  She stated that Phase One will be in Show Low, and Phase Two would be to establish a brick and mortar resource center in north Navajo County.  She spoke about the partners at the table and the wide range of services that could be provided. She stated that they are working up a business plan and funding plan to make sure that this is a project that is sustainable and does not require current county resources but could bring in additional resources.  She spoke about the plethora of grant opportunities and that there would not be an ask of the County for any cash funding. She stated the Arizona@Work partners that are under the local Workforce Development Board purview are legislatively required to cover infrastructure costs.  She further stated that the funding sources for center sustainability will be Arizona@work, WellCare/Care 1st Health Plan, NACRN members and foundation grants. Supervisor Thompson thanked Ms. Ray for her proposal and reaching out to the different partners and stated that the Navajo Nation would be a really good partner in this. He spoke about how the transportation barrier is always troublesome, therefore, much communication would be needed. Vice-Chairman Whiting thanked Ms. Ray, Paul Watson and the Workforce Development team for their efforts. He spoke about the presentations made and invited all to attend the RealAz meeting where this will be discussed more.  Vice-Chairman Whiting made a motion to form the Northeastern Arizona Community Resource Network as a 501(c)3 Nonprofit that will be operated through Navajo County; motion seconded by Supervisor Thompson .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE: Report from County Manager, County Attorney and Board members:  Glenn Kephart indicated that staff will research more about going out again for a jail district. He also spoke about budget meetings that have been scheduled with all of our elected officials. He further spoke about the strong team that we have and stated that he knows that we can get through this and work together. Supervisor Thompson wished everyone a belated Happy New Year and Happy Birthday to Vice-Chairman Whiting .  He spoke about meeting with the various communities and informing them about Navajo County projects.  He stated that there is concern about the referendum.  He spoke about the meetings he attended honoring Supervisor Jack as a council delegate. He spoke about meetings next week, including CSA and the State Transportation Board meeting and also a meeting with the Tomhaves this Friday. Supervisor Jack stated that the Navajo Nation is trying to save Navajo Generating Station. He indicated that once that closes, Navajo Nation will lose about 30% of their revenues, and Hopi will lose about 75% of their revenues, and, of course, Navajo County will be impacted.  He thanked his fellow Board members, staff and Navajo County employees. Vice-Chairman Whiting stated that we are going into a tough season and are going to have to have some hard conversations. He indicated that every relationship begins and ends with trust, and in order for us to find a path forward, it's going to require us to have honest conversations, not only with the public, but with other elected officials throughout the cities that we represent and internally with the various departments that we have here.  He stated that we have to learn to trust each other to have open and candid conversations in the realm of trust so that we can find our way forward.  He indicated that he and the Chairwoman are looking to meet with many of the departments over the coming weeks and have some of those conversations, and he invited each of the departments here internally to start us on the right path by doing that.  He indicated that he wants to partner with the department heads and elected officials and be able to lean on them and trust them.   He stated that he does not want to play the games and have the fights, but wants to work with the departments because, in doing so, that's the only way we're going to find the best solutions.  He indicated that if there are games going on or fighting that's going on, unfortunately, he will not be able to trust that department anymore and will have to make the decisions without their help.  He stated that we need partnership and need to work with the departments, and he is committed wholly and fully to listen to the departments and work with the departments to find out what's best for them moving forward.  He indicated that the same is true for each of the cities and towns that he represents and for all of the members and citizens that he represents.  He stated that he wants to listen and find the way forward.  He indicated that we have to have a balanced budget, but it will be so much better if the meetings were constructive and productive and that we were working together rather than being divisive and manipulative and trying to throw rocks in each other's departments.  He stated that he has no interest in that and does not want to go into one department and have them talk to him about another department.  He indicated that he sees no value in that, and if that starts to happen, he will not be listening.  He stated that he wants to listen to each department head and elected official about their department over which they have control and what they want to do and work with them to find solutions.  He indicated that he has no interest in gamesmanship.  He stated that he wants to have a candid and honest conversation because that is the way we move forward together rather than divisive and apart. Chairwoman Whitesinger spoke about a women's organization of which she is a part where they try to empower women and spoke about a pancake breakfast with Santa Claus over the Christmas holiday.  She stated that they are working on their first scholarship to be given in the spring to young Tribal people within our communities.  She spoke about the tough conversations that are upcoming about the budget and the importance of understanding one another's department and the support that it offers our community.  She stated that, collectively, we are a family and need to help each other. She spoke about having apathy and understanding for our departments in being able to understand that our services are not about us, but about the public, and we need to ensure that we continue to provide the best services we can, even under challenging circumstances.  The Board sang Happy Birthday to Vice- Chairman Whiting .
