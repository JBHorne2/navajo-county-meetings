BOARD OF SUPERVISORS :

a.

Update on an Arizona Public Service Broadband Project :  Neil Traver, APS Division Manager for Northeastern Arizona, discussed the Arizona Public Service Broadband Project and shared a short presentation.  Mr. Traver indicated that APS has been a big proponent in supporting our communities with economic development, and there is a need for additional and more reliable broadband in the region.  He stated that APS has a purpose internally to bring broadband for their own systems which will then enable reliable broadband connectivity for our region and assist with economic growth.  He indicated that this will be done in three phases.  He spoke about the capacity that this will give the region.  He indicated that they will bring the fiber into the regions and will sublease the extra capacity to the telecom organizations.  He stated that this is a great project for our economic growth.  Supervisor Williams indicated his excitement for the possibilities that will come to the region through this project, including economic growth and quality of life for our community members and businesses. He thanked APS for their partnership on this. Supervisor Thompson asked about possible expansion, and Mr. Traver indicated that there should not be a need to expand again. Supervisor Thompson asked about the life span of the project, and Dominic Pagliuca, APS IT Senior Manager, indicated that it would be 20 to 30 years plus. Supervisor Jack asked whether this line will be underground or overhead, and Mr. Traver stated that it will be at the top of the transmission lines.  Supervisor Jack asked about a line which was supposed to lead to Hopi and if the Chapters could tie into that line, and Mr. Pagliuca indicated that they have nothing north of I-40 and suggested that they speak with NTUA. Glenn Kephart asked about the expectations of how this will benefit our business community, and Mr. Traver indicated that they will build out the main line, and others will be able to tap into that.  He stated that if Navajo County wants to upgrade their data capacity, then it would need to work with who provides that service capacity to connect to APS once it is through to Cholla.  Mr. Kephart thanked Mr. Traver. Supervisor Whitesinger thanked Mr. Traver and Mr. Pagliuca and spoke about the significant contributions to many organizations.

BOARD OF EQUALIZATION :  Supervisor Thompson made a motion to enter into the Board of Equalization; motion seconded by Supervisor Williams .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

a.

Discussion and action regarding Petition for Review of Real Property Valuation APN 210-44-182; Swearing in of Assessor, Appraisers and Petitioners : Melissa Buckley, Clerk of the Board, swore in the Assessor and the Petitioners.  Paul Bowden, homeowner, provided a handout to the Board of Supervisors and withdrew his letter which was mailed previously.  He stated that he believes his full cash value should be $457,376.  He spoke about the different comps which he used and those which the Assessor used.  He stated that he used his next-door neighbor as a comp, which the Assessor did not because the Assessor believes that his house is a higher quality than his neighbor's house due to the complex roof and perimeter, and the quality of building components and finish are better.  Mr. Bowden indicated that he does not believe that the complexity of his roof and perimeter is significantly different from his neighbor's. He stated that his neighbor's property and his property have very similar building components and finishes.  He indicated that two out of the three sales comps which the Assessor used are for single story homes, not two stories and are for smaller homes.  He stated that one of the three equity comps is for a single story home and is for a house that is two years newer than his property.  He indicated that two out of the six appraiser comps have a full cash value of $131 a square foot and $136 a square foot, which supports the $138.18 per square feet at which he is claiming his house should be assessed.  He stated, in summary, that his property has the same quality as his neighbors, the comps that the Assessor selected have been shown to be inconsistent and two out of the six comps that the assessor selected supports his opinion that the value of his house should be $138.18 a square foot. He indicated that he is asking the Board of Equalization to treat his property equally to his neighbor's property, since the quality of the two properties is similar, and lower his appraised value to $457,376 or $138.18 a square foot.  Mr. Bowden answered questions of the Board of Equalization.

Chris Frayer, Deputy Assessor, indicated that the subject property is a two-story, good qualtiy, single family residence, built in 2015 and is 3,310 square feet.  She stated that Mr. Bowden's basis for his petition is that the neighboring property, parcel 210-44-181, has a lower full cash value than his property and is a higher quality, but Mr. Bowden's home is listed as good quality and parcel 210-44-181 is listed as an average-plus.  She indicated that on March 14, 2019, she visited the subject property along with the petitioner's comparable, and determined that the subject property is, indeed, of higher quality than the neighboring property.  She stated that during the field inspection, she discovered errors in the original listing that when corrected resulted in the full cash value being reduced from $491,886 to $483,200, and the limited value was not reduced.  She indicated that they removed the land value and are just looking at the square foot of the improvement.  She stated that the adjusted full cash value is supported by sales of like properties in the same subdivision ranging from $155.67 per square foot to $176.25 per square foot.  With regard to the sales comps that were used, she indicated that they can only use what has sold, and the Assessor also found three equitable comparisons with the same quality ranking as the subject property and are within the same subdivision with full cash values ranging from $122.37 per square foot to $137.41 per square foot.  She stated the reason that she believes Mr. Bowden's next-door neighbor's property is inferior to the subject is based on roof complexity and complexity of perimeter.  She indicated that Mr. Bowden also has a larger entry and many porches and a bay.  She stated that the components on the interior of his neighbor's home are more in line with a good quality house and, perhaps, the Assessor needs to review that property rather than bringing down the quality of Mr. Bowden's home.  She indicated that the Assessor asks the Board to find no further reduction of the cash value of Parcel No. 210-44-172.

Supervisor Williams asked if the Assessor usually uses single story homes when valuing a two-story home, and Ms. Frayer indicated that they do.  She stated that a second story will result in a lower full cash value; however, they will use whatever sales are available with quality being the most important.  She stated that she chose to go with comparables which were similar in total square foot.  She indicated that she did try to address the construction year but was unable to find two-story homes in that construction year.  Supervisor Williams asked if the Assessor's Office would have used just the two-story homes as comps what the value per square foot would have been, and Cammy Darris, Assessor, stated that on the equity, we would be looking at roughly $125 a square foot; and on the sales, it would be $155 a square foot.  Supervisor Williams stated that if we just used the three comps that are two-story, what would the valuation per square foot be, and Ms. Darris stated that if we put the sale in there, it would be $155, $122 and $128, and that is just the improvement because they backed the land value out, so, they would have to use the $135 a square foot against the square footage of the home of 3310 and then the land value back into that for a total of about $497,000 using the sale, not the equity.  She stated that she is looking at $151 if she is just looking at those three two-story comps.  Supervisor Williams stated that if you take the three one-story homes out which have a higher value per square foot, the valuation per square foot should go down.  Ms. Darris stated that Mr. Bowden is basing his numbers just on equity.  She indicated if we just use the two-story homes and only the equity side, it would be roughly $451,000, and Mr. Bowden is asking for $457,000.   Supervisor Jack inquired regarding the quality of the neighbor's house, and Ms. Frayer stated that the neighbor's house is listed as an average plus, and the subject property is a good.

Supervisor Thompson inquired whether the petitioner has run into this issue before, and Mr. Bowden stated that he has appealed before but has not taken it to this step until now. Mr. Bowden indicated that the Assessor is stating that there weren't enough two stories for sale, but, to him, it is bad data.  He stated that if you can't support the two-story with the comps then don't have six comps.  He indicated that his neighbor has an average plus rating, and he has a good rating, and he thinks it should equalize, and if his neighbor is getting a break in 2020, he should get it, too.

Supervisor Williams stated that the Assessor's Office has trained appraisers who understand the standards and statutes, and he feels unqualified to question that; however, The Board has a legal obligation to evaluate what has been presented.  He indicated that he understands the petitioner's request and where he is coming from.  Based on what was presented, Supervisor Williams made a motion to accept Mr. Bowden's position to lower the assessed full cash value to $457,376, which is $138.18 per square foot; motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.  Chairwoman Whitesinger indicated that we rely heavily on our Assessor, and we appreciate the Assessor's Office and also the petitioner.

Supervisor Williams made a motion to return to regular session; motion seconded by Supervisor Thompson .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

TREASURER :

a.

Consideration and approval of the following items: Receipt of the Annual Report of Collections and Delinquencies in accordance with A.R.S. §42:18002; Settlement of Accounts; and Request for Authorization to Invest Funds Pursuant to A.R.S. §35-327 (G) :  Debra Kester discussed the item, indicating that the combined corrections to the roll resulted in lowering the levy by $15,212.17.  She stated that as of June 30th, we recovered roughly 97% of the real property tax levy.  She introduced Bob Moore, our portfolio manager, to give a presentation regarding the portfolio.  Mr. Moore stated that Navajo County's portfolio has a rating similar to U.S. Treasuries and U.S. agency securities.  He indicated that the portfolio is very liquid.  He spoke about the top 10 holdings and cash flow and maturity schedule.  He further spoke about the individual securities that are in the portfolio, and the yield to maturity at cost is at 2.20%.  He stated that the portfolio is generating above market rates.  He spoke about the Fed Funds and two-year Treasury yields and stated the global interest rates are plunging.  He stated that U.S. growth rates are good, and we had good inflation numbers.  He indicated that each recession, the Federal Reserve has cut interest rates on an average of 500 basis points and at this time, they only have 250 to work with.  He spoke about the interest rate reverse course and the stock market volatility.  Supervisor Williams thanked Mr. Moore for the education. Supervisor Thompson inquired where Navajo County stands, and Mr. Moore stated that Navajo County is in a very good position.  He stated that as leaders of the community, it's important to make sure there is a coordination between educators, business leaders, those who can identify skill sets, those who can teach skill sets and those who can help create jobs and making sure that we do something where we are coordinating the priorities with the budgetary priorities.  Supervisor Jack thanked Mr. Moore for the report andinquired what is going to happenif the trade war continues, and Mr. Moore indicated that it will be very hard for business owners to decide how and where to invest their money, and existing supply chains are being destroyed.  Ms. Kester stated that the Treasurer's anticipated C.P. Processing Fund collections for 2019-202 will be $10,000.  Supervisor Thompson made a motion to approve the Receipt of the Annual Report for Collections and Delinquencies in Accordance with A.R.S. §42:18002, Settlement of Accounts and Request for Authorization to Invest Funds Pursuant to A.R.S. §35-327 (G); motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
