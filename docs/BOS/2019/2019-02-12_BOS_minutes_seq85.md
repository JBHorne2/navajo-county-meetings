ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES :

a.

Budget Update :  Bryan Layton discussed the item and showed a brief presentation. He gave a recap of the curent financial reality which includes a $2.6 to $5.2 million loss of revenue and uncertain revenues.  He spoke about the 5-year forecast and what that looks like if we did nothing. He spoke about the two-year approach to balance the budget. He indicated that most analysts are predicting an economic slowdown in 2019.  He stated that the plan is to develop a $1.5 million reduction this year and a $2 million reduction the following year. He indicated that vacancies and attrition allow flexibility.  He stated that offices and departments have cut their budgets each year, and employees have gone with net pay decreases year after year.  He spoke about the expenses of hiring and retraining staff.  He provided a timeline for the jail district. Vice-Chairman Whiting asked about jail fees and medical costs to the cities and towns. Bryan Layton indicated that our jails fees to the cities and towns result when an inmate is charged with a misdemeanor, and the cities and towns are charged to house that inmate. He stated that what is most concerning to the County, as well as our partner cities and towns, is the issue of medical costs.  He indicated that when an inmate is housed and needs medical care, those costs right now are 100% on the County because once a person is in custody they lose AHCCCS coverage.  He stated that those costs would have to be shared with the cities and towns.  He indicated that the cost to transport an inmate to the hospital in the case of an emergency is $26,000 for the flight alone. Supervisor Thompson thanked Mr. Layton for bringing back the information which was asked for.  He stated that we have faced this challenge before, and we have done as much as we can, including our staff deciding to give up 2% of their salary themselves.  He indicated that we are looking to the partnerships to help us tell the story.  He stated that because of the closure of NGS, the coal mine and Cholla Power Plant, that will change how we provide services.

Gwendena Lee-Gatewood, Chairwoman of the White Mountain Apache Tribe, spoke to the Board about the relationship that the County has with the Tribe which is based on mutual respect and caring. She thanked Sheriff Clark, the Sheriff's Department and Navajo County.  She indicated that every one of us are here because we care. She stated that Navajo County has always been forthcoming to help the Tribe. She spoke about how our law enforcement accepts the responsibility of keeping us safe and puts their lives in danger every day. She thanked Sheriff Clark for his outstanding example of what a good law enforcement officer is and always being a good helper to the Tribe and asked that God always have his protective hand over Sheriff Clark.

President Jonathan M. Nez of the Navajo Nation thanked Sheriff Clark and Brad Carlyon for the clay pot.  He stated that he learned a lot from Mr. Carlyon and the Sheriff and the Board of Supervisors members. He spoke in support of the jail district.  He indicated that he was raised in Shonto, Arizona, and considers himself a permanent resident of Navajo County and still has his heart with the Navajo County constituents.  He stated that Proposition 419 was narrowly defeated.  He indicated that he doesn't think a lot of our constituents understood the major impacts to our budgets when they were voting on the proposed Jail District.   He spoke about how the Hopi Nation is looking at 80% of their general fund dollars going away due to the closure of the Navajo Generating Station.  He stated that we are not defying the vote of the people, but we have more information now, and we believe that the jail district can help supplement that shortfall to the general fund and continue that public safety partnership.  He stated that they are getting more officers on the street with cross-deputization, but if this jail district proposition does not pass, they would lose some of their public safety officers. He indicated that we are asking for the support of the Board of Supervisors to make this jail district a reality, which is a 1/3 cent sales tax increase. He stated that people from the Nation come into the border towns to spend their dollars.  He indicated that they do not have property taxes; however, they do purchase goods and services. He stated that we are doing our very best to save jobs and save the power plant, but the industry is going in a different direction now.  He indicated that we have always fought for PILT and impact aid in Washington DC, and he still fights for counties. He stated that as President of the Navajo Nation, he is there to assist, and a successful tribe is a successful nation which is a successful county and state. He thanked Sheriff Clark for the work he has done in helping our community.  He indicated that Sheriff Clark has a heart for all people and has gained our trust. He thanked Sheriff Clark, his wife and all of the deputies who have supported him.

SHERIFF'S OFFICE :

a.

Consideration of Resolution No. 13-19 to establish a Jail District in Navajo County for the purpose of providing a dedicated revenue stream to support the existing jail and relieve the general fund of the burden of supporting the entire cost of the jail:   Bryan Layton stated that the Sheriff is proposing the formation of the jail district and requested that the members of the  public have an opportunity to speak. Chairwoman Whitesinger opened call to the public.  Cory Johnson, Snowflake School Board and Snowflake Town Council, spoke in support of the proposed Jail District and stated that he will help to educate the citizens.  He thanked Sheriff Clark for his years of service. Daryl Seymore, Mayor of Show Low, 940 East Deuce of Clubs, spoke in favor of the proposed Jail District and expressed appreciation for the County coming to their study session.  He indicated that elected officials never really want to propose a tax; however, if this district is not created, the costs will affect his community, as well as the citizens and the city budget.  He indicated that Show Low City Council is behind this.  He stated that when Hopi gets their own jail, that will impact the County, as well. Bob Schlesinger and Samantha Criss, representing the City of Winslow, spoke in favor of the proposed Jail District. He expressed disappointment that it did not pass, but he feels that with more information and education to the general public, it will pass. Stephanie Irwin, 782 Navajo Lane, Mayor of Pinetop-Lakeside, spoke in support of the efforts to put the Jail District back out to vote for the citizens of Navajo County to decide and encouraged the County to do more education on the true ramifications of the cuts.  She indicated that the Pinetop-Lakeside Town Council does support these efforts and looks forward to its success. Phil Cobb, Mayor of the City of Holbrook, stated that the Holbrook City Council supports this jail tax.  He indicated that it is very important to the City and will cost the citizens a substantial amount of money if it doesn't pass.  He spoke about the importance of educating the citizens. Kerry Ballard, Vice-Mayor of Town of Snowflake, expressed appreciation to the County for looking at this Jail District again.  He indicated that the Snowflake Town Council supports the issue of the Jail District. He stated that if we have more education on the subject, more will be on board.  He expressed appreciation to Sheriff Clark from the community and from Mr. Ballard as a friend. David Smith, Mayor of Taylor, thanked the County for its partnership.  He indicated that Taylor Town Council feels that the proposed Jail District would relieve their town budget from having to pay fees.  He stated that they value the County's efforts to prosecute misdemeanors on their behalf.  He thanked Sheriff Clark and stated that he looks forward to working with the new Sheriff.

Lieutenant Emerson Lee from Dilcon PD stated that he is a product of Holbrook High School.  He indicated that cuts would have a tremendous impact to the community. He spoke about the partnerships in Navajo and Apache County and spoke in favor of the proposed Jail District and stated that he will do what he can to help.  Chief Dan Brown, Winslow Police Department, spoke in favor of Resolution 13-19 to put the proposed Jail District back on the ballot.  He spoke about the good effort of the County to get the word out.   He stated that in the City of Winslow, there was a 202% increase in how many people they booked in the Navajo County Jail.  He indicated that there are jail district taxes in most counties. He stated that there was some confusion at the last election, so, more education is encouraged. He indicated that if there is not a Jail District, it would be a huge cost burden on the taxpayers of Winslow. Chief Nathan Christensen, Holbrook Police Department, spoke in support of the Jail District.  He stated that if this does not pass, initial booking fees would go up 50% and daily fees would go up 36.65%.  He stated that last year, they booked over 450, and it cost $118,061.76 in fees.  He stated that without the jail district, they face over $200,000 in fees, not including prosecution of misdemeanors  He offered his support as the chief and publicly thanked Sheriff Clark for his mentorship.  He wished David Clouse success in the future.  Chief Joe Shelley, Show Low Police Department, 411 E. Deuce of Clubs, spoke in support of the sales tax increase.  He indicated that the sales tax increase would be shared both by our citizens and those visiting our area. He stated that we need to educate our residents all over to be sure that they know what this is about.  He indicated that this is a positive step for Navajo County.  He thanked Sheriff Clark.

Chief Jim Morgan spoke in support of the proposed Jail District on behalf of the Arizona Fire District Area One, the Northeastern Arizona Fire Chiefs Association and Pinetop Fire District.  He spoke about how some of their calls have a law enforcement component.  He indicated that all public safety officers are targets and need law enforcement to provide security so that they can provide the necessary care.  He spoke about the resiliency of this community.  He thanked Sheriff Clark and stated that it has been a pleasure to work with him and looks forward to working with the new sheriff.  Chief Bryan Savage, Timber Mesa Fire and Medical District, thanked Sheriff Clark and stated that he values the partnership and friendship.  On behalf of the Timber Mesa Fire and Medical District, as well as the Arizona Fire District Board, he spoke in support of the County putting the issue of the Jail District back on the ballot.  Brent Young, Joseph City Fire District, stated that they rely on the Sheriff's Department in their everyday calls. He spoke in support of the tax and what it will do for the County. Chief William McClusky, Heber-Overgaard Fire District, spoke about the great partnership with Sheriff Clark and the Sheriff's Department.  He stated that 25% of their 1000 calls involve a dual responsibility with the Sheriff.  He spoke in favor of Resolution 13-19 and stated that they will do what they can to help educate the community.  Chief Robert Garvin, Clay Springs/Pinedale Fire District, spoke in support of the jail district and thanked Sheriff Clark. Chief Willie Nelson, Taylor-Snowflake Fire and Medical Department, spoke in support of the jail district and spoke about the great relationship with the Sheriff's Department.

Dr. Mike Wright, resident of Pinetop-Lakeside and superintendent of Blue Ridge Unified School District, spoke about the impact to the schools if the proposition is unsuccessful.  He stated that the Treasurer's Department and the County Schools Office have been cut to the bone.  He indicated that the deadlines in school finances are hard and set, and there are real consequences.  He indicated that 85% of their revenue comes from local taxpayers, and until the tax bills go out, come back, are processed and then sent to the district office, they cannot make payroll.   He stated that they came within $1,000 of not making payroll because the staffs are shorthanded. He indicated that staff works very hard in the Treasurer's Office and the School Superintendent's office, but if we had to cut further, it would have a significant impact on the school district.  Mr. Wright stated that he will personally help in any way that he can. He indicated that times have never been tougher to help the most disadvantaged. Jalyn Gerlich, Navajo County Superintendent of Schools, stated that the impact of our current reality is their interconnection with the Treasurer's Office, and they are also shorthanded.  She stated that their motto is don't get sick because our schools need that payroll. She indicated that we are looking at back-up plans and contingency plans which are not sustainable.  She stated that our lines of credit for our districts can be impacted which impacts their budgets which is at a critical point.  Dr. Robbie Koerperich, superintendent of Holbrook Unified School District, stated that he is here to support any efforts to raise revenue in Navajo County, specifically, the proposed Jail District.  He indicated that they rely heavily on Navajo County for their school safety measure.  He handed out an overview of Navajo County Public Schools which impacts about 17,000 students and 2,400 employees. He stated that they count on Navajo County to issue payroll on a timely basis.  He expressed appreciation for the opportunity to support how he can help. He recognized Sheriff Clark for being very responsive. Brenda Thomas Martinez, Director of Finance for the Blue Ridge Unified School District and also representing all the districts in Navajo County, spoke about how heavily they rely on the Superintendent of Schools and the Treasurer's office.   She stated that they process all of their payroll vouchers every two weeks and accounts payable vouchers two to three times a week.  She spoke about the importance of property taxes being posted timely.  She stated that they pay about 225 employees every two weeks; and if they can't pay them, it would be devastating.

Amy Johnson, 974 W Pima Lane, Realtor, spoke about how a delay in recording time would take a toll on their business. Robin Jaeger, 3900 W Old Linden Road, current president of White Mountain Association of Realtors, stated that a delay in recording really just wouldn't work because when someone buys a home and the lender funds, the title company has 48 hours to release the money, or they send it back to the lender and the entire process would have to start over.  She stated that they do have political funds available to them and offered their support to help make sure the public understands what the proposition means to our county. Mikey Hallanor, Pioneer Title, stated that these budget issues affect every department in the County.  She indicated that real estate is heavily reliant on the Assessor's Office, the Recorder's Office and the Treasurer's Office.  She stated that the taxing district is greatly needed in order to support law enforcement.  She indicated that the real estate industry can help spread the word.

Ivan Sydney, former Chairman of the Hopi Tribe, stated that the closing of NGS and Peabody Coal Mine will result in an 80% decrease in revenue.  He indicated that they will survive.  He stated that they can help and will work together. He spoke about partnerships and collaborations on projects. He spoke in support of the Jail District. He thanked Sheriff Clark and Supervisor Thompson.  Chief Dan Barnes, Pinetop-Lakeside Police Department, spoke in favor of the jail district.  Earl Kester, Holbrook, spoke in support of the Jail District.  He stated that there are a lot of tourists who come to Navajo County who will also pay the sales tax.  Jarvis Williams, Kayenta Township, spoke in favor of Resolution 13-19.  He stated that Kayenta is taking steps to ensure public safety in the area.  He stated that he is also the Tribal Relations Manager for Peabody Western Coal Company.  He indicated that the Navajo Generating Station is being sought after by Navajo Transitional Energy Corporation to acquire both the generating station and Kayenta mine.  He stated that there are over 700 jobs at stake with millions in revenue.  He indicated that they will keep working hard to keep the revenue flowing.  He stated that they have been selling coal at an 8 million ton rate for the last 15 months. He indicated that when we look at coal energy, it fits in extreme weather.  He stated that he supports the County's efforts, and they will do what they can in Kayenta to get it passed. He expressed appreciation to Sheriff Clark. Sheriff Dedman, Apache County, spoke in support of the Jail District. He thanked Sheriff Clark for his leadership and for doing a job well done and his partnership with Apache County.  Bessie Allen, Pinon, spoke in support of the proposed Jail District.

Sheriff KC Clark stated that over his 32 years, he has made a lot of life and death decisions.  He indicated that yesterday, they cut over one-half million dollars in his budget and are being asked to do it again next year.  He stated that we did not do a good job with regard to Proposition 419.  He indicated that some are saying that we shouldn't go against the will of the voters; however, he is not sure that we are because we started too late with education.  He stated that the ballot was also a problem because the proposition was the last thing on the back page, and over 3,000 people did not cast a vote. He indicated that he heard from citizens saying they wanted this.  He stated that this is an important conversation to have with our citizens.  He indicated that a sales tax is a fair way to do this. He spoke about how we will not be able to staff the County Fair or the parade in Heber if these cuts are made. He requested the Board adopt Resolution 13-19.  David Clouse spoke about the proposed Jail District.  He stated that we have heard from a large array of community leaders and members that talked about how this will impact their individual communities, and Sheriff Clark spoke about how it will impact the Sheriff's Office.  He indicated that we are looking at a $500,000 cut, and our calls to the 9-1-1 dispatch center do not stop.  He stated that with these cuts, we are going to be a reactive agency as opposed to proactive.  He indicated that Sheriff Clark has been in every community, and we want those same things to continue throughout the County.  He spoke in support of taking this issue back to the voters.

Supervisor Whiting thanked all those who came and expressed their recommendations. Supervisor Whiting made a motion to adopt Resolution No. 13-19 to establish a Jail District in Navajo County for the purpose of providing a dedicated revenue stream to support the existing jail and relieve the general fund of the burden of supporting the entire cost of the jail.
Supervisor Thompson spoke about continuing to help the elderly and our young people. He thanked everyone for being present.   Supervisor Thompson seconded the motion. Supervisor Williams expressed his appreciation to those who came and spoke and stated that he is overwhelmed by the unity throughout the County. Supervisor Jack thanked everyone who is here. Chairwoman Whitesinger offered her appreciation for those who spoke today. She spoke about service to our communities. Vote unanimous approving the motion.

b.

Acceptance of Sheriff's Clark's Notice of Retirement effective February 12, 2019 and possible appointment of Deputy Chief David Clouse to fill the remainder of the unexpired term of the vacant office :  Sheriff KC Clark asked the Board to accept his retirement letter and appoint Deputy Chief David Clouse to fill the remainder of the unexpired term. Sheriff Clark stated that he has been with the Sheriff's Office for over 30 years, and it is his baby. He indicated that he has full confidence in handing it over to Deputy Chief Clouse.  He spoke about how Deputy Chief Clouse's grandfather, Glenn Flake, took a chance on him and then Sheriff Butler hired him full time.  He stated that he would be honored to give the badge back to the family who gave him the opportunity. Bessie Allen and Ramona Nellwood from the Pinon Community thanked Sheriff Clark and presented him with a bolo tie from the Pinon Community. Supervisor Williams made a motion to accept Sheriff Clark's notice of retirement effective February 12, 2019 and appoint Deputy Chief David Clouse as Navajo County Sheriff to fill the remainder of the unexpired term of the vacant office; motion seconded by Supervisor Thompson . Chairwoman Whitesinger spoke about Sheriff Clark and his service.  She stated that he has a big heart and serves people fairly.  She indicated that he has inspired her own leadership.  She spoke about the top of the mountain in Cibecue where people would stop and leave a prayer.  She stated that she left a little prayer for Sheriff Clark and his journey this morning. The Board of Supervisors recognized Sheriff Clark and presented him with a plaque and a Navajo handmade ring. Judge Robert J. Higgins stated that the Sheriff has been a great friend and a wonderful Sheriff to Navajo County. He indicated that Sheriff Clark has a great heart and has touched so many people.

Swearing-In of David Clouse by Judge Robert J. Higgins.

Sheriff Clouse thanked the Board of Supervisors for the vote of confidence and the trust they have enlisted with him. He stated that Sheriff Clark has become a dear friend. He thanked all the men and women in uniform today who go out and do this job.  He indicated that Sheriff Clark always gives credit to those who have done the work.  He thanked all those who have helped and supported him.  He spoke about a visit to National Law Enforcement Museum where there was a sense of brotherhood.  He stated that since he was a child, he wanted to be a Sheriff just like his grandad.  He indicated that when you put on the uniform, it changes you.   He spoke about the men and women who lose their lives to protect us every day.  He stated that Navajo County is a very supportive community, and these people have a tough job working in the jail and taking calls, and he will be their supporter. He thanked his wife, Angela, and stated that if it wasn't for her, he could not have achieved what he wanted to do.  He spoke about Sheriff Clark and how close they have become.  He stated that Sheriff Clark started our drug task force.  He indicated that Sheriff Clark is truly a man of God and loves the people of Navajo County.  He stated that Sheriff Clark did not want titles or accolades, but wanted to serve.  He indicated that Sheriff Clark has prepared and taught him.  He spoke about continuing to push forward and give quality service to all people of Navajo County.  He stated that this is a huge honor.

Patrick Redding, Homeland Security Investigations and DEA, recognized Sheriff KC Clark and presented him with a plaque. Terry Hill and Jack Latham, VFW Post 9907, recognized Sheriff KC Clark and presented him with a plaque. Supervisor Williams thanked Sheriff Clark and stated that it has been a blessing and a privilege working alongside him.  He stated that he is excited to have Sheriff Clouse on board. Supervisor Thompson thanked Sheriff Clark. Vice-Chairman Whiting thanked Sheriff Clark for his friendship, honesty and direct conversations and indicated that it has been a pleasure working with him.  He thanked Brenda, as well, for her support. He stated that he looks forward to working with Sheriff Clouse and feels that we are in good hands.  President Jonathon Nez spoke and recognized Sheriff Clark.  He spoke about taking care of the people and allowing his deputies to come out.  He spoke about them coming out in a Black Hawk to support our grandmas and dropping food out for his people. He thanked Brenda Clark for being a part of Sheriff Clark's successful career. On behalf of the Navajo Nation, President Nez recognized Sheriff KC Clark and presented him with a Pendleton Blanket. President Nez read a letter to Sheriff Clark.
