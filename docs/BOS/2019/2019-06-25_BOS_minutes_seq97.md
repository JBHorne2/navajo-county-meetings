PUBLIC HEARING: FINAL 2019-20 BUDGET PURSUANT TO §42-17104 :
Convene a Special Meeting of the Board of Supervisors for final budget adoption:   Supervisor Thompson made a motion to convene a special meeting of the Board of Supervisors; motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Consideration and possible adoption of Resolution Number 19-19 , approving the proposed final budget for fiscal year 2019-20 for Navajo County :  Paige Peterson discussed the budget and indicated that the budget schedules were published for two weeks, as well as the notice for public hearing, and she has not received any written comments.   Chairwoman Whitesinger opened the public hearing; there being no comments, closed the public hearing.  Supervisor Williams thanked the Finance Team, our Elected Officials and Department Directors and County employees for cutting $1.2 million from the budget with the anticipation of another $2 million to be cut next year.   Supervisor Williams made a motion to adopt Resolution Number 19-19 , approving the proposed final budget for fiscal year 2019-20 for Navajo County; motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Supervisor Thompson made a motion to return to regular session; motion seconded by Supervisor Williams .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

PUBLIC WORKS :

a.

Consideration of Task Agreement No. 17 to Memorandum of Agreement between the Bureau of Indian Affairs Department of Transportation, Navajo Region and Navajo County for the funding of purchasing gravel aggregate for BIA route 9101 within the Jeddito Community :  John Osgood discussed the item.  He indicated that this item serves as a great example of partnerships working together to have a positive impact in people's lives.  Supervisor Thompson stated that they have been working collaboratively on this item.  Supervisor Thompson made a motion to approve Task Agreement No. 17 to Memorandum of Agreement between the Bureau of Indian Affairs Department of Transportation, Navajo Region and Navajo County for the funding of purchasing gravel aggregate for BIA route 9101 within the Jeddito Community.  Supervisor Jack thanked Public Works for their efforts on this item.  The motion was seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

b.

Consideration of Task Agreement No. 18 to Memorandum of Agreement between the Bureau of Indian Affairs Department of Transportation, Navajo Region and Navajo County for maintenance repairs on BIA heavy equipment that County funded employees utilize for road maintenance :  John Osgood discussed the item indicating that this money will be used for a road grader to help repair roads.  He stated that the funding source for this item is the Highway User Revenue Funds.  Supervisor Jack indicated that this grader takes care of the school bus routes, as well as other roads.  Supervisor Jack made a motion to approve Task Agreement No. 18 to Memorandum of Agreement between the Bureau of Indian Affairs Department of Transportation, Navajo Region and Navajo County for maintenance repairs on BIA heavy equipment that County funded employees utilize for road maintenance.  Supervisor Thompson stated that this requires us to work together.  The motion was seconded by Supervisor Thompson .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

c.

Consideration of Intergovernmental Agreement between Navajo County and the Teesto Chapter documenting Navajo County's previous financial contribution to the Chapter as they proceed with their plans to rebuild the Teesto Community Center :  John Osgood discussed the item indicating that Navajo County contributed $20,000 in August of 2017 to the Teesto Chapter to assist the Chapter in their efforts to rebuild the Teesto Community Center which was destroyed in a fire.  He stated that the Teesto Chapter asked that we formalize the contribution that the County made in 2017.  Supervisor Thompson stated that we have always had a good partnership with the Navajo Nation and spoke of the background of this item.  Supervisor Thompson made a motion to approve the Intergovernmental Agreement between Navajo County and the Teesto Chapter documenting Navajo County's previous financial contribution to the Chapter as they proceed with their plans to rebuild the Teesto Community Center; motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

COUNTY BUSINESS UPDATE: Report from County Manager, County Attorney and Board members :  Glenn Kephart thanked Bryan Layton, Paige Peterson, the Board of Supervisors and all the Elected Officials for making this year's budget possible. Supervisor Williams spoke about the educational meetings regarding Proposition 421. He further spoke of the Passing of the Gavel Ceremony for the Chief Justice of Arizona who serve for 5 years. He spoke about the visions of the outgoing Chief Justice and the incoming Chief Justice.  He stated that this caused him to reflect that any service that he is providing is a benefit to those that he represents.  He expressed gratitude for serving with his fellow supervisors and Mr. Carlyon for providing legal counsel for the Board.  He stated that he was inspired by the service that we all provide to our community and for those who help make that happen. He thanked the Board of Supervisor's team:  Melissa Buckley, Donna Hurstrom, Debra Chapman and Preston Root.  Supervisor Thompson spoke about a ground-breaking ceremony in Dilkon for their Healthcare Center. He further spoke about the Arizona State Transportation Board meeting in Pinetop-Lakeside. Supervisor Jack thanked the County staff and his colleagues for all their work. He spoke about attending educational meetings regarding Proposition 421. He spoke about the Red Dog Project and is hopeful that it will get going soon. He thanked Bill Bess, John Osgood and Miles Begay for their work on that project. He spoke about the closing of Peabody. Vice-Chairman Whiting spoke about the annual ECO meeting and their future. He spoke about the annual legislative field trip that ECO does, and this year it will be in the Southern portion of Arizona. He spoke about events coming up in Navajo County and asked the public to get out and enjoy our communities. Chairwoman Whitesinger spoke about the Arizona Transportation Board meeting held in Pinetop-Lakeside and the conversation about Lion Springs.  She expressed appreciation for the discussion. She stated that a large part of industry relies on transportation and how we get that equipment and goods out to customers. She indicated that transportation relates to how we get children to school on a daily basis. She spoke about a meeting regarding Apache language revitalization. She further spoke about the educational meetings regarding Proposition 421.
