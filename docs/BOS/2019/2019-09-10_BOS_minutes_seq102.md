Presentation, discussion and direction to County Manager regarding First Knoll Cinder Pit :  John Osgood discussed the use of the First Knoll Cinder Pit. He indicated that for more than 20 years, Navajo County has been involved in mining operations at the First Knoll Cinder Pit, through a lease and operations permit from the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) Forest Service.  He stated that over time, the County's primary purpose for operating at First Knoll has evolved from processing and using the cinder material in its roadway maintenance program to using the material solely for its snow response operation, placing the cinders on public roads.  Mr. Osgood stated that while this arrangement has served the County well through the years, in early 2019 the County was approached by the U.S. Forest Service about the potential of consolidating mining operations in the area, a condition of which would be the return of the County's operational permit to the Forest Service.  He indicated that for the past several months, staff has been evaluating the feasibility of relinquishing the permit and offers the following points for the Board's consideration:

Mining and processing of aggregate material is not a core function of Navajo County's Public Works Road Maintenance program.  This type of operation is best conducted by private sector mining companies.

In addition to the costs incurred by the County for operationally processing the cinders for use on public roads, which includes, in some degree, crushing and screening the material, the County is responsible for significant site restoration and reclamation costs in the future.  Should the County return the permit to the Forest Serve, staff would look to negotiate with the new mining operation and the Forest Service as they take over those responsibilities.

The annual Mine Safety Health Administration (MSHA) training is required for personnel operating equipment in the mining area due to the employee safety and liability realities associated with a mining operation.  Again, since mining and processing cinders is not a core Public Works function, mining companies are best positioned to operate in these conditions.

According to the US Forest Service, consolidating the mining operations in the First Knoll area allows the cinder cone to come down over time in a systematic way from west to east on the property, which is more environmentally sustainable and the most aesthetically responsible approach as viewed from US 60.

He stated that with Board of Supervisors' direction to do so, their next steps in the process of returning the permit to the Forest Service would include working with the Forest Service, a new mining operator and other public agencies on the details associated with the availability of snow cinders for their respective snow response operations this winter and formalizing the requirements around maintaining and assuring the public purpose and use of the cinders on these public roads going forward.  He indicated that another thing they would work on is determining the processing cost for the cinders going forward.  He stated that the County has operational costs associated with processing the cinders, so, their goal in purchasing the material in the future is to be as close to cost neutral as possible, with the added benefits of reducing liability of our operators and redeploying resources currently used in the processing operation to more core road maintenance operations.  He indicated that they will coordinate with the Forest Service and mine operator in developing a restoration and reclamation plan for the First Knoll Cinder Pit area going forward.

Supervisor Williams asked if staff's initial assessment of this is that it could be close to cost neutral, and Mr. Osgood stated that yes, he feels that this would be cost neutral and will also reduce risk on behalf of Navajo County.
Vice-Chairman Whiting thanked Mr. Osgood and his team. Chairwoman Whitesinger inquired whether there is any permanent existing equipment there, Mr. Osgood indicated that everything there can be removed.  Chairwoman Whitesinger expressed appreciation to Mr. Osgood and his team.  Supervisor Williams requested that staff continue the conversations regarding relinquishing that operation to the Forest Service and continue those conversations as presented here today.
