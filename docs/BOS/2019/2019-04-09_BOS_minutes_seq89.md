BOARD OF SUPERVISORS :

a.

Recognition of Jason Owens for receiving the Arizona County Engineer's Association Esterbrooks Award for Engineering Support :  Bill Bess recognized Jason Owens for his outstanding work as an employee of Navajo County and what he's done by using this drone which he won as a door prize and turning it into a viable tool for Navajo County saving us thousands of dollars on surveys, documenting things that we need to quickly and having a photo survey record of it.  He stated that Brandt Clark and Mr. Bess nominated Mr. Owens for the 19th Annual Robert C. Esterbrooks Award for Engineering Support. He indicated that Mr. Owens is a great person between work and family, and he teaches students with this drone, as well as the NCSO.   He indicated that Mr. Clark and Mr. Owens set the Arizona Counties Insurance Pool (ACIP) standard for drone policies for the state.  Brandt Clark stated that in 2016, the FAA required someone to have a pilot's license to operate a drone for a government or commercial entity, and they changed it to a 107 license.  He indicated that Mr. Owens was the first person in our organization to do that, but ACIP said they would not insure us. He spoke about obtaining our own policy from a different entity and then being able to change ACIP's understanding of drones, and now ACIP does insure us.  He stated that all other counties who are members of ACIP are beneficiaries of this program that we developed in Navajo County under the direction of Mr. Owens.  He spoke about how we now have 6 drones in operation, and Mr. Owens works with Show Low High School and Linden Elementary with their drone clubs.  He indicated that Mr. Owens has changed the face of engineering and technology in the Public Works Department, the Sheriff's Office, Show Low High School and Linden Elementary School.  John Osgood thanked Mr. Bess and Mr. Clark for taking the time to recognize Mr. Owens for his professional contributions.   Mr. Owens thanked Navajo County and the Public Works Department and stated that it is a wonderful place to work with people who help us to grow as individuals and recognize when there is an opportunity to take technology and utilize that to make us as a county more efficient, operate in a safer manner and better serve the public.  He stated that we can gather about 2–1/2 to 3 miles of road a day instead of ½ mile. Supervisor Thompson thanked Mr. Owens for his work during the Bird Springs flood zone, and the next project is to fly over the First Mesa. Mr. Owens spoke about the First Mesa project. Supervisor Williams thanked Mr. Owens for who he is and what he does. Chairwoman Whitesinger expressed appreciation for the innovation brought to our community.

Lee Jack, Sr, Supervisor District I , is present.

b.

Presentation of APS Wildfire Mitigation :  Neil Traver, APS Division Manager, spoke about fire mitigation and the upcoming wildfire season.  He stated that it's been a beautiful winter.  He indicated that APS has changed some policies and procedures to be able to look at internally some of their infrastructure.  He indicated that one of their core values is safety.  He stated that they have looked at recent issues in California and have decided they want to change some things.  He indicated that during high-risk fire season, they have a team that looks at all of their distribution facilities and transmission lines and ranks them on how likely are they to start a fire and then based on those ratings, they go out into the field and change things.  He spoke about how the lines will reenergize if there is a branch or something else on the line for three times and then it locks down and requires a service man to go out and remove the obstruction and reenergize the line.  He stated that at the end of April or beginning of May, depending on conversations with the Forest Service, they will turn those devices off which means if a branch falls on a distribution facility, the entire distribution facility will shut down and stay off until an employee can be sent out and confirm that we don't have something on the line which will cause a spark when it is reenergized.  He indicated that instead of seeing a blinking on the line, we might have a power outage for an hour or two which will avoid having a fire start.
Vice-Chairman Whiting thanked APS for this proactive step. He spoke about the conversation about biomass at the Corporation Commission, and part of that solution is looking at ways to be able to deal with biomass.  He encouraged APS in those discussions to make sure the Corporation Commissioners understand the importance and necessity that something be done about biomass.  He stated that the Corporation Commission will have a work session at the end of April and turn it into a rule where it would require the utilities to purchase power directly from a biomass facility.  The Board thanked Mr. Traver for addressing the Board today.

Budget Presentations :  Bryan Layton addressed the Board.

a.
Public Works/Planning & Zoning - John Osgood :  John Osgood gave a brief presentation of the Public Works Budget. He introduced the leadership team for Public Works. He spoke about how Brandt Clark is a critical member of their team.  He stated that the Planning/Zoning and Building division of Public Works exists to protect quality of life and ensure the safe and orderly growth of development in the unincorporated areas of Navajo County.  He spoke about the core functions of each division.  He thanked the Board and current administration, as well as past Boards and administration, for the leadership it takes to face difficult financial realities, to make tough decisions and keep moving forward the best we can. He further thanked staff for taking on more and more responsibility.  He indicated that there has been a 62% reduction in staff since 2008. He spoke about the positions then and now and the increase in workload and responsibility.  He stated that the Chief Building Official/Administrator covers 3 areas which includes road maintenance operation. He spoke about the key budget elements with an extremely lean staff.  He stated that revenue generated by building activities has been trending upward for the past four or five years, while the staff levels have remained the same.  Jeanine Carruthers spoke about revenue starting to stabilize after the 2012 low.  Mr. Osgood spoke about the trend in total permits.  He stated that the 3 building inspectors perform approximately 9 inspections per day.  Mr. Osgood spoke about their aging fleet and the harder and more expensive it is to maintain. Rick Denton stated that when we have mechanical failures on these older vehicles, they are not quick fixes, and they are expensive to fix which takes one inspector out of the field due to not having another vehicle in place. Mr. Osgood spoke briefly on the salary comparison across the state in Public Works departments and indicated that we are the only county which does not have a Planning and Zoning Director.  He stated that if revenues continue to decrease, the reduction target for Planning and Zoning and Building in year two of the plan would be about $43,000, which is about 10% of their budget, and they would have to eliminate a position which would have serious impacts to their services.  He spoke about those impacts.  He stated that we would have to have a policy discussion with the Board about not being able to have Code Enforcement.  He indicated that a reduced staffing level could impact our community insurance service office rating.  He spoke about conversations with our neighboring cities and towns regarding our ability to provide septic inspections for those agencies.

Vice-Chairman Whiting stated that what he is understanding is that the activity levels is back to the highs; however, staff is down from 13 to 5, yet, they still have plan reviews within 2 to 3 weeks, 24 hours notice on inspections, so, there have been significant cuts already. He spoke about how most of the staff drive their own vehicles, and the County vehicles in use have an average of 171,000 miles on them.  He stated that even though we have been down in staff, we are providing services at a similar level, but that won't be able to continue.  He indicated that he is not proud that we pay our people the least of any other county.

Supervisor Williams thanked Mr. Osgood for his presentation, and he believes that these presentations have been a great education for the Board and our communities. The Board thanked Mr. Osgood for his presentation.

Navajo County Public Health Services District, Board of Director's Session - Jeff Lee :
Supervisor Thompson
made a motion to move into the Navajo County Public Health Services Board of Directors Session; motion seconded by
Supervisor Williams .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Jeff Lee presented the Navajo County Public Health Services District budget. He spoke about accomplishments over the past year, including the Community Health Assessment and Community Health Improvement plan, Tobacco Youth Coalition engagement with City Officials, Children with special needs resource fair, accreditation site visit and interactive dashboard for the public.  He stated that animal control responded to over 1300 calls for service this past year, and our medical investigators accepted 140 medical examiner cases.  He spoke about staffing levels in 2008 being 62 staff, and now they have 42 staff and are therefore asking staff to do more with less.  He indicated that every staff member is asked to take on multiple programs and multiple projects with salaries that do not compare, and recruitment is scarce.  He stated that last week our Environmental Health Program Manager submitted his retirement letter, and we have over 700 establishments which we inspect in  a year. He spoke about Navajo County Health Department vs Coconino County Health Department and indicated that both departments do the same thing, with Navajo County staff at 42 and Coconino County at 118.  He gave kuddos to our staff for doing as much as they do with as little as we have.

He stated that our property tax levy is a little over $2.1 million, 59% of which is paid out to nontraditional public health programs; 22%  goes to state mandated payments; and 18% is the Health Department operating budget for the year, which includes 16% to non-grant funded positions.  He stated that we will have to make some significant changes due to the closing of Cholla and if Proposition 421 does not pass. He indicated that as a result, we will not be able to maintain the level of services that we currently offer.  He spoke about the specific impacts, including the following:

In order for Public Health to be able to continue providing those services, the distribution of our funding has to change.  Prop 421 to establish a Jail District would help alleviate the amount spent from Public Health to pay for Detention (Jail) Nursing services.  Without this new revenue source to help pay for Detention Nursing, we simply do not have a way to overcome the loss of property tax revenue from Cholla closing.

STD's

Navajo County has the 2nd highest rates of Gonorrhea, Syphilis and Chlamydia in Arizona

1,168 positive STD cases were identified, and surveillance completed - 1.5 staff

Disease Outbreaks

Vaccination rates will decrease, decreased ability to perform disease surveillance, will create a backlog of disease investigations - increasing exposure

2,484 infectious disease cases were investigated, and surveillance completed - 1.5 staff

2,218 clients received 1 or more Vaccinations this year - 2.5 staff

Mr. Lee stated that if Proposition 421 does not pass, combined with Cholla closing, unless a grant pays for the program or service, we cannot do it.  He spoke about the Emergency Management Department and stated that for every dollar we spend on the grant, we have to match it with a non-federal source of funds, which comes from the Health District general fund and also the County general fund.  He stated that the Emergency Management Coordinator position will remain vacant until the results of Proposition 421.   He spoke about the Department of Homeland Security Grants and stated that any reduction would cause us to not be able to sustain those grants, which would be roughly $900,000.  He indicated that, currently, they split fund two positions with other departments, and we would have to look at that more and look at the non-matched grant and see how we can better utilize it.  He stated that we already rely heavily on other departments to staff the EOC, and any reduction would cause them to rely more heavily on those departments who are facing reductions, as well. He indicated that we would have to only do 2 to 3 community events a month nstead of 4 to 5. He stated that there would be an increased need for volunteers.  He spoke about some of the trends they are seeing is increased grant deliverables with decreased funding amounts and increased match requirements, and most of the new grant opportunities are short term, one to two years, or one-time funding.  Mr. Lee spoke about the changes and innovative use of funds that are currently used.

He spoke about the road ahead for the Health District, including the following:

Must replace property tax revenue from Cholla.

Continued investment into staff and assets

Professional development for staff, need to maintain certifications and training required to receive grant funding.

Reclassification of positions to meet the new position demands.

National conferences, travel, equipment that is also required to receive grant funding and maintain nursing accreditations.

Increasing billable nursing service.

Well woman's, biometric, shot clinics

While sustaining our levels in family planning, vaccinations, infectious disease surveillance, STD Surveillance, education, intervention and increasing our clinics quality and infection control.

Department Plans

Strategic Plan

Workforce Development

Quality Improvement.
