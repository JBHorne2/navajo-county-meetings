ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES :

a.

Budget Update :  Bryan Layton gave a brief budget update.  He distributed State Shared Sales Tax Data and Navajo County Excise Tax Data.  He indicated that we had a good year, and we are leveraging that time to create strategic reductions, a sustainable 5-year budget and a responsible fund balance which will allow us to adapt to the ebb and flow of our economy and plan for challenges as they come along.  He gave an update on our facilities.  He stated that due to the projected costs of the needed repairs to the existing Show Low Court, the Courts were relocated to the White Mountain facility.  He indicated that it is a County-owned property and can accommodate Justice Court, Superior Court and Magistrate Court functions.  He stated that the majority of the renovation was done by facilities staff, and we used State Court funds.   He indicated that we also added appropriate security.
Supervisor Williams stated that he believes that this is a good example of using what we have to its maximum capacity through our partnership with Show Low.  He stated that he is hopeful in the future that there comes a point where we can talk about building a more permanent structure.   Chairwoman Whitesinger concurred with Supervisor Williams .  She stated that this is an example of managing our finances in a way that we are frugal.  She spoke about the sacrifices of other departments to best serve the public.

b.

Consideration of adoption of Resolution 16-19 updated fee schedule for Jail Usage Fees in accordance with ARS 11-252.08 and ARS 31-121(D) :  Bryan Layton discussed the item.  He spoke about speaking with city and town managers regarding the need to increase our jail fees as they have not been increased for five years.  He indicated that this fee schedule is a result of those conversations.  Supervisor Williams made a motion to adopt the updated fee schedule for Jail Usage Fees in accordance with ARS 11-252.08 and ARS 31-121(D); motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Budget Presentation:
1. Navajo County Library District Board of Directors Session : Geneva Durkee provided a children's book for each Board member.  She indicated that the Library District has a staff of three which will be changing soon due to an employee starting nurses training.  She spoke about the accomplishments of the Library District, including the rotation of the large print, music CD's, foreign and native language and ABC collections.   She spoke about the E-rate funding received and enhancing the E-book and E-audiobook collections by going with CloudLink.  She further spoke about the Polacca Branch library at the First Mesa Youth Center.  She stated that the Office of Navajo Nation Library was migrated into our Symphony system.  She spoke about the progress of the McNary Library.  She stated that she applied and received a Bookapalooza Grant from ALSC.  She indicated that she was able to obtain a lot of items at the Arizona State Surplus for very little money.  She spoke about the challenges that the Library District faces due to budget cuts, including aging computers and technology, increasing demand for expensive E-books and E-audiobooks, increase in minimum wage, small District Offices which need improvement, an aging vehicle, and program increases and processing materials for 8 libraries with a small staff.  Supervisor Thompson thanked Ms. Durkee for the book and the good things that she does.   The Board thanked Ms. Durkee for the presentation and all she does.

d.

Presentation from the Auditor General :  Paige Peterson introduced Donna Miller, director of the Financial Audit Division, from the Office of the Auditor General.  Ms. Miller stated that the new auditor general is Lyndsey Perry.  She introduced the members of the audit team who will be conducting Navajo County's FY19 audit.  She stated that the Office of the Auditor General is charged with the responsibility to audit the financial statements of all counties of the State of Arizona, as well as federal programs. She indicated that they value their relationship with Navajo County in getting financial statements and reports done in a timely manner.  She stated that in FY2010, Navajo County issued its first ever comprehensive annual financial report, and the County received its certificate of excellence in Government financial reporting.  She indicated that the County also received the certificate this last year.  She congratulated Navajo County and Ms. Peterson's team for doing a great job in getting that certificate.  She stated that the County continues to be receptive to improving its internal controls over financial reporting.  She indicated that they will work closely with Navajo County to implement recommendations to eliminate the need to report findings.    She spoke about the single audit report where findings are found with regard to internal control responsibilities and audit over federal grant programs.  She stated that her office appreciates the assistance of the County to help complete these audits.  She spoke about Ms. Peterson's commitment to resolve any auditing questions and that the County allowed auditors direct access to their network so that they have not had to travel as often which helped them complete the audit on a timely basis.  She thanked Ms. Peterson and the finance staff for their hard work.  Vice-Chairman Whiting thanked Ms. Miller for the ongoing support and teamwork.  He spoke about how Ms. Peterson works hard and that she and her team are responsive.   Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked Ms. Miller for the presentation and spoke about the importance of checks and balances.

SHERIFF'S OFFICE :

a.

Presentation of the Navajo County Sheriff's Office (NCSO) Veteran's Program :  Sheriff Clouse spoke about the NCSO Veteran's Program.  He indicated that they are looking for programs to help offenders not to reoffend.  He spoke about the successes in Veteran's Court and stated that not one veteran who has graduated has reoffended.  He indicated that on May 8th, they moved all the veterans who qualify into a separate pod which can hold 8 veterans, and they will be working towards getting those veterans into Veteran's Court.  He stated that he is hopeful that when someone is incarcerated, if they are a veteran, they can get them into this pod and help those veterans get the services which will help them to get on a pathway towards success.  He indicated that the veterans are dressed differently with a camouflage top to set them apart, and they have more privileges and higher standards to maintain.  He indicated that last week, he  went to Coconino County, and Sheriff Driscoll invited him to tour their jail.  He stated that Coconino County Jail has a lot of great programs, and in one of their programs, with those that enroll and graduate, they are only seeing 8% of those inmates reoffending, with the national average being 50% to 80%.  He indicated that in Navajo County, we are trying to find areas where we see the best return on our investment and will continue to build off this Veteran's Program.  He thanked Ernie Garcia and his lieutenants for making this happen.  Supervisor Williams inquired whether a veteran is automatically eligible, and Sheriff Clouse indicated that they are all considered, but there are disqualifiers, including if they are incarcerated for a short period, if they are a danger to self or others, and based on the crime they charged with.  He stated that if a veteran signs a contract to come into the pod, they are required to attend the classes.  Supervisor Jack thanked Sheriff Clouse for bringing these programs to the County and indicated that this is needed by the veterans.   He stated that these programs do work.  Sheriff Clouse stated that those who serve deserve this program.  Vice-Chairman Whiting expressed appreciation to Sheriff Clouse for looking for additional ways to provide benefits to our citizens, and he looks forward to hearing from him in the future.  Supervisor Thompson inquired as to what traditional healings are provided to go along with this program, and Sheriff Clouse stated that Coconino County has a Hogan and a Sweat Lodge, and they bring in a Medicine Man from Tuba City, and they are able to do that because of having a jail district and stable funding.   He stated that he is speaking with a gentleman from Pinon and Hardrock who will be doing a talking circle.   He indicated that there are great volunteers and mentors who work to make this happen.  Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked Sheriff Clouse for his work on this.

PUBLIC WORKS :

a.

Consideration of Task Agreement No. 15 to Memorandum of Agreement between the Bureau of Indian Affairs Department of Transportation- Navajo Region and Navajo County for the funding of purchasing culvert and aggregate material for BIA route 8068 within the Pinon and Blue Gap communities :  John Osgood discussed the item.  He stated that 90% of the school bus routes serving Pinon and Blue Gap are unpaved dirt roads, and this would repair approximately a 1-mile segment of N8068.  He indicated that approval allows Navajo County to partner with BIA Chinle Agency by providing gravel material and corrugated metal pipes for the drainage repair.  He stated that the source of funds is HURF which is dedicated solely for transportation and maintenance use, the fiscal impact is not to exceed $90,000, and there is adequate funding available in the Public Works Department CIP Special road projects for District I.  Supervisor Jack made a motion to approve Task Agreement No. 15 to Memorandum of Agreement between the Bureau of Indian Affairs Department of Transportation - Navajo Region and Navajo County for the funding of purchasing culvert and aggregate material for BIA Route 8068 within the Pinon and Blue Gap communities; motion seconded by Supervisor Thompson .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

Consideration of approving the acceptance of School House Lane into Navajo County Maintained Road System :  Bill Bess discussed the item.  He stated that if this is approved, they are going to put in about a half-mile county maintained road, one-tenth of a mile of pavement in front of the school and one-half mile of permanent graded road.  He indicated that we have the budget to maintain this road, and it will be in his annual HURF report which will provide additional monies.  Supervisor Williams stated that this has been a long effort, and he supports it.  S upervisor Williams made a motion to approve the acceptance of School House Lane into Navajo County Maintained Road System; motion seconded by Supervisor Whiting .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.
