SHERIFF'S OFFICE :

a.

Consideration of a Proclamation declaring April 23, 2019, as National Telecommunicator's Day in Navajo County, Arizona :  Sheriff Clouse read the proposed proclamation and requested that April 23, 2019 be designated as National Telecommunicator's Day in Navajo County, Arizona.  He indicated that these dispatchers are the voice behind the badge and answer an average of over 12,000 calls per month with 11 dispatchers for Navajo County.  He stated that there have been 12,838 calls this year to our deputies, fire and EMS personnel.  Vice-Chairman Whiting thanked the Sheriff's Office and asked that the Sheriff thank the dispatchers for the Board of Supervisors.  Supervisor Williams made a motion to approve a Proclamation declaring April 23, 2019, as National Telecommunicator's Day in Navajo County, Arizona; motion seconded by Vice-Chairman Whiting .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

b.

Consideration of a Proclamation declaring May 5-11 as "Correctional Officers Week" in Navajo County :  Sheriff Clouse read the proposed proclamation and requested that May 5 - 11, 2019 be designated as Correctional Officers Week in Navajo County.  He stated that on average, Navajo County Correctional Officers book 600 inmates per month and 7,000 per year, and they serve over 1,000 meals per day and 375,000 per year.  He spoke about the different programs that go on in the jail from GED programs to drug addiction programs.  He indicated that this is a critical part of the Navajo County Sheriff's Office.  Supervisor Williams thanked those correction officers and made a motion to approve a Proclamation declaring May 5-11 as "Correctional Officers Week" in Navajo County; motion seconded by Vice-Chairman Whiting .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

c.

Budget Presentations :  Bryan Layton introduced the budget presentations.

a.
Superintendent of Schools - Jalyn Gerlich, Superintendent of Schools, gave a budget presentation. She spoke about processing payroll for the 2,492 school district employees in Navajo County. She indicated that she has 4 employees who serve 14 districts. She stated that the turnaround for payroll is 7 business days every two weeks.  She spoke about the legal and moral obligation to pay people on time.  She further spoke about the Uniform System of Financial Records (USFR) for Arizona school districts, which is the Auditor General's interpretation of statutes and regulations by which they have to abide, in addition to the Uniform Accounting Manual for Arizona County School Superintendents. She indicated that during the summer, they are closing out the fiscal year, inputting and getting ready for the new school year, processing completion reports for grants, working on new grants coming in, working on tax rates, and there is still payroll and expense vouchers to process for district staff who work during the summer. She indicated that they processed over $20 million of funds this past year. She indicated that they are down a staff person and have the same duties and statutory obligations.  She thanked the Treasurer's Office and her office staff for taking on more responsibility and stepping up and stated that they are good and dedicated people who make a difference every single day.
Chairman Thompson stated that we are asking our staff members to take care of themselves as they are needed by their supervisors and the public.
Supervisor Williams inquired how many team members were in the Superintendent of Schools 10 years ago, and Ms. Gerlich stated that there were five and a few years before that, there were six.
Supervisor Williams indicated that one of the Superintendents indicated that if payroll was not processed on time, then the districts would have to get into their line of credit, and Ms. Gerlich stated that they were referencing if cash from property taxes does not get processed in a timely manner and their cash flow goes down below a certain level, then they have to trigger into their line of credit.
Supervisor Thompson and
Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked the Schools office and the reminder of how departments function and what the organization looks like.

c.
Public Fiduciary - Norman Turley gave a short budget presentation.  He spoke about the duties and roles of the Public Fiduciary.  He indicated that they do 61 guardianships/conservatorships, 9 personal representative cases, 29 representative payee cases, are the trustee for 2 trusts and have administered 8 indigent burials for the county.  He spoke about the accomplishments of the Public Fiduciary, including public surplus auctions, long-term care for clients, maintaining  required bi-monthly visits to clients and keeping their licenses current.  He stated that in 2008, they had 8 full-time employees and now have 5.75 employees. He stated that the case managers have between 20 and 30 cases each.  He indicated that the average miles on their vehicles is 176,000 miles.  He spoke about dealing with citizens who are most vulnerable, exploited or taken advantage of.  He stated that their numbers are increasing, and a reduction in staff would make it much more difficult.
Supervisor Thompson indicated that these services are needed.
Supervisor Jack thanked the Public Fiduciary for their hard work and heart.  He inquired whether these services are open to all citizens in the County, and Mr. Turley indicated that these services are for everyone. He stated that the Tribe usually steps up and takes care of those on the Reservation; however, we do have a few tribal members to whom they offer services.
Chairwoman Whitesinger inquired regarding the recommended average case loads, and Mr. Turley indicated that right now, we average about 69 wards with 8 pending, and the national average to be effective is 30-35 cases per case worker. He stated that if we had to cut anymore, it could limit the amount of cases that we could take.
Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked Mr. Turley for his presentation and recognized him and his team for their servant's heart.

d. Probation - Jason Cash   Bryan Layton requested that this presentation be tabled to the next meeting.

REGULAR AGENDA :

1.

Presentation on the projected 2019 wildfire season :  Catrina Jenkins gave a presentation on the projected 2019 wildfire season. She spoke about the widespread improvement in drought conditions across the state.  She further spoke about the significant wildland fire potential outlook for April, May, June and July, 2019.  She stated that our Energy Release Component (ERC) is below the maximum.  She spoke about our White Mountain Fire Restrictions Working (WMFRW) Group and introduced those present from that group. She stated that there are weekly phone calls where they discuss the weather outlook, forest conditions and area events that bring people to the area. She asked that our citizens and visitors be our partner in preventing wildfires. She stated that fire season is always a potential and not just a couple of months anymore. She spoke about the social media contacts and the Ready Navajo County alert system. Chairwoman Whitesinger thanked Ms. Jenkins and those present today for their efforts and time.

2.

Public Hearing: Consideration of the repeal of the Outdoor Fire Ordinance, Ordinance Number 01-17, and adoption of a new Outdoor Fire Ordinance, Ordinance Number 01-19 :  Catrina Jenkins discussed the item. She indicated that the WMFRW Group have been working for months on this ordinance. She spoke about some changes, including the smoking section of this ordinance; littering is a criminal offense; Stage 3 was removed, which is closure; and under the criminal penalties, it is now a class one misdemeanor, which allows judges to potentially sentence jail time.  Chairwoman Whitesinger opened the public hearing, there being no public comments, closed the public hearing. Supervisor Thompson thanked Ms. Jenkins for keeping the Board informed and that the Board will do their part to keep the communities informed.  Supervisor Thompson made a motion to repeal the Outdoor Fire Ordinance, Ordinance Number 01-17, and adopt Outdoor Fire Ordinance, Ordinance Number 01-19 ; motion seconded by Supervisor Jack .  Vote unanimous approving the motion.

PUBLIC HEARING : Consideration and adoption of Resolution Number 11-19 approving/denying a Special Use Permit to allow for the construction of a self-mini storage unit facility that will accommodate a commercial buffer, individual storage units, and covered storage spaces for boats and RVs on a parcel which is zoned C-R on the front 300ft and A-General for the back portion, APN 409-20-015S, a portion of Township 10 North, Range 21 East, Section 4, in the Show Low area :  Sandra Phillips discussed the item indicating that this property is zoned SD and is surrounded by R-3 land to the west, R-1 land to the north, municipal land to the east and C-R land to the south. She stated that the applicant has submitted plans, and Planning and Zoning approved the application unanimously. Chairwoman Whitesinger opened the public hearing. Ms. Christina Peterson, 1057 Chamberlain Way, spoke in opposition of the item.  She stated that she has never spoken to Mr. Fernau, so, she does not know where his statements that he has been working with neighbors comes from.  She spoke about the Linden Mini-Storage. She stated that this is the wrong plot of land for the wrong facility with the wrong builder.  She spoke about the flooding that happens to her land and will happen if this is approved. The applicant, Mr. Fernau, spoke about the flooding and indicated that the water does not come from his property.  He stated that he is building this with his own funds.  He indicated that what he wants to build will be an improvement and will escalate property values.  Supervisor Williams inquired whether Mr. Fernau has had a chance to review the 12 stipulations to which the applicant would need to conform, and  Mr. Fernau stated that he has reviewed those, and the only one he does not agree with is the solid fence.  Ms. Peterson spoke about requirements for retention basins.  There being no further comments, Chairwoman Whitesinger closed the public hearing.
At 10:54 Supervisor Jack left the meeting.
Sandra Phillips spoke about the comments regarding drainage. She indicated that the applicant had dug the retention basin in the interim since he cleared the lot, so, he did not adversely impact his neighbors downstream.  She stated that they will need to demonstrate in the design permit process that it meets all the draining concerns of Navajo County and ADWR.   As to the solid fence, she stated that they are going to work with the applicant on what is agreeable to shield the storage unit, and he further will have to conform to the stipulations.
