BOARD OF SUPERVISORS:  
a. Quality Emulsions and Navajo County Recognizing Excellence: Through the Quality Emulsions and Navajo 
County Recognizing Excellence program, Cheryl Powell recognized Bill Dalton for his outstanding

b. Navajo County Cost Cutting Awards: Through the Navajo County Cost Cutting Awards program, Dan Hinz 
recognized Bob Durant, Billy Johnson and Carlos Attakai for their efforts in cost savings as it relates to

c. PUBLIC HEARING: Liquor License: Consideration and possible recommendation for approval of new 
liquor license application #07090046 for Kelley’s Restaurant & Lounge located at 3973 White Mountain 
Boulevard in Lakeside, AZ  Lorinda Brown reviewed the proposed license application. She reported that this 
is a person transfer, location transfer and applicant has purchased the license from Pats. This property has 
been posted for the requested amount of time and all required departments have been notified with no 
negative reports. Gwendolyn Kelley addressed the board regarding her experience in the restaurant business 
and spoke of her father who recently passed. She indicated that she is dedicating the restaurant to her father. 
Supervisor Thompson asked if this was a packaged liquor carry out. Ms. Kelley indicated that there is a 
stipulation for carry out of packaged beer or wine. Ms. Kelley further indicated that customers can purchase 
a bottle of wine with dinner and then it can be packaged and carried out. Supervisor Tenney made a motion 
to recommend approval of the application for liquor license for Kelley’s Restaurant & Lounge located at 
3973 White Mountain Boulevard in Lakeside, AZ, APN 212-18-033B; motion seconded by Supervisor 
DeSpain; vote unanimous approving the motion.

d. Consideration and possible approval of the Navajo County 2009 list for Congression al funding: Homero Vela 
requested approval of the Navajo County 2009 list for Congressional funding. Mr. Vela requests the following 
projects be presented to the Congressional Delegation as the Navajo County 2009 priorities and that these 
priorities be used to drive efforts to obtain legislative funding. Mr. Vela requests input from the Board members 
and reads the list of items; Winslow Levee -the feasibility study that which will require $500,000 additional 
dollars of federal mo nies for the next fiscal cycle; Statewide Strategy for Restoring Arizona’s Forests -This 
project will provide for healthier and safer forests and create new jobs; Obed Bridge-This projects is estimated to 
cost $3.6 million and will provide a full level of safety for unrestricted on e lane traffic. The Obed Bridge design 
should be completed April of 2009; Dilkon Road Yard - Navajo County has partnered with Navajo Nation to 
build a road yard in the Dilkon area to support and make more efficient the NDOT road maintenance efforts. The 
cost for the Dilkon Road Yard is estimated at $1.8 million; Pinon Road Yard -Navajo County has partnered with 
Navajo Nation to build a road yard in the Pinon area to support and make more efficient the NDOT road 
maintenance efforts. The total costs for the Pin on Road Yard is estimated at $1.4 million; Lone Pine Dam Road 
Roundabout-The proposed roundabout will provide for a safer intersection. This project cost is estimated at 
$480,000 and all design is complete; Navajo County Jail Facility -A new jail facility i s urgently needed to 
accommodate inmate population.    Supervisor Thompson indicates that all these projects are very important and 
hopes that in time we will be able to bring the funding to get all of these projects done. Supervisor Thompson 
introduces Jackie Burbank with the Navajo Area Agency on Aging from Chinle Arizona. Supervisor Tenney and 
Dusty Parsons discussed the Winslow Levee. Supervisor Tenney re commends that the Statewide Strategy for 
Restoring Arizona’s Forests be switched to number one, placing the Winslow Levee at number two. Supervisor 
Tenney and Dusty Parsons discuss the top two projects on the priorities list. Supervisor DeSpain indicates that 
the County has maintained that we will continue to make the Winslow Levee a huge priority, as it  has been for 
years. Supervisor DeSpain suggests that we continue to make the Winslow Levee a priority and make the list a 
bulleted list rather than numbers. Supervisors continued discussion on the priority of each item on the list. 10:00 
a.m. Jonathan Nez , Supervisor participated by telephone which was on speaker in the chambers so all 
comments were audible to supervisors and participating audience members.   Mr. Parsons indicate d that 
switching the top two priorities will not hurt our efforts and our lobby ist will continue to lobby on each of these 
priorities. Supervisor Nez apologize d for his lateness. James Jayne gave an update on information received 
regarding HR1, the American recovery and Reinvestment Act, which is  currently being considered by the US 
Congress. He indicated that t hey have identified $4.5 billion going to the Army Corps of Engineers for flood 
protection and environmental restoration. Mr. Jayne states that the Tomhave’s are lobbying for some of that to 
go towards the Winslow Levee; Mr. Jayne states that un der Forestry $550 million is allocated u nder the current 
version of HR1. Supervisors and Dusty Parsons continue discussion on the 2009 priority list. By consensus of 
the Board, the Statewide Strategy for Restoring Arizona’s Forests will  be placed at the top of the list with the 
Winslow Levee to be placed below it; n umbers should be stricken and bullets be placed beside each priority.
